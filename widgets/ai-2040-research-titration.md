summary_for_tutor: "A static diagram placing four approaches to titrating AI research and development on a descending diagonal between two axes: ease of implementation on the horizontal axis, running from Hard on the left to Easy on the right and labelled 'How little regulatory competence is required', and regulatory accuracy on the vertical axis, running from Low at the bottom to High at the top and labelled 'How well the approach tracks the correct research speed'. Top left, hardest to implement but most accurate, sits 'Safety case burden of proof' (all R&D requires strong safety case arguments before approval), then 'Quality ad-hoc rules' (case-by-case decisions on permitted research directions and speed), then 'Human-interpretable requirement' (all approved techniques need to be understood end-to-end by some group of humans), and bottom right, easiest but least accurate, 'Compute caps' (simple caps on R&D compute). An arrow labelled 'Suggested progression' runs up the diagonal from the easy, low-accuracy corner to the hard, high-accuracy corner, with '~2030' marked beside compute caps and '~2035' beside the safety case burden of proof: start with the crude but verifiable cap and escalate towards the more accurate regimes as regulatory capacity grows and capabilities rise."
