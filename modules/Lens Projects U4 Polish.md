\## This unit prepares you for Meeting 4

In Meeting 4 you bring one point from your review that you rejected, and your reason. The group takes your reviewer's side and tests that reason. Then each person says what is finished and what is still a labelled gap. Bring the rejected point, your draft write-up, and your report card, filled in before the meeting.

\## In this unit

- Write a review of your partner's draft against the rubric, and send it within three days
- Revise from the review you received: what you changed and why

\## How much to do

- **Minimum:** a write-up of what you tried and why your idea changed. A pivot post counts.
- **Core:** a finished piece of work and a draft write-up.
- **Stretch:** feedback from a reviewer outside the course.

# Lens: Review your partner's draft
id:: 4633b6d6-edec-41c3-b64b-0250abbcf5a1
tldr:: Read your partner's draft against five questions and write a review they can act on. Send it within three days.
summary_for_tutor:: The learner writes a peer review of their partner's Lens Projects draft against five rubric questions: is the audience specific, is the change observable, are the claims supported, are limitations stated, is it in the author's own voice. Each answer is one open question. Help them make their review specific and actionable; never write the review for them, and do not judge the partner's draft yourself since you cannot see it.
duration_minutes:: 60
#### Text
content::
\## How to write a useful review

A good review is specific, kind and actionable. For each point, quote or point to the exact place in the draft, say what you noticed, and suggest one thing the author could do. Start with what works; then give at most three things to change, most important first.

\## The rubric

Read your partner's draft once all the way through, then answer the five questions below. Copy your answers into a message to your partner within three days.

# Lens: Revise from your review
id:: 0703cd31-2960-4890-875b-f1a2843c3ffa
tldr:: Decide what to act on from the review you received, and bring one point you rejected to Meeting 4.
summary_for_tutor:: The learner revises their own Lens Projects project after receiving a peer review. Two open questions: what they changed and why, and one review point they rejected with their reason. Respect their decisions; help them make the reasons specific.
duration_minutes:: 30
#### Text
content::
\## You decide what to change

A review is information, not instructions. Act on the points that make the work better for its audience. For any point you reject, write down why: in Meeting 4, the group will take your reviewer's side and test your reason.

#### Question: Open
id:: 0c3b35e5-934a-422d-84da-f4d78bb30e90
content:: What did you change because of the review, and why?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is reporting changes made after a peer review of their Lens Projects project. Under 100 words, one or two replies. Check each change is tied to a specific review point. If they changed nothing, ask which point came closest to changing their mind. No generic praise. Do not grade.

#### Question: Open
id:: 61adf61f-51b1-4c98-81fc-1bd5fcddf1e5
content:: Name one review point you **rejected**, and your reason.
feedback-instructions:: The learner names a peer review point they rejected for their Lens Projects project, and their reason. Under 100 words, one reply. Briefly argue the reviewer's side once, as the group will in Meeting 4, then ask one question that tests their reason. Respect their decision. No generic praise. Do not grade.

# Lens: Write it up
id:: aaf4b9b0-479f-4501-96d9-b302d345df90
tldr:: A write-up template, your three-sentence story, and an optional critique chat. AI critiques; you write.
summary_for_tutor:: The Lens Projects write-up template (problem, audience, what you did, what happened, prediction check, limitations, what's next, How I used AI), an open question for the learner's three-sentence story, and an optional chat where you critique their draft for clarity and unsupported claims. Never write or rewrite their text: the course rule is to use AI as a tool, not a decision-maker, and the write-up must be in their own voice.
duration_minutes:: 45
#### Text
content::
\## The write-up template

Whatever you made, write a short piece about it, in your own words. Use these headings:

1. **The problem**, and why it matters for AI safety
2. **The audience**: who this is for
3. **What you did**
4. **What happened**: results, responses, what you learned
5. **Prediction check**: did the change you predicted happen?
6. **Limitations**: what a sceptic would say, and what you are unsure of
7. **What's next**
8. **How I used AI**: one or two sentences, built from your report card disclosure lines

On the Fit track, this is your career doc or career transition post: what you tried, what you noticed, what you ruled in or out, and your next step.

Your write-up is written by you, with AI for critique only. Some programmes check applications for LLM-written text, and LessWrong moderates LLM-written posts.

#### Question: Open
id:: a4812d18-a2ad-484a-bedb-982834182d63
content:: **Your three-sentence story:** the problem, what you did, and what you found or why your idea changed.
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a three-sentence story of their Lens Projects project: the problem, what they did, what they found or why their idea changed. Under 100 words, at most two replies. Check each sentence does one job and the third states a finding or change, not a hope. Point to the weakest sentence only. Never rewrite it. No generic praise. Do not grade.

#### Text
content::
\## Optional: get a critique of your draft

Paste a section of your draft below and ask for a critique. The tutor points out what is unclear or unsupported; you do the rewriting.

#### Chat
instructions:: Optional critique chat in Unit 4 of Lens Projects. The learner pastes part of their write-up. Critique it for clarity and unsupported claims only: quote the exact phrase, say what a reader would stumble on or doubt, and ask one question. At most three points per reply, under 150 words. Never rewrite sentences or offer replacement text, even if asked; explain that the course rule is to use AI as a tool, not a decision-maker, and the write-up must stay in their own voice.
