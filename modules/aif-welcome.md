# Lens: Welcome

#### Text
content::
Welcome to **AI Futurism**, a four-week course on key future dynamics in frontier AI development, based on the reading list curated by Alexa Pan for Redwood Research's Astra Strategy Fellowship.

The readings introduce conceptual frames and hypotheses in AI futurism that many practitioners find promising and/or that have stood the test of time. As you read, keep asking: do I agree with the thesis? How have its predictions fared in light of recent evidence?

The four weeks build on each other: **timelines and takeoff** (how fast is this coming?), **takeover threat modeling** (what could go wrong?), **control** (what can we do about it?), and **governance** (who decides?). Each week also has an optional-readings module, and a final optional module collects extended readings for going deeper.

# Lens: How to use this course
id:: 0af36123-ce69-4296-b4f8-61c5c61a1ae8
tldr:: How much time do you actually need, what does the star mean, and what is that chat box at the end of each reading for? A quick orientation to how the course works, plus the questions to hold as Week 1 dives into timelines and takeoff.
summary_for_tutor:: Practical how-to lens for the course. Explains the weekly structure (under 8 hours of reading plus a group meeting), the priority-reading marker, that optional modules and lenses are genuinely optional, that exercises appear as standalone lenses meant for discussion groups, and that reading lenses end in a tutor chat intended for testing understanding rather than summarizing. Also previews Week 1's timelines-and-takeoff questions.

#### Text
content::
- Each week covers under 8 hours of foundational context on one topic, followed by a group meeting.
- Readings marked ⭐ are the priority if you only have ~1 hour.
- Modules and lenses marked *optional* are exactly that: follow curiosity, not completionism.
- Exercises come as separate lenses right after their reading, and are designed for discussion groups; bring your answers to the meeting.
- Most reading lenses end in a tutor chat; use it to test your understanding against the reading, not to summarize it.

**This week: timelines and takeoff.** Key questions to hold while reading:
- What are key milestones to track in AI development?
- When will powerful AIs arrive? (Timelines)
- How quickly will powerful AIs arrive? (Takeoff speeds)
- What do existing models say about these questions, and what are their key assumptions and parameters?
- How powerful are AIs currently?
- What implications does timelines/takeoff modeling have for AI strategy?
