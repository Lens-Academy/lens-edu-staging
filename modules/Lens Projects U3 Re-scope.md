# Lens: Small working versions win
id:: c3d42a77-ea86-4615-b5c5-dd049959c0f1
tldr:: Why small working versions beat big plans, why work expands to fill the time, and how to decide when time runs out instead of extending.
summary_for_tutor:: A short page on scope for Lens Projects: Gall's Law (complex systems that work grow from simple systems that worked), Parkinson's Law (work expands to fill the time available), timeboxing (when time is up, decide rather than extend), and start, stop, continue retrospectives. No questions; the exercises follow on the next page.
duration_minutes:: 15
#### Text
content::
\## Two laws worth knowing

**Gall's Law:** a complex system that works almost always grew out of a simple system that worked. A complex system designed from scratch rarely works. For your project, that means a small finished version first, then growth, not the full version all at once.

**Parkinson's Law:** work expands to fill the time available. If you give a task a week, it takes a week. Give it a fixed, small amount of time instead.

\## Timeboxing

Give each task a fixed time before you start. When the time is up, **decide** rather than extend: is it good enough, do you cut it, or is it worth one more box of time? Writing it up usually takes longer than people expect, so leave a box of time for the write-up too.

\## Start, stop, continue

A quick way to look back on a week of work:

- **Start:** what should you begin doing?
- **Stop:** what is wasting your time?
- **Continue:** what is working?

# Lens: Hand over your draft
id:: d45b4d9c-445a-4c54-bc49-5a178e92d481
tldr:: Give your review partner a draft a stranger could read, with the gaps labelled, so their reading time goes on what matters.
summary_for_tutor:: Instructions for handing a draft to the review partner in Lens Projects, and one open question confirming what they handed over and where the gaps are. Do not write the draft or the note.
duration_minutes:: 15
#### Text
content::
\## A draft a stranger can read

Your partner reads your draft live in Meeting 3. Before then, share it in whatever form fits your project (a document link, a notebook, a page) with a short note at the top:

- **What this is** and who it is for, in two sentences
- **What is finished** and what is not
- **Gaps, labelled:** mark each missing part with what will go there
- **The question you most want answered** by a reader

A rough draft with honest labels is more useful to a reviewer than a polished draft that hides its gaps.

#### Question: Open
id:: ba09778b-6189-4739-91c6-2055f8ae4c4d
content:: Paste the note you put at the top of your draft. Have you shared it with your review partner?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is handing a draft of their Lens Projects project to a review partner, with a note covering what it is and who it is for, what is finished, labelled gaps, and the question they most want answered. Under 100 words, one reply. Point out any of the four parts that is missing or vague. If they have not shared it yet, ask when they will. No generic praise. Do not grade.
