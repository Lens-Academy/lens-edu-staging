%% Library module of Emotional Abundance for the school "CBT and the third wave" (Beck and Ellis, ACT, MBCT, DBT, process-based therapy). Built around the one disagreement inside this school that a learner can test on themselves: change a thought's content (Beck, Ellis) or change one's relationship to it (ACT, MBCT). Primary voices: Beck's 2004 interview (Beck Institute), Ellis's essay on the ego, Hayes and Hofmann 2017, Hayes's TEDx talk, the ACBS definitions, Linehan on radical acceptance. Evidence page uses Cuijpers's meta-analyses, the ACT dispute (Öst versus Atkins and Hayes), the CBT decline dispute, Kuyken's MBCT analysis with its conflicts of interest, and van Agteren 2021 for non-clinical wellbeing. The main experiment (thought record week versus defusion week) is our combination of the CCI thought diary (modules 6 and 7) and the Hinton and Gaynor 2010 defusion homework, measured with the believability and discomfort ratings of Masuda 2004 and Larsson 2016 (Larsson compared restructuring and defusion over 5 days). Healy 2008 is cited for its surprise result (the "I am having the thought that" prefix raised believability). Required about 2 hours including the test, plus the experiment itself. The CCI modules, the Beck interview, Beck and Fleming 2021 and Hinton and Gaynor were sent to the importer on 2026-10-05, but the importer queue was stalled, so they are linked, not embedded. Once imported, the thought record steps on the Beck and Ellis page could become embedded CCI excerpts. DBT and process-based therapy are optional: DBT was built for borderline personality disorder and chronic suicidality, which is far from this course's learner, so only its idea of radical acceptance is offered. The WHO guide Doing What Matters could not be imported (the UN and WHO PDF hosts time out or block the importer), so it is linked, not embedded. %%
