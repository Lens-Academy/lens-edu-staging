# Lens: Welcome to Unit 5
id:: 86153237-ac49-4f0b-98a7-3d2724b2aac2
tldr:: Publish or share your work, check your prediction, write your project card, and practise telling the story.
summary_for_tutor:: Opening page of Unit 5 (Share) of Lens Projects. It lists the unit's goals and three levels of effort. If the learner's project is unfinished, point out that the minimum level is a project card with what they have.
duration_minutes:: 5
#### Text
content::
\## In this unit

- Publish or share your work where its audience will see it
- Check your prediction: did the change you predicted happen?
- Write your project card, including how you used AI
- Practise telling the story, out loud and in an interview roleplay

\## How much to do

- **Minimum:** a project card with what you have.
- **Core:** published or shared work, and a practised STAR story.
- **Stretch:** share it directly with three people in your audience, and write a short review of this course.

# Lens: Where to publish
id:: e365f885-257e-4906-970d-405ad3e576be
tldr:: Pick the place your audience already reads, by track, and check that place's rules on AI-written text.
summary_for_tutor:: Guidance on where to publish or share a Lens Projects project by track, including that LessWrong and the Alignment Forum moderate LLM-written posts. One open question asks where the learner will publish and why. Do not choose for them.
duration_minutes:: 15
#### Text
content::
\## Go where your audience already is

Publish where the people you named in your theory of change will actually see it.

| Track | Common places |
|---|---|
| Technical | LessWrong or the Alignment Forum, GitHub, your own site |
| Policy | The EA Forum, a briefing sent to a specific office, your own site |
| Fieldbuilding | Community channels, the EA Forum, a local group's mailing list |
| Fit | A career transition post on the EA Forum or LessWrong, or a private career doc shared with your reviewers |

Before posting, read the site's rules. LessWrong and the Alignment Forum moderate LLM-written posts, so your own writing matters there.

Publishing is not the end of sharing. Send the link directly to the people you talked to in Unit 2.

# Lens: Your project card
id:: f24a09fb-27d3-43ec-b8a0-947ee3f9f21e
tldr:: One short card you can link from any application: what it is, who it is for, what you did and learned, who reviewed it, the date, and how you used AI.
summary_for_tutor:: The learner fills in their Lens Projects project card: link, what it is and who it is for, what they did and learned, reviewers, date, and a How I used AI disclosure line. Make each field specific and honest; never write it for them. The disclosure line should reflect their report card entries.
duration_minutes:: 30
#### Text
content::
\## What a project card is

A project card is the short version of your work that you can paste into an application or link from your CV. Keep each field to a sentence or two.

The last field is your **"How I used AI"** line. Build it from the AI lines in your report cards, in your own words. Example: *"AI helped write the plotting code and red-team my claims; the question, analysis and writing are mine."*

# Lens: Tell the story
id:: 8c3bd2ce-bb65-4ae5-a6f3-cec8b99efd36
tldr:: Turn your project into a short spoken story with STAR, then practise it in an interview roleplay.
summary_for_tutor:: The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for telling the learner's Lens Projects project briefly, an open question for their STAR story and three talking points, and an interview roleplay chat where you play a mentor from a research programme interviewing them. In the roleplay, ask realistic follow-up questions; the learner answers in their own words.
duration_minutes:: 40
#### Text
content::
\## STAR: a short story that gets to the point

When someone asks "tell me about a project", answer in four parts, about a minute in total:

- **Situation:** the problem and who it affected
- **Task:** what you set out to do
- **Action:** what you actually did, including what you changed along the way
- **Result:** what happened, and what you learned

Then have three talking points ready: what you learned, what you would do differently, and what comes next.

#### Question: Open
id:: 216d9568-b594-4b3e-9006-4b1d6331f46d
content:: Write your STAR story (four short sentences) and your three talking points.
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a STAR story (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and three talking points about their Lens Projects project. Under 120 words, at most two replies. Check each part does its job in one sentence and the Result states something that happened, not a hope. Point to the weakest part only. Never rewrite it. No generic praise. Do not grade.

#### Text
content::
\## Practise out loud

Say your story out loud once before Meeting 5. Then try it below: the tutor plays a mentor from a research programme interviewing you about this project. Answer in your own words, as you would in a real interview.

#### Chat
instructions:: Interview roleplay in Unit 5 of Lens Projects. Play a mentor from an AI safety research programme interviewing the learner about their project. Open with "Tell me about a project you have worked on recently." Then ask realistic follow-ups one at a time: what they personally did, a decision they made and why, what went wrong, what they would do differently, and how they used AI. Keep each question under 40 words and stay in role. If the learner types "feedback", step out of role and, in under 120 words, name their strongest answer and one answer that was vague, then stop.
