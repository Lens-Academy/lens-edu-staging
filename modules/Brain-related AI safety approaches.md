id:: 616abdc2-dcad-49f2-89e5-ed74aaa16742
#### Text
content::
Most AI safety work treats the AI as the object of study. A distinct cluster of approaches instead looks at **brains** — because the brain is our one existing example of general intelligence that is (mostly) safe, cooperative, and steerable by innate drives. These approaches disagree about *how* the brain is relevant, and this module sorts them into three families, one per learning outcome:

- **A. NeuroAI for AI safety** — use neuroscience to *build, align, and interpret* the AI we make. The AI need not be brain-like; we import safety-relevant properties of brains into it. (Mineault et al.'s seven approaches.)
- **B. Brain-like AGI safety** — assume the dangerous future AGI is *itself* built on the brain's high-level learning algorithms, and align it by reverse-engineering the brain's innate reward/steering system. (Byrnes.)
- **C. Human-side strategies** — change the *human* side rather than (only) the AI: brain-computer interfaces and the "merge," cognitive augmentation, and whole-brain emulation.

Keep the sharpest distinction in mind as you go: **A informs the AI we build (which may not resemble a brain); B assumes the AI is brain-like; C changes the human side.** The families overlap — interpretability bridges A and mainstream alignment, and reward-function work appears in both A and B — but the core skill is placing any "brain + safety" idea in the right family.
