%% Unit 5, built 2026-08-11 in reading-list form: three readings, each with one
predict-first question and a short tutor mirror, then two reading-free exercises. No
graded outcomes, no rubrics, same deliberate form as Unit 3.

This is the lightest reading day of the course, and that is the design, not a shortfall.
The course description ends "...and where the leverage is", and the last two lenses are
where the student answers it in their own hand: one live transfer rehearsal on a real
dated development (The Lunch Table), and the final revision of the running ten-year
model read against the day-zero paragraph from Unit 1 (Day Zero, Revisited). The
synthesis is the content on the final day; every design reviewer endorsed U5-light, and
Elias's 2026-08-11 review asked for load cuts on top of that, capping this unit at or
under about 10,000 words total.

Reading load, measured as built: Carlsmith excerpt 2,544 (sections 1 through 2.1 =
2,014 plus section 3 = 530, wc -w against the stored article); the Better Futures
excerpt 1,806 (measured, the "1. The basic case" heading through "With that, let’s jump
in.", which is the whole essay body minus the footnotes and citations block, wc -w
against the stored article); the Concrete Projects excerpt 2,772
(measured, "## Introduction" through the space-governance closing paragraph; a tighter
2,288 cut to the end of "Tools for coordination" is the pre-approved fallback if the
total runs hot). Assigned readings 7,122. Scaffold ~2,030
(three framings and questions ~880, Lunch Table ~400, closer ~750). Total ~9.2k, still
under the 10k cap. Honest time: about 1.25 to 1.5 hours of plain-register reading,
leaving about an hour for the two exercises inside the 2.5 to 3 hour day. The Concrete
Projects excerpt contains footnote refs [^1] and [^2] whose definitions sit outside the
excerpt range; they render as dangling refs, accepted as-is.

Import provenance, flagged for Lauren and Elias review. "Introducing Better Futures" was
first imported from its EA Forum crosspost (license-clean venue, same basis as our LW
imports) rather than forethought.org; relay path
articles/william-macaskill-should-we-aim-for-flourishing-over-mere-survival-the-better-futures-series.
The 2026-08-11 scoping pass found that import defective: it captured only the comment
thread, not the essay body, so no excerpt was possible from the stored file, and the
Suppose We Survive lens shipped the design's approved fallback for one day: a
639-word course-written summary with the essay linked, not embedded, verified
claim-by-claim on 2026-08-11 against the live Forethought original
(https://www.forethought.org/research/introducing-better-futures). Resolved 2026-08-12:
a clean direct import from forethought.org landed at
articles/forethought-introducing-better-futures, carrying the real essay body, and the
lens now embeds a 1,806-word excerpt in a proper Article segment. The summary segment is
gone and the lens framing, tutor brief, and AI-attribution footer were updated to match
the sibling reading lenses. Note the essay is shorter than the ~2.5k excerpt anticipated
here: 1,806 words is the whole body, not a cut. The defective crosspost file is now
referenced by nothing in the course; whether to delete it is an Elias/Lauren call, not a
pending build step. Licensing for the Forethought main site still needs confirming.
"Concrete projects to prepare for superintelligence" was imported from the Forethought
newsletter (newsletter.forethought.org, a Substack with possibly separate terms from the

articles/macaskill-concrete-projects-to-prepare-for-superintelligence. Licensing for
both is not yet confirmed; Elias is handling the Forethought and Lauren coordination
himself.

Ordering: the handoff first, because it repositions everything from Unit 4 (the
playbook's plans end with AIs doing most of the remaining safety work, which is where
the alignment door signposted since Unit 1 actually leads). Then the target (preventing takeover is not the
same as a future worth wanting). Then the menu of concrete work. Then the reading stops
and the student takes over.

Day Zero, Revisited is the terminal exercise of the whole course, and its max-time:: 15
reflects that: it is the one time the student is told to read their day-zero paragraph
BEFORE writing, reversing the write-first-compare-after order every earlier revision
used. The tutor cannot see that day-zero text and its brief says so. The Lunch Table
needs a build-time selection of one real, dated, primary-sourced development; house rule,
no fabricated specifics.

Meeting fodder (90 min): facilitator brings that week's headline and the room replays
the lunch-table exercise live; students read their day-zero quote and final signposts
aloud; track choices compared against final hundred-point allocations. %%
