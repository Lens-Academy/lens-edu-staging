#### Text
content::
Most of AI safety is *object-level* work — alignment, evaluations, policy. This module is about the **meta layer**: growing the field that does that work, and advocating for safety in public. It follows the debate as it actually runs, in four learning outcomes:

- **Why field-building.** The theory of change, resting on the premise that AI safety is talent- and capacity-constrained, so meta-work multiplies every object-level plan.
- **How to build the field.** Courses, fellowships, and org-building — and the 2026 shift in the binding constraint from junior-talent *supply* to **mentorship / absorptive capacity**.
- **Advocacy and communication.** The inside-game vs outside-game debate, contrasting communication doctrines, and advocacy's real risks.
- **The critique.** What the movement has gotten wrong, the sharp case *against* building a broad movement (narrow issue-orgs instead), and lessons from other social movements.

The module is deliberately two-sided: it pairs the case *for* public advocacy (Elmore) with the case *against* a broad movement (Leicht), and grounds both in the field's own diagnosed mistakes and in social-movement research. The goal is not a verdict but the ability to reason about these strategies well.
