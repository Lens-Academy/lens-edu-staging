\## What this is

This course is LessWrong's *Highlights from the Sequences*: 50 essays on avoiding common reasoning errors and forming more accurate beliefs, which LessWrong calls its foundational texts.[^cite-lw-highlights] You read them in the same order and in the same six parts as on LessWrong.

The Sequences are a series of blog posts by Eliezer Yudkowsky on human rationality and irrationality, published on Overcoming Bias and LessWrong between 2006 and 2009.[^cite-lw-rationality] Most of the 50 essays come from there. LessWrong's selection also includes essays by Anna Salamon, Scott Alexander and Nate Soares, and three Yudkowsky essays from outside those years.

\## Who it is for

Anyone who wants to reason more accurately and make better decisions, or who wants to know the ideas that LessWrong builds on. You don't need any background. Part 3 has some arithmetic, and one long essay there explains Bayes's theorem step by step with worked numbers.

\## How it works

The course has six parts:

1. Thinking Better on Purpose
2. Pitfalls of Human Cognition
3. The Laws Governing Belief
4. Science Isn't Enough
5. Connecting Words to Reality
6. Why We Fight

Each part opens with a short page on what it is about. Then every essay gets its own page: the full essay, followed by one question that asks you to apply its main idea to a new case. When you answer, the AI tutor replies with what your answer got right and what it missed, and you can talk it through or answer again. The course is self-paced, with no meetings.
