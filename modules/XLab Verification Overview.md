\## What this course is about

How could parties that do not trust one another verify meaningful limits on advanced AI development? That is the question this course works through, from motivation and policy scope to concrete evidence, adversarial evasion, and regime design. It was built by [XLab](https://xrisk.uchicago.edu/) at the University of Chicago and is offered here on Lens.

:::callout {title="Alpha version" tone="amber"}
XLab is testing and calibrating this material with a small cohort before official launch, and the curriculum will likely change after that. Treat sequence, framing, and exercises as provisional. If you find something unclear, wrong, missing, or especially useful, please send feedback through [XLab's feedback form](https://forms.gle/KkWcHkKh87pygDzw9).
:::

\## The path

:::callout {title="Module 0: Foundations" tone="blue"}
Why verification matters at all. Connects AI risk and international coordination to the core verification problem, builds intuitions by stress-testing a concrete slowdown plan, and examines arms-control precedents and their limits.
:::

:::callout {title="Module 1: Policy scoping and actors" tone="blue"}
What exactly must be verified, and by whom? Compares compute and capability thresholds, tests effectiveness against feasibility, dissects the anatomy of a pause agreement, and maps actors and leverage across the compute supply chain.
:::

:::callout {title="Module 2: Evidence streams" tone="blue"}
How can compliance produce evidence? Evaluates hardware, cloud, intelligence, and human or institutional mechanisms by the claims they test, the evidence they produce, their implementation demands, confidentiality costs, and failure modes.
:::

:::callout {title="Module 3: Covert development" tone="blue"}
How might technical verification work when parties have limited trust? Studies the assumptions, design choices, adversarial circumvention, and unresolved problems of near-term low-trust compute verification.
:::

:::callout {title="Module 4: Capstone" tone="blue"}
What would be enough for a three-month emergency pause? Combines feasibility judgment, research practice, and regime design. You layer imperfect mechanisms, defend your choices, state your assumptions, and name the residual risk.
