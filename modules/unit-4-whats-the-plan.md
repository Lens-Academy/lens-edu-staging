predict-first question and a short tutor mirror. No graded outcomes, no rubrics, no
multi-turn tutor lenses.

Reading load, post-revision: six required lenses carrying about 10,750 words of reading
(2,142 + 1,164 + 2,486 + 2,238 + 2,724; the 2,238 is Elmgren's two excerpt blocks at
1,739 + 228 plus the 271-word Epoch insight, measured wc -w), about 12.5k with scaffold. Plan for 2 to 2.5
hours, not the 1 hour 15 that 150 words per minute suggests. The Unit 3 caveat applies:
150 wpm is a prose rate for prose readers, and the live Unit 1 cohort (2026-08-11) called
the naive timing "very optimistic". This unit is friendlier than Unit 3 (plain blog and
policy prose, no MIRI dialect, no order-of-magnitude estimation), so the multiplier is
smaller, but the side-commitments and the closing allocation take real minutes.

The treaty lens ("Plan A, Up Close", section 4 of the Scher, Abecassis, Barnett, and
Abeyta paper, 1,859 words) is
OPTIONAL by Elias's post-review decision, 2026-08-11: "see if you can cut it down a bit,
this seems like it might be very overwhelming to students." The lens file is unchanged;
only the module row carries optional:: true, same mechanism as the two optional Unit 2
lenses. Knock-on effects handled: the opener does not assume everyone reads the treaty,
and the closer's treaties row carries its own one-line family summary and points at the
optional lens for the detail.

Governance altitude, recorded as decided: the governance and coordination strategy family
(treaties, regulation, evals and if-then commitments, pause) is carried at survey
altitude, named in two sentences of framing, with the one real dissection living in the
optional treaty lens. Reason: the family is core to the course's "dissect prevention
strategies" promise, but the Redwood list's own governance coverage routes through
control, which is ruled out (below), and a second required governance reading did not
survive the load cut.

Ordering: the conditional playbook first, because it names the axis (how much cooperation
the world supplies) that every later lever sits on. Then three levers in falling order of
required optimism: inside the lab, the national project, and the factual premise under
the race. Then the blind spot that survives all of them. Then the student allocates.

Control appears exactly once, in one sentence of the opening framing, as the argument the
student already met at the end of Unit 3. Lauren, 2026-08-11: "control is a transiently
useful, serious-externality-carrying thing, which we can mention in passing but isn't
central. Redwood is all in on control."

One disagreement runs through the unit and is adjudicated nowhere: The Ten People treats
the lab as where the solution lives; Aligned to Whom treats the lab, specifically its
org chart, as where the threat lives. The Aligned to Whom framing points at the tension
in passing; the closer lays it out in full. The course does not think that question is
settled. The 90-minute meeting stages it. %%
