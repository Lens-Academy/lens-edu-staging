# Lens: Module 4: Mindset
id:: 91b226c8-23f8-4701-baf4-0da9299fcf1d
tldr:: The habits of thought that help alignment researchers look for how a plan fails, in five readings by Eliezer Yudkowsky.
summary_for_tutor:: Opening page of Module 4 of Agent Foundations for Superintelligence-Robust Alignment, a course by Alignment Ecosystem Development reproduced on Lens. The module asks what mindset helps a researcher stay vigilant in the difficult terrain of aligning superintelligence. The five required readings that follow, all by Eliezer Yudkowsky, cover the mindset AI safety borrows from computer security, foreseeing difficulties before they show up, the difference between security mindset and ordinary paranoia, what makes a powerful AI relevant to the larger situation, and the principle of doing the least dangerous thing. The first two are marked as the most important. Three optional bonus readings follow, by Buck Shlegeris (on reasons for worst-case thinking), Adam Shimi (on deconfusion) and Paul Christiano (on his research method).
duration_minutes:: 1

*Module 4 of the [Agent Foundations course](https://agentfoundations.study/#module4) by Alignment Ecosystem Development.*

What mindset can we cultivate to stay vigilant in this difficult terrain?

- **AI safety mindset** – Eliezer Yudkowsky [2015, 2 mins]
- **Method of Foreseeable Difficulties** – Eliezer Yudkowsky [2015, 2 mins]
- Security Mindset and Ordinary Paranoia – Eliezer Yudkowsky [2017, 35 mins]
- Relevantly powerful agent – Eliezer Yudkowsky [2015, 2 mins]
- Minimality Principle – Eliezer Yudkowsky [2017, 1 min]

Readings in bold are the most important ones.
