\## From general uncertainty to the AI case

The previous module did not establish that advanced AI is an existential risk. It gave us tools for evaluating a claim of that kind. We now need to build the claim itself, separate its steps, and see where the real disagreements live.

Several questions are often compressed into one sentence such as "advanced AI could cause extinction." Could advanced systems become powerful enough that humans could no longer reliably control the outcome? Could those systems behave in ways that conflict seriously with human interests? Would they seek influence or resist intervention? Would humans notice in time? Could institutions respond effectively? Would failure actually become irreversible rather than merely costly?

We will first construct a strong version of the argument, then try to break it. The aim is not to decide whether one camp has won. It is to leave with a threat model that is explicit enough to criticise, update, and connect to action.

Some sources in this module also make claims about when advanced capabilities may arrive. Those forecasts were written at particular times and can age quickly. We use them as examples of the kinds of evidence people appeal to, not as timeless statements of the course's own forecast.
