#### Text
content::
In the previous modules, we looked at what different researchers expect from the future of AI, what they think could go wrong, what outcomes they would like to bring about, and some of the difficulties we have already encountered in trying to get there.

Now we turn to a more practical question: what can be done about all this?

One way to start thinking about our options is to distinguish between two broad kinds of interventions:

- **Delay bad AI.** Change when, whether and under what conditions potentially dangerous AI systems are developed or deployed.
- **Make AI good.** Find ways of developing and using AI systems without bringing about the outcomes we are concerned about.

There are other ways to draw this distinction, and some interventions contribute to both objectives. But it gives us a starting point.

If we want to delay the development or deployment of dangerous AI, we might investigate what kinds of international agreements are feasible, how compliance with these agreements could be verified, or how we could establish whether a particular system is too dangerous to deploy.

If we want to make AI good, we might investigate how neural networks acquire their objectives, how to supervise systems that outperform their supervisors, or how to prevent a potentially misaligned system from causing harm.

In either case, there is a question worth asking repeatedly: *how does this actually help us get to the future we want?*

The answer is not always obvious. A technique developed to make AI systems safer might also make them more capable. A method for detecting dangerous behaviour might turn out to work only on systems that are not yet capable of concealing it. An international agreement might be difficult to enforce even if everyone agrees that it would be desirable.

To reason about these possibilities, we need to understand both the problems we are trying to solve and the ideas behind the proposed solutions.

This module is about the latter.

By the end of it, we want you to have a better picture of what AI safety researchers are working on, why they have chosen to work on these particular things, and how you might go about making sense of a research direction you have never encountered before.
