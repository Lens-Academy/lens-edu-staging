The learner sees only two sections: the Synopsis and 'Highly theoretical justifications for having fun'; the opening dialogue and the appendix are collapsed. The Synopsis opens: 'Your mind wants to play. Stopping your mind from playing is throwing your mind away.' It asks readers not to tell or hint to others that they shouldn't pour energy into things whose benefit isn't visible, names this as 'in conflict with coordinating around reducing existential risk', and suggests a long walk without electronics, staring at objects without a goal. The justifications section is a list of reasons, including: interrupting playful thinking causes learned helplessness about it; play puts ideas in a form ready to combine and indexes them; your sense of taste and fun knows things you don't explicitly know; play exercises setting up problem contexts, 'especially needed in pre-paradigm fields'; people far from maximal exertion face no large tradeoff between fun and alignment research; your sense of fun decorrelates you from others and from 'systems of deference'; requiring explicit consequentialist justification forces you to lie or not act. It closes: 'Intensity in the face of existential risk is appropriate. To enable intensity in the face of existential risk, don't require yourself to throw your mind away in order to be intense.'"
