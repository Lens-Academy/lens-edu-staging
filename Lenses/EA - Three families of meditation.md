#### Text
content::
The map's method column lists many contemplative practices. This paper groups practices from Buddhist, Christian, Sufi, Hindu and Greco-Roman traditions and from clinical therapies by what they do to the mind, and by what each does to the sense of self. That makes it a second way to compare the contemplative and therapy rows.

The authors are contemplative neuroscientists at the Center for Healthy Minds (University of Wisconsin-Madison), which Richard Davidson founded. Read their framing paragraph and Table 1. Read the table for the three families and their subtypes. You do not need to know every practice in it.

force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner read Dahl, Lutz and Davidson 2015: the framing, Table 1 and the opening paragraph of each family. They were given a flawed claim: "All meditation is basically the same thing. You focus on your breath and calm down. So once you have tried one kind, you know what meditation does." This is practice, not a test.

What a strong answer says:
- The paper sorts practices into three families by their main mechanism and target. Attentional: train attention and meta-awareness (being aware of thinking, feeling and perceiving), against experiential fusion. Examples: breath counting, jhana practice, mantra, MBSR, open monitoring. Constructive: change the content of thoughts and emotions through reappraisal and perspective taking, against maladaptive self-schema. Examples: loving-kindness and compassion, centering prayer, contemplation of mortality, well-being therapy. Deconstructive: self-inquiry to produce insight into how experience and the self are built, against reification. Examples: vipassana, koan, analytical meditation, Dzogchen, shikantaza, Advaita self-inquiry.
- Breath focus is one subtype of one family. Calming down is not the stated aim of the constructive or deconstructive families.
- So trying one kind tells you little about the others. The authors also say most practices mix all three and research on the constructive and deconstructive families is thin.

Confirm or correct each example family in one sentence. If one family is missing or misplaced, name it and give one example from Table 1. 80 to 140 words. At most two replies. No generic praise.
