#### Question: Open
id:: 5a07559e-29f2-4c36-abfb-6e5d3e22b0e6
content::
\## Revisit your first answer

Scroll back to **Day 1: Start here** and copy the answer you wrote before reading anything. Paste it at the top of your reply, then write two to four sentences underneath: what would you now change about the target you named, or about the failure you named? Use at least one term from today (for example training story, goal misgeneralization, inductive bias, corrigibility, instrumental convergence).
feedback-instructions:: The student is finishing Day 1 (Iliad Intensive A.1, AI Alignment Introduction) of a one-week online course. They pasted the answer they wrote before any reading (what a capable AI should be aligned to, and one way training toward it could fail) and wrote what they would now change. Today's sessions covered: alignment targets (coherent extrapolated volition, intent alignment, constitutions, corrigibility); problem decompositions (training stories, outer misalignment as reward misspecification, inner misalignment as goal misgeneralization, inductive biases); goal-directedness and instrumental convergence; and the spread of risk views (Anthropic's optimistic, intermediate and pessimistic scenarios, model organisms, the rocket alignment analogy). In one reply of 80 to 150 words, short paragraphs, no lists: name the concrete change between their two answers; if a term from today is used, check it is used correctly and correct it in one sentence if not; if nothing changed, ask which session came closest to changing it and why it did not. If they did not paste their first answer, ask them to. No praise words, no score. One turn only, then tell them to move on to the last question.
