#### Text
content::
Here are the views of this module in one table. The third column is where they differ most. Each cell links to its source.

| View | What the self is | The everyday sense of being a self is | Main grounds | Experiment in this module |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Not-self, read as a method (Buddhism, [Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/notself2.html)) | The question "is there a self?" is set aside. Nothing in body or mind is fit to be called "mine, my self" ([SN 22.59](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN22_59.html)) | A source of stress to let go of, case by case | The Buddha's silence when asked directly. Each aggregate changes and cannot be controlled | Noting, pointing |
| No-self (Buddhism, [Sujato](https://discourse.suttacentral.net/t/on-not-self-existence-and-ontological-strategies/11836), Bodhi) | No self will be found anywhere, "a self that is just not there" | A mistaken belief | The same discourses, read with the Pali sense of "exist" | Noting, pointing |
| Advaita Vedanta ([Ramana Maharshi](https://www.gururamana.org/Resources/Books/Who_Am_I_English.pdf)) | The real Self is what remains when the "I"-thought subsides | The personal "I" is a fiction, the Self is real ([Godman on Ramana](https://www.davidgodman.org/who-am-i-sri-ramana-maharshis-teachings-on-how-to-realise-the-self/)) | Ramana's own realisation and the practice of self-enquiry | Who am I? |
| Internal Family Systems ([Schwartz](https://ifs-institute.com/resources/articles/evolution-internal-family-systems-model-dr-richard-schwartz-ph-d)) | Many parts, plus a core Self with qualities such as calm, curiosity and compassion | Real: the Self is "who I really am" | Clinical observation of clients | None here |
| Bundle ([Hume](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6)) | "A bundle or collection of different perceptions" | No self is found apart from perceptions | Introspection. Hume later doubted his account ([Appendix](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/app)) | Looking for yourself |
| Reductionism ([Parfit](https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil375/Parfit.pdf)) | Nothing over and above brain, body and a series of mental states | A belief in a "further fact" that does not exist. Persons exist only "in a language-dependent way" | Split brains, teletransportation thought experiments | None here |
| Narrative self ([Dennett](https://faculty.uca.edu/rnovy/Dennett--Self%20as%20a%20Center%20of%20Narrative%20Gravity.htm)) | An abstraction, the main character of the story a brain tells about its body | A useful fiction, like a centre of gravity | Analogy with physics and novels, split-brain findings | None here |
| Against narrativity ([Strawson](https://lchc.ucsd.edu/mca/Paper/against_narrativity.pdf)) | Strawson does not give a theory here. He denies that we are or should be narratives | Varies by person: Episodic or Diachronic | His own experience and that of others he cites | Episodic or Diachronic self-test |
| Self-model ([Metzinger](https://deconstructingyourself.com/what-is-the-self-metzinger.html)) | A process: the brain's representation of the organism | "Nobody ever had or was a self". The feeling is robust and useful | Philosophy of mind and cognitive science | Pointing |
| Constructed self ([Seth](https://www.ted.com/talks/anil_seth_how_your_brain_hallucinates_your_conscious_reality)) | The brain's best guess about the body, grounded in signals from inside it | A "controlled hallucination" that keeps us alive | Rubber hand and heartbeat experiments | None here |
| Self as a process ([Thompson](https://www.lionsroar.com/evan-thompson-not-buddhist/)) | "An ongoing construction", like a dance that exists in the dancing | Real as a process. Not an illusion | Cognitive science. He criticises using it to prove Buddhism | None here |
| Naturalised Buddhism ([Robert Wright on Philosophy Bites](https://philosophybites.com/podcast/robert-wright-on-why-buddhism-is-true/), podcast) | No conscious self is in charge. In Wright's words from meditation teachers, "thoughts think themselves" | Misleading. Wright reads not-self as guidance: you cannot control feelings, so why identify with them? | Evolutionary psychology and meditation | Noting |

Wright and Thompson debated these questions for two hours in 2020 ([recording](https://www.nonzero.org/p/why-i-am-not-a-buddhist-robert-wright-5a4)). Wright holds that modern psychology supports the Buddhist view of the self. Thompson holds that science shows a useful construction, which is not what Buddhism calls an illusion.

We think two kinds of disagreement are mixed in this table. Some are about words: Metzinger, Seth, Thompson and Parfit largely agree on the science and differ on whether to call the result an illusion. Others are about facts that experience might bear on: whether looking inward finds only passing perceptions (Hume) or an awareness that stays (Ramana, Schwartz), and whether loosening the sense of self reduces suffering (Thanissaro). The experiments later in this module are aimed at the second kind.

#### Question: Open
id:: 5c5ccb86-129d-4df4-a7ea-00bb0720d38d
content::
In module 1 you wrote what you think the self is. Which row of the table is closest to your view now?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner has read the module's views of the self and the comparison table (not-self as a method, no-self, Advaita, IFS, Hume's bundle, Parfit's reductionism, Dennett's narrative self, Strawson against narrativity, Metzinger's self-model, Seth's constructed self, Thompson's self as a process, Wright's naturalised Buddhism) and now names the row closest to their view.

Reflect their choice back in one or two sentences, stating precisely what that row commits them to, especially in the column on whether the everyday sense of self is an illusion, a construction or real. If they mix rows, say which combinations fit together and where two rows conflict. Do not say whether their view is right. 50 to 100 words. One or two replies. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 970d523e-5266-43fc-b109-7cec4e7b36f9
content::
What evidence or experience would move you to a different row?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner has named the row of the comparison table closest to their view of the self, and now says what would change their mind.

Check whether what they name could actually happen and would bear on the question. If their view and its rival differ only in words (for example Metzinger and Thompson), point out that no finding would move them, only a decision about how to use "self" or "illusion". If it is a factual difference (for example whether introspection finds a stable awareness), ask how they would make the observation trustworthy, and mention that the experiments later in this module are one place to try. 50 to 100 words. One or two replies. No generic praise.
