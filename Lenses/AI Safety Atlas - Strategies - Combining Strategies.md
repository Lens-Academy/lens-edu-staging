One illustrative sequence, explicitly not a definitive roadmap, stacking the chapter's strategies from near-term to long-term. Step 1 is foundational risk management and governance, the bedrock, without which technical solutions do not get implemented properly; it notes the EU AI Act's Code of Practice has limits, with capped fines and exemptions for military and internal research, so binding international governance is needed, which in turn needs broad public and political consensus. Step 2 is mitigating catastrophic misuse, chosen next because it is a present danger requiring only sub-AGI capability, through access controls and defensive acceleration, plus legislation for already proliferated models. Step 3 is control and alignment of AGI, on the assumption that alignment is unsolved, prioritising architectures with transparent thoughts, control protocols and evaluations, pausing development when audits reveal failures, and scaling only as fast as control can be demonstrated. Step 4 is a robust ASI solution, using controlled AGI to automate alignment research toward safe-by-design systems and possibly a pivotal act, with world coordination and then deterrence as last resorts. The section closes by calling the plan fragile and citing an AI-2027 scenario where humanity survives not through the plan but because a warning shot galvanises labs into just enough mitigation.

- The warning-shot scenario has survival come from luck rather than planning. What should a planner do with that?
- Every step assumes the previous one bought time. Which link in that chain is weakest?
