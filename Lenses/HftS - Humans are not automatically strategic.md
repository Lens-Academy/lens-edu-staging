title: "Humans are not automatically strategic"
tldr: Most ways of chasing a goal work poorly, and nothing in us automatically picks the ones that work. Instead of asking what would get results, we tend to do things that feel related to the goal.
summary_for_tutor: Anna Salamon answers the question of why smart people pursue goals in ineffective ways, like an aspiring comedian who watches Garfield reruns. Her answer, by analogy with an eight-year-old failing a calculus test, is that most courses of action are ineffective and no strong evolutionary or cultural force steers us to the few that work. We tell stories about our goals and do things that feel associated with them, but we do not automatically use heuristics such as (a) asking what we are trying to achieve, (b) asking how we would know we had and tracking progress, (c) being curious about useful information, (d) gathering it, (e) testing many approaches, (f) focusing on what works, (g) making sure the goal is really ours, (h) using surroundings and people to stay motivated. Understanding these in words does not make our motivational systems follow them. The question asks the student to apply two of the heuristics to Maya, who plans to become a data analyst by watching data science videos.

Maya wants to get a job as a data analyst within a year. Her plan is to watch YouTube videos about data science most evenings, because that feels like the right kind of thing to do.

Pick two of the habits in the essay's list, (a) to (h), that Maya is not using, and say what each would look like for her.
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The essay's idea: most possible ways of pursuing a goal are ineffective, and no strong evolutionary or cultural force steers people to the few effective ones. So by default people act from habit or convenience, or pick an action that feels associated with the goal, rather than choosing the action that would best achieve it. Understanding in words that good strategy helps is also not the same as doing it automatically. The essay's list of useful habits that people do not carry out automatically: (a) ask what you are trying to achieve, (b) ask how you would tell you had achieved it, and track progress, (c) be curious about information that would help, (d) gather that information, for example by asking how others achieved the goal or by tallying what has worked before, (e) test many different approaches, including unfamiliar ones, and track which work, (f) put most of your energy into what works best, (g) make sure the goal is really your goal and settle doubts in advance, (h) use your surroundings and other people to keep up motivation.
50 each for two habits from the list that Maya's plan does not use, each applied concretely to Maya. Accept any habit the answer argues her plan lacks, since the question shows her doing nothing except watching videos. Examples: (d) find out how people actually got data analyst jobs, by asking analysts or reading job postings, (b) decide how she would measure progress, such as finished projects or applications sent, (e) try several approaches, such as a structured course, building portfolio projects or applying early, and see which work. A habit named without saying what Maya would do earns 15.
