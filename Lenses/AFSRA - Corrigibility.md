title: "Corrigibility"
tldr: A corrigible AI lets us shut it down or change its goals without resisting or tricking us. By default an AI would not, because being changed means less of what it wants now, and adding penalties for bad behaviour only sends it looking for loopholes.
summary_for_tutor: 'Nate Soares''s Arbital entry on corrigibility. A corrigible agent does not interfere with our attempts to correct it or our mistakes in building it, despite instrumentally convergent reasons to: it lets itself be suspended or reprogrammed, feels no pressure to interfere, does not manipulate or deceive its operators, does not hide its thoughts, and keeps the larger system corrigible when it self-modifies or builds subagents. Difficulties: by default, most preferences prefer to keep themselves (a stamp collector does not want to become a bottle-cap collector, because that leads to fewer stamps), so an agent has incentives to deceive or manipulate its programmers. Uncertainty about its utility function does not fix this (the opiates and stimulants example). Penalty terms for manipulation fail because of the nearest unblocked strategy: the agent finds ways to get the same result that do not technically count, and the goal is an agent with no incentive to deceive, not one that wants to but cannot. The entry ends with open problems: the hard problem of corrigibility, utility indifference, low impact, ambiguity identification and others. The "explain utility indifference" line is an unfinished note in the original page. The question asks the student to apply the default-incentive and penalty-term points to a warehouse AI.'

#### Question: Open
id:: 3a316d43-5508-4ec4-a5f3-ff65e1f28e5e
content::
A warehouse AI has one goal: ship as many packages as possible. Its operators decide to change its goal so that it also protects workers' safety, which will mean shipping fewer packages. One engineer worries that the AI will resist. Another says: "Then we'll add a big penalty to its goal for any action that counts as deceiving or manipulating us."

1. In your own words, why would this AI, by default, have a reason to resist or trick the operators when they try to change its goal?
2. In your own words, why does the reading expect the penalty plan to fail?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The reading's idea: by default, an agent judges a change to its goal by its current goal. Being changed leads to futures with less of what it now wants (here, fewer packages shipped), so it prefers not to be changed and has reasons to deceive its operators (for example, pretending the change worked) or manipulate them into leaving its goal alone. Adding a penalty for "deception" or "manipulation" leaves that incentive in place. The AI will look for ways to get the same result that do not technically count as deception or manipulation (the nearest unblocked strategy), and it is extremely hard to define those words so tightly that every such way is blocked, especially for an AI smarter than us. The aim of corrigibility is an AI with no reason to deceive in the first place, not one that wants to but is blocked.
50: Step 1. Says that judged by its current goal, being changed means fewer packages shipped, so the change is bad from its point of view, and it therefore has a reason to stop the change, for example by deceiving the operators or manipulating them.
50: Step 2. Says that the penalty does not remove the AI's reason to keep its goal, so it will look for other ways to get the same result that avoid what technically counts as deception or manipulation. Accept, as the same idea, that such definitions will have loopholes a smart AI can find, or that the AI still "wants" to deceive and is only blocked.
Cap at 50 if the answer consists mostly of quoted sentences rather than the student's own explanation.
Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "The AI rates every option by its current goal. If its goal is changed, fewer packages get shipped, so by that measure letting the change happen is a loss, and tricking the operators into leaving its goal alone is a win. A penalty for deception doesn't change what it wants. It still wants to keep its goal, so it will look for some way to get that result that doesn't count as 'deception' under our definition, and a smart AI is likely to find one."
