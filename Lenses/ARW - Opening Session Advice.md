\## Advice from earlier workshops

At the start of each workshop, CFAR gave four or five tips, often framed as what earlier participants wished they had been told at the start.[^cite-opening-2022] They were written for four days in person, so where they say "the workshop" or "this weekend", read "these six weeks". Some tips are followed by a short question about you.

[^cite-opening-2022]: CFAR!Duncan (2022), *Opening Session Tips & Advice*. [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/gKeHcikcXA3bApyoM/opening-session-tips-and-advice)

#### Question: Open
id:: e596ebf0-37ff-47bb-a75c-e1b9546cf468
content:: This course happens at home, between everything else. When and where in your week will you do it, and what will you put out of reach while you do?
placeholder:: e.g. Tuesday and Thursday evenings, 8 to 9:30, at the kitchen table, phone in the other room
feedback-instructions:: The learner has read CFAR's "Be Present" tip and said when and where they will do this six-week course and what they will set aside. Ungraded. Reply in under 60 words, one reply. If the answer has no specific time ("when I have time", "evenings"), ask which day and hour they mean. If it is specific, confirm in one sentence and tell them to move on. No praise, no lecture.

#### Question: Open
id:: fff56482-5f6a-4294-bfa5-be44271d1323
content:: The tip names three unhelpful stories: "I'm too dumb/old/lazy to learn this", "I already know this part", and "I've got important things to do, and this lesson can wait". Which one, if any, do you expect to hear in your own head during this course?
feedback-instructions:: The learner has read CFAR's "Be Present" tip and named which of three unhelpful narratives (too dumb/old/lazy, already know this, more important things) they expect in themselves, or none. Ungraded and personal. Reply in under 60 words, one reply. Reflect their answer in one sentence. If they named one, ask what they could do in the moment they notice it, using the tip's own suggestion of setting it aside as an experiment. If they said none, accept that. No praise, no reassurance speeches.

#### Question: Open
id:: fec7cabb-d316-449f-b910-997a3bb6377a
content:: What would you let yourself want from these six weeks, if you were not protecting yourself from being disappointed?
feedback-instructions:: The learner has read CFAR's "Let your wants come alive" tip and named what they would let themselves want from the course. Ungraded and personal. Reply in under 60 words, one reply. Reflect it back in one sentence. If it is very modest, you may ask once whether there is a bigger version they held back, and accept any answer. Do not promise the course will deliver it. No praise.

from:: ## **Eat the instructions**
to:: try, _then_ tinker.

#### Question: Open
id:: 4eef8581-8347-4445-8af5-4e9ebccc7a55
content:: Think of a method you once tried: a study technique, an app, a workout plan, a way of running meetings. Did you drop it after one go, or keep following it long after it stopped fitting? What would "try, then tinker" have looked like there?
feedback-instructions:: The learner has read CFAR's tips "Try Things", "Make good quiche" and "Eat the instructions" (try a technique as written first, then adapt it to fit you) and applied the idea to a method from their own past. Ungraded. Reply in under 80 words, one reply. Name in one sentence what "try" and what "tinker" were in their example. If their answer only covers one half (only dropping, or only sticking rigidly), ask what the other half would have looked like. No praise.
