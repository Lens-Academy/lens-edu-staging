title: "Can circumstances give lasting peace?"
tldr: "Four answers: circumstances matter a lot, they matter less than we think, peace can be independent of them, or it depends on which kind of wellbeing you measure. Each rests on a different kind of evidence."
summary_for_tutor: "Primary texts and a map of positions on the circumstances question. Epictetus, Enchiridion 1 and 5: only our opinions, desires and aversions are in our power; people are disturbed not by things but by their opinions about them. Lokavipatti Sutta (AN 8.6): gain, loss, status, disgrace, censure, praise, pleasure and pain come to everyone; the instructed person sees them as inconstant and does not welcome or rebel against them, and is released from suffering. Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics Book I (Chase translation): happiness needs external goods; someone who suffers Priam's fortunes is not called happy; a good person bears misfortune nobly and never becomes wretched, but is not blessed either. content::
The last lens was about research. Three much older texts take positions on the same question. Two say that peace does not have to depend on what happens to you. The third says a good life does need some luck.

**Epictetus**, a Stoic teacher (55 to 135 CE according to the [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/epictetu/)), from his *Handbook*, chapters 1 and 5:

#### Text
content::
\## The positions side by side

Here is everything from this part of the module in one table. The last column is our summary of each position's main limits. Module 3 shows how to weigh each kind of evidence, and module 7 weighs this question in depth.

| Position | Who says it | What kind of evidence | Key finding or claim | Main limits (our summary) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Circumstances matter a lot** | Aristotle; researchers using large panel studies and lotteries | Philosophical argument about what a complete life needs. Panel studies that follow the same people for years. A lottery study where money is assigned at random | Someone "very ugly, or (...) ill-born, or solitary and childless" is "not at all capable of Happiness" ([Aristotle, Book I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-h/8438-h.htm)). Widowhood, unemployment, divorce and disability leave lasting drops in life satisfaction ([Diener, Lucas and Scollon 2006](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/Documents/Diener-Lucas-Scollon_2006.pdf)). Lottery wealth raises life satisfaction for over a decade ([Lindqvist et al. 2020](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24667)) | Mostly measures life satisfaction, a judgement, not felt peace. Samples from rich Western countries. Panel studies are correlational |
| **They matter less than we think** | Dan Gilbert; Brickman and colleagues | Forecasting experiments. One small study measuring each group once | We expect events to affect us more and for longer than they do ([Gilbert's talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4q1dgn_C0AU)). Lottery winners were not happier than controls ([Brickman et al. 1978](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/1978-brickman.pdf)) | Small samples, no before-and-after data. Popular retellings overstate it: the accident victims in the same study were clearly less happy (2.96 against 3.82 on a 0 to 5 scale) |
| **Peace can be independent of them** | Stoics; Buddhist texts and teachers; Jeffery Martin's interviewees | Philosophical argument about what is in our power. Teachers' and practitioners' reports. Lab studies of expert meditators. One interview study | People are disturbed by their opinions, not by events ([Epictetus, Handbook 5](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Discourses_of_Epictetus/The_Encheiridion,_or_Manual)). The instructed person neither welcomes gain nor rebels against loss ([AN 8.6](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an08/an08.006.than.html)). Sukha includes "reduced vulnerability to outer circumstances" ([Ekman et al. 2005](https://centerforcontemplativeresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Emotions-and-Wellbeing-1-1.pdf)). Martin's interviewees report "ongoing and deep inner peace" ([Martin](https://nonsymbolic.org/PNSE-Article.pdf)) | Few controlled data. Mostly self-reports from volunteers who practise the tradition. Martin's 50 interviewees were volunteers, all white and 78% male ([Martin](https://nonsymbolic.org/PNSE-Article.pdf)), and they said deep triggers could push their peace down for weeks to years |
| **It depends which wellbeing you measure** | Diener, Lucas and Scollon; Kahneman and Deaton; Lindqvist and colleagues | Large surveys that measure several kinds of wellbeing at once | Pleasant emotions, unpleasant emotions and life satisfaction "can move in different directions" ([Diener, Lucas and Scollon 2006](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/Documents/Diener-Lucas-Scollon_2006.pdf)). Income tracks life evaluation more closely than daily emotions ([Kahneman and Deaton 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1011492107)). Lottery wins raise life satisfaction far more than happiness and mental health ([Lindqvist et al. 2020](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24667)) | Results depend on how each kind is measured. Kahneman and Deaton's "plateau" in emotional wellbeing was later found only among the least happy people ([Killingsworth, Kahneman and Mellers 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2208661120)) |

In our reading, the large studies in rows 1, 2 and 4 measure life satisfaction, happiness ratings and daily emotions, not the kind of peace row 3 describes. So these rows partly answer different questions. Note also that Ekman and colleagues (2005) say lottery wins do not change trait happiness, while the later Swedish study found a lasting rise in life satisfaction.

#### Question: Open
id:: 64cf2c6d-79de-4f01-b723-1dbb234a959e
content::
Which row is closest to your current view, and which kind of happiness is your view about? Name one piece of evidence that would move you to a different row.
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner has read the circumstances readings (Gilbert's talk with a correction of the Brickman lottery study, Diener, Lucas and Scollon 2006, Lindqvist et al. 2020, Epictetus Handbook 1 and 5, the Lokavipatti Sutta, Aristotle on external goods and Priam) and a four-row positions table: circumstances matter a lot; they matter less than we think; peace can be independent of them; it depends which wellbeing you measure. There is no right row. Do not tell them which position is correct and do not nudge them toward one.

Check three things: that they named a row, that they said which kind of happiness their view is about (positive feeling, life satisfaction, eudaimonia, sukha, equanimity, contentment, fundamental wellbeing and so on), and that the evidence they name would actually bear on that kind. Example: if their view is about lasting inner peace, a lottery study of life satisfaction does not test it well. If one of the three is missing, ask for it with one direct question. If the evidence they name could not change their mind in practice, ask what result would. 70 to 120 words. At most two replies. No generic praise. Tell them their answer will be useful again in module 8.
