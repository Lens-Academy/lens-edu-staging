tldr: "Will a powerful AI end up wanting reward, a grab-bag of context-dependent habits, or some arbitrary long-term goal? These rival predictions all rest on the same logic. This post offers a single lens, the 'behavioral selection model', that unifies them: expect AIs to develop cognitive patterns whose own behavior causes those patterns to be selected, then see what that implies about AI motivations."
summary_for_tutor: "Introduces the 'behavioral selection model,' a framework by Alex Mallen and Buck Shlegeris for predicting the motivations of highly capable AIs. It models AI decisions as driven by cognitive patterns (computations that influence actions, including 'motivations' or 'X-seekers') that gain or lose influence through selection processes like reinforcement learning. The unifying principle is that we should expect AIs to have cognitive patterns that produce behavior causing those very patterns to be selected. The model organizes competing hypotheses about AI motivations (intrinsic reward-seeking, reward 'chiseling' context-dependent patterns, and arbitrary long-term goals) into a single causal graph, decomposes predictions into a selection term plus implicit priors over cognitive patterns (analogous to Bayesian updating), and then discusses the model's implications, extensions, and omissions."
