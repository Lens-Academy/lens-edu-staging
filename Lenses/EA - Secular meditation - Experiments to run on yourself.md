#### Text
content::
Each experiment below tests a claim from this module, using the teacher's own instructions. Module 3 showed how to test a claim on yourself: take a baseline, fix the dose, decide what you will measure before you start. Where we add our own suggestion, the text says so.

Pick one. The first three test claims about suffering and wellbeing. The last one tests a claim about the self.

#### Callout: 1. Does dropping resistance shrink suffering?
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** Shinzen Young: suffering is pain multiplied by resistance, so suffering falls as resistance falls, even when the pain stays the same ([Natural Pain Relief](https://www.shinzen.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/art_synopsis-pain.pdf)).

**What to do.** Use his short exercise for meditating on pain, from a synopsis of his book *Natural Pain Relief*:

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/young-natural-pain-relief]]
from:: I would like to give you a tangible sense of the experience of mindfulness.
to:: This, however, is a temporary phenomenon.

#### Text
content::

**Dose.** Four sessions on four days, 15 to 20 minutes each. Four days is the training length in a lab study by Zeidan and colleagues of a different mindfulness training, where meditating during heat pain cut how unpleasant people rated it by 57% and how intense they rated it by 40%, compared with resting ([Zeidan et al. 2011](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/31/14/5540)). The session length is our suggestion.

**Measure.** Before and after each session, rate two things separately from 0 to 10: how intense the sensation is, and how much it bothers you. Zeidan's study used the same split between intensity and unpleasantness. If Shinzen is right, the "bothers me" number should fall more than the intensity number.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 2. Eight weeks of MBSR at home
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** MBSR reduces stress and helps you live "with greater calm" ([UMass](https://www.ummhealth.org/services-treatments/center-mindfulness/mindfulness-programs/mbsr)). The trials in this module found small to moderate drops in anxiety, depression and stress, and a smaller rise in wellbeing.

**What to do.** Palouse Mindfulness is a free online eight-week course built on MBSR by a certified MBSR teacher, with the same practices: body scan, sitting meditation, mindful movement ([Palouse Mindfulness](https://palousemindfulness.com/index.html)). It recommends the 32-minute body scan for the course ([body scan page](https://palousemindfulness.com/meditations/bodyscan.html)).

**Dose.** The original course asks for about 45 to 60 minutes of home practice a day ([UMass](https://www.ummhealth.org/services-treatments/center-mindfulness/mindfulness-programs/mbsr)). Across 43 studies, participants actually did about 64% of what was assigned, about 30 minutes a day six days a week, and those who practised more improved somewhat more (r = 0.26) ([Parsons et al. 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5501725/)). Log your minutes so you can check this on yourself.

**Measure.** Before week 1 and after week 8, fill in the five-item Satisfaction with Life Scale ([Penn](https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/resources/questionnaires-researchers/satisfaction-life-scale)). Each evening, rate your stress that day from 0 to 10 (our suggestion). Goyal and colleagues found no evidence that meditation programmes beat other active treatments such as exercise ([Goyal et al. 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1809754)). If you want to know whether meditation does more for you than something else would, we suggest tracking the same numbers for eight weeks of another activity first, for example regular exercise.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 3. A month of sitting, scored by Culadasa's stages
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** Culadasa: progress in meditation runs through stages that you can recognise yourself, and some people get through all ten in months to a few years of daily practice ([Culadasa 2008](https://dharmatreasure.org/wp-content/uploads/LightOnMeditationHandout.pdf)).

**What to do.** Sit once a day using Harris's nine instructions from the first lens ([Harris](https://www.samharris.org/blog/how-to-meditate)). Sit 20 to 45 minutes for 30 days (our suggestion: Culadasa's own figure for fast progress is one to two hours a day).

**Measure.** Score yourself with Culadasa's criteria. Stage 1 is mastered when you do not miss a daily sit unless it is unavoidable. Stage 2 is mastered when, in a sit, your attention stays on the breath noticeably longer than it is lost, and periods of attention last minutes while mind-wandering lasts seconds ([Culadasa 2008](https://dharmatreasure.org/wp-content/uploads/LightOnMeditationHandout.pdf)). After each sit, write down the share of time you think you were on the breath and how often you noticed you had wandered. At the end of the month, compare week 1 with week 4.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 4. What is the self made of? A noting practice
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** Shinzen Young: when body sensations, emotions, mental images and mental talk get tangled, they create the impression that the self is a thing, and sorting them apart dissolves that impression ([What is Mindfulness?](https://www.shinzen.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/WhatIsMindfulness_SY_Public_ver1.5.pdf)). Ingram makes a similar claim: looked at closely, sensations come and go, do not satisfy, and "ain't us" ([MCTB2](https://www.mctb.org/mctb2/table-of-contents/part-i-the-fundamentals/7-the-seven-factors-of-awakening/)).

**What to do.** Label what you notice as "see", "hear" or "feel", spoken softly or in your head, at a comfortable pace of one label every one to five seconds ([Shinzen, See Hear Feel](https://www.shinzen.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/SeeHearFeelIntroduction_ver1.8.pdf)). Ingram's faster version notes the breath as "rising" and "falling" at perhaps one to five notes per second ([MCTB2](https://www.mctb.org/mctb2/table-of-contents/part-i-the-fundamentals/7-the-seven-factors-of-awakening/)).

**Dose.** 15 minutes a day for two weeks (our suggestion). Do not take this to retreat intensity on your own: Ingram warns against skipping sleep, fasting and very long sits on retreat ([MCTB2](https://www.mctb.org/mctb2/table-of-contents/part-ii-light-and-shadows/21-a-clear-goal/)), and both he and Shinzen describe hard stages that intensive practice can bring.

**Observe.** Each time you have a strong sense of "me", label what it is made of: a feeling in the body, a mental picture, inner talk. After each session write one or two lines on whether you found anything else.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: If it goes badly
tone:: amber
#### Text
content::
Shinzen's remedy when "too much too soon" comes up is to change the technique, reduce your practice time, or both ([What is Mindfulness?](https://www.shinzen.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/WhatIsMindfulness_SY_Public_ver1.5.pdf)). In Britton's study, lasting bad effects went with signs of hyperarousal (for example agitation or poor sleep) and dissociation (for example feeling unreal or detached) ([Britton et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8845498/)). If you notice these, cut back. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, stop and contact a doctor or a crisis line.
#### End Callout

Which claim will you test?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner read four experiment protocols from the secular and pragmatic meditation module: (1) Shinzen Young's claim that suffering is pain multiplied by resistance, tested with his pain exercise; (2) MBSR's claim that it reduces stress and helps you live with more calm, tested with the free Palouse Mindfulness course; (3) Culadasa's claim that progress runs through stages you can recognise yourself, tested with 30 days of sitting; (4) Shinzen's and Ingram's claim that the sense of a solid self comes from tangled sensations, tested with noting. They now name the claim they will test. Check that it is a claim someone made, stated so that it could turn out false. If it is vague ('meditation helps'), ask which result would show it. If they say they do not understand, ask which of the four experiments interests them most. Reply in 40 to 80 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open

content::
What result would count against the claim?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is finishing a protocol for a meditation experiment (the pain-and-resistance test, home MBSR, a month of sitting scored by Culadasa's stages, or a noting practice) and now says which result would count against the claim. A good answer is specific and decided in advance: for the pain test, the unpleasantness rating does not fall more than the intensity rating; for MBSR, no change in life satisfaction or stress beyond what a comparison period shows; for Culadasa, no increase in time on the breath from week 1 to week 4; for noting, finding something in the sense of 'me' that is not a body feeling, image or inner talk. If their answer is 'nothing could', point out that then the experiment cannot test the claim. If it is vague, suggest a threshold. Reply in 40 to 80 words. No generic praise. One turn.
