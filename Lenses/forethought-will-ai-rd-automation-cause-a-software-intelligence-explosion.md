summary_for_tutor: "A Forethought report analyzing whether AI systems capable of fully automating AI R&D (which it labels ASARA) would trigger a 'software intelligence explosion' (SIE): accelerating AI progress driven by software improvements alone, with computing hardware held constant. It first argues that once ASARA exists, the compute used to train it could instead run the equivalent of millions of top human researchers, sharply raising the pace of progress. It then builds a toy model and an economic framework centered on r, the returns to software R&D (how many times software capability doubles per doubling of cumulative R&D effort): r > 1 yields an accelerating explosion, r = 1 sustained exponential growth, and r < 1 a 'fizzle' that can still run fast for a while. Reviewing empirical efficiency-doubling data across image recognition, language translation, games, and LLMs (software efficiency doubling roughly every ~6 months) plus Epoch's r estimates, it puts current r plausibly around 1 to 4, suggesting an SIE is plausible. It examines two major bottlenecks that could suppress an SIE even with favorable r (fixed compute limiting how many experiments can run, and the months needed to train each new model generation) and argues plausible workarounds mean these would likely delay rather than prevent an SIE. It closes with governance implications, warning that hardware-centric AI policy could be outpaced, and proposes measuring software progress, evaluating models' AI-R&D capabilities, and adopting thresholds on AI-driven acceleration backed by precommitted safeguards."
