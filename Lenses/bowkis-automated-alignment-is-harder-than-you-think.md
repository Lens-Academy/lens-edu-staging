title: "Automated alignment is harder than you think"
summary_for_tutor: "Abstract of the paper by Aleksandr Bowkis, Marie Davidsen Buhl, Jacob Pfau and Geoffrey Irving (2026). Argues that using AI agents to automate alignment research could produce compelling but catastrophically misleading safety assessments, even if the agents are not scheming. The reason is that alignment research involves many hard-to-supervise 'fuzzy' tasks with no clear evaluation criteria, so outputs will contain undetected errors and may be aggregated into overconfident conclusions. Gives four reasons this is worse for AI than for human researchers: optimisation pressure concentrates errors where reviewers are least likely to catch them, AI errors don't resemble human mistakes, AI arguments may be beyond human evaluation, and shared weights and training make AI outputs more correlated. Names generalisation and scalable oversight as the main remedies, but says both face new challenges here."
