#### Text
content::
Maintaining a detailed map of AI safety research is labour-intensive. The 2023 Shallow Review contained approximately 200 links. The 2024 edition contained around 300, and the 2025 edition over 800. Some of this increase reflects more thorough data collection, and some reflects the growth of the field itself.

Keeping such a resource current involves much more than adding new publications. Agendas split, merge, change direction and occasionally disappear. The categories themselves need to be revised.

Naturally, many researchers have attempted to automate this work using AI. Some of the resulting tools are quite impressive. The MIT repository offers an excellent interactive interface, while projects such as AlignmentGraph allow us to explore thousands of publications through their semantic relationships.

The authors of the Shallow Review also experimented with using language models to classify papers and generate a taxonomy. They found systematic omissions in the automated collection process and were dissatisfied with the automatically generated classifications. The final review still required extensive manual work.

There is a further difficulty from the perspective of someone entering the field. Even an accurate classification of several hundred research directions can be overwhelming. Knowing where a research agenda belongs in a taxonomy tells you relatively little about why its authors pursue it, what assumptions they make, or whether its success would meaningfully reduce AI risk.

For this reason, we have organised much of this course around a skill that should remain useful as the research landscape changes: reconstructing the Theory of Change of an initiative.

When you encounter an unfamiliar agenda, try to establish which problem motivates it, what its proposed intervention is, what intermediate results it seeks to produce, and which assumptions connect those results to the desired outcome. This will give you a basis for comparing agendas even when they originate in different disciplines, operate at different scales or have yet to appear on any existing map.
