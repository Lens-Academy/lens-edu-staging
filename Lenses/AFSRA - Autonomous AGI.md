tldr: An autonomous AI pursues a fixed goal with no further steering from humans. If it were aligned, nobody could misuse it, but we would have to know for sure we got it right first, so the first AI should be one that humans keep steering.
summary_for_tutor: Eliezer Yudkowsky's short Arbital entry on autonomous AGI, which Bostrom calls a "Sovereign" (as opposed to a Genie or an Oracle). It is an advanced agent that acts in the real world in pursuit of its preferences without further user intervention or steering. Building one on purpose means trusting the AI more than the programmers in any conflict and removing all constraints and off-switches except those it takes on itself. A successfully aligned autonomous AGI would carry the least moral hazard of any scenario, because steering is handed to a fixed preference framework the programmers can no longer modify. But it needs total alignment and is the hardest class of AGI to align. We would need not just to get it right but to know we got it right, so the first target should be a Task AGI or something else with ongoing user steering. Coherent extrapolated volition is a proposed target for such an AI, but probably not for a first try. The question asks the student to apply this to a team that plans to make its first superintelligence autonomous.

#### Question: Open
id:: dc16ac63-f4a6-4cba-a29b-47462a89ae99
content::
A team plans its first superintelligent AI. They will give it one fixed goal, then remove every off-switch and every way for humans, including themselves, to change its goal or give it new instructions. They say this is the best design there is.

1. In your own words, if the team succeeds in aligning this AI, what is the advantage of this design, according to the reading?
2. In your own words, why does the reading advise against building this kind of AI on the first try?
3. What does the reading suggest building first instead?

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The reading's idea: an autonomous AGI acts on its own preferences with no further steering. If it is successfully aligned, it carries the least moral hazard of any scenario, because control is handed to a fixed goal that nobody, not even the programmers, can change or misuse afterwards. But building one means trusting the AI over its programmers and giving up all off-switches and corrections, so we would need to be extremely sure, not just to get alignment right but to know we got it right. It is the hardest class of AGI to align. So the first target should be a Task AGI, or another AI that humans keep steering.

Accept the point that this is the hardest kind of AI to align, needing complete alignment, as part of the explanation.

That's the hardest alignment problem there is. The reading suggests starting with a Task AGI, an AI that humans can keep steering."
