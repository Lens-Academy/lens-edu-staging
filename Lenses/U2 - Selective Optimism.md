\## The gap between yours and his

Reread the criticism you wrote on the last page. Which of Ngo's criticisms is closest to yours? Then one line each: what did he see that you did not, and what did you see that he did not. If you think he is wrong about the scenario, say where.

This takes about five minutes.

feedback-instructions:: The student wrote their own criticism of AI 2040 on the previous page of this module (it is earlier in this conversation) and has now read Richard Ngo's "Selective Optimism". Ngo's structural objection, from this lens's summary: the scenario presents contingent political choices as features of the landscape, which narrows the actions its readers can imagine. One turn. Reflect back in one sentence the criticism the student named as closest, and check that their "what he saw" line is a point Ngo actually makes rather than a restatement of their own. If they wrote no "what you saw" line, ask, without waiting for a reply, what in their criticism Ngo's does not cover. If they disagree with Ngo and give a reason, treat that as a full answer. Do not grade. Do not praise. Do not settle who is right. Response length: 60 to 100 words. Do not invite further dialogue.
