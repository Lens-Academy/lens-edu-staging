title: "What is Evidence?"
tldr: "Evidence is an event that comes out differently depending on the thing you want to know about. A belief you would hold whatever you saw is not connected to the facts, so it is not evidence for anyone."
summary_for_tutor: "Yudkowsky defines evidence as an event entangled, by links of cause and effect, with whatever you want to know about: it has to happen differently for different states of the target. Examples: light from your untied shoelaces reaching your eyes, a machine that beeps for winning and losing lottery numbers alike (useless), a rock that photons hit without changing (useless witness), photographic film (picks up the entanglement). If your eyes and brain work, your beliefs become entangled with the facts and are themselves evidence, which an honest speaker can pass to a listener. So beliefs held unconditionally are blind, and a claim that your reasons are private and cannot be shared is suspicious. The question asks whether a friend's unshakeable, unexplainable certainty that a stock will double is evidence, and what her inability to explain her reasons tells you."

#### Question: Open
id:: 0d106a88-68f4-4de9-9f6a-dce259ec6810
content::
Your friend says: "I'm sure this company's stock will double this year. Nothing I could read or hear would change my mind, and I can't explain my reasons to you. I just know."

1. Should her certainty count as evidence that the stock will double? Explain why, using the essay's idea of what evidence is.

2. What does it tell you that she says she cannot explain her reasons, and why?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Background for the grader: the essay defines evidence as an event linked by cause and effect to what you want to know, so that it comes out differently depending on how the world is. A belief formed this way is itself evidence, and an honest person can pass it on to others by telling them. A belief held no matter what you see is like a retina that ends up the same whatever light hits it: blind. A claim that your reasons are private and cannot be shared is suspicious, because beliefs that track reality should be shareable among honest people. Score out of 100. 60: says her certainty is not evidence (or almost none), because she would hold it whatever the company's real prospects were. Her belief does not come out differently depending on the facts, so it is not linked to them. 40: says her being unable to share her reasons is a warning sign that her belief is not linked to the facts, because a belief that tracks reality could be passed on to an honest listener. Cap at 30 if the answer says her strong confidence is itself good evidence. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "No. Her belief would be the same whatever was true about the company, since nothing could change it, so it doesn't track the facts and tells me nothing about the stock. And if her belief really were connected to how the company is doing, she should be able to pass that on to me. Saying her reasons can't be shared suggests there's no real link."
