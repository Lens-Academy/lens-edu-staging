Lead author Thomas Kwa pushes back on his own graph's biggest misreadings: it's not how long an AI can work alone, it's not precise to better than a factor of two, and it varies wildly by domain. A guide to reading the trend honestly, and the conclusions he still stands behind."
summary_for_tutor: "Author Thomas Kwa of METR clarifies limitations of the time-horizon methodology and corrects common misinterpretations. Establishes that time horizon measures the amount of serial human labor an AI can replace at a 50% success rate, not how long an AI can work independently, and details its imprecision (error bars around a factor of two), its order-of-magnitude variation across domains, its inapplicability to some task distributions, gaps between benchmark and real-world tasks, why a 50% horizon does not mean tasks shorter than that can be safely delegated, and why 20%/80% and 99%-reliability horizons cannot be estimated independently or at all with current data. Concludes with what still holds: time horizon converts benchmark performance into an intuitive capability measure, and the robust findings are the long-run doubling every 6-7 months and the extrapolation to a one-month horizon around 2030."
