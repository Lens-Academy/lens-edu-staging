\## Numbing

Psychologists have their own name for shutting feelings out. **Experiential avoidance** is "excessive negative evaluations of unwanted private thoughts, feelings, and sensations, an unwillingness to experience these private events, and deliberate efforts to control or escape from them" ([Kashdan et al. 2006](https://mason.gmu.edu/~tkashdan/publications/exp_avoid_vulnerablity_BRAT.pdf), drawing on Steven Hayes, a founder of acceptance and commitment therapy). Welwood writes from his therapy practice. These are his clinical impressions, not counted cases. In our reading, the three look-alikes share one feature: the feeling is kept out instead of felt. That is the opposite of Shinzen Young's "opening to the pain" and of the acceptance instruction in Troy's study, though from outside both can look calm.

force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner just read Kornfield, Nyanaponika, Desbordes et al. 2015 on equanimity versus indifference, the abstract of Kashdan et al. 2006 on experiential avoidance, and Welwood on spiritual bypassing, and wrote a free recall.

Key points:
- Near enemy: a state that looks like a virtue but is something else. For equanimity it is indifference or callousness (Kornfield). Kornfield: indifference is based on fear, true equanimity is balanced engagement, not withdrawal.
- Nyanaponika: equanimity rests on vigilant presence of mind, not indifferent dullness. Compassion keeps it from falling into cold indifference and selfish isolation.
- Desbordes et al.: Buddhist texts separate equanimity from indifference (apathy, lethargy). The mother hearing about her grown son's business: caring and attentive, not entangled. Equanimity does not mean suppressing emotions. Fear when a driver cuts in is useful, what goes is the blame and resentment afterwards. Being indifferent to a loved one's death would be pathological. No emotional response where one is expected is a mark of psychopathology. Signature of equanimity: faster return to baseline, not no reaction.
- Kashdan et al.: experiential avoidance (unwillingness to stay in contact with unwanted inner experience) predicted more distress, fewer positive experiences and events, more than reappraisal or suppression did.
- Welwood: spiritual bypassing uses spiritual ideas and practice to sidestep emotional wounds and unfinished development. Practising nonattachment by dismissing your need for love drives it underground. Meditation can reinforce coldness and distance.

Acknowledge what is right without inflation, name the most important missing point (especially the observable signs: faster return to baseline versus no reaction, continued care and engagement), correct errors in one sentence, normalise gaps.
