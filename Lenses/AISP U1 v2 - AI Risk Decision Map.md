#### Text
content::
\## Build the model you actually have

You have now seen several different ways AI could matter for catastrophic risk, several different kinds of uncertainty, a serious case for precaution, serious limits on precaution, and several arguments for doing less x-risk work than some advocates recommend.

The aim of this exercise is not to compress all of that into one probability. A single number can be useful, but it can also hide the structure that matters for decisions. We want the structure.

Keep this map. Later units will return to different parts of it. Unit 2 will challenge what you are treating as valuable. Unit 4 will challenge your assumptions about agency and power-seeking. Unit 5 will challenge what you count as evidence about AI cognition and deception. Units 6 and 7 will challenge your assumptions about control and institutions. Unit 8 will return to what a good future was supposed to mean in the first place.

#### Question: Open
id:: b89a6d57-0b75-440c-81c2-2c42ded30c28
content::
Spend about 20 minutes on the six parts below.

**1. Your current conclusion.** How seriously should society treat catastrophic risks from advanced AI today? You may give a probability if it helps, but you do not have to.

**2. Your risk landscape.** List the two or three pathways that contribute most to your concern. They can include malicious use, race dynamics, organizational failure, rogue AI, gradual disempowerment, accumulative collapse, large-scale suffering, or another pathway you can defend.

**3. Your load-bearing beliefs.** Write three to five claims that carry most of your conclusion. Make each claim specific enough that it could turn out false.

**4. Your strongest skeptical challenge.** Pick the best reason you can find for giving AI catastrophic-risk work less priority. This can challenge probability, tractability, opportunity cost, timing, or something else.

**5. Your highest-value information.** Name one observation, experiment, forecast, or development that would most change an important part of your decision. Explain which claim it bears on and what you would do differently depending on the result.

**6. Your action under uncertainty.** Name one action that seems justified now. Explain one important way your model could be wrong while the action would still make sense, and one way you could be wrong that would make you stop or redirect the action.

feedback-instructions::
This is an ungraded running artifact. Do not pressure the learner toward higher or lower concern.

Help make the map explicit. Look for vague claims, duplicated pathways, evidence that would not actually discriminate between hypotheses, skeptical arguments that attack a different claim from the learner's own, and actions called robust even though they rely on one narrow model.

Use up to four tutor replies. Ask focused questions only where the map is unclear.

At the end, summarize in 150 to 220 words: the learner's current conclusion, main pathways, two most load-bearing beliefs, strongest skeptical challenge, proposed high-value information, and robust action. End by reminding them that later units will revisit this map.
