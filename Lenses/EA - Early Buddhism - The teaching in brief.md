#### Text
content::
**Early Buddhism** here means what the oldest surviving Buddhist texts teach: the discourses (**suttas**) of the **Pali canon**. **Theravada** is the school that still lives by those texts, together with later commentaries. It is the main form of Buddhism in Sri Lanka, Thailand, Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos (both points from the reading below).

The overview is by John Bullitt, written for Access to Insight, a free online library of Theravada texts. He writes as someone inside the tradition, so read it as the tradition describing itself. Two of its claims matter most for this course: that stress and dissatisfaction (**dukkha**) come from **craving** (*tanha*, "for sensuality, for becoming, or for not-becoming" in the [Access to Insight glossary](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/glossary.html)), and that craving can end completely, which Bullitt calls "supreme happiness".

Key points from the reading:
- Theravada draws on the Pali canon, which scholars generally agree is the earliest surviving record of the Buddha's teachings. Nobody can prove it contains his actual words. Its teachings are meant to be put into practice and checked by results.
- Four noble truths: dukkha (stress, suffering, discontent), its cause (craving for sensuality, for becoming, for non-becoming), its cessation (relinquishing that craving), and the path (the noble eightfold path: right view, resolve, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration).
- Tasks: the first truth is to be comprehended, the second abandoned, the third realized, the fourth developed.
- Kamma and rebirth are part of the picture Bullitt gives: craving drives the cycle of rebirth.
- The path factors fall into discernment (view, resolve), virtue (speech, action, livelihood, condensed in the five precepts) and concentration (effort, mindfulness, concentration, leading to jhana). Mindfulness of breathing balances tranquillity (samatha) and insight (vipassana). The meditator learns to see experience in terms of anicca (inconstancy), dukkha and anatta (not-self).
- Four stages of awakening: stream-entry (doubt, belief in rites and the notion of an abiding self fall away), once-returning, non-returning, arahantship (all craving and conceit end).
