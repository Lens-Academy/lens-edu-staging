The last page was about calm that only looks like equanimity. This one is about what meditation itself can do, good intentions included. The first reading reports a study by Willoughby Britton's lab at Brown University, which asked people in eight-week mindfulness programmes specific questions about bad effects.

#### Text
content::
A review of 83 studies with 6,703 people found adverse events in 8.3% of meditators overall, 3.7% in experimental studies and 33.2% in observational ones, and found that they "may occur in individuals with no previous history of mental health problems" ([Farias et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32820538/)). In a survey of 1,370 regular meditators, unpleasant or adverse effects were more likely in people with a previous mental disorder, people who had been on retreats, and people prone to repetitive negative thinking ([Pauly et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8693904/)).

\## A checklist

Here are the failure modes from this part of the module, each with a sign you can check and the source the sign comes from. The signs are things you or someone close to you could observe.

| Failure mode | A sign to check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Indifference**, the near enemy of equanimity | Do you still care about and act for the people affected? Equanimity is "balanced engagement", not withdrawal | [Kornfield](https://jackkornfield.com/equanimity-vs-indifference/), [Nyanaponika](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/nyanaponika/wheel006.html) |
| **No reaction at all** | Do you still react, and then come back quickly? A faster return to baseline is the proposed sign of equanimity. No response where one is expected is a warning sign | [Desbordes et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350240/) |
| **Experiential avoidance** (numbing) | Do you avoid thoughts, places or people because they bring a feeling up? | [Kashdan et al. 2006](https://mason.gmu.edu/~tkashdan/publications/exp_avoid_vulnerablity_BRAT.pdf) |
| **Spiritual bypassing** | Do you use a teaching, such as non-attachment or no-self, to dismiss a need, for example for love? | [Welwood](https://www.johnwelwood.com/articles/TRIC_interview_uncut.pdf) |
| **The self-report blind spot** | Would someone close to you say you sleep, eat and seem as usual? | [Martin](https://nonsymbolic.org/PNSE-Article.pdf) |
| **Lost repair** | When you have wronged someone, do you still make amends? | [Hafenbrack, LaPalme and Solal 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000298) |
| **Lost motivation** | Do ordinary tasks you value still get started? | [Hafenbrack and Vohs 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.05.001) |
| **Adverse effects** | Do you feel flat, disconnected, anxious or over-stimulated, and is your daily functioning worse? | [Britton et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8845498/) |

Britton's study found that open questions missed most adverse effects, so ask yourself these as specific questions, and ideally let someone else ask you. If your functioning drops, stop the practice and talk to a doctor or therapist.

#### Question: Open

content::
Run the checklist on yourself now, or on a time when you practised a lot. Which item would be hardest for you to notice from the inside?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner applies the module's failure-mode checklist (indifference, no reaction at all, experiential avoidance, spiritual bypassing, the self-report blind spot, lost repair, lost motivation, adverse effects) to themselves and names the item hardest to notice from the inside. This is personal reflection, not a test, and the learner may have no meditation practice to speak of; then the question applies to any way they keep calm.

Confirm in one or two sentences why the item they chose is hard to see from inside, using the source behind it (for example Martin's participant reported no stress while his partner saw poor sleep and tension; Britton found open questions missed most adverse effects; Hafenbrack's participants felt less guilt and so gave less, without noticing a loss). If they disclose current distress, flat mood, disconnection or worse functioning, say plainly that this is worth taking to a doctor or therapist and that stopping or reducing the practice is reasonable. Do not diagnose. 50 to 100 words. One reply.

force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner names an outside check for the checklist item they said would be hardest to notice from the inside (their previous answer is just above; if you cannot see it, ask which item in one line). Judge whether the check would actually catch that item: a partner asked specific questions about sleep, appetite, mood and tension (Martin), specific rather than open questions about adverse effects (Britton), or a record of whether they still make amends or start ordinary tasks (Hafenbrack). If it would not, suggest one concrete check from the checklist. If they have no one to ask, accept a written log as a fallback without insisting. Do not diagnose. 50 to 100 words. One reply, then send them on.
