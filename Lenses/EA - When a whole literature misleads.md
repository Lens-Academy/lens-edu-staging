\## The control group for science

Scott Alexander uses parapsychology, the study of psychic powers, as a test case. Most scientists think psychic powers do not exist, yet the field publishes positive results. Read his first section, the start of the second and a passage from near the end.

\## Two longer readings

Both are optional and long.

- [Mind the Hype](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/56912114/Mind_the_Hype.pdf) (Van Dam and colleagues, 2018) is a critical review of meditation research written by meditation researchers. It discusses unclear definitions of mindfulness, the limits of self-report questionnaires, unmonitored adverse effects and media hype.
- [Why Most Published Research Findings Are False](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124) (Ioannidis, 2005) argues that findings are more likely to be false when studies are small, effects are small, and a field is popular. Its conclusion is disputed: [Jager and Leek (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxt007) estimated a false discovery rate of about 14% in top medical journals, and other statisticians in turn criticised their estimate.
