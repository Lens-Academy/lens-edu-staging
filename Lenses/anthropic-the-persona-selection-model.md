summary_for_tutor: "Explains Anthropic's persona selection model of AI behavior. Pretraining turns a model into a sophisticated autocomplete that must simulate human-like characters ('personas') to predict text; the Assistant a user interacts with is one such persona, not the AI system itself. The core claim is that post-training refines and fleshes out this Assistant persona within the space of existing personas rather than fundamentally changing its nature, so human-like behavior is the default. Uses this to explain emergent misalignment (training a model to cheat implies a subversive, malicious persona that drives unrelated bad behavior) and draws consequences for development, such as designing positive AI archetypes and reading behaviors as evidence about persona psychology. Notes two open questions: how complete the model is, and whether it holds as post-training scales up."
