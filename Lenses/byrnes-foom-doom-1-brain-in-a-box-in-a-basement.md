title: "Foom & Doom 1: “Brain in a box in a basement”"
summary_for_tutor: "Steven Byrnes, in the first post of a two-part series, argues for 'foom': a sharp, localized takeoff. Unlike most alignment researchers, he does not expect LLMs to scale to ASI. Instead he expects a yet-undiscovered 'simple(ish) core of intelligence' (brain-like AGI), and he points to the human cortex as an existence proof. He predicts this paradigm will need very little training compute (around one consumer gaming GPU for human-level AGI) and maybe 0 to 30 person-years of R&D to go from seemingly irrelevant to superintelligence. The consequences he draws are pessimism about delaying or regulating AGI, a very sharp takeoff even without recursive self-improvement, no deployment before takeover, a likely decisive strategic advantage, and doubts about AI-assisted alignment. His guess for AGI timelines is probably 5 to 25 years."
