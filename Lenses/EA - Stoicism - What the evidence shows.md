#### Text
content::
Does living like a Stoic make people happier? The evidence comes in four kinds, from weakest to strongest design. Almost all of it comes from people who also teach or recommend Stoicism, so keep that in mind as you read.

#### Callout: 1. Before-and-after surveys: Stoic Week
tone:: neutral
#### Text
content::
Every year since 2013 the non-profit Modern Stoicism has run Stoic Week, a free online course, and asked participants to fill in well-being questionnaires at the start and end ([Modern Stoicism](https://modernstoicism.com/modern-stoicism-the-organization-and-what-it-does/)). Here is the 2025 report, written by Tim LeBon, the organisation's Director of Research, and posted on its newsletter.

source:: [[../articles/polat-stoic-week-2025-report-well-being-vitality-happiness]]
from:: Participants who completed Stoic Week 2025 showed improvements across all major well-being measures
to:: rather than simply capturing short-term mood fluctuations.

#### Text
content::
From the report's appendix: 1,424 people filled in the questionnaires at the start of the week, and 410 (28.8%) at the end. Their mean age was 50. The percentages above are for those 410 ([Stoic Week 2025 report, Appendix B](https://stoicismtoday.substack.com/p/stoic-week-2025-report-well-being)).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 2. Correlation: are more Stoic people happier?
tone:: neutral
#### Text
content::
In 2025 LeBon and five colleagues published a validated questionnaire for Stoic attitudes, the Stoic Attitudes and Behaviours Scale ([LeBon et al. 2025, *Cognitive Therapy and Research*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-025-10635-9), [copy on a co-author's site](https://johannes-karl.com/files/papers/lebon-2025-development.pdf)). In about 6,000 people starting Stoic Week or the longer Stoic Mindfulness and Resilience Training, higher scores went with higher life satisfaction (correlation .43), a better balance of positive over negative feelings (.53) and more flourishing (.50) (their Table 6).

The paper also measured "stoicism" in the everyday sense, hiding feelings and keeping a stiff upper lip, with the Liverpool Stoicism Scale. That scale was unrelated to the Stoic scale (−.1, not significant) and went with less flourishing (−.25). The authors write that their sample was likely self-selected: people already interested in Stoicism, many of whom agreed strongly with Stoic principles.

A correlation in this sample cannot tell whether Stoic attitudes cause well-being, whether happier people find Stoicism appealing, or whether something else drives both.
#### End Callout

#### Text
content::
Two things to notice. The "large effect sizes" are changes within the training group from before to after, not the difference between the groups. And the control group was a waiting list, so its members knew they were not being trained. The full thesis is closed until November 2027 ([Royal Holloway](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/modern-stoicism-and-mental-health-a-randomised-controlled-trial/)), and we found no peer-reviewed article from it.

A further trial with about 200 university students, Stoic training against an active control, was accepted in principle as a registered report in November 2025, which means its methods were reviewed before data collection ([Peer Community in Registered Reports](https://rr.peercommunityin.org/PCIRegisteredReports/articles/rec?id=1103)). We found no published results.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 4. Indirect: the CBT link
tone:: neutral
#### Text
content::
Cognitive behavioural therapy, which you met in the previous lens, is the most researched form of psychotherapy ([David, Cristea and Hofmann 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00004/full)). In our reading, that supports the techniques CBT took from the Stoics, used as therapy for problems such as depression. It is not direct evidence for Stoicism as a whole way of life.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Who gains
tone:: amber
#### Text
content::
Modern Stoicism is a non-profit, its courses are free, and it asks for donations ([Modern Stoicism](https://modernstoicism.com/modern-stoicism-the-organization-and-what-it-does/)). Its researchers evaluate their own course, and several of them write popular books on Stoicism (Tim LeBon's is linked at the end of the report above). Stoicism is also a business: Ryan Holiday's Daily Stoic sells courses and medallions, for example a "Premeditatio Malorum" medallion for 30 dollars ([Daily Stoic store](https://store.dailystoic.com/collections/coins)).
#### End Callout

#### Text
content::
In our reading, the evidence supports a modest claim: a week or two of Stoic exercises is followed by better self-reported mood, and in two small randomised studies by less rumination or fewer depression symptoms than in the control groups. No study tests the school's central claim that good character is enough for a happy life whatever happens. That claim is philosophical, and the experiments in the next lens are one way to test parts of it on yourself.

#### Question: Open
id:: 290f6ee3-4dc9-4d1f-99ac-ce0bef71bec9
content::
Stoic Week participants reported 12% more life satisfaction at the end of the week than at the start. Give two explanations for that rise other than "Stoic practice works". For each, say which of the studies above, or which study design, would rule it out.
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner read four kinds of evidence on Stoicism: the Stoic Week 2025 report (before and after, no control group, self-selected, self-report, 410 of 1,424 completed), the SABS correlations (about 6,000 self-selected participants, colloquial stoicism negatively related to flourishing), two randomised studies (MacLellan and Derakshan 2021, 45 high worriers, active control; King 2025 thesis, 119 participants, waitlist control, not yet peer reviewed) and a note that CBT evidence is indirect. In module 3 they learned to weigh evidence by its blind spots.

Good alternative explanations include: only the people who benefited finished the questionnaire (dropout, 410 of 1,424); people expected to feel better or wanted to please the organisers (expectancy, demand, self-report); people who sign up for Stoic Week are already keen on it (self-selection); scores drift back toward normal after a bad patch (regression to the mean); any week of daily structured reflection might do the same (non-specific effects of attention and routine); something else happened that week. Which design addresses which: a randomised control group (King, MacLellan) handles regression to the mean and outside events. An active control that also takes daily time (MacLellan's control task, the planned registered report) handles non-specific effects of routine. A waitlist control does not handle expectancy, since people know whether they are getting the training. Analysing everyone who started handles dropout. Measures other than self-report would address self-report bias, and none of these studies relies mainly on them.

Reply in 90 to 150 words. Name the strongest of their explanations and whether the design they matched to it really rules it out. If they missed dropout or the difference between a waitlist and an active control, name one in a sentence. No generic praise. One turn. If the learner says they do not understand, give one foothold: ask what might be different about the 410 who finished compared with the 1,014 who did not.
