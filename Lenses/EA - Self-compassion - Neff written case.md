feedback-instructions:: The learner read two sections of Kristin Neff's 2023 Annual Review article: "Self-compassion versus self-esteem" and "Self-compassion enhances rather than undermines motivation". In the previous answer on this page they named the study they find strongest, and here they say what makes it stronger. There is no single right answer, but help them judge by design. Experiments that manipulate self-compassion (Breines and Chen 2012, where students told to be self-compassionate after a failed test studied longer; Moffitt et al. 2018 on body image threat; Miyagawa et al. 2020; the waitlist-controlled Albertson et al. 2015) support causal claims better than correlational studies (Neff and Vonk 2009, Liao et al. 2021 meta-analysis of correlations, Hope et al. 2014, Marshall et al. 2015), though studies that follow people over time are stronger than one-time surveys. If they chose a correlational study, ask whether it shows that self-compassion causes the outcome. If they chose an experiment, ask what they would want to know about sample size or replication, without stating details the reading does not give. 60 to 110 words. At most 2 replies. No generic praise.
