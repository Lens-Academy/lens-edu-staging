#### Text
content::
Jeffery Martin is a social scientist who has spent more than 15 years interviewing people who say they live in a lasting state of the kind contemplative traditions call awakening or enlightenment ([his site](https://drjefferymartin.com/)). His academic name for it is **persistent non-symbolic experience (PNSE)**: a lasting change in the sense of self, thought, emotion, perception and memory, which he counts as persistent once it has lasted more than a year ([Martin's paper](https://nonsymbolic.org/PNSE-Article.pdf)). In his popular work he calls the same thing **fundamental wellbeing** ([Life Itself, summarising Martin](https://lifeitself.org/learn/fundamental-wellbeing)). He sorts the reports into **locations**, groups of similar experiences that lie along one continuum, from Location 1 near ordinary experience to Location 4 and beyond at the far end. He also designed a paid online course that aims to bring people into this state, and this module looks closely at its numbers.

This module covers what Martin reports, what stays the same in the people he studied, what his course claims, what critics say, and a few things you can test on yourself. First, Martin describes the state in a short interview on the TV series *Closer To Truth*.

#### Question: Open
id:: 3809c054-3207-4e43-a338-c61ff472ef09
content::
Martin says the far end of the continuum has "no emotion" and "no sense of agency whatsoever", but "tremendous well-being", and that people there would never want to go back. The interviewer says "count me out". Where do you stand right now?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner just watched a short Closer To Truth interview in which Jeffery Martin describes persistent non-symbolic consciousness: the individualized sense of self dissipates, agency declines until it disappears, it is a continuum, and the far end has no emotion and no agency but, he says, "tremendous well-being". Outsiders say "count me out", while people in the state say they would never go back. The learner says where they stand. The next question asks what they would want to know.

This is a reflection, not a test. There is no right position. In 40 to 80 words, short paragraphs, no lists: restate their position in one precise sentence and name the value it rests on (for example emotion, agency, peace), without judging it. Do not argue for or against the far end. No generic praise. One turn, then send them to the next question.

#### Question: Open
id:: 4fd6f9b6-037a-4a08-bb63-a8da0955bdc2
content::
What would you want to know before you would trust either reaction, the interviewer's "count me out" or the insiders' "I would never go back"?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner just watched Jeffery Martin describe the far end of persistent non-symbolic experience (no emotion, no agency, "tremendous well-being") and the two reactions: outsiders say "count me out", insiders say they would never go back. They now say what they would want to know before trusting either reaction.

This is a reflection, not a test. In 60 to 110 words, short paragraphs, no lists: pick the most useful thing they named and say briefly which later part of this module bears on it. The module next reads Martin's paper on the locations and what did not change in his participants (partner reports of stress, implicit bias tests, people who lost or rejected the state, and that participants tend to rate whatever location they are in as the best), then the evidence for his course, then experiments. If they did not name anything concrete, ask one question: whose report about someone's wellbeing would they trust more, the person's own or someone who lives with them, and why? No generic praise. One turn, then send them on.
