tldr: "An argument is only as good as the process that chose its conclusion. If the conclusion was written first and the reasons were collected afterwards, the conclusion tells you nothing about the world, however good the reasons sound."
summary_for_tutor: "Yudkowsky's essay about two sealed boxes, one of which holds a diamond. A clever arguer paid by box B's owner first writes \"therefore, box B contains the diamond\" and then lists only the signs that favour B. A curious inquirer first lists the signs of both boxes and only then writes a probability. The arguer's conclusion is entangled only with who paid more, the inquirer's with what is in the boxes. Your effectiveness is set by whichever algorithm actually writes your bottom line (the squealing brakes: the real algorithm is \"never repair anything expensive\"), and the arguments written above it change nothing. The essay says this is a caution for your own thinking, not a general counterargument against people you disagree with: the cleverest arguer may say the Sun is shining, and it is still probably daytime. #### Question: Open
id:: 968e9e28-9143-4662-986e-9339bcb0bf77
content::
Lena, a team lead, has already decided that her team will switch to project-management tool A. She asks an analyst to write a report "showing why tool A is the best choice". The analyst spends a week on it, and every fact in the final report is true.

1. What is the report's conclusion, "therefore, tool A is the best choice", evidence of, and why?

2. How would the analyst have had to work for that conclusion to be evidence about which tool is best?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

The idea the answer applies: a conclusion is evidence about the world only if the process that produced it depended on the evidence. Here the conclusion was fixed by Lena's decision before any evidence was looked at, and facts against tool A were never going to be included, so the conclusion would read the same whichever tool is really best. That every listed fact is true does not change this. A conclusion written only after weighing the evidence about all the options would have come out differently if the evidence had been different, so it would be evidence about the tools.

50: Part 1. The conclusion is evidence of Lena's decision (what she wanted, or who commissioned the report), not of which tool is best, because it was settled before the evidence was gathered. Give 25 if the answer only says the conclusion is biased or weak evidence without saying why.

50: Part 2. The analyst would have had to look at the evidence on all the options, including what counts against tool A, and only then decide the conclusion, so that different evidence could have led to a different conclusion. Give 25 if the answer only says to be neutral or to include downsides, without saying the conclusion must come after the evidence and depend on it.

Cap at 40 if the answer says the conclusion is good evidence for tool A because all the facts in the report are true.

Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "1. Mostly of Lena's decision. The conclusion was fixed before anyone looked at the evidence, and facts against A were never going in, so it would say the same whichever tool is best. 2. Gather the strengths and weaknesses of all the tools first, weigh them, and only then write the conclusion, so it could have come out for another tool."
feedback-instructions::
The student has just read Eliezer Yudkowsky's "The Bottom Line" and answered a question about a report written to support a decision that was already made. Key ideas of the essay: a conclusion is evidence only of the process that wrote it. The clever arguer writes the bottom line first and then picks favourable signs, so the conclusion is entangled only with who paid. The curious inquirer weighs the signs of both boxes first, so the conclusion depends on them. True arguments written above a fixed bottom line change nothing. The essay is a caution for your own thinking, not a general counterargument.

The most likely gaps: not saying that the conclusion was fixed before the evidence and so cannot depend on it, or a fix that only says "be balanced" without the conclusion coming last.

Reply in 80 to 150 words. Short paragraphs only. No lists. Do not over-validate. Avoid generic praise (great job, excellent, well done). Name the strongest part of the answer in one sentence. Below 100, name the most important thing the answer missed or got wrong, as the gap above describes, and say it plainly. At 100, confirm briefly and add nothing new to fix.

If the student says they do not understand, do not dismiss it and do not repeat the question. Give one concrete foothold from the essay: the two sheets of paper, the clever arguer who writes the conclusion first and the curious inquirer who writes it last. If their next message still does not attempt the question, rephrase the whole question in different terms instead of giving another foothold.
