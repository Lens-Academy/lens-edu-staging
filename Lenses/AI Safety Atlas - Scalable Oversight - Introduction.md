The chapter's premise is that once systems work past human expertise, our usual way of telling good work from bad stops functioning.

Does that strike you as a genuinely new problem, or the same one every field already has with its own experts?

TLDR of what they read:
The opening of the scalable oversight chapter. The problem is how to supervise and give accurate training signals to systems doing work that exceeds human expertise, since the usual method, a human judging whether the output is good, stops working. It names two parts of the difficulty: generating feedback for complex fuzzy tasks whose success criteria are subjective and ill-defined, unlike a game with a score; and the observation that verification is generally easier than generation, which is the foothold the rest of the chapter builds on. It then previews the techniques: task decomposition and factored cognition, process-based oversight including externalised reasoning oversight and procedural cloning, iterated distillation and amplification, AI safety via debate, and weak-to-strong generalization along with how it is evaluated.
