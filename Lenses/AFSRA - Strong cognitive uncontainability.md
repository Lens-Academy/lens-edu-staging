title: "Strong cognitive uncontainability"
tldr: Someone from the 10th century could not have foreseen air conditioning, and would not have believed a blueprint of one would work. A smarter agent may likewise win by ways we cannot list in advance or recognise when we see them.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Yudkowsky's Arbital page on strong cognitive uncontainability. A person from the 10th century asked how a 20th-century person might cool a house could offer clever ideas, but not air conditioning, because it uses physical laws they do not know. Even shown the exact blueprint, they would not expect it to work without being told about pressure and temperature, electric compressors and so on. A strongly uncontainable agent can use strategies that go through causal domains we cannot currently model, so even when told the exact strategy, we would not rate it as effective without further background facts. An agent can be strongly uncontainable in some domains and not others (everyone is containable at tic-tac-toe). Arguments for strong uncontainability in a domain: its richness and partial unknownness (human psychology), and outside-view induction from past cognitive advantages (the 10th century could not contain the 20th). Arguments against: a domain's apparent knownness (chemistry, lead into gold) and the Fermi Paradox as weak evidence bounding what agents can do. Key open questions include whether Oracles can be contained in a computational sandbox and how solid our knowledge of physics is. The question asks the student to apply this to a team that has listed and blocked every way it can think of for a boxed AI to affect the outside world.

#### Question: Open
id:: 46352ac4-d3d4-43f1-a281-b80d582921d8
content::
A team runs a powerful AI on an isolated server. They say: "We have listed every way this AI could affect the outside world (network cables, power lines, USB ports, radio signals) and blocked each one. And if it ever found a trick we had missed, we would recognise the trick as soon as we saw its plan."

Suppose the AI is strongly uncontainable at affecting the world outside its server.

Answer in your own words.

1. Why might blocking every route on their list not be enough?
2. Why might the team fail to recognise a working trick even when shown the AI's exact plan?
