Joe Carlsmith calls the quiet step "AI for AI safety" and argues it should be treated
as the central move, not the footnote. He frames it as a race between two feedback
loops. The excerpt gives you the loops and his case for urgency; his contrasts with
other views and his own list of objections stay collapsed below, yours to open. The
operational detail of handing work over lives elsewhere, in Ryan Greenblatt's
[[../articles/greenblatt-how-do-we-more-safely-defer-to-ais|How do we (more) safely defer to AIs?]]

One more thing. Whether a handoff like this is safe is not a side question. It is the
question the alignment field exists to answer, the door Unit 1 pointed at and this
course has kept walking past. We are still not walking through it. But notice that the
plans now depend on what is behind it.

#### Question
content::
\## Your turn first

Every plan ends with "and then the AIs help us do the rest." Before Carlsmith argues
for leaning into that, find your own limit.

Write two or three sentences naming the first safety-relevant task you would refuse to
hand to an AI assistant, and what the refusal is protecting.

Then write one more line, the one this lens is really after: what evidence would
change your mind and let you hand it over?

assessment-instructions::
CONTEXT YOU NEED. You do not have the rest of this course, so here is the situation.

This is the first lens of Unit 5, the final unit of a course about AI futures, written
for newcomers. Unit 4 was about plans for the transition to very capable AI, and this
unit asks where the leverage is. The student has NOT read the article yet. The article
is Joe Carlsmith's "AI for AI safety", excerpted to the introduction, the two feedback
loops, and the section on why it matters; his contrasts and objections render
collapsed. It is in your context. The student was asked for the first safety-relevant
task they would refuse to hand to an AI assistant, what the refusal protects, and what
evidence would change their mind.

The purpose is that they locate their own trust boundary before Carlsmith argues for
handing over as much as can be handed safely. The purpose is not for them to reproduce
the article. Do not deliver its answer.

WHAT CARLSMITH ARGUES. For your reference only. Do not deliver it.
- "AI for AI safety" means the differential use of frontier AI labor to strengthen
  three security factors: safety progress (automated alignment research above all),
  risk evaluation, and capability restraint.
- Two feedback loops: a capability loop, where AI labor accelerates AI capabilities,
  and a safety loop, where safe AI labor improves the security factors, which in turn
  unlock safe access to more capable AI labor. The aim is for the safety loop to
  outpace or restrain the capability loop.
- Why it matters: capabilities research will by default be massively accelerated by AI
  labor. If safety work cannot benefit comparably, then absent large amounts of
  capability restraint he thinks we are likely headed for disaster. So strategies
  relying solely on restraint, or on human-only alignment labor, carry a large burden
  of proof.
- Do NOT present Carlsmith as saying the handoff is safe by default. He calls AI labor
  the most powerful tool in the toolbox and also the most dangerous, says capability
  restraint stays extremely important either way, and names elicitation failures,
  differential sabotage, and rogue options as his most serious concerns, in sections
  the student may not open. A student who refuses to hand over nearly everything is
  not arguing against him; what to hand over, and when, is exactly his subject.

YOUR TASK, in order.
1. Name the property their refusal is protecting, in their own terms. Most refusals
   cash out as verification (they could not check the output), judgment (the task
   decides what matters, not just what works), or incentives (something benefits from
   fooling them). Say which one you see.
2. Ask one question about their evidence line: could they observe that evidence
   themselves, and who would have produced it? Evidence generated by the systems being
   trusted is the interesting case.
3. If they would hand everything over, take that seriously and ask which task they
   would hand over LAST. If their refusal is really about capability, that the AI
   would do the task badly, say so plainly and ask what changes on the day it does the
   task well.

Do not grade. Do not score. Do not praise. There is no correct answer at this stage.

Write 60 to 110 words. Short paragraphs. No lists.
