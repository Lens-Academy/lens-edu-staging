#### Text
content::
The readings so far each looked at one kind of evidence. The table puts them side by side, with one example from this course for each. Two kinds are new. An **observational study** follows people without assigning them anything, for example measuring the wellbeing of people who did or did not lose a spouse. Its main weakness is **confounding**: a third factor that causes both the supposed cause and the outcome ([Rohrer 2018](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3224114_7/component/file_3570047/content)). The second new kind is agreement between traditions that grew up separately.

| Kind of evidence | What it can show | What it cannot show | How it typically misleads | Example in this course |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Randomised trials and meta-analyses | Whether a practice changes an average outcome compared with a control group | What it does for you, what happens after the follow-up ends, outcomes nobody measured | Results that do not replicate ([Open Science Collaboration](https://langcog.stanford.edu/papers_new/osc-2015-science.pdf)), narrow samples ([Henrich et al.](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~henrich/pdfs/WeirdPeople.pdf)), hand-picked studies ([Alexander](https://slatestarcodex.com/2014/12/12/beware-the-man-of-one-study/)), passive controls and unblinded self-reports ([Galante et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1003481)), easy outcomes instead of the goal ([Desbordes et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350240/)) | Mindfulness programmes beat doing nothing but not other active programmes ([Galante et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1003481)) |
| Observational studies | What happens to people over years, after events nobody can assign, such as widowhood | Cause, unless chance decided who got what | Confounding ([Rohrer](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3224114_7/component/file_3570047/content)) | Lasting drops in life satisfaction after widowhood and unemployment in German panel data ([Diener, Lucas and Scollon](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/Documents/Diener-Lucas-Scollon_2006.pdf)). Swedish lottery prizes, where chance decided who won ([Lindqvist et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdaa006)) |
| First-person reports | What an experience is like, fine distinctions a questionnaire misses, where to look in other data ([Lutz and Thompson](https://evanthompson.me/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/jcs-neurophenomenology.pdf)) | Whether the report is accurate, or whether the experience shows what it seems to show about reality | Unreliable introspection ([Schwitzgebel](https://gwern.net/doc/philosophy/mind/2008-schwitzgebel.pdf)), expectations from a tradition's maps shaping the experience ([Anālayo](https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/meditationmaps.pdf)) | Martin's interviewees describing persistent non-symbolic experience (module 2) |
| Independent traditions that agree | Possibly a shared experience, or a shared feature of human minds | Whether the traditions describe the same experience, or whether it is accurate | Similar words for different experiences, or traditions that influenced each other | Epicurus, the highest grade of sukha and contentment all say the best happiness remains when wanting stops (module 2, our reading) |
| Testimonials | That something is possible for someone, ideas worth testing | Whether the practice caused the change | Regression to the mean ([Colquhoun](https://www.dcscience.net/2015/12/11/placebo-effects-are-weak-regression-to-the-mean-is-the-main-reason-ineffective-treatments-appear-to-work/)), expectation ([Szigeti et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7925122/)), survivorship bias | Microdosers who felt better on empty capsules ([Szigeti et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7925122/)) |
| Your own test | What a practice does for you, in your life | Whether it would work for others, whether expectation caused it when you cannot blind yourself | Expectation, other changes at the same time, stopping too early | The second half of this module |

Whether agreement between traditions is evidence of a shared reality is an open question in philosophy. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on [mysticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mysticism/) sets out both sides (optional, and module 4 has a page on it).

We think no row is useless and no row is enough. A claim is stronger when kinds of evidence with different blind spots point the same way. For a practice that is cheap and safe, we think your own test is often the best tie-breaker, because it answers the question you care about: what it does for you. The second half of this module is about running one well.

#### Question: Open
id:: 4a8ca4d5-3760-4d15-8752-e535a74fd5e2
content::
In module 2 you read Jeffery Martin's study of **persistent non-symbolic experience** (PNSE), his term for a lasting change in the sense of self that he elsewhere calls fundamental wellbeing. That page said this module would come back to how to weigh it. The facts: 50 interviewees who volunteered and described themselves as having PNSE, all white and 78% male, almost everything self-reported, and Martin also runs a course that aims to bring people into this state.

Martin's interviewees report an ongoing deep peace that does not depend on circumstances. Using the table, which kinds of evidence does this claim rest on? What can that evidence show, what can it not show, and what evidence would make the claim stronger?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner applies the evidence table to Martin's PNSE study, which module 2 promised module 3 would weigh. Facts given on the page: 50 interviewees who volunteered and described themselves as having PNSE, all white and 78% male, almost all self-report, Martin runs a course (the Finders Course) aimed at the state. The claim: interviewees report an ongoing deep peace that does not depend on circumstances. This is practice, not graded.

A good answer says: the evidence is mainly first-person reports, gathered as interviews, from a self-selected group (close to testimonials). It can show that some people sincerely describe such a state and what they say it is like, which is the only evidence of what it is like. It cannot show that the peace is as constant as reported (introspection can be wrong, and people who identify with a state may report it as they expect), that it does not depend on circumstances (no one measured them through bad events), how common it is, or what caused it. Weaknesses: volunteers who believe they have it (selection, survivorship), a narrow sample, expectation, and a researcher who also sells a course (a stake in the result). Stronger evidence: measures that do not rely only on self-report (reports by people who know them, behaviour, physiology), following people over time including through hard events, comparison with a matched group, independent researchers, a sample not selected by self-description, testing whether a course reliably produces the state.

Reply in 120 to 180 words. Name the strongest point in their answer and the most important thing they missed. Do not say whether PNSE is real. If they dismiss the study entirely, ask what, if anything, the reports can tell us that no other evidence could. One reply, then offer one more exchange if they want it. No generic praise. When the learner is stuck, give one concrete foothold from the table.
