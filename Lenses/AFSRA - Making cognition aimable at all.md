sidebar_author: Nate Soares
tldr: Choosing the right goal for an AI matters little if you cannot make it pursue any goal you choose. Soares says the first AIs will be a messy pile of parts with no clean place to put a goal, and building one that has such a place is most of the problem.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Nate Soares (posting as So8res) clears up a misunderstanding. When he says the lion's share of alignment is about pointing powerful cognition at anything at all, rather than figuring out what to point it at, he does not mean (1) that any practical AI will be cleanly organized around a single objective, so that the hard part is putting your goal into its "goal slot". He means (2) that the first minds humanity makes will be a spaghetti-code mess of interconnected kludges with no clearly factored-out goal, whose behaviour depends on how it irons out its internal tensions over time, for example under reflection. Making an AI even have something close to a goal slot that stays stable under pressures like reflection requires mastery of cognition in its own right. Separately, he expects a fully superintelligent AI to have ironed itself out around a particular goal, but by then it will not let you touch it. An AI that can force the future into a narrow band can usually be talked about as pursuing some objective, even while it is still a kludgey mess. The diamond maximizer problem was meant to point at cleanly factoring cognition around something like a goal slot, so training regimes that might make an AI care a little about diamonds in some contexts shed little light on it. The question asks the student to apply this to two research teams.

Two research teams want to make future AI go well.

Team A spends a year debating which goal humanity's first powerful AI should be given, comparing candidates such as "maximize human flourishing" and "do what a fair democratic process decides".

Team B rewards a model for collecting diamonds in a game. Afterwards, the model goes after diamonds in 80% of new test levels. Team B says: "We've shown we can give an AI the goal of collecting diamonds."

1. In your own words, which part of the alignment problem does Soares say is the larger part?
2. Does Team A's work address that part? Why?
3. In your own words, why would Soares say that Team B's result tells us little about that part?
