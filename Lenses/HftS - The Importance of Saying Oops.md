tldr: When you are wrong in a big way, admit the whole mistake at once. People who concede only small pieces of it, one at a time, change only a little and keep repeating the error.
summary_for_tutor: Yudkowsky uses Enron, whose executives never admitted a large mistake and hid each failure as a small local one, to argue that every improvement needs a change, and a big change needs the admission of a big mistake. Traditional Rationality asks you to concede to evidence eventually, but not quickly. Looking back on his own large mistake, he saw he had made a series of minimal, grudging concessions, when he could have moved much faster by saying "Oops". He warns against being proud of admitting errors, and argues that one large loss is better than many small ones and that people who defend their pride with "I was right in principle" repeat the mistake. The question describes a team lead who blames a new small cause each quarter for an app nobody uses, and asks for the pattern, what saying "oops" would look like, and why the essay thinks it is better.

#### Question: Open
id:: 7c7818f7-29ba-46c3-9675-0c8767f6f1ec
content::
A team has spent two years building an app that almost nobody uses. Each quarter the team lead names a new small cause: "the onboarding needs tweaking", then "the marketing was off", then "the timing wasn't right". Each time the team makes a small fix and carries on.

1. Using the essay, describe the pattern the team lead is following.
2. What would saying "oops" look like for this team?
3. In your own words, why does the essay think that is better?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The essay's idea: people who have made a big mistake often admit only small, local errors one at a time, conceding as little as possible each time and never admitting a fundamental mistake, usually to protect their pride. Yudkowsky argues it is better to admit the whole mistake at once: every improvement requires a change, and only admitting a big mistake motivates a big change, while small admissions lead only to small fixes. Seeing the error all at once is faster than being argued into it piece by piece, one large loss is better than many small ones, and defending your pride now means making the same mistake again.
30: Step 1. Describes the pattern: admitting only small, local mistakes one at a time and fixing them with small patches, never admitting that something bigger or fundamental might be wrong.
35: Step 2. Saying "oops" here means admitting at once that the problem may be fundamental, for example that the app idea or the whole approach was a mistake, instead of naming another small cause.
35: Step 3. Gives at least one of the essay's reasons: a big change only comes from admitting a big mistake, while small admissions bring only small fixes, or seeing the whole error at once is faster than conceding it bit by bit, or one large loss is better than many small ones, or protecting pride leads to repeating the mistake.
