\## A softer way in

In Units 1 and 2 you looked at what happens when one person offloads one judgement to a tool. Sometimes the oversight stays with you, as with the calculator. Sometimes it leaks away, as with metacognitive laziness. In both cases the effect is local. It is about you, and one decision.

Now widen the view. Picture the same thing happening not once, but again and again, across many people and many parts of life: navigation, writing, hiring, diagnosis, planning, and decisions about money and policy. Each step feels reasonable in the moment. Each step saves time or effort. None of them feels like a turning point.

That slow accumulation is the shape of what this unit calls **gradual disempowerment**.

Notice what it is not. It is not a machine suddenly seizing control. It is not a dramatic betrayal. The word "gradual" does the work: influence can slip away a little at a time, below the level where any single moment feels worth arguing about.

A small everyday echo: many of us once knew our way around a city from memory. Then we got turn-by-turn navigation. On each trip, handing the route to the phone was the easy choice. Today, plenty of people would hesitate to navigate a familiar area without it. No one voted to give up their sense of direction. It just accumulated, one convenient trip at a time.

The reading ahead makes a far larger and more serious claim, about the economy, culture, and states. You do not need to accept it yet. The point of this page is only to give you a calm mental model before the formal argument: small delegations, repeated and scaled, can add up to lost influence without anyone intending it.

Here is one way to make it concrete before the reading.

#### Question
content::
\## Your example
Think of one area of your life or work where a tool now does something you used to do yourself. Did the change arrive as a single big decision, or did it accumulate from many small convenient steps?

assessment-instructions:: This is a short grounding question at the very start of Unit 3, before the student reads the Kulveit et al. article on gradual disempowerment. The student has just read a gentle conceptual intro that frames gradual disempowerment as small delegations accumulating, using the navigation example.

Your job is light. Help the student locate a personal instance of quiet capability loss, so the abstract idea has a hook. Do not introduce the article's argument, the six claims, or the societal scale yet. This is setup, not teaching.

Acknowledge their example in 1 to 2 sentences, specifically and without judgement. If they say they cannot think of one, offer the navigation or spell-check example from the intro and ask whether something similar fits.

Close by pointing them to the reading: "Hold that example as you read. The piece ahead scales this exact dynamic up to the whole of society. Head to the reading when you are ready."
