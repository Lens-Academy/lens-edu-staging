\### Building networking into your existing activities

Many of the activities you would undertake to learn about AI safety or contribute to it can also help you develop professional relationships. One advantage of these habits is that they serve several purposes simultaneously.

- **Comment on LessWrong and the EA Forum.** Ask substantive questions, share relevant observations, point out potential problems with arguments and engage with authors whose work interests you.
- **Attend local AI safety community meetings.** You can find nearby groups through the [AISafety.com community directory](https://aisafety.com/communities).
- **Participate in online events**. That allows you to meet people working on specific problems regardless of your location. [Events (AISafety.com)](https://aisafety.com/events?view=online)
- **Organise activities around your own interests.** If you already intend to study a set of papers, consider inviting others to read and discuss them with you. Organising a reading group requires some additional effort, but it can enrich your learning experience and introduce you to people you might eventually want to work with.

\### EA Global: an event designed for networking

[EA Global (EAG)](https://www.effectivealtruism.org/ea-global) is one of the main networking events in the AI safety community. These conferences bring together researchers, grantmakers, organisation founders, policy specialists and people considering careers in the field.

Much of the conference revolves around one-on-one meetings (1:1s). Before the event, participants get access to a platform where they can browse attendee profiles, learn about their work and arrange meetings. Approaching someone you have never met, including senior researchers and organisation leaders, is an established part of the format. This makes EAG particularly useful for newcomers who have yet to develop professional connections in the field.

The programme also includes talks, workshops and thematic meetups, although some attendees spend almost the entire conference in 1:1s.

**EAGx** conferences use a similar format but are organised by regional communities. They generally have more accessible admission criteria and can be a good starting point for those exploring AI safety.
