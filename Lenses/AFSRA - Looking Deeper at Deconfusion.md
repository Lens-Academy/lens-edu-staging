title: "Looking Deeper at Deconfusion"
tldr: >-
  Deconfusion means breaking a confused idea down into clearer ideas you already understand, for a purpose you have in mind. You can tell it worked when the result fits your original intuitions and helps with that purpose.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Adam Shimi proposes a take on deconfusion: "the process of dissolving confusion, by reducing the object of thought to less confused and better understood ideas, with an application in mind." There is always an application, which can be anything, and it gives deconfusion its direction. Signs of confusion: incoherence, too many threads, self motte-and-bailey (changing what a word means without noticing), underdeterminacy, cruxless disagreement and language inadequacy. Confusion from inferential distance is not what deconfusion dissolves. Conceptual tools: handles (the end product, from a formal definition through a definition in terms of simpler concepts, an extensive definition as a list of examples, computational models, to a story), languages (new building blocks, like APIs) and translations (links to a better understood field). He compares deconfusion to writing a function you do not yet know how to write. Deconfusion is not limited to fundamental research and is a bottleneck for alignment. Handles are checked by accuracy (do they fit the initial intuitions?) and usefulness (do they help the application?). The question asks the student to spot signs of confusion in a team's arguments about an "honest" chatbot, propose a handle, and say how to check it.

#### Question: Open
id:: 6d9dbd1a-d4b6-44ae-a227-05bef0caf97f
content::
A research team keeps arguing about whether their chatbot is "honest". On Monday, one member says it is honest because it never says things it believes are false. On Thursday, the same member says it is not honest because it leaves out facts users would want, without noticing that they have changed what they mean by "honest". The team's debates never reach a point where they can say what exactly they disagree about. The team wants a notion of honesty they can use to test the chatbot before release.

1. Name two of the signs of confusion that Shimi lists which show up here, and say where each one shows up.
2. Propose one handle, in Shimi's sense, that the team could build for "honest", and say which kind of handle it is.
3. Using Shimi's standards for checking a handle, how should the team judge whether your handle works?

The debates never reach a statement of what they disagree about. The team wants a notion of honesty to test the chatbot before release. The reading's ideas: signs of confusion include incoherence (saying one thing, then the opposite), self motte-and-bailey (shifting between meanings of the same word without noticing), cruxless disagreement (never reaching a crux), too many threads, underdeterminacy and language inadequacy. A handle is the end product of deconfusion: the confusing concept explained in terms of clearer parts. Kinds include a formal definition, a definition in terms of simpler, less confused concepts, an extensive definition (a list of examples), a computational model and a story. Deconfusion always has an application, here testing the chatbot. Handles are judged by accuracy (does it fit the initial intuitions?) and usefulness (does it help the application?).
