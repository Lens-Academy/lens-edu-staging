tldr: "Not all AI risks are the same kind of thing, or the same size. Before diving into specific scenarios, this article offers a map: sorting risks by what causes them (human misuse, AI misalignment, or systemic effects) and by how bad they could get, from local harms to catastrophes to threats humanity could never recover from."
summary_for_tutor: "Introduces the framework used throughout the risks chapter, categorizing AI risks along two dimensions. By cause: misuse (humans intentionally using AI to cause harm), misalignment (AI pursuing goals different from human intentions), and systemic (emergent effects from AI interacting with societal systems). By severity: individual and local harms, catastrophic risks (affecting roughly 10 percent or more of the global population but allowing recovery), and existential risks (permanent curtailment of humanity's potential). Also defines ikigai risks (loss of purpose) and suffering risks (s-risks)."
