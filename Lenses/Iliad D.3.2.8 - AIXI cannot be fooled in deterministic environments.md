\## 8. AIXI Cannot Be Fooled in Deterministic Environments

:::callout {title="Theorem" tone="green"}

**Theorem 8.1 (AIXI cannot act poorly in good environments).** If $\mu \in {\mathcal{M}}$ is deterministic and $V_{\mu}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) > \varepsilon > 0$ for all $t$ along the history generated by $\mu$ and $\pi_{\xi}^{*}$, then $V_{\xi}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \geq w_{\mu}\, \varepsilon > 0$ for all $t$. (Hutter et al. 2024, Theorem 7.4.10)

:::

::::callout {title="Exercise" tone="amber"}
**Exercise 8.1 [10].** Show that $V_{\xi}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \geq w(\mu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \cdot V_{\mu}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t})$.

:::callout {title="Hint" tone="neutral" collapse="closed"}

Use linearity of $V^{\pi}$ (Exercise 4.2).

:::
::::

:::callout {title="Solution" tone="neutral" collapse="closed"}

By definition, $V_{\xi}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) = \sup_{\pi} V_{\xi}^{\pi}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \geq V_{\xi}^{\pi'}({\text{\ae}}_{<t})$ for any policy $\pi'$. By Exercise 4.2 (linearity of $V^{\pi}$ in the environment):

$$
V_{\xi}^{\pi'}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~=~ \sum_{\nu \in {\mathcal{M}}}w(\nu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t})\, V_{\nu}^{\pi'}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}).
$$

Choose $\pi' = \pi_{\mu}^{*}$, the deterministic optimal policy for $\mu$ whose existence is guaranteed by Exercise 2.7:

$$
V_{\xi}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~\geq~ \sum_{\nu \in {\mathcal{M}}}w(\nu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t})\, V_{\nu}^{\pi_\mu^*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}).
$$

Every term is non-negative ($w(\nu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \geq 0$ and $V_{\nu}^{\pi_\mu^*}\geq 0$). Dropping all terms except $\nu = \mu$:

$$
V_{\xi}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~\geq~ w(\mu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t})\, V_{\mu}^{\pi_\mu^*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~=~ w(\mu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t})\, V_{\mu}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}).
$$

:::

:::callout {title="Exercise" tone="amber"}
**Exercise 8.2 [15].** Show $w(\mu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \geq w_{\mu}$ when $\mu$ is deterministic, and combine with Exercise 8.1 to prove Theorem 8.1.
:::

:::callout {title="Solution" tone="neutral" collapse="closed"}

From Definition 0.1, the posterior weight is:

$$
w(\mu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~=~ w_{\mu} \cdot \frac{\mu({\text{\ae}}_{<t})}{\xi({\text{\ae}}_{<t})}~=~ w_{\mu} \cdot \frac{\prod_{k=1}^{t-1}\mu(e_{k} \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<k}a_{k})}{\prod_{k=1}^{t-1}\xi(e_{k} \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<k}a_{k})}.
$$

Since $\mu$ is deterministic, for the history that $\mu$ actually generates, $\mu(e_{k} \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<k}a_{k}) = 1$ for each $k$ (the environment produces each percept with certainty). So:

$$
\mu({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~=~ \prod_{k=1}^{t-1}1 ~=~ 1.
$$

Meanwhile, $\xi(e_{k} \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<k}a_{k}) \leq 1$ for each $k$ (it is a probability), so $\xi({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) = \prod_{k=1}^{t-1}\xi(e_{k} \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<k}a_{k}) \leq 1$.

Therefore $w(\mu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t}) = w_{\mu} \cdot 1 / \xi({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \geq w_{\mu}$.

Combining with Exercise 8.1:

$$
V_{\xi}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~\geq~ w(\mu \mid {\text{\ae}}_{<t}) \cdot V_{\mu}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~\geq~ w_{\mu} \cdot V_{\mu}^{*}({\text{\ae}}_{<t}) ~>~ w_{\mu} \cdot \varepsilon ~>~ 0.
$$

:::

:::callout {title="Note" tone="blue"}

**Remark.** With the Solomonoff prior, $w_{\mu} = 2^{-K(\mu)}$ may be astronomically small. The result guarantees non-zero value, not near-optimal value.

:::
