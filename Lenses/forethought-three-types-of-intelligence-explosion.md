summary_for_tutor: "A Forethought research report distinguishing three feedback loops in AI development, each of which could drive an intelligence explosion (IE): a software loop (AI improving training algorithms and post-training enhancements), a chip-technology loop (AI improving chip design), and a chip-production loop (AI and robots building more chips). These map to three IE types, likely occurring in this order because of increasing time lags (software takes months to train; chip technology months to build and integrate; chip production years to build fabs): a software IE (fastest and most sudden, most likely to concentrate power in a single US country or company), an AI-technology IE (software plus chip technology, distributed across the US and semiconductor-supply-chain allies such as Taiwan, South Korea, Japan, and the Netherlands), and a full-stack IE (all three loops, most broadly distributed, more likely to involve industrial powers like China and the Gulf states). For each loop it analyzes whether progress accelerates after automation (rough likelihoods ~50%, ~75%, ~90% for the three IE types) and how far each can push effective compute before hitting physical limits (~12 orders of magnitude for software, ~18 combined for AI-technology, ~23 to ~32 for full-stack). It sketches gradual, bumpy, and rapid scenarios, argues the slower AI-technology and full-stack IEs have been under-analyzed, and draws out the strategic implications for how concentrated power over AI would become."
