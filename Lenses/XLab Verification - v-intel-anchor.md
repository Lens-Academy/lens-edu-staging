#### Text
content::
The previous section catalogued what undeclared development might show. This one
asks the empirical question: when undeclared facilities have actually been
found, what found them?

The answer is the module's anchor, and it comes from Baker's Appendix E. Of the
four states where the IAEA investigated what turned out to be undeclared
facilities — Iraq, North Korea, Iran, Syria — **three investigations began
mainly or entirely because of national intelligence tips, and in none did the
IAEA find the facilities through its own safeguards processes.** Baker's
assessment of those tips: they "appear to be irreplaceable."

That is the identify/resolve split stated as evidence rather than as theory.
Intelligence generated the lead. The regime did the resolving — requesting
clarification, inspecting sites, collecting samples, comparing evidence against
declarations, and assessing compliance.

\## Where undeclared activity can be happening

Before the cases, the space of possibilities. Undeclared activity may occur:

- At a completely undeclared facility;
- Inside a declared facility performing an undeclared activity;
- Across several facilities whose combined activity crosses a treaty threshold;
- At a military or otherwise access-restricted site;
- Through a shell company, contractor, or proxy organization;
- In a third country;
- Within existing commercial cloud or industrial infrastructure;
- During a short operational window that is difficult to observe afterward.

Note how many of these are invisible to a signature catalogue that assumes one
large, dedicated, civilian building.

\## The caveats, taught with the cases

Two honest qualifications travel with this finding, and they matter as much as
the finding does.

The cases mostly predate in-force Additional Protocols — the safeguards regime
they indict is not quite the regime that exists now. And even the 2019–20 Iran
complementary-access successes began with an intelligence tip (the archive),
with treaty tools used for resolution. The split holds, but it is not evidence
that safeguards are useless; it is evidence about which step each tool performs.

Intelligence evidence is also characteristically **suggestive but not decisive
or internationally credible**. Satellite images are ambiguous. Shared documents
might be fake. That is precisely why regimes pair intelligence with escalation
mechanisms rather than treating a tip as a verdict.

Cover stories, though, are falsifiable: Iran's denial of activity at a site was
contradicted by commercially available satellite imagery.

\## Reading

- Mauricio Baker, [*Nuclear Arms Control Verification and Lessons for AI
  Treaties*](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2304.04123) (2023). Appendix E is
  the source of the finding above.
