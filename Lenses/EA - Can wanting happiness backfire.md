#### Text
content::
If wanting causes trouble, what about wanting happiness itself? Three studies, read through their abstracts. Each link opens the full paper.

In 2011 a team led by the psychologist Iris Mauss asked whether valuing happiness could backfire. Both of their studies had only female participants.

from:: Happiness is a key ingredient of well-being. It is thus reasonable to expect
to:: valuing happiness may lead people to be less happy just when happiness is within reach.

#### Text
content::
Thirteen years later, a team including Mauss separated two things that "valuing happiness" can mean: **aspiring** to happiness and being **concerned** about it. A **meta-emotion** is a feeling about a feeling, for example being disappointed that you are not happier.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/zerwas-unpacking-the-pursuit-of-happiness-being-concerned-about-happiness-but-not-aspiring-to-happiness-is-linked-with-negative-meta-emotions-and-worse-well-being]]
from:: Previous work suggests that sometimes the more people value happiness, the less happy they are.
to:: however, concern and judgment about one’s happiness can undermine it.

#### Text
content::
A third study was much larger. Kuan-Ju Huang followed 8,331 Dutch adults from 2019 to 2023. In the words of the abstract, "those who valued happiness generally exhibited higher well-being", while increases in valuing happiness "did not result in changes in life satisfaction 1 year later and had mixed emotional consequences (i.e., increasing both positive and negative affect)" ([Huang 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976241263784)). The full paper is paywalled.

In our reading, being concerned about happiness looks like what Jayasaro called craving: attention fixed on the result rather than on what you are doing. The studies did not test that link.
