title: "An Intuitive Explanation of Bayes's Theorem"
tldr: "To know what a piece of evidence means, you need three numbers: how common the thing was to begin with, how often it produces the evidence, and how often something else produces the same evidence. Evidence moves your old estimate. It does not replace it."
summary_for_tutor: "A long, gentle introduction to Bayes's Theorem. The mammography problem (1% prevalence, 80% true positives, 9.6% false positives) has the answer 7.8%, while most doctors guess 70 to 80% because they replace the prior with the chance that a sick woman tests positive. Other examples: pearl eggs (40% pearls, 30% of pearl eggs and 10% of empty eggs blue, so a blue egg is 67% likely to hold a pearl), natural frequencies, degrees of freedom, the likelihood ratio, odds and decibels for combining several tests, and the gizmo problem (30% blocked hoses, sparks 45% of the time if blocked and 5% if not). Bayes's Theorem is P(A|X) = P(X|A)P(A) / [P(X|A)P(A) + P(X|~A)P(~A)]. Later sections link it to Popper: falsification is strong evidence and confirmation is weak. #### Question: Open
id:: a7e972c8-a71a-4f30-92e4-ba2aa8eff908
content::
The essay poses the gizmo problem. When a gizmo stops working, its hose is blocked 30% of the time. Prodding a gizmo produces sparks 45% of the time if the hose is blocked, and 5% of the time if it is not. A broken gizmo sparks when you prod it. The essay writes out the arithmetic for this problem but never gives the final number.

1. What is the probability that the sparking gizmo has a blocked hose? Show your working. Counting gizmos (for example, out of 1,000) is fine.

2. A new mechanic says: "Sparks show up 45% of the time when the hose is blocked, so this gizmo is 45% likely to have a blocked hose." What mistake is he making?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Background for the grader: out of 1,000 broken gizmos, 300 have blocked hoses and 135 of those spark, while 700 have clear hoses and 35 of those spark. So 170 spark, and 135 of them are blocked: 135/170, about 79%. Equivalently (0.45 x 0.30) / (0.45 x 0.30 + 0.05 x 0.70). Score out of 100. 50: gives about 79% (accept 79% to 80%, 135/170 or 27/34) with working that combines all three figures (the 30% base rate, the 45% and the 5% spark rates). If the method is right but there is an arithmetic slip, give 35 of these 50. A correct final number with no working gets 35. 50: says the mechanic mixes up the chance that a blocked gizmo sparks with the chance that a sparking gizmo is blocked, or equivalently that he ignored how common blocked hoses are (30%) and how often clear hoses also spark (5%). Either description earns the full 50, since they are the same mistake. Cap at 30 if the answer concludes that the probability is 45%. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "Out of 1,000 gizmos, 300 are blocked and 135 of them spark. 700 are clear and 35 of those spark. 135 of the 170 sparking gizmos are blocked, so about 79%. The mechanic swapped 'blocked gizmos that spark' for 'sparking gizmos that are blocked'. He used the 45% alone and ignored that only 30% are blocked and that clear gizmos spark too."
