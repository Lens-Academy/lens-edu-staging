#### Text
content::
This post holds three flash classes. The middle one, policy-level decisions, is about choices you make again and again. It asks what policy you would follow every time the choice comes up, and the question below asks you to write one.

#### Question: Open
id:: 5c9bbf7d-4e6e-4c21-9cfe-e563871d7826
content::
Pick one decision you keep making again, for example whether to go to a weekly reading group when you are tired, or whether to answer messages in the evening.

1. Which decision?

2. Write a policy for it that would work on a good day and a bad day, with the exceptions written in.
feedback-instructions:: The learner read the CFAR Handbook's flash class on policy-level decisionmaking (for recurring decisions, choose a policy that works whatever the mood or day, including the exceptions that make sense, so the policy itself is not set aside) and wrote a policy for a decision they keep re-making. Ungraded. Reply in 2 to 3 sentences, one turn. Check one thing: does the policy include its exceptions, so that a bad day is covered by the policy instead of breaking it? If not, ask which situation would most likely make them set it aside, and whether that belongs in the policy. If it does, say so in one sentence. No praise.
