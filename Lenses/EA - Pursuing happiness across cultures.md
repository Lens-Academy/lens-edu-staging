#### Text
content::
The 2011 study on valuing happiness was done in the United States. A later study by Brett Ford, Iris Mauss and colleagues asked whether the result holds elsewhere. They proposed that "in collectivistic (vs. individualistic) cultures, pursuing happiness may be more successful because happiness is viewed, and thus pursued, in relatively socially engaged ways". In the United States, Germany, Russia and East Asia, "motivation to pursue happiness predicted lower well-being in the United States, did not predict well-being in Germany, and predicted higher well-being in Russia and in East Asia". The differences "were explained by cultural differences in the socially engaged pursuit of happiness" ([Ford et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4658246/), abstract, dashes replaced by commas).
