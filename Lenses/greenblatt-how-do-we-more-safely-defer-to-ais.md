summary_for_tutor: "Ryan Greenblatt's analysis of how to safely hand off (defer) high-stakes, hard-to-check work such as alignment research, strategy, and managing exogenous risk to AI systems. Assuming scheming is handled separately, it argues for deferring to AIs only slightly above the minimum capability needed to automate safety work, targeting roughly top-human-expert-level alignment and epistemics. Introduces the 'Basin of Good Deference' (AIs bootstrapping their own alignment and wisdom across generations), the 'deference-goodness tax,' and a prosaic strategy centered on behavioral testing for capabilities and alignment while ruling out conditions (scheming, data poisoning, input hijacks) that would make such tests misleading."
