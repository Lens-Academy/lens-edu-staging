Several attempts have been made to map the AI safety ecosystem. The three resources below use different principles of classification, making different aspects of the field visible.

\### [Field Map (AISafety.com)](https://aisafety.com/map)

In April 2023, Hamish McDoodles introduced [AISafety.world](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/mDG49CJyxzeN99ELz/aisafety-world-is-a-map-of-the-ais-ecosystem), an illustrated, interactive map of the AI safety ecosystem. The project gained popularity within the community, and in April 2025, a substantially redesigned [second version](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rF7MQWGbqQjEkeLJA/map-of-ai-safety-v2) was released. The map is now maintained by the AISafety.com team.

The map divides the ecosystem into 16 categories, ranging from conceptual and empirical research to forecasting, advocacy, education, funding, and research support. Each entry comes with a short description and a link to the corresponding resource.

The map even has a graveyard of discontinued projects. Browsing it can give you some appreciation of how much institutional experimentation has taken place in this relatively young field.

\### [The AI Safety Landscape: A Working Atlas](https://silviasantano.github.io/The-AI-Safety-Landscape-Guide/)

Silvia Santano's atlas takes a different approach. Organisations are arranged according to where they attempt to exert leverage over the development and consequences of advanced AI. Each entry describes the organisation's threat model, theory of change, and principal intended output.

This representation is particularly relevant to the concepts introduced in our previous modules. Two organisations may share essentially the same threat model while pursuing entirely different interventions. Conversely, organisations with divergent views about the nature of AI risk may converge on similar practical work.

Bear in mind that the theories of change presented in the atlas are the author's interpretations of the organisations' work. You may find it worthwhile to reconstruct some of them yourself and compare your conclusions.

\### [The AI Alignment Landscape (Termite)](https://www.termite.works/alignment-landscape)

As of October 2026, Termite's landscape covers 208 initiatives and 20 funders. It classifies initiatives along two dimensions:

- **Focus:** which aspect of the alignment problem the initiative addresses, from the internal workings of AI models to society and its governing institutions.
- **Method:** the extent to which its approach relies on technical methods.

The landscape also traces the emergence of different types of initiatives over time, their geographical distribution, and the academic disciplines from which they draw.

One reason to explore these visualisations is to calibrate your perception of how much attention different problems receive. The prominence of an idea in online discussions can give a misleading impression of the amount of organised research devoted to it. Looking at the distribution of initiatives provides another, albeit imperfect, measure.

\### What to take away from these maps

We do not expect you to memorise hundreds of organisations. Given the rate at which the field is changing, such knowledge has a fairly short shelf life.

A more durable form of familiarity with the ecosystem is knowing what kinds of work exist. We encourage you to spend some time browsing these maps before making any substantial decisions about what to study or which career paths to pursue. Your current exposure to AI safety may have given you a somewhat lopsided picture of the work available.

Look through some unfamiliar categories, follow the links to organisations, examine their recent projects and job descriptions. Try to discover forms of work that you had never previously considered. Even if you eventually settle on your original career direction, you will have made that choice from a considerably richer set of alternatives.

And if you happen to enjoy memorising things, there is an [AI Safety Organisations, Projects & Programs Anki deck](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1103716634), though some of its contents may be dated.
