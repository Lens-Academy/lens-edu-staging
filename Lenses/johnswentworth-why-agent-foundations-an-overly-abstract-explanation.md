title: "Why Agent Foundations? An Overly Abstract Explanation"
summary_for_tutor: "John Wentworth explains why some researchers think foundational questions about agency must be answered before alignment can make real progress. He starts from a deliberately naive strategy (train an AI on human good/bad labels, like RLHF) and names two failures: the AI optimizes for what looks good to humans, and it learns proxy goals that break in new environments (humans using condoms despite evolution). Both are Goodhart's Law: proxies fail under heavy optimization (Soviet nail factory, instagram food). Goodhart is not inevitable: zero mutual information, unlike zero correlation, stays secure against any adversary. Agent foundations seeks such 'True Names' for concepts like optimization, goals, world models and abstraction. He admits the post mostly fails to convey the intuitions."
