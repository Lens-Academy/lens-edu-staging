\## Limiting Beliefs on Impact

This journaling tab is an optional but highly recommended space for weekly personal reflection alongside the curriculum.  Before diving into thesis decisions (or any major decision for that matter), we highly encourage you to ground your choices in your own vision, values, and goals - not just external expectations. Reflection helps you tune into your inner wisdom and clarify what is truly meaningful to you. This ensures that your thesis - and the impact you aim for - is aligned with what really matters to you.

- The world is changing rapidly, so use this as an opportunity to attune to what matters to you.
- Your observations and lived experience matter - notice what you care about in improving the world, and also learn to back it up with evidence and strategic thinking to identify clear pathways to impact that you can take, and that you would enjoy taking!

\#### Limiting Beliefs: Notice and Reframe with Kindness

All of us carry limiting beliefs - thoughts and assumptions that quietly tell us what we can and can’t do. These beliefs are often rooted in past experiences, societal messages, or fear of failure. Psychology research shows that these beliefs shape our decisions, motivation, and even what we consider possible. But here’s the empowering truth: beliefs are not facts. They are mental habits, and they can be noticed, questioned, and transformed.

- ⭐ **The key is to approach them with kindness and curiosity, not judgment**. Your mind is simply doing its best to protect you, even if that protection sometimes feels limiting.

When it comes to your path to high-impact work, limiting beliefs can quietly block you from pursuing meaningful projects, taking bold steps, or trusting your unique perspective. Reflection gives you the chance to bring these beliefs into awareness, question them, and consciously choose a different, empowering mindset.

\#### How to Work with Limiting Beliefs

- **Notice, don’t judge:** When a limiting belief arises, pause and observe it. Treat it like a passing cloud that you can observe with kindness, rather than a permanent truth. Example: *“Here’s the thought that I’m not qualified. I notice it, and I acknowledge it without judgment.”*
- **Ask yourself: what need might this belief be protecting or signaling?** *Example: “I notice the thought ‘I’m not qualified.’ Maybe this is connected to my need for competence or confidence.”*
- **Choose when to reframe:** You don’t have to challenge every belief immediately. Sometimes simply noticing it is enough. When you feel ready, consciously **reframe it into a belief that empowers you**.
- **Reframing with agency:** Ask yourself: *Is this belief serving me? How might I reframe it to align with my values, potential, and vision? What small action could I take now that reflects this new perspective?*
   - ***What would you say to a close friend who expressed this belief?*** *See if you can reframe this belief with the same warmth, understanding, and encouragement you would offer them. This helps you approach your own mind with curiosity and kindness rather than judgment.*

By noticing these beliefs and **reframing them**, you can choose actions that are aligned with your **values and potential** rather than fear or external pressure. Remember that changing thought patterns is a gradual practice. Be patient and kind to yourself as you notice, question, and reframe limiting beliefs.

\#### Examples of Limiting Beliefs and Reframes

| Limiting Belief | Underlying Needs | Possible Reframe |
|---|---|---|
| “Experts know best; I should just follow what they say.” | Need for guidance / safety | I can seek guidance from experts to feel supported while also trusting my own judgment and perspective; this allows me to add unique value while staying safe. |
| “I don’t know enough to identify what is impactful.” | Need for competence | I can build my competence step by step by researching, seeking guidance, and learning as I go; I don’t need to know everything to contribute meaningfully. |
| “I’m not qualified to work on this problem.” | Need for recognition / confidence | My skills and perspective matter. I can grow expertise while making meaningful contributions, which meets my need for confidence and acknowledgment of my capabilities. |
| “This topic is too big for a student to make a difference.” | Need for agency / efficacy | I can focus on a tractable part of the problem where my work has real influence, which satisfies my need to act effectively and see tangible results. |
| “I need permission to act or start.” | Need for safety / external validation | I can take initiative now, trusting myself. Taking action meets my need for safety while also fostering independence and impact. |
| “I should just do what others say is impactful; they know better than me.” | Need for belonging or approval | I can integrate guidance from others while critically evaluating what aligns with my values. This allows me to feel connected and supported while also honoring my own judgment. |
