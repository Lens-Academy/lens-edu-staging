tldr: "Can we align AI without solving the whole alignment problem first? Jan Leike argues yes: instead of a perfect once-and-for-all solution, build an AI just aligned enough to accelerate alignment research itself. He lays out five reasons for optimism, from LLMs arriving pre-loaded with human values to the principle that judging work is easier than doing it, then takes on the strongest objections to the plan."
summary_for_tutor: "Presents Jan Leike's case for OpenAI's alignment strategy (perfecting RLHF, AI-assisted human evaluation, and automated alignment research) via five sources of optimism: (1) positive empirical updates, since LLMs come pre-loaded with human values and RLHF/InstructGPT worked better than expected; (2) a more modest goal of building an AI aligned enough to do alignment research rather than solving alignment outright; (3) the general principle that evaluation is easier than generation; (4) setting ourselves up for iterative, measurable progress; and (5) conviction that language models will be capable enough to be useful. The second half rebuts common objections, including that recursive reward modeling fails, eliciting latent knowledge, that automating alignment is too close to automating ML research, that the needed systems are too dangerous to run, inner misalignment, and over-reliance on generalization."
