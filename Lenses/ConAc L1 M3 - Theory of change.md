title: "Theory of change"
tldr: >-
  A theory of change spells out how an intervention's activities are supposed to lead to the results you want, and which assumptions, mechanisms and risks that chain depends on. For AI interventions it is harder to build, because AI systems can pursue objectives in unintended ways.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Two excerpts from UK government guidance. First, the Government Analysis Function's Theory of Change toolkit introduces theory of change (TOC) as a methodology for planning, monitoring and evaluating an initiative. It makes explicit the assumptions about the effects of actions and the dependencies on other stakeholders, and it is most useful at the design stage, though it can be applied at any stage. Second, section 2.2 of the Magenta Book annex "Guidance on the Impact Evaluation of AI Interventions" gives the framework: contextual factors, inputs, outputs, outcomes, impacts, mechanisms, risks and unintended consequences, assumptions behind each stage, and supporting activities. It names three extra challenges for AI interventions: the black-box nature of AI systems and the alignment problem, hard-to-predict public attitudes and user behaviour, and limited evidence for key assumptions. It recommends a fully specified TOC that covers unintended impacts, close work with stakeholders including pre-mortem sessions, and using evidence from assurance work such as red teaming.
