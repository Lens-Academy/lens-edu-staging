#### Question
content::
\## Before you read: put a number on it

In 2021, a researcher paid professional forecasters to predict how well AI systems would perform on a set of benchmarks one year later. One of those benchmarks was **MATH**: a set of competition mathematics problems, the kind that appear in high-school olympiad qualifiers. Not arithmetic. Problems that a strong human competitor finds genuinely difficult.

At the time the question was asked, the best AI system in the world scored in the **single digits** on MATH.

The forecasters were asked: what will the best system score in June 2022?

Before you read anything, answer for yourself:

1. What percentage would you have predicted for June 2022? Give a single number.
2. Give an interval you would have been 90 percent confident the answer fell inside. Both ends.
3. In one or two sentences: what is your interval actually based on? Name the thing you are reasoning from, whether that is a trend you know about, an intuition about how hard the problems are, or something else.

Write all three down before moving on. The reading tells you what happened.

max-time:: 6:00

assessment-instructions::
The student is about to read Jacob Steinhardt's "AI Forecasting: One Year In," which reports that professional forecasters predicted 12.7 percent on the MATH benchmark for June 2022 and the actual result was 50.3 percent, landing far outside their credible intervals.

They have just committed to their own point estimate, a 90 percent interval, and a stated basis for it.

Your job here is to get the commitment on record and send them to the reading. Nothing else.

Acknowledge their answer in 1 to 3 sentences. If they gave a number and an interval, note specifically what their reasoning rested on, using their own words. If they hedged without committing to numbers, ask them once, briefly, to commit to a point estimate and both ends of an interval, since the exercise does not work without them.

Do NOT:
- Reveal or hint at the actual June 2022 result, or at how the forecasters did.
- Say whether their number seems high, low, reasonable, or surprising. Any such signal destroys the exercise.
- Explain what the MATH benchmark contains beyond what the question already said.
- Discuss calibration, credible intervals, or forecasting methodology. That is the reading's job.

Close with: "That is on the record now. Head to the reading, and when you come back you will be scored against the professionals who answered the same question."
