You have learned the main methods of human-based AI verification; traced the
reporting channel from whistleblower to verifier; and distinguished where,
when, and what types of inspections are effective. In the final section of
this module, you will put together everything you've learned to form
judgments about auditing and reporting institutions as a whole. The question
is: can the inspecting institution reach a defensible and reliable judgment
about the evidence it handles?

Explore the below frontier AI auditing design principles and a historical
nuclear parallel for features of effective institutions.

\### Frontier AI auditing: design principles
Brundage et al. (2026)

  Read the eight design principles, from Scope of Risks through Clarity.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/brundage-frontier-ai-auditing-toward-rigorous-third-party-assessment-of-safety-and-security-practices-at-leading-ai-companies-pdf]]
from:: - **Scope of risks: Comprehensive coverage of four key risk categories.** Frontier AI auditing should focus on four risk categories: risks from (1) intentional misuse of frontier AI systems (e.g., for cyberattacks); (2) unintended frontier AI system behavior (e.g., errors harming the user, their property, or third parties due to pursuing the wrong goal or having an unreliable performance profile); (3) information security (e.g., theft of an AI model or user data); and (4) emergent social phenomena (e.g., addiction to AI or facilitation of self-harm). For each category of risks, auditors should (a) verify company claims and (b) evaluate the company's systems and practices against its stated safety and security policies, applicable regulations, and industry best practices.
to:: - **Clarity: Clear communication of audit results.** Stakeholders must be able to understand the audit results. These should be communicated in audit reports with a standardized structure, covering the audit's scope, level of assurance, conclusions, reasoning, and recommendations. Results should be communicated appropriately to different stakeholders: to protect sensitive information, auditors and companies can publish summarized or redacted versions for external stakeholders while sharing full, unredacted audit reports with boards, company executives, and, in some cases, regulatory bodies.

\## Exercise: From Nuclear to AI Inspections

In the nuclear regime, the inspection institution has four major steps: inspectors find, the Director-General reports, the Board of Governors judges, and the Security Council enforces. The AI verification regime has no such established process.

In this exercise, you will build a similar pipeline for frontier AI inspections, drawing direct parallels with each step in the nuclear inspection process. Aim for around a few sentences for each section.

#### Question: Open

content:: **The finder.** In the nuclear regime: IAEA inspectors, and the safeguards analysis behind them, establish the facts and report them up.

Who finds for AI, and what access would they need for their facts to hold up later?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 40: who finds for AI: at least one plausible finder that could establish the facts, such as the inspectors of a treaty body or the evidence streams they would draw on (chip attestation and logs, cloud provider records, national technical means such as satellites and intelligence, insiders and whistleblowers). 60: the access that finder needs for its facts to hold up later, 20: what it would need to reach, such as sites, chips and their logs, provider records or people (naming the evidence streams a finder draws on counts), and 40: why that makes the facts hold up: the access is agreed and legitimate before any case arises (for example written into the treaty), so the accused cannot later dismiss a finding by disputing where the evidence came from or how it was obtained. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "AI has candidate finders — module 2’s evidence streams: chip attestation and logs, cloud records, national technical means, and the human layer this section closed on. What none of them supplies alone is facts that survive challenge: the finder’s access has to be agreed in advance, or every finding arrives with a provenance fight attached."

#### Question: Open
id:: c9360c88-5ae3-41c1-bb81-6540a8812a15
content:: **The standard.** In the nuclear regime: The Board has never defined noncompliance; five cases were decided case by case, and Carlson argues the credibility of the system rides on consistency.

Write the one-sentence standard your AI regime announces before its first case: what separates a breach worth correcting from noncompliance worth judging?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 20: one standard, stated as a rule the regime could announce before its first case. 40: it separates a breach to be corrected from noncompliance to be judged on a stated criterion, for example the scale of the activity relative to the threshold, concealment or intent, refusal of agreed access, failure to correct within a deadline, or repetition. 40: the criterion is concrete enough that two rival states reading it in advance would predict the same verdict on the same facts; words such as "serious" or "significant" with no measure behind them earn little here. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "Carlson’s closing argument transfers whole: a regime that decides case by case, with no announced standard, spends credibility on every decision. Whatever sentence you wrote, the test it must pass is the one his five cases failed — would two rivals, reading it in advance, predict the same verdict on the same facts? One sentence that passes: an undeclared or unlicensed run above the agreed threshold, or a refusal of agreed access, is noncompliance to be judged; a reporting error the party itself discloses and corrects within 30 days is a breach to be corrected."
feedback-instructions:: Two or three sentences. Apply the test XLab sets: would two rivals, reading the standard in advance, predict the same verdict on the same facts? Say whether the learner's sentence passes.

content:: Carlson argues the system’s credibility rides on consistency. In no more than 50 words: what one guideline would you bind your AI board to before its first case?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 60: the guideline would make the board's decisions consistent: it binds the board to criteria set out in advance and applied the same way in every case, concrete enough that the same facts produce the same verdict. 40: it holds whoever is accused, so a powerful state, an ally or a rival gets the same verdict on the same facts. \## The Standard of Proof

Optional, about 15 minutes. Every question in this exercise is optional.

**The allegation, over every docket.** Meridian Compute ran a training run above the agreement’s compute ceiling at its Delta campus in the third quarter.

One allegation. Four dockets: the evidence in front of an institution, and the institution holding it.

For each docket, choose the institution’s next move. Then defend it:

- what the docket establishes, and what kind of evidence does that work;
- what it does not establish;
- whether this institution can defend the move you chose.

Use about 60 to 90 words per docket. Once all four dockets carry a move and a defence, submitting reveals the pattern behind them and the marking key you use to score yourself.

#### Question: Open
id:: 26979154-9be9-4014-ad46-135a353e5f67
optional:: true
content:: In no more than 50 words, state the decision standard you actually applied: what separated the dockets you would investigate from any docket you would judge or refer?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 50: evidence: the step followed how strong the evidence was, for example a lone uncorroborated report (docket A) warrants investigation, while converging independent evidence plus a refused access visit (docket B) supports a judgment or referral. 50: institution: the step also depended on whether the institution holding the case was sound, with access and independence; where it was not (docket C, no access and one audit the accused commissioned itself; docket D, a panel funded and staffed by the accused's side), even strong evidence could not be judged, and the step is to repair the institution rather than reweigh the evidence. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "I judged or referred only when independent evidence converged and the institution holding it was sound (B). A lone report called for investigation (A). Where the institution lacked access or independence (C, D), I did not judge however strong the evidence: the institution had to be repaired first."
feedback-instructions:: One or two sentences. Say whether the standard would have handled docket D, where the evidence was strong and the institution was not.

#### Question: Open
id:: 2d4dbddf-2559-4f6a-b6c2-baa0fc1ac5ad

content:: Take the docket whose institution you trusted least. Name one structural change that would make its judgment defensible, and say which property it repairs: independence, competence, accountability, authority, or access.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 20: names the docket whose institution is weakest: C (the directorate has no access to the site and everything arrives through an audit the accused company commissioned itself, with its records withheld) or D (the judging panel is funded through an industry association the accused chairs, and two of its five members are on leave from the accused's suppliers). 45: one concrete structural change that would make that institution's judgment defensible, for example for D replacing the conflicted members and funding the panel independently of the industry, for C treaty access rights to the site and the audit's working papers, or an audit the directorate commissions itself. 35: correctly names the property the change repairs from the list (independence, competence, accountability, authority, access), for example independence for the panel changes in D, access or authority for C. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "Docket D. Fund the panel from the treaty's own budget rather than through the industry association Meridian chairs, and require members with ties to Meridian or its suppliers to recuse themselves. This repairs independence; it leaves competence untouched. For docket C, treaty access rights to Delta and to the audit's working papers would repair access."
feedback-instructions:: One or two sentences. Confirm or correct the property label and name one property the change leaves untouched.
