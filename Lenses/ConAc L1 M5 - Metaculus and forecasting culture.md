Probabilistic forecasting is an established practice within the community. [Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/) is a forecasting platform where participants assign probabilities to future events, update their predictions as new evidence becomes available, and receive scores based on their accuracy. The platform aggregates individual predictions and allows users to examine how collective expectations change over time.

Here are several collections of questions relevant to AI safety:

- [AI 2027](https://www.metaculus.com/tournament/ai-2027/): forecasts concerning specific developments anticipated in the AI 2027 scenario.
- [AI Pathways Tournament](https://www.metaculus.com/tournament/foresight-ai-pathways/): questions about AI capabilities, international coordination, governance and institutional responses.
- [AI Demonstrations](https://www.metaculus.com/tournament/ai-demonstrations/): forecasts about observable milestones in AI capabilities.

The comments beneath forecasting questions are also worth reading. Forecasters discuss their reasoning, identify relevant evidence and explain why their estimates differ from the community prediction.

\### Why make predictions yourself?

Forecasting occupies a distinctive place in the epistemic culture of AI safety. Many of the field's most consequential disagreements concern events that have yet to happen. In this context, a record of accurate predictions is one of the few ways to demonstrate that your understanding of AI development has predictive value.

Making forecasts on Metaculus creates a measurable record of your judgement. Over time, you can identify systematic biases in your reasoning, improve your calibration and establish a track record that others can examine. A strong performance on AI-related questions can therefore serve as evidence of your ability to reason about AI progress and its implications. This is one reason why forecasting track records receive considerable attention within the community.

Forecasting can also be directly relevant to your work. Consider which assumptions your Theory of Change makes about future technological or institutional developments. Formulating these assumptions as explicit predictions and revisiting them as new evidence becomes available can help you recognise when your strategy needs updating.

If you have never practised forecasting, [How to forecast on Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/how-to-forecast/) provides a practical introduction.
