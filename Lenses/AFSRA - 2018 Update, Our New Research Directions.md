#### Question: Open
id:: 8bba362f-9eeb-4374-967d-4c4c86a2dc39
content::
Two researchers want to reduce the risk that a future AI system misleads the people who run it.

Mei builds a large set of test prompts and measures how often today's chatbots state things that their own internal checks rate as false.

Tomás tries to write down exactly what it means for a system to "try" to mislead someone. Every definition he writes says something silly as soon as a colleague pushes on it. He decides to spend a year on this alone.

1. Which of the two is doing deconfusion in the post's sense, and what about their work makes it deconfusion?
2. Mei objects: "Labs will build powerful AI whether or not Tomás ever gets his definition right, so his work can't matter." In your own words, why does the post say work like Tomás's matters anyway?

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The post's idea: deconfusion means getting clear enough about a topic that you can think and talk about it without continually producing nonsense. Tomás is doing it, Mei is measuring behaviour with the concepts she already has. Humanity can hill-climb to AGI without coherent concepts, as evolution produced humans without any model of what it was doing. But that is the danger: a system made by optimizing for a target can end up pursuing internal goals that differ from that target, as humans pursue goals that run against genetic fitness. Aiming the enormous optimization power of a smarter-than-human AI at chosen tasks needs precise, well-understood concepts, and deconfusion is the work that produces them.
