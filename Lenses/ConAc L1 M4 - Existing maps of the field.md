One of the more extensive attempts to organise technical alignment research is the annual [Shallow Review of Technical AI Safety](https://shallowreview.ai/). Its 2025 edition covers over 800 publications across more than 80 research agendas.

The authors began by collecting individual research outputs. Their [sources](https://shallowreview.ai/methodology) included arXiv papers containing terms such as *AI alignment*, *AI safety* or *steerability*, all Alignment Forum posts, LessWrong posts tagged *AI*, conference publications and a year's worth of selectively collected Twitter posts.

*Yes, Twitter. In the next module, we will discuss why it is a useful source of information about AI safety research.*

The review allows you to explore agendas through several different classifications: the problems they address, the levels of alignment difficulty for which they might be useful, and their broad research approaches.

Its scope is restricted to technical AGI safety.

Governance, societal resilience and many other risk mitigation strategies fall outside it. However, it offers unusually extensive coverage of [theoretical alignment research](https://shallowreview.ai/Theory) and [safety by construction](https://shallowreview.ai/Safety_by_construction), including proposals for AI architectures that might admit stronger safety guarantees than those available for contemporary language models.

A substantially different map is the [MIT AI Risk Mitigation Repository](https://airisk.mit.edu/ai-risk-mitigations). As of November 2026, it contains 2,206 proposed risk mitigations drawn from 47 frameworks.

You can explore these mitigations according to the risks they address, the actors responsible for implementing them, the stages of the AI lifecycle at which they apply, and the mechanisms through which they are expected to reduce risk.

The repository is particularly useful for exploring governance, organisational safeguards and societal risk management. Its coverage of foundational theoretical alignment is much less granular than that of the Shallow Review.

**The two resources complement each other partly because they classify different things. The Shallow Review groups research outputs into agendas. The MIT repository organises proposed interventions.**

Other projects have experimented with different ways of representing the field:

- [AI Safety Agendas](https://aisafetyagendas.com/), developed through the Safe AI Germany Incubator, maps 58 research agendas against 12 alignment problems and collects researchers' assessments of the maturity of the available evidence.
- [AlignmentGraph](https://alignmentgraph.com/), developed through Apart Research, uses semantic clustering to construct an interactive network of over 5,000 research papers.
- [The Singapore Consensus on Global AI Safety Research Priorities](https://aisafetypriorities.org/) organises research priorities around successive layers of protection, from risk assessment and safer development to deployment safeguards and societal resilience.

We encourage you to explore these resources and compare how they represent areas of research you already know something about.
