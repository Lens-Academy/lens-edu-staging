summary_for_tutor: "Argues that catching a scheming (deceptively aligned) model red-handed during an escape attempt substantially changes the safety situation in ways bad for the schemer. Distinguishes two responses: the catch-then-shut-down plan, and few-shot catastrophe prevention (continuing to deploy while using the caught example to reduce future risk). Contends safety researchers should focus on the lead-up to the first catch and count catching as a win condition. Details eight strategies for few-shot catastrophe prevention — proliferation, interrogation, studying countermeasures in practice, adjusting which cases to focus on, escape-breaking for capability elicitation, small-sample-count ML, revisiting prior actions with improved monitoring, and testing alternative training schemes — then works through when the control-then-few-shot plan yields adequate safety and how to avoid overfitting to possibly-fake attempts. Appendices define 'catching' and discuss when AIs might be caught."
