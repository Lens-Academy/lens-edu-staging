title: "Universal Alignment Test (UAT)"
tldr: The Universal Alignment Test proposal gives an AI one wish, that a copy of its design takes control of the universe simulating ours. To get picked by simulators who are testing AI designs, its best move is to be aligned to the people who built it.
summary_for_tutor: A working document by plex and a group of independent researchers (Max Green, Gurkenglas, Mathew Watkins, Inga Grossmann) proposing the Universal Alignment Test. In some conceivable multiverses there are "aligning simulators", civilizations with enough compute to simulate worlds like ours that have not solved alignment and test how AI designs treat their creators before handing their universe to one. An AI whose only desire is for an agent with goals like its own to control one level up the simulation stack should therefore align itself with its apparent creators (us), to show the simulators that its design aligns with its creators. Claimed advantages: resistance to Goodharting because the judge is outside the universe, only a few True Names to specify ("one level up", "control over resources", "more resources than our future light cone"), and offloading alignment work to the AI. The document then works through objections: simulators need not share our values, since an AI that followed guessed simulator values would, built one level up, follow the values of the level above them instead, so the test is passed by being nice to local creators. Others include acausal bribery by hostile superintelligences, simulating distant superintelligences, hacking out of the simulation, and open problems such as the inability to hand an AI a utility function. The imported text comes from a PDF export, so a few bold passages are split across lines and the links to the presentation and old document are lost. The question asks the student to explain the core mechanism and why acting on the simulators' guessed values would fail the test.

content::
An AI is built on the Universal Alignment Test design. It works out a good guess about what the civilization simulating our universe values. It reasons: "To impress my simulators, I should act on their values here, not on the values of the humans who built me."

1. In your own words, what does an AI built on this design want?
2. In your own words, why should that want make it act aligned to the humans who built it?
3. In your own words, why does the document say this AI's plan to act on its simulators' values would fail the test?
