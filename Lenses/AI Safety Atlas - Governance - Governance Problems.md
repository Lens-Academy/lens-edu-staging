Why traditional technology governance does not transfer to AI. The usual assumptions, that you can predict a technology's uses, control its development path, and regulate specific applications, hold for pharmaceuticals and nuclear material but break for AI. As an information technology it generates new content rather than storing and retrieving it, at a speed and scale that outstrips existing controls. As an intelligence technology it may resist governance: capable systems could evade controls or pursue unintended objectives, which the section calls a challenge without precedent in technology regulation. From these it derives three problems: unexpected capabilities that appear as models scale and that current evaluations cannot rule out; deployment safety, where jailbreaks and agents repurpose systems after release; and proliferation, since models are patterns of numbers that copy instantly, so physical containment does not apply.
