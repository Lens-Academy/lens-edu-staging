In AI safety, where many organisations depend on philanthropic funding and there is substantial disagreement about research priorities, understanding how resources are allocated is particularly useful for understanding the field itself.

In this submodule, we will look at the scale and structure of AI safety funding, the major actors responsible for allocating it, and the mechanisms through which resources reach researchers and organisations.

\### How are established organisations funded?

Individual grants can range from several thousand dollars for an independent research project to tens of millions for the expansion of an established organisation.

Large research organisations often receive grants from several funders over multiple years. These may cover salaries, computational resources, administrative expenses, research infrastructure and the development of new research programmes.

You can explore the funding histories of individual AI safety organisations in [Manifund's organisation directory](https://manifund.org/orgs).

\### Major actors in the current funding landscape

[**Coefficient Giving**](https://coefficientgiving.org/funds/navigating-transformative-ai/) (formerly Open Philanthropy) funds a broad range of activities, spanning technical AI safety, security, governance and field-building. [**Longview Philanthropy**](https://www.longview.org/artificial-intelligence/) focuses on risks from frontier AI, with an emphasis on governance, verification mechanisms and the concentration of power. [**Survival and Flourishing Fund**](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/) (SFF) operates within a broader mission of ensuring humanity's long-term survival and flourishing, supporting a diverse range of approaches to AI risk reduction.

Several foundations have more distinct thematic priorities. [**Macroscopic**](https://macroscopic.org/) supports AI safety, cooperative AI and other global challenges. [**Astralis Foundation**](https://astralisfoundation.org/) focuses on reducing catastrophic risks from advanced AI.

Other funders approach AI safety through scientific research and public policy. [**Schmidt Sciences**](https://www.schmidtsciences.org/) supports research into the scientific foundations of trustworthy AI alongside other AI research programmes. The [**Future of Life Institute**](https://futureoflife.org/) combines its broader focus on existential risk reduction with funding for AI governance and research.

[**Manifund**](https://manifund.org/) facilitates decentralised grantmaking, enabling individual grantmakers to support projects across multiple AI safety agendas. The [**Transformative AI Fund**](https://funds.effectivealtruism.org/) (formerly the Long-Term Future Fund) supports research, governance and other initiatives aimed at reducing long-term risks from AI.

\#### Survival and Flourishing Fund: distributed grant evaluation

The Survival and Flourishing Fund uses a distinctive grant evaluation procedure called the **S-Process**, where S stands for Simulation.

For each funding round, a group of four to twelve recommenders evaluates applications. They use a specialised grantmaking simulation tool and participate in a series of meetings to develop funding recommendations. The funders retain final authority over the allocation of money.

Read more: [S-Process for Making Grant Recommendations](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/s-process).

\#### Cooperative AI Foundation: developing a specific research direction

A funder can also attempt to develop an entire research area by supporting a collection of related projects.

The Cooperative AI Foundation was established with a $15 million philanthropic commitment from Macroscopic Ventures. Its activities include academic grants, fellowships, workshops and other forms of support for cooperative AI research.

[Scaling AI Safety for a Multi-Agent World](https://www.cooperativeai.com/grants/multi-agent-safety).

The programme focuses on risks emerging from interactions between advanced AI agents. These include failures of coordination, collusion, conflict and other collective dynamics that studying individual systems may fail to capture.
