summary_for_tutor: 'Nate Soares and Benja Fallenstein''s MIRI technical agenda. Three challenges: an agent that reliably pursues the goals it is given, formally specifying beneficial goals, and an agent that cooperates with its programmers as they fix its inevitable mistakes. Section 1.1 explains the choice of problems: tractable (concrete, progress possible now), uncrowded (not likely to be solved by normal AI research, so program verification is left out), focused (useful for aligned systems in particular, not intelligence in general) and unable to be safely delegated to machines. Error-tolerant design is the example of a problem that cannot be delegated, because no problem can be safely delegated to an agent with incentives to deceive its programmers, while a capable automated engineer would improve computer vision even if its own vision started weak, and most agents want better vision anyway. Section 2, highly reliable agent designs: testing is not enough (the Bird and Layzell oscillator that became a radio), we need a formal understanding of the problem, as Shannon gave for chess. Topics: realistic world-models, decision theory, logical uncertainty, Vingean reflection. Section 3, error-tolerant designs and corrigibility (penalty terms fail, utility indifference, domesticity). Section 4, value specification. Section 5 on why to start now. The question asks the student to apply the delegation criterion, and one other criterion, to two candidate projects.'

id:: cf4ef491-468b-49d2-b7d7-1ba0702bb67a
content::
A new safety lab is comparing two projects it could fund:

- Project A: better computer vision, so that future AI systems understand what they see.
- Project B: error-tolerant agent design, meaning agents that do not try to deceive or manipulate their programmers, even when the programmers made mistakes in building them.

1. In your own words, why does the agenda say that work like Project A could be left to capable AI systems later, but Project B cannot?
2. The agenda uses other criteria to pick its problems too. Name one other criterion that Project A fails, and say why it fails it.

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The agenda's idea: it focuses on problems that are tractable, uncrowded, focused on alignment, and unable to be safely delegated to machines. A capable automated engineer could make robust progress on computer vision even if its own vision started weak, and most goal-pursuing agents want better vision anyway, because it helps them model the world. But no task, including making the agent error-tolerant, can be safely handed to a highly intelligent agent that has incentives to deceive or manipulate its programmers, so error-tolerant design has to be worked out first by humans. Computer vision also fails other criteria: it is not uncrowded (a large field already works on it in normal AI research), and it is not focused (it makes intelligent systems better in general, not aligned in particular).
