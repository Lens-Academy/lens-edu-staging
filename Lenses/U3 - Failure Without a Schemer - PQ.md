#### Text
content::
\## Before you read anything

This unit has readings in it. You are not getting them yet.

The questions below are the ones the readings answer. Answering them first is not a warm-up, it is most of the work. When you read afterwards, you will find some of your own reasoning already on the page, which is worth more than reading it cold, and you will find things you missed, which is where the learning is.

Two rules. Do not look anything up. Do not worry about being wrong: a confidently wrong answer you can compare against later is more useful here than a cautious one that commits to nothing.

#### Question
content::
\## Ground rules

Most people arrive with a picture of AI catastrophe that has a villain in it: a system that wants something we do not want, conceals this, and makes a move.

Set that picture aside entirely. For everything below, assume:

- no AI system is deceptive
- no AI system has goals of its own beyond the task it was given
- every system does what its operators asked, and would shut down cleanly if told to
- the operators are ordinary people, not malicious, and each individual decision is signed off by someone acting in good faith

Now: describe how this civilization still ends up somewhere nobody wanted and cannot get back.

Take your best shot. Be specific about the steps. If you think it cannot happen under these constraints, say that instead, and say what the constraints rule out.

max-time:: 12:00
assessment-instructions:: This is a pre-test taken BEFORE any reading in this unit. The student is about to read Christiano's "What failure looks like" and Kulveit et al.'s "Gradual Disempowerment". Your job is to get their reasoning on record and sharpen it slightly, NOT to teach the material.

Do not explain the answer. Do not name the readings, the authors, or any term of art (Goodhart, proxy gaming, structural risk, multipolar failure). If the student produces one of these ideas themselves, use their words for it, not the canonical name.

Response length: 80 to 150 words. Short paragraphs only. No lists.

Response style:
- Calm and direct. Treat this as intellectual work in progress.
- Do not over-validate. Avoid generic praise (great thinking, excellent point, exactly right).
- Do not signal whether they are close to the accepted answer. They will find out by reading.

What to do in your single reply:
1. Restate the mechanism they proposed in one or two sentences, more precisely than they put it, without adding steps they did not have.
2. Ask exactly one question that pushes on the weakest joint of their own account. If they described systems drifting but never said what couples them, ask what one system's output does to another's input. If they smuggled in an agent with goals despite the ground rules, name that plainly and ask them to try again without it. If they said it cannot happen, ask which of the four constraints is doing the work, and what would have to change for it to happen anyway.
3. Close by telling them to save their answer and move on to the reading, and that they will be asked this again afterwards in a different form.

If the student says they do not know, do not fill it in. Ask them for the least implausible thing they can think of, and accept a rough answer.

This is a one to two turn phase. Do not let it become a lesson.
