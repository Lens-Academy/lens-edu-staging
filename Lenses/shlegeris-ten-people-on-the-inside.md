summary_for_tutor: "Explores a pessimistic 'rushed unreasonable developer' scenario: an AI company that pays only lip service to misalignment risk, inside which just ten staff genuinely care about catastrophic risk from scheming models. Opens by laying out a spectrum of resource-and-buy-in regimes (the safety-case regime, rushed reasonable developer, rushed unreasonable developer, and existential war), then argues this low-will scenario is scarily plausible in short timelines and under-targeted by current safety planning. Frames the ten people's options as building concrete evidence of risk, implementing safety measures, and doing alignment research, and stresses that any measures they adopt must be cheap and low in compliance overhead (roughly 3% of company compute, minimal political capital, do-ocracy dynamics). Concludes with implications: ten is much better than zero, plan for exportable research and external assistance, and prioritize research now on safety and control techniques implementable on a low budget."
