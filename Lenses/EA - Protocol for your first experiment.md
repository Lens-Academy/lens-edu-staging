content::
This page turns the first experiment you chose into a protocol you can follow from day one. The parts are the ones from module 3's checklist, one question each. Dose and duration are now two separate questions. You can copy from your module 3 draft wherever it fits.

::card[[../Lenses/EA - Draft your first experiment]]
> The protocol you drafted in module 3.

::card[[../Lenses/EA - Designing a self-experiment]]
> Module 3's checklist and the designs it explains: alternating, reversal, multiple baseline.

First, one broad measure. In module 3 you took the WHO-5, the World Health Organization's five-question wellbeing index. Take it again now, from the table on that page. It gives you a starting point for the practice part of the course.

#### Question: Open
id:: 3531c152-039e-49c3-9b26-ab4ae6d4d9f1
content::
What is your WHO-5 score now, out of 100?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner retook the WHO-5 (five items about the last two weeks, each 0 to 5, raw total 0 to 25, times 4 gives 0 to 100). Reply in 40 to 80 words. If the number is not a multiple of 4 or is above 100, ask them to check the arithmetic. If they mention their module 3 score, note the difference in one sentence and add that a single change between two dates cannot show what caused it: life events, the season and regression to the mean all move such scores. Say this number is their starting point for the practice part. If the score is below 50, say calmly that the WHO document suggests this as a reason for a check with a doctor, worth doing alongside the course. Do not interpret the score further. One reply.

**The claim.** Which claim does your first experiment test, in the words of the person who makes it?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for the first experiment they chose in module 8, one part per question. This part asks for the claim and who makes it. In one or two sentences: confirm what they wrote, and if the source or the claim itself is missing or vague (for example "meditation helps"), ask for it with one concrete suggestion, such as the claim on the experiment's page. 20 to 50 words. Do not judge whether the claim is true, no generic praise. One turn.

content::
**Dose.** How much will you do on each day you practise: what, for how many minutes, how many times?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for the dose per day. In two or three sentences: confirm it, and check where it comes from. The experiments pages say which doses come from the source (for example Thanissaro's 20 minutes, Brasington's at least 45 minutes, MBSR's 45 to 60 minutes, Greater Good in Action's 15 minutes for best possible self) and which are the course's own suggestion. If they lowered the source's dose, say that a null result then says less about the claim. If the dose is an hour a day or more, or intensive noting or jhana practice, mention in one sentence that the stop rule later on this page matters more. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: c23b008c-f942-48ed-b993-5ab0da26210d
content::
**Duration.** For how many days or weeks will you run it?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for the duration. In one or two sentences: check that the duration is long enough for the effect the source claims (minutes for a single labelling or a self-compassion break, a week or two for a daily exercise, eight weeks for MBSR, months or longer for claims like fundamental wellbeing, where a short test says little). If it is too short, say so and suggest either a longer run or a smaller claim to test. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: cfc0fbb6-8da6-4b0d-8568-294466e23779
content::
**Your measure.** What is the one main measure you will take, chosen now?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for one main measure chosen in advance. In one or two sentences: check that it is the same number taken the same way and at the same time each time (a daily 0 to 10 rating, a count, a recovery time, a scale such as WHO-5, SPANE, the Satisfaction with Life Scale, EQUA-S, NAS-7 or NADA-S), and that its timing fits the effect: a same-day effect needs a daily measure, while scales that ask about weeks suit slow effects. If the measure is vague, suggest the one on the experiment's page. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: d45d8203-66a9-4a33-9131-2f398a6ebfb4
content::
**Your baseline.** How long will you measure before you start practising?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for a baseline: measuring before the practice starts until the measure is steady, ideally 10 to 20 days and at least 5 (Seth Roberts, module 3). For a one-off practice such as the pointing experiment, a short baseline like the NADA study's 5 minutes of sitting is enough. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if it is missing or too short for a daily measure, say so. 20 to 50 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: db8c2e50-9878-43ee-961f-fefbe3d3651e
content::
**Your design.** Alternating days, a reversal (on, off, on again), or a multiple baseline? Say why it fits this claim.
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for a design that fits the claim (module 3): alternating days for fast effects that wear off quickly, a reversal for effects that should fade when stopped, a multiple baseline or a careful long baseline-then-practice run for changes that cannot be undone. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if the design does not fit, name the one that would and why. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 725e4d70-345f-49d9-8de4-f5002ad0a45d
content::
**Confounders.** What else could change your measure during the experiment, and how will you note it?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for confounders: things noted daily (sleep, illness, stress, big events), nothing else changed on purpose at the same time, and regression to the mean if they start in an unusually bad week. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and name the most likely missing one for this experiment. 20 to 50 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 4997d559-7339-430e-aebd-ed940078d45a
content::
**Expectation.** How will you keep your hopes from producing the result?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for handling expectation: blinding is rarely possible, so an active comparison of the same length (for example a week of neutral writing, as in Seligman's 2005 trial, or a coin toss between two practices, as on the self-compassion page), a written prediction, and a measure that is harder to fool (a count, a partner's view, recovery time). In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if nothing is planned, suggest one of these. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 557ee66a-2b8c-4517-b4c7-f8aea9f0e1a0
content::
**Stopping.** When will you stop, and what would make you stop early?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their first chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for a planned end and an early stop rule. For any meditation or self practice, the rule should name the warning signs from the course's safety boxes: feeling unreal, detached from the body or feelings, emotional flatness, agitation or poor sleep (Britton, Cheetah House), and what they will do (cut back or stop, and talk to a doctor, therapist or Cheetah House if it lasts). For other experiments, stopping if their measure or daily life clearly gets worse. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if a needed part is missing, name it. Do not alarm them. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 56b67457-284c-4af0-8867-ac6b5e1eee7a
content::
**Looking at the results.** How will you look at your data at the end?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner has now written every part of a protocol for the first experiment they chose in module 8: claim and source, dose, duration, prediction, main measure, baseline, design, confounders, expectation, stopping, and now analysis (module 3: plot the main measure against the day and look at level, trend and how long the change took to appear). Review the whole protocol, using their earlier answers on this page. Reply in 150 to 230 words. Check, in this order, and comment on what is weak or missing: (1) does the design fit how fast the effect is claimed to come and whether it should fade; (2) does the dose come from the claim's source, and if not, is that said; (3) is there one main measure chosen in advance, with a baseline before the practice; (4) is there a stop rule, and for intensive practices (an hour or more a day, retreats, intensive noting or jhana practice, practices that loosen the sense of self) does it name the warning signs and where to get help, without alarming them; (5) could the plan realistically be kept up, and if not, suggest a smaller first test. Name the two most important fixes, most important first, with one concrete suggestion each. Do not choose the claim for them, do not say whether it is true, and do not say which school is right. Offer one more exchange to revise. No generic praise.
