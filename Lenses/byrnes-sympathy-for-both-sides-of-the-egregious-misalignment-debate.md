title: "Sympathy for both sides of the egregious misalignment debate"
# tldr: "Yudkowsky and Soares say careful reasoning about superintelligence predicts egregious misalignment by default. Most LLM researchers say careful reasoning about current models says today's alignment techniques are adequate. Byrnes argues both are right about their own object of study, and the real crux is whether LLMs scale into ASI."
summary_for_tutor: "Steven Byrnes says both sides of the egregious misalignment debate are partly right. (1) Careful reasoning about ASI gives good reasons to expect it to be egregiously misaligned, scheming and ruthless without yet-to-be-invented alignment breakthroughs (Yudkowsky and Soares). (2) Careful reasoning about current LLMs gives good reasons to think existing alignment techniques are adequate now and may stay so (almost everyone who studies LLMs). He reconciles them by holding that LLMs will not scale to ASI. He also faults each side within its own frame: Yudkowsky and Soares treat LLMs 'waking up' as plausible and current LLMs as clear evidence, when that evidence is mixed; LLM researchers ignore that more RLVR or open-ended continual learning needs a ground truth that acts as an objective function, which would dilute pretraining-derived niceness into ruthless maximization."
