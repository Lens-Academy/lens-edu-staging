summary_for_tutor: "Steven Byrnes reconciles two camps that each reason carefully within their own domain and reach opposite conclusions. Position (1): reasoning about the properties of ASI predicts egregious misalignment, scheming, and ruthlessness absent yet-to-be-invented alignment breakthroughs (Yudkowsky and Soares). Position (2): reasoning about current LLMs finds existing alignment techniques adequate now and plausibly in the future (most LLM researchers). Byrnes holds both are true and reconciles them by doubting that LLMs, specifically, scale to ASI, so the debate is less about who reasons better and more about whether the object each side studies (current LLMs vs. ASI in general) is the same object. Serves as a steelman-both-sides companion to the module's default-misalignment argument: shows the strongest form of the objection (today's systems do not look egregiously misaligned) and the strongest reconciliation (the default-misalignment claim targets ASI, not necessarily today's models)."
