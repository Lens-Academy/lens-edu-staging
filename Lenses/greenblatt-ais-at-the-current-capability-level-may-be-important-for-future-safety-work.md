summary_for_tutor: "Argues that models at roughly current capability may remain important even once AI poses substantial existential risk, pushing back on the view that present-day safety research is low-value. Gives two main reasons: (1) our best trusted (confidently non-scheming) models might stay near current capability, making them essential for control protocols, alignment-experiment baselines, model-organisms research, and sandbagging detection via scale extrapolation; and (2) once safety research is automated, compute constraints may push large fractions of experiments onto relatively weak models through experiment miniaturization. Concludes that techniques with principled reasons to generalize from weak to strong models, and test beds built at current capability, are correspondingly more valuable."
