# tldr: "Everyone in the debate thought AI might destroy the world, and they still could not agree on pausing it. Scott Alexander sorts the positions into five camps, from a six-month pause to a total stop to no pause at all, and lays out what each one has to get right."
summary_for_tutor: "Scott Alexander's October 2023 summary of the EA Forum AI pause debate. It sorts positions into five camps. Simple Pause (e.g. the FLI six-month letter): criticized for compute overhang (hardware keeps improving, so progress jumps when the pause ends) and for burning a safety-conscious leader's lead in a race, with the benefit of more alignment time. Surgical Pause: pause right before dangerous AI, for slightly less than the leader's margin over rivals; problem is knowing when that is. Regulatory Pause (Manheim, Larsen): an FDA-like agency that fast-tracks small models and scrutinizes frontier ones; regulation reliably prevents harm at the cost of slowing everything, but may fail against deceptive alignment. Total Stop (Bensinger, Elmore): faces non-participating countries, algorithmic progress, and hardware progress that eventually let AI be trained on a laptop, so it likely buys decades at most. No Pause (Belrose): a pause backfires via illegal labs, brain drain, loopholes, politicization, and international conflict. Also covered: Elmore on public advocacy, Barnett on pauses becoming indefinite, Pope on AI centralizing power, the nuclear and CFC treaty analogies, and whether prosaic alignment success is evidence of real alignment or of deception. Alexander's own view: world-dictatorship fears are overblown, a world without AI faces other serious risks, a pause is probably temporary, impossibility is not an argument against starting negotiations, and he is open to a Regulatory or Surgical Pause. Written in 2023, so 'frontier' means GPT-4. In this submodule it follows Yudkowsky's TED talk (a total stop) and the Statement on Superintelligence (a conditional prohibition)."
