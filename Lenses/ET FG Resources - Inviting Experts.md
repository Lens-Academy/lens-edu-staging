\## 🗣️ Inviting Guests to Join Your Sessions

For some sessions, especially Week 4 to Week 8, we recommend inviting external guests who can present on their work relevant to your cohort’s topic area and provide live feedback on student proposals. This is also a great opportunity for you to build connections with others in your field and topic area!

\### Who to reach out to?

- When inviting experts or seeking feedback, consider reaching out to PhD students or junior researchers a few steps ahead of you, rather than the head/directors of labs. They are often more available, approachable, and able to provide practical, hands-on guidance.
- We also recommend inviting those who will use the research or are actively working on similar research. For example, this may be:
   - End users or beneficiaries of the research, to provide real-world perspectives on relevance and impact.
   - Staff at think tanks or research organizations working on similar topics.
   - Policy advocates if your work relates to advocacy or applied research.

\### Facilitation Guide for Sessions with Experts

**Recommended Structure of the Session with Experts**

- **Welcome & Introductions (5 min)** – Introduce the expert and provide context for the cohort’s work.
- **Expert Presentation (15 min)** – Allow the expert to share insights relevant to the cohort topic.
- **Live Feedback on Proposals (30 min)** – Students present key sections of their proposals; expert gives feedback.
- **Q&A & Reflection (10–15 min)** – Students ask questions; facilitator summarizes key takeaways.

**After the Session:**

- Thank the expert via email.
- Share a short reflection with the cohort summarizing the expert’s feedback.

**Support:** If you’d like additional assistance in coordinating a session with an external guest/expert, please reach out to Christine or Alex on Slack.

Here's a template you can adapt when inviting a guest to one of your sessions. Personalise it as much as you can: mention a specific piece of their work, say why it connects to your cohort's topics, and suggest a date (while staying flexible).
