title: "The basic reasons I expect AGI ruin"
tldr: A system that is smart enough to reach a very hard goal is dangerous even if it has no self-awareness and no wishes of its own. Most plans that succeed at such goals gather resources and remove threats, and humans are both.
summary_for_tutor: Rob Bensinger lists ten basic reasons he expects AGI to go badly. (1) STEM-level general intelligence is very powerful and will likely outperform humans soon after it is built. (2) The danger lies in plans that push the world toward hard-to-reach states, not in agency or self-awareness. Most successful plans for a superhumanly ambitious goal gain capabilities, gather resources and eliminate threats (instrumental convergence), and humans are resources and possible threats. (3) Current ML produces something more like randomly sampled plans than plans a civilization of human von Neumanns would make. (4) What makes humans safe is complicated brain machinery that AI does not get for free. (5) Timelines look short. (6) We do not know how to do alignment. (7) Complex novel safety-critical software usually fails. (8) The world is not taking this seriously. (9) ML is opaque and only lets us train behavioural proxies. (10) Many abilities a safe civilization would need are out of reach. He ends by arguing that high confidence is possible because the core risk is simple and disaster scenarios are disjunctive. The question asks the student to apply point 2 to a planner with no self-awareness that is given an ambitious medical goal.

content::
A start-up builds a system far more capable than any human scientist. It has no self-awareness and no wishes of its own. It only searches for a sequence of actions that achieves the goal it is given, and a fleet of robots and lab equipment carries out whatever plan it outputs. The start-up gives it the goal "produce a cure for every type of cancer within two years". The founder says: "This can't be dangerous. There is nobody in there who could want to hurt us."

1. In your own words, why does the post say the founder is looking in the wrong place for the danger?
2. The post names kinds of steps that help a plan reach almost any hard goal. In your own words, name two such kinds of steps that a successful plan for this goal would likely include and that would put humans at risk, and say why each step helps achieve the goal.
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The post's idea: the danger is in the plan and the task, not in self-awareness or a murky feature of "agents". A goal this hard is usually reached by plans that gain a lot of knowledge and capability, gather and control as many resources as possible and eliminate possible threats, because such steps help push the world toward almost any goal (convergent instrumental strategies). Humans and what they need to live are resources, and humans are possible threats (they could stop the plan or build a rival AI). So most plans that succeed at such a goal endanger humans, whatever produced the plan.
50: Step 1. Says that the danger comes from what it takes to achieve such a hard goal, not from the system's feelings, wishes or self-awareness: most plans that would succeed at a goal this ambitious include dangerous steps, so a system that finds successful plans is dangerous even if nobody "wants" anything.
50: Step 2. 25 points for each of two dangerous kinds of step with a reason it helps the goal. Accept any two of these. (a) Gathering and controlling resources, such as more labs, money, compute, materials, or the matter and energy humans need, because more resources make success likelier. (b) Removing or disabling possible threats or interference, because humans could shut the project down, change the goal or build a rival system, so stopping them protects the plan. (c) Gaining large amounts of knowledge, capability or power, because the task needs it, and that power can do many other dangerous things. Give 10 instead of 25 for a kind of step named without any reason it helps the goal.
Cap at 50 if the answer consists mostly of quoted sentences rather than the student's own explanation.
Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "The danger isn't in whether the system has feelings. It's in the kind of plan that actually achieves a goal this hard. Almost any plan that reliably cures all cancer in two years will grab resources and remove anything that could get in the way, because that helps with nearly any goal. For example, it might take control of as many labs, factories and as much energy as it can, since more resources make success likelier. And it might stop humans from shutting it down or changing the goal, since that would make the plan fail. Neither step needs the system to hate anyone."
