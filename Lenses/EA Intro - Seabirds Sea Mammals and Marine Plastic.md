tldr: "Marine plastic kills up to a million seabirds a year, surely one of the ocean's great animal killers? Vasco Grilo runs the numbers and finds humans catch roughly two million times as many wild fish as plastic kills seabirds. A short worked example of how a back-of-the-envelope Fermi estimate can upend which problem looks biggest."
summary_for_tutor: "A worked Fermi (back-of-the-envelope) estimate by Vasco Grilo comparing animal deaths from marine plastic pollution to the wild fish catch. Starting from per-capita figures (about 1 kg of plastic emitted to the ocean per person per year, up to 1 million seabirds and 100,000 sea mammals killed annually, and roughly 200 wild fish caught per person per year), it derives that the wild fish catch is about 2 million times the number of seabirds and 20 million times the number of sea mammals killed by plastic. Illustrates quantitative cause-prioritization reasoning and how estimating scale can reveal counterintuitive differences in magnitude."
