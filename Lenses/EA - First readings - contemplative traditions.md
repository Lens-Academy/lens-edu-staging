#### Text
content::
Open any tradition you want a first look at. Each box has one short source. The longer ones are linked rather than shown here.

#### Callout: Early Buddhism and Theravada
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
An essay by the monk Thanissaro Bhikkhu on anatta, usually translated "no-self". He argues that the Buddha taught not-self as a strategy for ending suffering, not as a claim that there is no self. This is his reading, not the only one. About 5 minutes.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/bhikkhu-no-self-or-not-self]]
from:: One of the first stumbling blocks that Westerners often encounter
to:: or whether or not it's a self?
#### End Callout

#### Callout: The jhanas
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
Leigh Brasington, a teacher of jhana practice, gives step-by-step instructions for entering the first jhana: [Instructions for Entering Jhana](https://www.leighb.com/jhana3.htm) (about 12 minutes).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Zen
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
The prologue to Shunryu Suzuki's *Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind*, on what he calls beginner's mind: [Prologue](https://www.cuke.com/bibliography/ZMBM/prologue.html) (about 3 minutes).
#### End Callout

Yongey Mingyur Rinpoche, a teacher in the Tibetan tradition, on awareness as the essence of meditation (TED talk, 11 minutes).

#### Callout: Advaita Vedanta and modern non-dual teachers
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
Ramana Maharshi's *Who Am I?*, published free by his ashram. Read the opening and the first three questions (about 2 minutes): [Who Am I? (PDF)](https://www.gururamana.org/Teachings/who_am_I.pdf).

For something to try rather than read, Douglas Harding's [pointing experiment](https://www.headless.org/experiments/pointing.htm) takes a minute.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Yoga
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali. The opening paragraph places the text, and the sections on the mind and the eight limbs cover the practice: [Yoga (Patanjali)](https://iep.utm.edu/yoga/) (opening about 2 minutes).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Secular and pragmatic meditation
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
Jon Kabat-Zinn, who created Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction, defines mindfulness, in a short piece on Mindful.org: [Jon Kabat-Zinn: Defining Mindfulness](https://www.mindful.org/jon-kabat-zinn-defining-mindfulness/) (about 3 minutes).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Christian and Sufi mysticism
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
The Persian theologian al-Ghazali (died 1111) describes why he turned to Sufism, in his autobiography *Deliverance from Error* (translated by Claud Field). About 2 minutes.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/ghazali-munkidh-min-al-dalal-deliverance-from-error]]
from:: When I had finished my examination of these doctrines
to:: not of instruction, but of ecstasy and initiation.

#### Text
content::
For the Christian side, chapter 7 of the anonymous 14th-century *Cloud of Unknowing* gives its method in a page: [The Cloud of Unknowing, chapter 7](https://ccel.org/ccel/anonymous2/cloud/cloud.xiii.html).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Taoism

The story of Cook Ding, the butcher whose knife never dulls, from the *Zhuangzi* in Robert Eno's translation. It is in section 3 of the PDF (about 3 minutes): [Zhuangzi, tr. Robert Eno (PDF)](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/handle/2022/23427).
#### End Callout
