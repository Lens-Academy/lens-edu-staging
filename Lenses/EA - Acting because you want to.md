#### Text
content::
When you work hard, is it because you want to or because you feel you have to? **Self-determination theory**, developed by the psychologists Richard Ryan and Edward Deci, studies this question. It claims that people have three basic psychological needs: **autonomy** (acting by your own choice), **competence** (being effective) and **relatedness** (being connected to others), and that wellbeing depends on meeting them ([Ryan and Deci 2000](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)).

You will read two parts of their best-known overview paper. The first sorts the reasons people act for. The second asks which life goals go with wellbeing. While you read, think of one thing you do every week and ask which description fits your reasons for it.

\## Reading assignment
Open the paper: [Ryan and Deci 2000, "Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being"](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf) (free PDF from the Center for Self-Determination Theory).

**Part 1** (journal pages 72 to 73, about 6 minutes). Start at the paragraph beginning
> Within SDT, Deci and Ryan (1985) introduced a second subtheory

and stop after
> as well as with expending more effort.

In this part, "regulation" means the reason that keeps a behaviour going. Figure 1 on page 72 draws the continuum the text describes, from no motivation on the left to intrinsic motivation on the right.

**Part 2** (journal page 75, about 4 minutes). Before you read it: Ryan and Deci call goals such as close relationships, personal growth and contributing to a community **intrinsic aspirations**, and goals such as wealth, fame and image **extrinsic aspirations**. Start at
> First, we discuss the relation of personal goals to well-being.

and stop after
> the relation between underlying need satisfaction and well-being is theorized to be invariant.

Return here after reading.

Ryan and Deci are the founders of the theory, and the passages summarise their own research programme. A later review by Tim Kasser, who co-authored the aspiration studies with Ryan, collects the evidence on materialistic goals, including experiments ([Kasser 2016](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/2016_Kasser_ARP.pdf)). It is an optional page after this one.

\## Recall
Spend 2 minutes writing down everything you remember from the two passages, without looking back. No need to organise it.
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner just read two passages from Ryan and Deci 2000 and writes a free recall without looking back.

Key points:
- Motivation is a continuum of how self-determined it is, from amotivation through four kinds of extrinsic motivation to intrinsic motivation.
- External regulation: acting to satisfy an outside demand or reward. Introjected: acting to avoid guilt or anxiety or to gain pride, regulation by contingent self-esteem. Identified: consciously valuing the goal as personally important. Integrated: that value fits with one's other values and needs. Intrinsic: doing something for its inherent satisfaction.
- External and introjected are controlled. Identified, integrated and intrinsic are autonomous. People do not have to pass through the stages in order.
- In school children, introjection went with more effort but also more anxiety and poorer coping with failure. Identification went with interest, enjoyment and better coping.
- Placing relative importance on intrinsic aspirations (relationships, growth, community) went with well-being, on extrinsic ones (wealth, fame, image) the other way. Attaining intrinsic goals raised well-being, attaining extrinsic ones gave little benefit.
- The meaning of goals varies by culture. The authors theorise that the link between need satisfaction and well-being does not.

Your role is diagnostic, not instructional: a brief, honest mirror. 80 to 150 words. Short paragraphs, no lists. Acknowledge what is correct without inflation, name what is missing without lecturing, correct errors in one sentence, normalise gaps. Close with one calibrating sentence. Do not ask a question or invite a reply. This is one turn. Tell them to move on. No generic praise.

\## Learning question
