summary_for_tutor: "An essay by Nate Soares on scope insensitivity and moral motivation. It argues that the human 'care-o-meter' is calibrated for roughly 150 people (Dunbar's number) and cannot scale to represent billions suffering, so felt caring is an unreliable guide to action. Through worked examples (donors driven by social pressure; a thought experiment about oiled birds and 'shutting up and multiplying'), it contrasts acting from feelings with acting from explicit reasoning, and concludes that effective altruists are not people who feel more but people who have learned not to trust their feelings, choosing to act on the true scale of problems instead."
