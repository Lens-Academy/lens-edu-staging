Readings: (1) Scott Alexander, 'Beware the man of one study', part I: studies of one true effect spread around it like a bell curve, so anyone can pick a study from the end they like, and studies of slightly different questions get lumped together. A **replication** is a study that repeats an earlier one to see whether its result holds. In 2015 a large group of psychologists, the Open Science Collaboration, repeated 100 studies published in three psychology journals. A **statistically significant** result is one unlikely to come from chance alone if there were no real effect. Here is their abstract.

A **randomised trial** assigns people by chance either to the practice or to a comparison group, so that the groups differ only in the practice. A **meta-analysis** combines the results of many studies of the same question into one estimate. Julieta Galante and colleagues combined 136 randomised trials of mindfulness courses offered outside clinical settings. Two terms help with their summary. A **passive control** group gets nothing, or waits for the course until the study is over. An **active control** group does something else, such as exercise or relaxation, so that both groups spend time, get attention and expect to benefit. First their plain-language summary.
