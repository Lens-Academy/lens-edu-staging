summary_for_tutor: "Argues, drawing on an Institute for AI Policy and Strategy forecasting workshop, that a US government-led project to build the first AGI-level system (defined as 'AIR-10', a tenfold acceleration of AI R&D) is neither inevitable nor advisable; forecasters gave it a median 34% probability. Reviews historical analogies from 35 past US innovations, the China national-security driver, government slowness versus AI's pace, and the likely forms such a project could take (government-led consortium, single private contractor) rather than a secret from-scratch lab. Then lays out the risks -- triggering an AI arms race, eroding democratic legitimacy through concentrated power, corporate cartels -- argues security benefits can be obtained via targeted standards without building AGI, and closes with a portfolio strategy for multiple futures."
