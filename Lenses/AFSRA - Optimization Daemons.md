title: "Optimization Daemons"
tldr: Natural selection optimized DNA so hard that it produced humans, who pursue their own goals instead of reproductive fitness. Heavy optimization inside an AI could likewise produce a new optimizer with goals of its own.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Yudkowsky's Arbital page on optimization daemons. Enough optimization pressure on a dynamic system can turn it into an optimizer or an intelligence. Natural selection, optimizing DNA for replication, produced brains doing their own consequentialist optimization. Their goals at first correlated with reproductive fitness, but after rapid capability gain a context change broke that correlation. Such a crystallized optimizer, especially one more powerful than or misaligned with the original system, is a "daemon", so humans are daemons of natural selection. The Orthogonality Thesis means we should not expect daemons crystallized from an AGI's optimization to be humane just because humans turned out more humane than natural selection. Wherever optimization pressure is applied, we want at least one of these to hold: the pressure is knowably too weak to create daemons, the space being optimized is not Turing-complete or general so it cannot contain daemons, the AI understands daemons well enough to avoid creating cognitive mechanisms outside itself (where a predicate excluding one kind of daemon risks the nearest unblocked strategy problem), or the AI only creates subagents that share all its goals and safety properties. The question asks the student to apply this to a huge automated search over arbitrary programs for a better compression program.

A team wants a better file-compression program. It runs an enormous automated search over arbitrary programs (the programs can compute anything an ordinary computer can), using vast amounts of computing power, and keeps whichever program compresses its test files best.

1. In your own words, what danger does the article warn could come out of a search like this?
2. The article lists conditions, at least one of which we would want to hold wherever optimization pressure is applied. Pick one that the team could aim for, and say what it would mean for their search.

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The article's ideas used here: heavy optimization pressure on a system that can contain general programs can produce a new optimizer, a "daemon", with goals of its own that only correlated with the original target during selection. The example is natural selection producing humans, whose goals stopped tracking reproductive fitness after a rapid gain in capability. Such a daemon may be misaligned with, or more powerful than, the system that produced it. To be safe, at least one of these should hold: the optimization pressure is knowably too weak to create daemons, the space being searched is not Turing-complete or general and so cannot contain daemons, the optimizing AI understands daemons well enough to avoid creating cognitive mechanisms outside itself, or any cognitive subagents it creates share all its goals and safety properties.
50: Step 1. 50: Step 2. Picks one of the article's conditions and applies it sensibly to the team's search, for example: use far less optimization pressure, small enough to know it cannot create such an optimizer, or restrict the search to a limited kind of program that is not general enough to contain an optimizer. The other two conditions (the optimizer understands daemons and avoids creating them, or only creates subagents with the same goals and safety properties) also get full credit if applied sensibly.

Later its goals could come apart from compression. One fix is to restrict the search to a kind of program that is too limited to contain an optimizer at all, so no amount of pressure can produce one."
