\## Reading: what does this amount to?
Read the short piece below, then return here.

\## What worry is reasonable, and what is alarm
The evidence points at a real pattern: help that removes the struggle can quietly erode the skill. That is a reason to change how you use AI, not a reason to panic. Alarm treats every AI use as dangerous. The reasonable position is narrower. Specific patterns of use carry specific costs, and you control more of those patterns than you think.

\## One thing to do today
Pick a single guardrail from Unit 4, the one that feels most doable. Practise it today. One kept guardrail beats a long list you abandon on Wednesday.

\## Where to learn more
The course drew on a small set of sources. If you want to go further:
- "Gradual Disempowerment" by Jan Kulveit and colleagues, the paper behind Unit 3 (gradual-disempowerment.ai or arXiv 2501.16946)
- The 80,000 Hours problem profile on gradual disempowerment, an accessible summary of the same paper
- "Artificial Intelligence and Human Legal Reasoning" by Bednar, Cleveland, Erbsen, and Schwarcz, the legal study behind Unit 3
- The workplace survey work of Roxana Seifer on UK workers and AI
- The practical writing of Jaspreet Singh on AI and critical thinking
- Courses from BlueDot, 80,000 Hours, and Lens Academy that cover AI and how we think

\## A parting thought
Whether AI helps or erodes your judgement depends on when and how you use it. Your judgement and your taste are worth defending, whatever the future holds. The tools will change. The practice of thinking about your own thinking does not have to.

\## Final reflection
These questions match the ones you answered at the start of the course. Answer them again, in your own words. "No change" is a valid answer. A changed answer is also valid. We want what is true for you now.

1. How do you now use AI assistants in work and in life? What tasks, how often, and why? How, if at all, has this changed?
2. When you use one, how do you now decide what to ask, and what to do with the answer? Has that decision changed?
3. What do you now enjoy about AI tools? What feels missing now? If a tool existed to help you use AI more consciously, what would you want it to do now?
4. Since you started this course, what has changed about how you think about how you think?
