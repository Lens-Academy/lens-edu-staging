We wanted to share that for most people, the hardest part of this week isn't finding stakeholders, it's actually hitting send! Oftentimes, a voice may come up, like “*who am I to be emailing this person*, or *they're going to know how unqualified I am”*. And you’re definitely not alone in experiencing imposter syndrome - we all experience it in different degrees and contexts!

A few things we wanted to share to encourage you when this comes up:

- **This feeling is so common, universal, and NOT a sign you're failing!** Luisa Rodriguez, a researcher at Rethink Priorities, has [written really openly](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/gp94EeYgbh5qjfu65/my-experience-with-imposter-syndrome-and-how-to-partly) about feeling this even years into her career, including around this exact kind of outreach. If people deep into their careers still feel it, that tells you something, the feeling just doesn't track reality very well.
- **The experts you're nervous about were once exactly where you are.** Most people you'll reach out to remember being a student cold-emailing someone senior, and many genuinely enjoy being that person for someone else now. You are also giving them the gift of being able to share the work they spend their lives on!
- **People are often way more receptive than you'd expect.** And the people who do reply are almost always genuinely kind about it - most people love being asked about their work.
- **A "no" or silence really isn't a judgement on your worth or value**. Oftentimes, this might mean a busy week or a full inbox, not a judgement on whether your question or your work matters. And **rejection can be reframed as realignment to opportunities and people who are more aligned with your path!**
- **You don't need permission to be curious.** Wanting to learn from someone's work is itself a beautiful and good enough reason to reach out!
- **And honestly, you're on the same team!** You and the person you’re reaching out to both want to see this problem taken seriously, and that shared mission is real common ground that you can build on.

We also recommend grounding and reminding yourself before sending a batch of emails, on your mission, and on what's actually driving you to do this work in the first place. Oftentimes, our nervousness is just a sign for how deeply we care about this going well, and we can tap into being grateful that we have this intention for the world to be a better place!
