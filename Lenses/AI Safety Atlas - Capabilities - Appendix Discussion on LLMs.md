summary_for_tutor: "A capabilities-chapter appendix from the AI Safety Atlas that assesses common critiques of large language models to judge whether their limitations are fundamental or likely surmountable through scaling, tools, and better training. It organizes objections under four headings: empirical insufficiency (creativity, sample efficiency, robustness to distribution shift), shallow understanding (the stochastic-parrot memorization-versus-compression debate and hallucination, with mitigation techniques like RAG, process supervision, self-consistency, and calibration), structural inadequacy (missing System 2 reasoning, internal world models, continual learning, long-term memory, and planning), and points of convergence with the brain. It closes with arguments for continuing to scale LLMs (scaling laws, parameter counts relative to animal synapse counts, training-cost comparisons), concluding that scaling current algorithms could plausibly reach AGI."
