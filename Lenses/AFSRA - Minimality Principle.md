title: "Minimality Principle"
tldr: >-
  The first powerful AI should do the least dangerous thing that still prevents disaster. "Least dangerous" is measured by the kind of thinking the plan needs from the AI, not by how risky its physical actions look.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  A short Arbital page by Eliezer Yudkowsky. The Principle of Minimality, or Principle of Least Everything: when building the first sufficiently advanced AI, a marginally more powerful AI is marginally more dangerous, so the first AGI should carry out the least dangerous plan for preventing the AGIs that follow from destroying the world. The least dangerous plan is not the one with the fewest actions that look risky in a conventional sense, but the one that needs the least dangerous cognition from the AGI. Inside the AGI, a dangerous but sometimes necessary class of thought should happen as rarely as possible. Example: searching for strategies across every possible domain is dangerous, so most operations should search within one domain, and cross-domain searches should be rarer, visible to the programmers, need permission and have their results checked. Task-directed AGI, taskishness and mild optimization inherit from this principle. The question asks the student to choose between a plan with few actions that needs cross-domain strategy search and a plan with many risky-looking lab actions that needs only chemistry.

#### Question: Open
id:: 70b6cdf4-9095-4719-a7ee-2bc4c7c35302
content::
The first AGI ever built has to carry out a task that stops other AGI projects from destroying the world. Its team compares two plans.

**Plan A** needs only a few physical actions. To find them, the AGI must search for strategies across every domain it knows, from persuading people to finance to engineering.

**Plan B** needs thousands of robot actions in a chemistry lab, and many of them look risky, such as handling toxic chemicals. For each step, the AGI only searches for strategies within chemistry.

Either plan would complete the task.

Which plan does the Minimality Principle favour? Why?
