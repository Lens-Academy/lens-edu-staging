\## What Lens is

Lens Academy runs cohort-based courses on AI safety. This page is a **lens**: the unit a learner moves through, one page at a time. A course is an ordered list of lenses, grouped into modules, split into weeks by group meetings.

What makes a lens different from a slide or a PDF is that the source material, the tutor, and the assessment all live on the same page. A learner reads the actual text of the actual paper, argues about it with an AI tutor that knows what they just read, and is then tested against a stated learning outcome. Nothing is assumed to have been understood.

:::callout {title="What this tour is" tone="blue"}
Four pages after this one, about 20 minutes end to end. Every page opens by naming the platform features it demonstrates.

The sources are real, a Wikipedia overview of existential risk from AI and a Kurzgesagt explainer, because a course that reads a real source in place is the thing we are showing you. **You are not here to learn them.** Every question, the roleplay and the closing test are about this platform instead, and each is answerable in seconds from what you have just seen. On a real course they carry the subject matter.

Sign in before you start. The tutor, the graded questions and the roleplay only respond for a signed-in reader.
:::

\## One thing to watch for

A module ends with a test you have to pass, not a button you have to click. :note[This annotation is a Lens note: an editorial aside we can attach to any text, including to text inside an imported article.] The test comes from a learning outcome, which is a separate file holding one testable skill and the rubric that checks it.[^tour-outcome]

On a real course those outcomes are shared across the whole curriculum, so a course selects the skills it teaches rather than inventing them. The one at the end of this tour is a throwaway written for this tour: it asks what you make of what you have seen, and what you would want proof of. Your answer is more useful to us than a score is to you.

[^tour-outcome]: Learning outcomes are course-agnostic on purpose. A course does not own its outcomes; it selects them, and the platform then tracks which lenses actually get learners through which outcomes.
