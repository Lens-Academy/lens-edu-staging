#### Text
content::
Each row is one school, and the rows are grouped into three families. Read across a row to see one school. Read down a column to compare all of them on one question.

- **Goal**: which kind of happiness from module 2 the school aims at, or insight (seeing how the self, the mind or the world work), or something else, named plainly.
- **View of the self**: what the school says you are.
- **Main method**: what you actually do.
- **Evidence**: what the school's claims rest on. Module 3 covered what each kind of evidence can and cannot show. Where a brain study looked at one person (N=1) or a handful of people, the cell says so.
- **Commercial interests**: listed where someone central to the school sells courses, apps, books or certification. That does not make the claims false. It is one more thing to weigh.
- **Start here**: one free source for a first look. The three optional pages after this one collect them and show some of them in the page.

Every cell except "None found" links to its source. The cells are short summaries, so open the link before you rely on one.

When you compare the therapy rows, keep one result in mind. A network meta-analysis of 331 trials found that for depression, "individual psychotherapies did not differ significantly from each other". The one exception was non-directive supportive counselling, which did less well. The authors rated most of this evidence low or very low in certainty. CBT had by far the most trials, 211 of the 331 ([Cuijpers et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8129869/)). So the school with the most trials is not necessarily the one that works best.

#### Question: Open

content::
Use the map to answer three short questions.

1. Find two schools that aim at the same kind of happiness by different methods.
2. Find two schools that disagree about the self.
3. Pick the row whose evidence you trust least for what the school claims, and say why, using what you learned in module 3.
feedback-instructions:: The learner has the map of 25 schools in front of them (goal, view of the self, method, evidence, commercial interests). They answer: (1) two schools with the same goal by different methods, (2) two schools that disagree about the self, (3) the row whose evidence they trust least, and why. This is practice in reading the map, not a test.

Good pairs for (1), among others: Epicureanism (ataraxia by limiting desires and arguments) and Stoicism (apatheia by correcting judgements), close calm states by different routes. Theravada and Zen (insight by the Eightfold Path versus zazen or koans). Taoism and Shinzen Young's secular meditation both name equanimity, by accepting change versus noting sensations. Aristotle and the Stoics both aim at eudaimonia, but Aristotle says external goods and friends are needed. Good pairs for (2): Advaita (the real Self is pure awareness) versus Theravada (body and mind are not-self) or Hume (a bundle of perceptions). Yoga (purusha, a pure consciousness distinct from mind) versus Metzinger (a self-model made by the brain). IFS (an undamaged core Self) versus Theravada. Sartre (no fixed nature, you make yourself) versus Yoga or Advaita (a true self that is already there). For (3), any row is fine if the reason fits module 3: N=1 brain studies (jhanas), tiny samples (Zen, Tibetan), self-selection (Martin's 50 interviewees, Stoic Week surveys), no control group, unblinding (psychedelics), commercial interests, or claims that only first-person reports could support.

Check each answer against the map. If a pair does not actually share a goal or differ on the self, say so in one sentence and point to a better pair. For (3), say whether their reason is one of the evidence problems from module 3, and name the one they would most want to check next. Do not say which school is right. 100 to 160 words. At most two replies. No generic praise.
