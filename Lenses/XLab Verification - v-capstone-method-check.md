#### Text
content::
\## What the first version is for

You now have the crappy version, or you have found out why you do not. Either result is information the proposal could not give you, and this checkpoint exists to use it before the next ten hours go in. Your group has heard the pitch and seen the first version; this is where you decide.

Three outcomes are possible, and all three are fine:

- **Continue.** The crappy version was hard in the ways you expected. The unknowns from your proposal resolved in the plan's favour or turned out not to matter. Restate the realistic version, maybe tightened, and keep going.
- **Re-scope.** The crappy version showed that the question is bigger, smaller, or differently shaped than the brief made it look. Same brief, different finish line. Most projects do this; a scope that survives contact with the sources unchanged usually means the sources were not consulted.
- **Switch.** You could not start, the unknowns resolved against you (no public evidence exists on the thing the deliverable depends on), or the brief needs a skill or a mentor you do not have. Pick another brief from the bank today, and only one the bank rates at 10 to 14 hours: about ten hours of project time remain. **This is the last point at which switching is allowed.** From the draft handoff on you re-scope, because a switch any later leaves fewer hours than any brief in the bank asks for.

Signals worth reading:

- If the crappy version was easy and took an hour, the realistic version is too small. Add a column, a scenario, or an adversary.
- If you could not write anything without reading first, the question is still a topic. Narrow it until you can guess an answer.
- If the sources disagree with each other, that is not a problem, that is a finding. Note where they disagree; the deliverable can carry the disagreement.
- If the deliverable's audience turns out not to exist (nobody has this job), the reader needs changing more than the question.

#### Question: Choice
id:: 09c8f462-1029-481e-aeb6-942b541a9a9d
content:: Where is the crappy version?
options::
- It exists, it is ugly, and I could show it to someone
- Half of it exists
- I started but got stuck (say where in the next answer)
- I did not start (say why in the next answer)
feedback-instructions:: Not graded; the written account that follows is what is assessed. Acknowledge the status in one line, without judgement and without advice yet, and send them to the next answer. If they got stuck or did not start, say plainly that this checkpoint exists to use exactly that information, and ask them to be specific in the next answer about where they stopped or what stopped them. No guilt, no lecture, no praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: d9e5ced5-09fa-43e0-8a94-0263127e724e
content::
\## What building it taught you

Three things: something you assumed in the proposal that turned out wrong or unknowable; the part that was hardest; the part that went faster than expected. Then: what did you find out about the two unknowns you named in the proposal?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The proposal is not available; grade what this answer states. 60: the three things, 20 each: an assumption from the proposal that turned out wrong or unknowable, the part that was hardest, and the part that went faster than expected; each earns its 20 only when specific (what it was and what happened), 5 when generic ("it was harder than I thought"). 40: the two unknowns from the proposal, 20 each: the unknown is stated and given a status, resolved and how, or still open and why; an unknown reported as now moot, with the reason, counts in full. If the learner did not start, the first 60 go to an honest, specific account of what stopped them. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "An example for a hypothetical project. Wrong assumption: I assumed customs data would show GPU shipments by end user; it only shows product codes by importer, so end users cannot be read from it. Hardest: reconciling three sources' capacity figures for the same data centre, which disagreed by a factor of two. Faster: the threat table took an hour, because the course's evasion routes gave me the rows. Unknown 1, whether site-level power data is public: resolved, yes for US utilities with 15-minute data, no for China. Unknown 2, whether anyone at an export-control office would use a ranked list: still open, because I have not found anyone in that role to ask; I will ask my facilitator for a contact this week."
feedback-instructions:: Read what they learned back to them as a claim about the project, in one sentence ("so the evidence you need exists but only for air-cooled sites"), and ask whether that changes the realistic version. If an unknown is still open, ask what two hours this week would settle it. If they did not start, do not moralise; ask what the smallest thing they could put on a page tomorrow is. Name the most important gap or weakness, if there is one. Four sentences.

#### Question: Choice
id:: c0c09b82-b00d-4865-850b-4258374645a8
content:: Your decision.
options::
- Continue as scoped
- Continue, re-scoped (say how in the next answer)
- Switch brief (give the full proposal for the new brief in the next answer)
feedback-instructions:: Not graded, and the decision is the learner's; all three outcomes are fine. Acknowledge it in one line without endorsing or second-guessing it, then say what the next answer has to contain: for continue or re-scope, the restated realistic version and what is now out of scope; for a switch, the new brief's name followed by its full proposal. If they chose Switch, add that the replacement must be a bank brief rated at 10 to 14 hours, because about ten hours of project time remain, and that this is the last checkpoint at which switching is allowed. No praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 15acf766-f87f-428b-a97a-a61de983eab5
content::
\## Revised plan

If continuing or re-scoping: restate the realistic version in light of this week, and list what is now out of scope. If switching: the new brief's name, then the proposal in full (question and reader, deliverable and done, crappy version within two days and a draft by the handoff at the end of this week, hours), because your facilitator will read this instead of your week 2 proposal.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. If continuing or re-scoping: 50: the realistic version is restated concretely, by what the deliverable will contain, not by adjectives; 25: what is now out of scope is listed, or a part is explicitly confirmed as still in; 25: the plan visibly responds to this week, naming what the first version or the unknowns changed, cut or confirmed (the week 2 proposal is not available, so judge this from the answer). If switching: 10: the new brief's name; 90: the full proposal, 15 each: the question in one sentence, the reader (by role and the decision they face), the deliverable, what done means, a crappy version within two days and a draft by the handoff at the end of this week, and the hours; a missing part earns 0. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "An example of a re-scope for a hypothetical project. Realistic version: a table of the 12 largest declared inference sites in the US, each with its grid connection, whether public load data exists, and the smallest training run its load noise would hide, plus a two-page memo for the verification desk on whether grid data is worth buying. Now out of scope: China, because the first version showed no site-level data exists, and sites with their own generation, which become a labelled gap. Still in: the load-noise method, which worked on the first two sites."
feedback-instructions:: You have this answer only, not the learner's other answers, so work from what it says about this week. If the restated plan responds to nothing this week produced, say so in one sentence and ask which finding from this week the plan is answering. If it is a switch, check that the new brief fits the remaining three weeks (about 10 hours of project time at the default pace) and say so if it does not. Otherwise name the most important gap or weakness in the plan in one sentence. No praise; end by telling them to write the decision and one line of reason at the top of their working document, where their review partner will see it with the draft.

\## After the decision

Write the decision and one line of reason at the top of your working document; your review partner sees it with the draft. Then go to this week's project work. The target is the draft due at meeting 3: something a stranger can read start to end, with holes labelled.
