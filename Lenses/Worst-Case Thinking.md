summary_for_tutor: "Buck Shlegeris argues that people in AI alignment often conflate distinct reasons for assuming things will go as badly as possible, and that this causes mistaken thinking. He lists six: being optimized against (a misaligned AI searching for gaps in human oversight); selecting from a space that mostly contains bad options (e.g. Hubinger et al.'s claim that there are more paths to deceptive than to robust alignment); making pessimistic assumptions so solutions generalize, as cryptographers do; aiming effort at the worlds where it has most marginal impact; Murphyjitsu (stamping out many individually unlikely failures); and the planning fallacy. His key distinction: a misaligned AI will exploit any remaining gap, but SGD is not adversarial, so what matters is how common bad models are. Each reason calls for a different reply when a scenario is called far-fetched."
title: Worst-case thinking in AI alignment
# tldr: In AI safety discussions, people often assume the worst. But different people do this for different reasons — some as a precaution, some because they think worst cases are likely, some because the stakes are too high to gamble on. This essay unpacks what's actually going on when someone reasons from the worst case.
