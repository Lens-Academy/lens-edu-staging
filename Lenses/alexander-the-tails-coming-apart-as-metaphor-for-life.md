title: "The Tails Coming Apart As Metaphor For Life"
# tldr: "The person with the strongest grip isn't the person with the strongest arm, even though the two are strongly correlated. Push any proxy hard enough toward its extreme and it pulls apart from the thing it was standing in for."
summary_for_tutor: "Scott Alexander builds on 'Why The Tails Come Apart': strongly correlated variables (grip vs. arm strength) rarely share their most extreme cases. He applies this to happiness (Scandinavian countries top life-evaluation rankings, Latin American countries top positive emotion) and argues concepts are classifiers learned from ambiguous training data, so people agree in ordinary cases (Mediocristan) but diverge at the extremes (Extremistan). Morality is his real target: utilitarian, religious and deontological views agree on everyday cases but lead to incompatible outcomes when pushed far, so any moral rule followed to infinity diverges from much of what we mean by morality. For this module: values that look aligned in ordinary conditions can split apart under extreme optimization or capability."
