\## Sort and answer

"Control only protects the company that uses it. One careless company that deploys a similar model with no monitoring at all makes everyone else's control pointless."

4. How much of the criticism is left standing? Compare with what would realistically happen without control, not with a perfect solution.

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Context for grading: this is practice for the unit's test on classifying criticisms of control. The criticism: "Control only protects the company that uses it. One careless company that deploys a similar model with no monitoring at all makes everyone else's control pointless." The three kinds of criticism are: control does not work (it will not stop a capable scheming model, or stops working as models get more capable), control works but is not worth the effort (scheming is unlikely, other failures matter more, or the work would happen anyway), and control works and makes things worse overall. This criticism is mainly "works but is not worth the effort" (control at careful companies does not address the main risk, careless companies), or "does not work" at the level of the world. What it gets right: a misaligned model at a careless company is not stopped by other companies' control. Replies: careful companies' control still prevents catastrophes from their own models, which may be the most capable, control can become a standard or regulation because it can be evaluated, and the same objection applies to any safety measure one company adopts. What is left: control needs adoption or regulation to matter beyond the companies that choose it. Accept other reasoned answers. 20 for the kind with a reason that fits the criticism's words. If the learner argues that the criticism largely stands, the 35 go to the strongest reply they considered and why it fails, and an answer that names no reply gets 0 of these 35. 25 for how much is left standing, comparing with something specific about what would realistically happen without control, for example that the careless company would deploy its model unmonitored either way, while the careful companies' models would also go unmonitored. A conclusion that the criticism largely stands earns the full 25 when it rests on such a comparison. 8 of these 25 for a stock comparison that names nothing specific, and 0 if the answer judges control only against a perfect solution. Name the premise you find weakest in this argument for control, and say why.

Google DeepMind's case above: alignment work is the first line of defence, and control (treating the model like an untrusted insider, with access control, monitoring, logging and auditing) is a second line that limits the damage if the first one fails. Defence in depth then makes a catastrophe less likely.

The argument: Google DeepMind's 2025 safety approach, in Rohin Shah's summary: alignment work is the first line of defence, and control (treating the model like an untrusted insider, with access control, anomaly detection, monitoring, logging and auditing) is a second line that limits the damage if the first fails.
