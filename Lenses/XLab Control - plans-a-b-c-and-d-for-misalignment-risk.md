The whole lesson is Ryan Greenblatt's post 'Plans A, B, C, and D for misalignment risk', split into five Article excerpts with a recall question after each of Plans A, B, C and D, exactly where XLab places them. The taxonomy is the backbone of this module and the next lens ('Running the model') has the learner apply it, so hold the four plans precisely. Plan A: enough will for a strong international agreement that mostly eliminates race dynamics and allows slowing down for a reasonably long period (about 10 years), plus massive investment in security and safety; roughly 10 years of lead time to spend; Greenblatt puts 5% on it and expects 7% takeover risk in it. Plan B: the US government treats buying lead time for US AI companies as among its top few national security priorities, not necessarily out of misalignment concern, buying 1-3 years for misalignment work; less moonshot research, more prosaic handoff; 10% likely, 13% takeover risk. Plan C: no serious government involvement, but the leading company holds a 2-9 month lead and is willing and institutionally functional enough to spend much of it on misalignment; very fast, half-baked handoff with janky control; 25% likely, 20% takeover risk. Plan D: the company does not take misalignment seriously and takeoff is not slowed, but 10-30 people inside have enough buy-in for about 3% of compute; organisational control is mostly given up on and the safety team aims at extracting non-sabotaged research and evidence of risk; 45% likely, 45% takeover risk. Greenblatt adds a Plan E, where almost nobody inside is working on the risk, at 15% likely and 75% takeover risk, and notes you cannot have a technical Plan E because there is nobody to run it. He concludes he is inclined to focus on Plans C and D. If a learner asks which plan we are in, do not answer for them: ask what level of political will they observe and which of the four descriptions it matches."

\## Reading Assignment

**Read Ryan Greenblatt's "Plans A, B, C, and D for misalignment risk".**

The plans are ordered by how much political will there is to handle misalignment risk, from a strong international agreement down to a handful of people inside a company that is not really trying. Four recall questions sit inside the reading, one after each plan. Answer each from memory before reading on.

Plan A's target, keeping capabilities below the maximum controllable level, is the argument you met in [[../Lenses/XLab Control - the-case-for-controlling-powerful-ais|The case for ensuring that powerful AIs are controlled]].
