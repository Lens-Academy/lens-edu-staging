#### Text
content::
The affect labelling row of the comparison table rests partly on a theory of what emotions are. Lisa Feldman Barrett, a psychologist and neuroscientist, presents her theory of constructed emotion in this talk. She presents it as settled. It is not: basic emotion theories, which hold that some emotions are evolved responses, are still a live alternative ([van Heijst, Kret and Ploeger 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12065949/)). Watch the talk, then read the review after it.

#### Text
content::
A 2023 review in *Perspectives on Psychological Science* weighs Barrett's view against basic emotion theories. Read the abstract. "Tinbergen's four questions" are the questions biologists ask about any behaviour, which the review uses to sort the evidence: what causes it, how it develops, what it is for, and how it evolved.

#### Text
content::
In our reading, the advice that matters for this module does not depend on who is right: name what you feel as precisely as you can, and check whether a bad feeling has a bodily cause such as hunger or tiredness. Both can be tested on yourself.

#### Question: Open

content::
Barrett suggests asking, when you feel intense distress, whether it could have a purely physical cause. Think of a recent bad mood. How could you find out next time whether a mood like it has a physical cause?
feedback-instructions:: The learner considers Barrett's suggestion to check whether intense distress could have a purely physical cause (tiredness, hunger, dehydration, too little sleep), thinks of a recent bad mood, and says how they would find out next time. Respond to their example in one or two sentences. If their way of finding out is vague, suggest one concrete check (for example noting sleep and the last meal next to a mood rating for a week). Do not say Barrett's theory is right or wrong; if they ask, say the review they read finds the evidence limited on both sides. Do not suggest that serious low mood is only physical; if they describe lasting low mood, say it is worth taking to a doctor. 60 to 100 words. One reply.
