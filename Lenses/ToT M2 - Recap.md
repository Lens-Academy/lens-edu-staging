\## Unit 2 recap: putting the pieces together
You have finished Unit 2. It gave you two pairs of ideas to hold together. First, thinking itself (cognition) versus thinking about your thinking (metacognition). Second, offloading a task while the judgement stays with you, versus offloading the oversight itself so the judgement leaves.

Use those distinctions to answer the check below in your own words.

#### Question
content::
\## Chapter check
Two people explain how they use a tool.

Person A: "I use a calculator to add up my monthly budget. I know what addition means, I enter the numbers, and I check the total against what I expect."

Person B: "I ask an AI to plan my whole report: how to structure it, what to research, which parts to cut, and whether I should even do it. I don't really follow the reasoning, I just follow the plan."

In your own words, what is the difference between what Person A and Person B are doing? In which case does the judgement leave the person, and in which case does it stay? Give one further example of what this unit's reading calls "arbitrary offloading."
