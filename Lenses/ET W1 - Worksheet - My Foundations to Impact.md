#### Text

This worksheet is designed to help you explore and clarify the foundations of your impactful thesis and emerging career path. content::
\## Part 1: Exploring Your Ikigai

Take 10–15 minutes to reflect honestly on the questions below. There are no “correct” answers - the goal is awareness, not optimisation! Remember, your thesis doesn’t need to hit the perfect overlap in all four areas, but exploring where it does (and where it doesn’t) gives you insight into crafting your Ikigai and how you could experiment with this in your thesis journey.

#### Question: Open
id:: 185bcbb9-380c-4afc-9a69-3461dd4abb73
content::
**1. What you love**

- When do I feel absorbed, alive, or excited while working on something?
- Which problems energise you enough to dedicate hundreds of hours to them?
- What kind of research, projects, or problems naturally hold my attention?

#### Question: Open
id:: 44f181e5-6f8e-492f-8764-bac3715d52e9
content::
**2. What you’re good at**

- What do I naturally excel at?
- What do others come to me for help with?
- What feels effortful for others but energising for me?
- Which skills can you bring, refine, or develop through your thesis?

#### Question: Open
id:: 4b4e48e0-7622-45e5-8360-66a506de6938
content::
**3. What the world needs**

- Who do I want to help and benefit from my research or insights?
- If I could improve one thing about the world, what would it be?
- What problems are important and currently overlooked?

#### Question: Open
id:: af67ca91-292c-48c4-ba4e-4437720febc8
content::
**4. What can sustain me (i.e., career path)**

- Which career pathways or roles could realistically support me while allowing impact?
- How could this project connect to future career paths or opportunities?
- What kinds of work environments might I enjoy?

\#### 2b. What career paths may fit this Ikigai?

Here, explore drafting a few career hypotheses that you are interested in, and that may fit with your draft of your Ikigai. For each, reflect on how this path aligns with your Ikigai and how your thesis may be able to test this out. For the last column, think about:

- **Thesis topic:** What specific thesis topic would give you insight into what it may take to meaningfully contribute to this problem area in the long term?
- **Collaboration & mentorship:** Who could you collaborate with or seek feedback from to learn more about this career or problem area?
- **Testing fit:** How will your thesis experience help you explore whether you enjoy this type of work and whether it aligns with your sense of meaning? How could your thesis help you learn what working in this path realistically involves?
- **Skill development:** What skills or methods used in this field could you deliberately practice through your thesis?

Before listing out your career hypotheses, we recommend reviewing the list of impactful career paths in Week 1’s reading. Pick a few resources to look through, especially paths you may not have yet considered!

\### Closing Note

Thank you so much for your time in reflecting on and exploring your Ikigai.

Remember: your Ikigai isn’t something you simply “find” - it’s created and cultivated through reflection, experimentation, and action. This framework is designed to help you make sure that your thesis isn’t just a project to complete, but a tool for learning about yourself, testing your fit, and exploring the problems and roles that matter to you. By intentionally connecting your work to your emerging Ikigai, you’re setting up a thesis and a career path that is meaningful, energising, and impactful.
