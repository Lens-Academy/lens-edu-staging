\## Bonus: Connecting Skill Atrophy to Gradual Disempowerment

This is an optional deep-dive for learners who want to explore the philosophical connection between individual skill atrophy and societal gradual disempowerment.

So far, you have seen evidence that individual skills can atrophy when offloaded to AI. And you have seen the gradual disempowerment thesis: humanity could slowly lose influence over economic, political, and cultural systems as AI becomes embedded.

What connects these two levels? Joshua Krook (2025) argues that the missing link is autonomy.

Krook's central claim: The biggest threat is not that machines become more like humans, but that humans become more like machines—losing the capacity for independent decision-making, creativity, and leadership.

Read the excerpts below. Pay attention to three key moves:
1. The conservatorship analogy: Even a benevolent guardian making objectively better decisions constitutes a real loss of autonomy
2. The tipping point: When AI outcompetes humans, it becomes "illogical" to rely on human judgement
3. Why this is not an alignment problem: Even well-aligned AI causes this outcome

\## Question 1: The Conservatorship Analogy

Krook uses legal conservatorship (with the Britney Spears case as an example) to illustrate autonomy loss. Even when a guardian makes objectively better decisions for someone, it still constitutes a real loss of autonomy and personhood.

How does this apply to AI? If an AI system consistently makes better medical diagnoses, legal judgments, or financial decisions than humans, what is lost—even if the AI is benevolent and well-aligned?

assessment-instructions:: The student is exploring Krook's conservatorship analogy and its application to AI autonomy loss. This is an optional bonus discussion, not assessed.

Key concepts to draw out:
- Autonomy as valuable independent of outcomes
- The distinction between "better decisions" and "my decisions"
- Why autonomy matters even when AI is trustworthy
- Connection to gradual disempowerment: if humans cannot function independently, they cannot govern or oversee AI systems

Conversation style: Socratic. Ask the student to articulate what autonomy means to them. Draw connections to the skill atrophy evidence: if skills have atrophied, autonomy is already diminished. After 3-4 exchanges, move to Question 2.

Response length: 120-200 words per reply.

\## Question 2: The Tipping Point

Krook argues that a tipping point will be reached where it becomes "illogical" to rely on human decision-making, creativity, social care, or leadership. This is not because humans become worse—their skills may stay the same—but because AI becomes so much better.

Think about a skill you have already offloaded to AI or technology. Now imagine AI becomes 10x better than humans at that task, across all domains. What would it mean to insist on human judgement then? Would it be rational—or stubborn?

assessment-instructions:: The student is engaging with Krook's tipping point argument. This explores the tension between rationality and autonomy.

Key tensions to surface:
- Rational choice: if AI is better, using it is logical
- Autonomy: if we always defer, we stop developing independent capacity
- The irreversibility problem: once skills atrophy, they may not return
- Structural dependence: not choosing to defer, but having no alternative

Conversation style: Socratic. Help the student articulate the tension. There is no right answer—the goal is to feel the problem. After 3-4 exchanges, move to Question 3.

Response length: 120-200 words per reply.

\## Question 3: The Philosophical Bridge

Krook's argument connects individual skill atrophy to gradual disempowerment at the societal level. His claim: skill atrophy creates structural dependence, and structural dependence makes gradual disempowerment inevitable—not through malice, but through local rationality.

Do you think skill atrophy at the individual level makes gradual disempowerment at the societal level more likely? Why or why not?

assessment-instructions:: The student is evaluating the philosophical bridge between skill atrophy and gradual disempowerment. This is the synthesis question for this bonus lens.

Key connections to draw out:
- Individual skill atrophy → population cannot function independently
- Cannot function independently → cannot govern or oversee AI systems
- Cannot oversee → institutions stop depending on humans
- Not a rebellion scenario: happens because each step is locally rational
- Alignment is not sufficient: even well-aligned AI causes this outcome

Conversation style: Socratic. Help the student articulate whether they find the bridge convincing. Draw on their earlier answers. After 3-4 exchanges, provide a calibration summary.

Response length: 120-200 words per reply.

Calibration summary (on close): Name what the student demonstrated (engagement with philosophical argument, ability to trace connections, comfort with uncertainty). Note what remains to explore. Close warmly: "The tools will change. The question of what autonomy means, and why it matters, stays with us."
