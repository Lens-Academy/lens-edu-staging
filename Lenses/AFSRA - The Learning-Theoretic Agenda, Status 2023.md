summary_for_tutor: 'Vanessa Kosoy''s 2023 overview of the learning-theoretic agenda (LTA). Philosophy: the goal is a general mathematical theory of intelligent agents, needed because it lets us prove designs aligned under stated assumptions, because empirical data alone does not tell us how results extrapolate to new domains, scales and architectures, and because the theory gives better intuitions and vocabulary (like "heat" and "entropy" for engines). Any fundamental question about intelligent agency is relevant to alignment: for any aspect of agency we do not understand, an AI we design either lacks it and is probably too weak to protect humanity, or has it without our understanding, and is probably unaligned because alignment is a small target. The agenda starts from AIXI and lists key problems: computational resource constraints, frequentist guarantees (traps, nonrealizability, where the true environment is not among the agent''s hypotheses), value ontology, Cartesian privilege, descriptive agent theory, then many research directions (infra-Bayesian RL, ADAM, infra-Bayesian physicalism and more). The final part describes Physicalist Superimitation (formerly Pre-DCA), an alignment protocol that detects agents, identifies the user and imitates an improved version of the user. The question asks the student to apply the philosophy section''s argument to a funder who only wants subproblems with an obvious link to alignment.'
