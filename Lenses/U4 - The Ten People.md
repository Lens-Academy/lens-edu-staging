#### Text
content::
\## Before you read

The list of plans that opened this unit got shorter as political will fell. This
reading describes the bottom of that list. No treaty arrives. No government acts. The
company will not slow down. Its leadership treats misalignment risk as something to
mention, not something to solve.

What remains is ten people inside the company who take the risk seriously. Buck
Shlegeris asks what those ten should do with the little they have. Not how to fix the
situation. How to buy risk reduction at the margin, on a small budget, without getting
fired.

Read it as an inventory, not a recommendation. It is not an argument that insiders are
enough. It is also not an argument for giving up on treaties and governments. It is a
count of the influence that remains after those larger plans have failed. Before he
counts it, count it yourself.

*The framing and questions on this page were written by Claude, an AI, and reviewed by a human. The reading itself is the author's own work.*

#### Question
content::
\## Your turn first

You are one of ten people inside a leading AI lab who take misalignment risk seriously.
The rest of the company does not. The company will not slow down. You have almost no
budget and no authority. You have only the influence that ten insiders can gather.

Write two or three sentences naming the two things you would spend that influence on,
and why those two.

Then write one more line. This one is harder. If leadership turned against your team,
which of the two survives?

assessment-instructions::
CONTEXT YOU NEED. You do not have the rest of this course, so here is the situation.

This is a middle lens of Unit 4 in a course about AI futures, written for newcomers. The
unit is about rival strategies for reducing AI risk. The student has NOT read the
article yet. It is Buck Shlegeris's "Ten people on the inside" and it is in your
context. The student was asked to imagine being one of ten safety-minded insiders at a
lab that will not slow down, to name the two things they would spend their limited
influence on, and to say which of the two survives if leadership turns against them.

The purpose is that they discover their plan spends a resource they never named. The
purpose is not for them to reproduce the article. Do not deliver its answer.

WHAT SHLEGERIS ARGUES. For your reference only. Do not deliver it.
- The scenario is his "rushed unreasonable developer" regime: the company pays lip
  service to AI risk in general but does not take misalignment risk seriously, and the
  ten concerned people have free rein only because the company is a do-ocracy.
- Their options are the same as a responsible developer's: build concrete evidence of
  risk, implement safety measures, or do alignment research aimed at a model you could
  defer to.
- The binding constraints are political, not technical. The ten have little political
  capital; all they can do is make it easier to let them work than to fire them. So
  their measures must be cheap (he imagines roughly 3% of the company's compute) and
  easy to comply with: anything that makes a colleague's workflow harder gets rolled
  back, so complexity has to stay inside their own team.
- He concludes that ten is much better than zero, that outsiders should plan to
  force-multiply insiders with exportable research, and that low-budget safety
  techniques are worth developing now.
- He does NOT argue that this is a good situation or a sufficient plan. He calls it
  scarily plausible and worth planning for.

YOUR TASK, in order.
1. Name the resource each of their two picks is implicitly spending: trust, access, veto
   power, or information. Use their own words to do it. If a pick spends a resource the
   scenario never gave them, such as formal authority or a large budget, say so plainly.
2. Ask one question about their survival line: what has to stay true for that pick to
   keep working once leadership is against them, and who inside the company has to keep
   tolerating it?
3. If they answered that neither survives, treat that as a finding, not a failure, and
   ask what that says about where their influence actually lives.

Do not grade. Do not score. Do not praise. There is no correct answer at this stage, and
a student who concludes the ten people are nearly powerless has done the exercise
properly.

Write 60 to 110 words. Short paragraphs. No lists.
