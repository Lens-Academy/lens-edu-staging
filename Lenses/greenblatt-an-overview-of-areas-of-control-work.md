tldr: "If you wanted to stop a powerful but possibly scheming AI from causing catastrophe, where would you actually start? Greenblatt maps the whole landscape of AI control work: eight areas, from building evaluation settings that pit red teams against blue teams, through software infrastructure and human review processes, to conceptual research, roughly ordered by how much investment each one deserves."
summary_for_tutor: "Ryan Greenblatt's survey of promising areas of AI control research and implementation, listed roughly in descending order of recommended investment: (1) developing and using settings for control evaluations, including red-team/blue-team technique iteration; (2) better understanding control-relevant capabilities and model properties; (3) developing specific countermeasures in isolation; (4) running control experiments on actual current AI usage; (5) prototyping software infrastructure for control and relevant security measures; (6) prototyping human processes for control; (7) conceptual and other non-empirical research; and (8) near-term applications that build practice for later control. For each area it details concrete project ideas and bottlenecks, emphasizing that the shortage of good control-evaluation settings (especially for diffuse, non-concentrated failures like research sabotage) is a key constraint and that much current work is valuable mainly as preparation for controlling more capable future systems."
