#### Text
content::
This week you practised three techniques on your own bugs:
- **Goal factoring:** take an action apart into what it is for, and look for a cheaper way to get all of it.
- **Aversion factoring:** split something you avoid into its parts, fix the parts that are about the situation, and recalibrate the reactions you would rather not have.
- **Units of Exchange:** count everything a trade costs and earns, and fix the rates that don't match your other choices.

A technique you practised once in a lens is not yet a technique you use. The test is whether it changes something in your week. Pick **one** technique and **one** real bug, and plan a small experiment. It may be one of the plans you already wrote in the practices:

A good experiment is small enough that you will actually do it, and specific about when. It also says what you would see if it worked: a task done, an hour back, a conversation had, a feeling that changed. Next week opens by asking what happened, so plan something you can report on.

The techniques are starting points. If one didn't fit you, you can adapt it, or test a version of your own. Anna Salamon, one of CFAR's founders, advises remembering that the buck stops with you, and to "check whether it is producing fruits you directly feel good about".[^cite-salamon-2025]

#### Question: Open
id:: 32b8773a-d576-40ce-b249-fd18b2781ec3
content::
Your experiment for this week:

1. Which technique, and which bug?

2. What exactly will you do, and when? Name a day or a moment.

3. How will you know it worked? What would you see change on the bug?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is committing to one small real-life experiment for the coming week, using one technique from week 3 (Goal Factoring, Aversion Factoring or Units of Exchange, or their own adapted version) on a real problem from their bugs list. This is a plan, not a test.

Your job is to check the plan's quality in one turn, then stop. Reply in 50 to 100 words, no lists.
- If the "when" is vague ("this week", "at some point"), ask for a day or a trigger moment.
- If the "how will I know" is about feelings about the technique ("I'll see if I like it"), ask what they would see change on the bug itself.
- If the plan is large, suggest the smallest version that would still tell them something.
- If they adapted a technique or chose to test something of their own, treat that as legitimate.
- Make at most one suggestion. Do not use guilt, pressure or AI-safety stakes. No generic praise.
Close by saying that next week starts by asking how it went, and that "it didn't happen, and here's why" is a useful report too.
