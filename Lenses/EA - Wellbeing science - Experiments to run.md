content::
Positive psychology's exercises are short written tasks with exact instructions, so you can copy them and test them on yourself. Below are five that were tested in trials, each with the instructions from the source, the dose and what the study found. Module 3 covers how to design a test on yourself. This lens gives what you need to apply it here.

\## Measuring yourself

Take a **baseline**, a measurement before you start that later results are compared with. Two free scales by Ed Diener's group:

- **Scale of Positive and Negative Experience (SPANE).** Think about the past four weeks and rate how often you felt each of twelve feelings, from 1 (very rarely or never) to 5 (very often or always): positive, negative, good, bad, pleasant, unpleasant, happy, sad, afraid, joyful, angry, contented. Add the six positive words for your positive score (6 to 30) and the six negative words for your negative score (6 to 30). Positive minus negative is your balance, from -24 to 24 ([SPANE with scoring](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/Documents/Scale%20of%20Positive%20and%20Negative%20Experience.pdf)).
- **Satisfaction With Life Scale.** Five statements, such as "In most ways my life is close to my ideal", each rated from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree), for a total from 5 to 35 ([SWLS with score bands](https://eddiener.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SWLS-Scale.pdf)).

Diener's group lets anyone use both for free for non-commercial purposes, with credit to the authors ([Diener's scales page](https://eddiener.com/scales/)). Because SPANE asks about four weeks, take it again four weeks after you start. If you want a daily measure as well, one option, which is our own adaptation and not a validated scale, is to rate the same twelve SPANE words each evening for that day only.

\## Five exercises

| Exercise | Instructions (from the source) | Dose | What the trial found |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three good things** | "Write down three things that went well each day and their causes every night for one week", giving a causal explanation for each ([Seligman, Steen, Park and Peterson 2005](https://wholebeinginstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/Positive-Psychology-Progress-Seligman-Steen-Park-Peterson-2005.pdf)). Detailed steps: [Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/three-good-things) | About 10 minutes each night for at least a week ([Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/three-good-things)) | In the 2005 trial (577 adults recruited through the website of Seligman's book), participants were happier and less depressed from one month to six months later. Those who kept doing the exercise after the week benefited most ([Seligman et al. 2005](https://wholebeinginstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/Positive-Psychology-Progress-Seligman-Steen-Park-Peterson-2005.pdf)). A replication found lasting gains in happiness, but a "positive placebo" exercise did as well, and depression fell no more than in the control group ([Mongrain and Anselmo-Matthews 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.21839)) |
| **Gratitude visit** | Within a week, "write and then deliver a letter of gratitude in person to someone who had been especially kind to them but had never been properly thanked" ([Seligman et al. 2005](https://wholebeinginstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/Positive-Psychology-Progress-Seligman-Steen-Park-Peterson-2005.pdf)). How to write and read the letter: [Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/gratitude_letter) | One letter. About 10 minutes to write and at least 30 minutes for the visit ([Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/gratitude_letter)) | The largest boost in the 2005 trial, lasting one week and one month, gone by three months ([Seligman et al. 2005](https://wholebeinginstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/Positive-Psychology-Progress-Seligman-Steen-Park-Peterson-2005.pdf)) |
| **Best possible self** | Imagine your future life at its best in all relevant areas (career, relationships, hobbies, health), then write continuously about it. Be specific, for example exactly what you would do in a new job, with whom and where ([Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/best_possible_self)) | 15 minutes a day for two weeks ([Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/best_possible_self)) | Students who did it daily for two weeks had more positive emotions right afterwards (Sheldon and Lyubomirsky 2006, as summarised by [Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/best_possible_self)) |
| **Act extraverted for a week** | "For the next 7 days, try to act as talkative, assertive, and spontaneous as you can." First list five specific ways and times you will do it ([Margolis and Lyubomirsky 2020](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Margolis-Lyubomirsky-2020.pdf)) | One week | Wellbeing rose in the week people acted extraverted and fell in the week they acted introverted ([Margolis and Lyubomirsky 2020](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Margolis-Lyubomirsky-2020.pdf)) |
| **Treat a cashier as a person** | When you buy a coffee, have a genuine interaction with the cashier (smile, make eye contact, have a brief conversation) instead of being as efficient as possible ([Sandstrom and Dunn 2014](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/15301/)) | One purchase | People who did this had more positive affect afterwards than people told to be efficient, and felt more belonging ([Sandstrom and Dunn 2014](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/15301/)) |

Of these, gratitude and social exercises (talking to strangers, acting extraverted) are the ones with support from large preregistered experiments in Folk and Dunn's review, which you met in the previous lens.

\## Points of design from the trials

- **Use a comparison week.** In the 2005 trial, the control exercise (writing about early memories every night for a week) also made people happier and less depressed right after the week. Only from one week later did the control group fall back to where it started ([Seligman et al. 2005](https://wholebeinginstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/Positive-Psychology-Progress-Seligman-Steen-Park-Peterson-2005.pdf)). So a week of a neutral writing task like that is a fair comparison for any of these exercises.
- **Expect your expectations to matter.** You know you are doing a happiness exercise and you may want it to work. Sheldon and Lyubomirsky report that only people who signed up for a "happiness" study got happier from the exercises, and argue that buy-in is part of how they work ([Sheldon and Lyubomirsky 2021](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Sheldon-Lyubomirsky-2021.pdf)). Critics would call part of the same effect **placebo**: improvement that comes from expecting to improve rather than from the exercise itself. Write down your prediction before you start.
- **Check what happens when you stop.** In the 2005 trial, long-term benefit went with continuing the exercise on one's own. So measure again a few weeks after you stop.
- **One exercise at a time.** If you change several things at once, you cannot tell which one did it.
- **Be ready for a small or no signal.** In one study built like a test on yourself, each participant did three good things, a strengths exercise and the gratitude visit in random order over 9 to 10 weeks and rated their feelings every day. The exercises did not reliably change daily positive feelings ([Woodworth, O'Brien-Malone, Diamond and Schüz 2016](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6225023)).

These scales measure how often you feel good or bad and how you judge your life. None of them measures directly whether a calm state holds up when circumstances get worse. If that is what you care about, note in your log how you were on your hardest days.

These exercises were tested in people without a diagnosed condition. They are not a treatment. If your scores stay low for weeks or you feel depressed, Diener's advice from the first lens applies: prolonged serious unhappiness may need help from a professional.

The next questions build a protocol for one exercise, one piece at a time. First: which exercise will you test, and at what dose and for how long?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner picks one positive psychology exercise from this lens and states dose and duration. The sourced doses: three good things (about 10 minutes every night for at least one week), gratitude visit (one letter, about 10 minutes to write, at least 30 minutes to read it in person), best possible self (15 minutes a day for two weeks), acting extraverted (one week, after listing five specific ways), treating a cashier as a person (one purchase, repeatable). Check that the dose matches the source, and if not, say what the source used. If they choose the gratitude visit, mention that its effect in the 2005 trial was gone by three months. Do not tell them which exercise to choose or whether it will work. 40 to 80 words. One reply. No generic praise.

force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner says what they will compare their chosen positive psychology exercise with. The lens recommends a comparison period, for example a week of a neutral writing task such as the 2005 trial's control (writing about early memories every night for a week), which itself raised happiness right after the week.
