summary_for_tutor: "A fictional Socratic dialogue by Eliezer Yudkowsky that works as an allegory for AI alignment. Skeptical Alfonso presses Beth of the Mathematics of Intentional Rocketry Institute (a stand-in for MIRI) on why her institute does abstract math rather than practical spaceplane engineering or dramatic 'malevolent celestial spirit' scenarios. Beth argues the real, overlooked problem is that nobody yet knows how to aim a rocket at a prespecified destination like the Moon -- pointing the nose at it will not get it there -- and that current designs are not almost-right but conceptually far from a solution. She explains why MIRI tackles deliberately simplified, crisply stated foundational problems (stable orbits / 'tiling positions', and 'logical undiscreteness', going from acceleration functions to position functions) to become less confused, rather than giving premature engineering advice or chasing impossible mathematical certainty. The rocket-targeting problem maps onto alignment: reliably steering a powerful AI toward an intended goal."
