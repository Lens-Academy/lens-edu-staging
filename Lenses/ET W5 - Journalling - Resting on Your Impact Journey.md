\## Resting on Your Impact Journey

For this week’s journaling, as we’re at the mid-point of the program - we wanted to introduce the concept of different types of rest. This matters for sustainable impact specifically: a thesis, a career, or a life spent contributing to something meaningful is a long game, not a sprint, and it's much easier to sustain when you're not quietly compromising on what you truly need!

Rest is often reduced to just sleep, but sleep is only one piece of what actually restores you. Physician and researcher Dr. Saundra Dalton-Smith identified seven distinct types of rest, each addressing a different kind of depletion, and found that many people can sleep a full eight hours and still wake up exhausted, simply because the kind of tired they're carrying isn't the kind sleep fixes.

**The seven types are:**

- **Physical rest** - both passive (sleep, napping) and active (stretching, a gentle walk, anything that releases tension in the body)
- **Mental rest** - giving your mind a break from constant analysis and decision-making
- **Emotional rest** - space to express how you actually feel, without needing to manage it for someone else
- **Social rest** - relationships that replenish you, as distinct from the ones (even good ones) that take effort to maintain
- **Sensory rest** - a break from screens, notifications, and overstimulation
- **Creative rest** - exposure to beauty or inspiration that isn't about producing anything
- **Spiritual rest** - a sense of connection to something larger than yourself, meaning, purpose, or belonging

If you're depleted in one area, more of a different kind of rest usually won't fix it. For example, someone that is lacking social rest won't feel restored by more sleep, and someone overloaded on sensory input won't feel restored by socialising more.

This week, we encourage you to notice what types of rest you are actually craving and needing, and to see if you can experiment with adding some moments of rest into your week!

Counterintuitive as it might sound in a program all about impact: rest isn't the thing competing with your impact, it's often what makes more of it possible. Depleted people often burn out, make worse decisions, and quietly disengage from the very work they care about. Genuinely resting isn't time taken away from your impact, but a key part of how you sustain it!
