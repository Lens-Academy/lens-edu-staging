#### Text
content::
Each experiment below tests a claim from this module on yourself, using the researchers' own instructions. Module 3 showed how to do this: take a baseline, fix the dose, decide what you will measure before you start. Where we add our own suggestion, the text says so.

For two of the experiments the measure is the **Self-Compassion Scale Short Form**, Neff's 12-item questionnaire. Neff gives permission to use it for any purpose, and the scoring key is on the same page ([SCS-SF](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/SCS-SF-information.pdf)). If you read the optional page on the dispute over the scale, also score its kind items (2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10) and its unkind items (1, 4, 8, 9, 11, 12) separately, so you can see which half moves.

Pick one experiment, or two that do not overlap in time.

#### Callout: 1. The self-compassion break
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** Neff: responding to a stressful moment with mindfulness, common humanity and kindness toward yourself helps you cope better than self-criticism ([Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/self_compassion_break)). The break was one of the practices in the eight-week Mindful Self-Compassion course, whose first trial is on the evidence page.

**What to do.** Here are the instructions:

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/berkeley-self-compassion-break]]
from:: Think of a situation in your life that is difficult and is causing you stress.
to:: “May I be patient.”

#### Text
content::
**Dose.** About 5 minutes. The source suggests at least once a week to start and lists it as once a day ([Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/self_compassion_break)). We suggest once a day for two weeks, plus whenever a real difficulty comes up.

**Measure.** Fill in the Self-Compassion Scale Short Form before day 1 and after day 14. Each time you do the break, note before and after how distressed you feel about the situation, from 0 to 10 (our suggestion).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 2. A week of self-compassionate letters
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** Writing to yourself with kindness about something you dislike in yourself makes you happier and less depressed, and the effect lasts.

**What to do.**

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/berkeley-self-compassionate-letter]]
from:: First, think of something about yourself that makes you feel mildly ashamed
to:: as a reminder to be more self-compassionate.

#### Text
content::
**Dose.** 5 to 15 minutes a day for one week ([Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/self_compassionate_letter)). This is the dose of the study the page cites: people who wrote such a letter every day for a week reported less depression and more happiness three months later than people who wrote about early memories, and their gain in happiness held at six months ([Shapira and Mongrain 2010](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17439760.2010.516763)). The same page reports a study where it did not go well: Hong Kong students who wrote three letters reported more negative emotion and no drop in depression ([Wong and Mak 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/aap0000041)).

**Measure.** Before day 1, after day 7 and again a month later, fill in the Self-Compassion Scale Short Form. Also rate, from 0 to 10, how bad you feel about the thing you wrote about (our suggestion). A month later is our suggestion. The study measured at three and six months.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 3. Does kindness make you lazy?
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** The most common fear about self-compassion is that it will undermine the motivation to improve. Neff says it does the opposite ([Neff 2023](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf)). Gilbert says the same about self-criticism, which he calls focused on punishing past errors rather than on improving ([Gilbert 2010](https://www.getselfhelp.co.uk/docs/gilbert-compassion-handout.pdf), page 55).

**The study behind it.** Juliana Breines and Serena Chen asked students to write for 3 minutes about their biggest weakness. One group got this prompt: "Imagine that you are talking to yourself about this weakness from a compassionate and understanding perspective. What would you say?" A control group got: "Imagine that you are talking to yourself about this weakness from a perspective of validating your positive (rather than negative) qualities." Their summary of all four experiments:

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/breines-self-compassion-increases-self-improvement-motivation]]
from:: In four experiments, the authors examined the hypothesis
to:: may make people more motivated to improve themselves.

#### Text
content::
**Incremental beliefs** here means seeing the weakness as something you can change. The experiments were small, about 70 to 90 people each, and three of the four used students at one university. Some of the differences from the self-esteem group were only marginal ([Breines and Chen 2012](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/publications/selfimp.motivation.pdf)).

**What to do.** Our own design, built on their two prompts. For the next three weeks, each time you notice a real setback or a weakness that bothers you, toss a coin. Heads: write for 3 minutes using the compassion prompt. Tails: write for 3 minutes using the self-esteem prompt.

**Measure.** Right after writing, rate from 0 to 10 how much you want to work on the problem. The next day, write down what you actually did about it, if anything. After three weeks, compare the heads entries with the tails entries. The coin matters: it stops you from choosing the kind prompt on the days you already feel better.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: 4. Soothing rhythm breathing and the three systems
tone:: blue
#### Text
content::
**Claim.** Gilbert: the threat system is easy to trigger and hard to calm, and practices that engage the soothing system can rebalance the three systems ([Gilbert 2010](https://www.getselfhelp.co.uk/docs/gilbert-compassion-handout.pdf), pages 16 to 19 and 28). His first exercise is soothing rhythm breathing.

**What to do.** Follow his Exercise 1 on pages 37 and 38 of the handout. Sit with both feet on the floor. Let the breath come down to the diaphragm. Find a slightly slower and deeper rhythm, about 3 seconds in, a short pause, about 3 seconds out, smooth and even. When your mind wanders, notice it and bring your attention back kindly. He stresses that you are not trying to relax or to empty your mind ([same source](https://www.getselfhelp.co.uk/docs/gilbert-compassion-handout.pdf)).

**Dose.** Gilbert sets no length ("for as long as we wish"). We suggest 5 minutes a day for two weeks.

**Measure.** Before and after each session, rate from 0 to 10 how much you feel each of the three: threat (anxious, angry, on guard), drive (wanting, striving, excited) and soothing (content, safe, not wanting). This is our simplification of Gilbert's diagram. A 2026 study had 88 adults rate the three systems on a digital scale while their heart rate variability was recorded, and found that the ratings moved as the model predicts across rest, rumination and recovery ([University of Queensland summary](https://learning-lab.uq.edu.au/article/2026/06/new-research-puts-physiology-behind-three-circles-compassion)).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: If it goes badly
tone:: amber
#### Text
content::
Gilbert writes that for very self-critical people, kindness toward themselves can at first feel strange or threatening, and that some people find focusing on the breath makes them more anxious. His advice is to do only what helps and to try other exercises if one does not ([Gilbert 2010](https://www.getselfhelp.co.uk/docs/gilbert-compassion-handout.pdf), pages 28 and 37). If a practice brings up strong distress, stop it and talk to someone you trust or a professional. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, contact a doctor or a crisis line.
#### End Callout

feedback-instructions:: The learner read four experiment protocols from the self-compassion module: (1) the self-compassion break once a day for two weeks, with the Self-Compassion Scale Short Form before and after and a 0 to 10 distress rating around each break; (2) the self-compassionate letter daily for a week, with the short form before, after and a month later; Shapira and Mongrain 2010 found benefits at three and six months, Wong and Mak 2016 found more negative emotion in Hong Kong students; (3) a coin-flip comparison of Breines and Chen's compassion prompt and self-esteem prompt after real setbacks over three weeks, rating wish to improve and recording what they did next; (4) Gilbert's soothing rhythm breathing 5 minutes a day for two weeks, rating threat, drive and soothing from 0 to 10 before and after. They now write their own protocol.

Check the protocol for six parts: the claim, the practice, the dose (frequency and duration), the measure and its timing, a result that would count against the claim, and a stopping rule. Name the parts that are present in one sentence, then name the most important missing or vague part and suggest a concrete fix from the page (for example "fill in the short form before day 1 and after day 14"). If there is no baseline, say that a before-measurement is needed to see a change. If they expect self-compassion to work, mention that expectation alone can move self-ratings, and that experiment 3's coin flip is one way to control for it. Reply in 90 to 150 words. No generic praise. At most two replies.
