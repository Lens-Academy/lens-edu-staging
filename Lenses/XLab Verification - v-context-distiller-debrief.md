#### Question: Open
id:: e5ec7bb4-41a4-4706-9ec1-37c9cb23d770
content:: After you delivered: did any reader question go unanswered? If one did, name it, name the passage you left in the pool that would have answered it, and say what it would have changed for that reader. If every question was answered, say so. Either way, say what your clipping rule would be for the next report.
feedback-instructions:: Check their answer against their widget state. If every reader question was answered, confirm it plainly and do not look for a flaw that is not there; respond to their clipping rule. If a question went unanswered and they named it, say whether the passage they named would have answered it, and if they missed it, name the passage that would have. If the learner named a gap that is not in their widget state, say which questions actually went red. If their rule is "clip more", point at the notebook cap and ask which clip they would drop. Close on the one thing they would carry into the next report. Two to four sentences, no praise. An example of a strong answer when a question went unanswered: "The business reporter's question, what shows deepening commitment rather than a stumble, went unanswered. I left Order ¶14 in the pool: the three-year Strategic Cooperation Agreement that named Seagate Huawei's strategic supplier, signed months into the violations while competitors had stopped selling. With it, the reporter could show that Seagate chose this path rather than stumbling into it. Next time I would list each reader's questions before clipping and clip only what answers one of them."
