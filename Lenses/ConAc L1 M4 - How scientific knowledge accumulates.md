#### Text
content::
One of the difficulties in technical AI safety is establishing a sufficiently productive relationship between theoretical and empirical research.

On the theoretical side, researchers have developed mathematical frameworks for reasoning about intelligence, learning, agency and optimisation. However, the number and specificity of the predictions these frameworks allow us to make about state-of-the-art AI systems can fall considerably short of what we would need to establish their safety.

On the empirical side, researchers are accumulating a growing body of observations about the behaviour and internal structure of contemporary AI systems. Some of these observations are difficult to situate within a broader explanatory framework. It can remain unclear why a particular measured quantity is causally relevant to a proposed failure mode, whether an observed regularity reflects a general property of learning systems, and under what conditions we should expect it to persist.

Different research agendas embody different expectations about how these difficulties might eventually be overcome.

#### Callout: Guiding questions
#### Text
content::
We will examine four examples.

- From abstract theory towards practical solutions: The Learning-Theoretic Agenda
- From empirical observations towards general explanations: Emergent misalignment
- Developing theory and empirical science together: Developmental interpretability
- Making the relationship between theory and practice a research problem: UK AISI research agenda

As you read them, try to identify:

- What determines the authors' choice of research questions?
- What kinds of knowledge do they expect their research to produce?
- How do they expect this knowledge to accumulate?
- How do they intend to establish its relevance to future AI systems?
- What assumptions does this vision of scientific progress depend on?
