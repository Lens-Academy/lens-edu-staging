content::
The same parts as for your first experiment, for the third one you chose. If it will run at the same time as another, say so in the design question, and choose a main measure the other experiments do not also move.

#### Question: Open

content::
**The claim.** Which claim does your third experiment test, in the words of the person who makes it?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for the third experiment they chose in module 8, one part per question. This part asks for the claim and who makes it. In one or two sentences: confirm what they wrote, and if the source or the claim itself is missing or vague (for example "meditation helps"), ask for it with one concrete suggestion, such as the claim on the experiment's page. 20 to 50 words. Do not judge whether the claim is true, no generic praise. One turn.

content::
**Dose.** How much will you do on each day you practise: what, for how many minutes, how many times?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for the dose per day. In two or three sentences: confirm it, and check where it comes from. The experiments pages say which doses come from the source (for example Thanissaro's 20 minutes, Brasington's at least 45 minutes, MBSR's 45 to 60 minutes, Greater Good in Action's 15 minutes for best possible self) and which are the course's own suggestion. If they lowered the source's dose, say that a null result then says less about the claim. If the dose is an hour a day or more, or intensive noting or jhana practice, mention in one sentence that the stop rule later on this page matters more. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

content::
**Duration.** For how many days or weeks will you run it?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for the duration. In one or two sentences: check that the duration is long enough for the effect the source claims (minutes for a single labelling or a self-compassion break, a week or two for a daily exercise, eight weeks for MBSR, months or longer for claims like fundamental wellbeing, where a short test says little). If it is too short, say so and suggest either a longer run or a smaller claim to test. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for a prediction of the form "if I do X for Y, Z will change by about W". In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if a part is missing (most often W, the size), ask for it with one concrete suggestion. Do not say whether the prediction is realistic. 20 to 50 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 26d30c1d-4ee7-49dd-bb53-3a2949cf3573
content::
**Your measure.** What is the one main measure you will take, chosen now?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for one main measure chosen in advance. In one or two sentences: check that it is the same number taken the same way and at the same time each time (a daily 0 to 10 rating, a count, a recovery time, a scale such as WHO-5, SPANE, the Satisfaction with Life Scale, EQUA-S, NAS-7 or NADA-S), and that its timing fits the effect: a same-day effect needs a daily measure, while scales that ask about weeks suit slow effects. If the measure is vague, suggest the one on the experiment's page. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: db527cc1-3bbe-4088-9ad7-10ccfe0c36f4
content::
**Your baseline.** How long will you measure before you start practising?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for a baseline: measuring before the practice starts until the measure is steady, ideally 10 to 20 days and at least 5 (Seth Roberts, module 3). For a one-off practice such as the pointing experiment, a short baseline like the NADA study's 5 minutes of sitting is enough. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if it is missing or too short for a daily measure, say so. 20 to 50 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: b6326fc1-4051-4c6e-b3bb-2c6a24f053e6
content::
**Your design.** Alternating days, a reversal (on, off, on again), or a multiple baseline? Say why it fits this claim.
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for a design that fits the claim (module 3): alternating days for fast effects that wear off quickly, a reversal for effects that should fade when stopped, a multiple baseline or a careful long baseline-then-practice run for changes that cannot be undone. If they say it runs at the same time as another experiment, check that the two have different main measures. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if the design does not fit, name the one that would and why. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 5ff38c61-44f1-478f-a3da-32ba184a595a
content::
**Confounders.** What else could change your measure during the experiment, and how will you note it?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for confounders: things noted daily (sleep, illness, stress, big events), nothing else changed on purpose at the same time, and regression to the mean if they start in an unusually bad week. Another experiment running at the same time is itself a confounder. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and name the most likely missing one for this experiment. 20 to 50 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 5d874436-7dcd-4b65-825f-1a66f245caee
content::
**Expectation.** How will you keep your hopes from producing the result?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for handling expectation: blinding is rarely possible, so an active comparison of the same length (for example a week of neutral writing, as in Seligman's 2005 trial, or a coin toss between two practices, as on the self-compassion page), a written prediction, and a measure that is harder to fool (a count, a partner's view, recovery time). In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if nothing is planned, suggest one of these. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: fbd02cb1-61c3-4d7d-83ac-9874fe7419dd
content::
**Stopping.** When will you stop, and what would make you stop early?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is writing a protocol for their third chosen experiment, one part per question. This part asks for a planned end and an early stop rule. For any meditation or self practice, the rule should name the warning signs from the course's safety boxes: feeling unreal, detached from the body or feelings, emotional flatness, agitation or poor sleep (Britton, Cheetah House), and what they will do (cut back or stop, and talk to a doctor, therapist or Cheetah House if it lasts). For other experiments, stopping if their measure or daily life clearly gets worse. In one or two sentences: confirm it, and if a needed part is missing, name it. Do not alarm them. 20 to 60 words. No generic praise. One turn.

#### Question: Open
id:: 6a982ed0-15c2-4f2f-ad0e-ba7be1695a0a
content::
**Looking at the results.** How will you look at your data at the end?
feedback-instructions:: The learner has now written every part of a protocol for the third experiment they chose in module 8: claim and source, dose, duration, prediction, main measure, baseline, design, confounders, expectation, stopping, and now analysis (module 3: plot the main measure against the day and look at level, trend and how long the change took to appear). Review the whole protocol, using their earlier answers on this page. Reply in 150 to 230 words. Check, in this order, and comment on what is weak or missing: (1) does the design fit how fast the effect is claimed to come and whether it should fade; (2) does the dose come from the claim's source, and if not, is that said; (3) is there one main measure chosen in advance, with a baseline before the practice; (4) is there a stop rule, and for intensive practices (an hour or more a day, retreats, intensive noting or jhana practice, practices that loosen the sense of self) does it name the warning signs and where to get help, without alarming them; (5) could the plan realistically be kept up alongside their other experiments, and if not, suggest running it after the others. Name the two most important fixes, most important first, with one concrete suggestion each. Do not choose the claim for them, do not say whether it is true, and do not say which school is right. Offer one more exchange to revise. No generic praise.
