#### Text
content::
In the map, the philosophy rows rely mostly on argument. The French philosopher Pierre Hadot argued that ancient schools such as the Stoics and Epicureans were also ways of living, with exercises meant to change how their members lived. If so, you can test a philosophy partly the way you test a meditation practice or a therapy: by trying it. This short essay explains his idea and turns it into four steps you can use with any school.

#### Question: Open
id:: 52ddb3a2-ef1b-43da-a927-d93184e1e24c
content::
Pick one claim from a philosophy row of the map (Stoicism, Epicureanism, Aristotle, philosophy of the self, or existentialism, absurdism and nihilism). Run it through Darr's four steps in a few sentences: the claim, which of your current habits it bears on, the habit you would try for a week, and what you would watch for in yourself.
feedback-instructions:: The learner read Christine Darr's 1000-Word Philosophy essay on Pierre Hadot's "philosophy as a way of life": critical reflection plus spiritual exercises (practised habitually), and a four-step process: learn a theory, evaluate your habits in its light, adopt a new habit, notice your reactions and reassess the theory. They now apply the steps to one claim from a philosophy school (Stoicism, Epicureanism, Aristotle and virtue ethics, philosophy of the self, existentialism, absurdism and nihilism).

Check that the four parts are there and fit together: is the claim stated as the school states it (correct a misstatement in one sentence), does the habit actually follow from the claim, and is what they will watch for something they could notice in a week? Name the strongest part, then the one change that would make the week more informative (for example: decide in advance what they will record, or what result would count against the claim). Mention that module 3 covers designing such a test properly. Do not design the experiment for them and do not say whether the school is right. 70 to 120 words. At most two replies. No generic praise.
