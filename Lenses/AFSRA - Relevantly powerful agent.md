title: "Relevantly powerful agent"
tldr: >-
  An AI is relevant if it can change the larger situation we face with AI. Many safety proposals restrict an AI so much that it can no longer do that, and letting it do enough usually brings back the hard safety problems.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  A short Arbital page by Eliezer Yudkowsky. A cognitively powerful agent is relevant if it is powerful enough to be a game-changer in the larger dilemma faced by Earth-originating intelligent life, and irrelevant if it is too weak or the tasks it is authorized to do do not change the overall situation. Positive example: an agent that bootstraps to nanotechnology and shuts down other AI projects, in a way its programmers can reasonably know is safe. Negative examples: an agent allowed only to settle the Riemann Hypothesis, and an oracle that outputs only verified proofs until someone can say which theorems would be a game-changer (hoping someone else will is a "hail Mary"). Importance: many safety proposals restrict the AI so much that it can no longer solve the larger dilemma, while proposals impactful enough to solve it run into all the usual advanced safety problems, especially in the rich domain of the real world, which tends to require full trust. Open problem: a relevant, limited AI that is much easier to make safe. The question asks the student to judge whether a yes/no maths oracle is relevant, and what happens when it is given free rein instead.

#### Question: Open
id:: 9c3b3ee4-4f45-4c03-9d49-8ffd15269b5d
content::
A researcher proposes an AI that she says is completely safe. It can only answer yes or no to one kind of question: whether a given mathematical statement is true. Nobody has yet said which statements they would ask about, or how the answers would change the overall situation with AI, such as other groups building dangerous AI.

1. In the reading's sense, is this AI relevant? Why?
2. To fix this, she proposes letting the AI act freely in the real world to do whatever helps most. According to the reading, what problem do proposals like this usually run into?

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The scenario: an AI that can only answer yes or no to whether a mathematical statement is true, where nobody has said which statements to ask or how the answers would change the overall AI situation. Then the researcher proposes letting the AI act freely in the real world. The reading's ideas: an agent is relevant if it can be a game-changer in the larger dilemma we face with AI, and irrelevant if what it can or is allowed to do does not significantly change that situation. An oracle limited to answers about mathematical facts is not relevant until someone can say how those answers would change the situation, and hoping someone else will think of a use is a "hail Mary" step. Proposals that let the AI do things impactful enough to solve the larger dilemma run into the usual hard (advanced) safety problems, especially when the AI must act in the real world, which tends to require full trust.
50: Step 1. Says the AI is not relevant, because answering yes or no about mathematical statements does not change the larger situation with AI, and nobody has shown how the answers would. An answer that says it would become relevant only if someone found statements whose answers changed the situation also gets full credit.
50: Step 2. Says that an AI powerful and free enough to change the situation runs into the full, hard safety problems again: acting freely in the real world means we would have to trust it completely, so the restriction that made it safe is gone.
Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "No. A yes/no maths oracle doesn't change the situation we're in with AI, and nobody has said which answers would. Hoping someone finds a use later is a hail Mary. But letting it act freely in the real world brings back all the hard safety problems, since we would have to trust it fully. That is why a relevant, limited AI is an open problem."
