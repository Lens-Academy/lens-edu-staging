# tldr: "People often dismiss language models as 'just predicting the next token'. This short video argues that the 'just' does a lot of work: predicting text very well can require very capable reasoning."
summary_for_tutor: "Robert Miles' short video arguing that 'LLMs just predict the next token' places no meaningful ceiling on capability. Examples: predicting the sum at the bottom of a table requires actually doing addition (you can't memorize every sum); predicting the results section of a medical paper from its methods requires an accurate model of biology, and even predicting the scientists' mistakes. So a sufficiently good next-token predictor would be radically superintelligent. Key point: token prediction is a task, not a description of the system doing it; to reason about what models can and can't do, look at the models themselves (e.g. the transformer architecture). Actual models are far from perfect predictors. Sets up the simulator framing that follows."
