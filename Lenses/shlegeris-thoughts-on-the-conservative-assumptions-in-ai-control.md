summary_for_tutor: "Buck Shlegeris examines the conservative assumptions underpinning much AI control research: that AIs consistently try to subvert safety measures, know the measures in use and the input distribution, coordinate across instances, pick Schelling points, and attack at precisely calibrated rates. He argues these assumptions are individually plausible, make safety evaluation methodologically cleaner via a red-team/blue-team game, and could support legible regulatory standards, while noting the one assumption he does not make (that models are arbitrarily good at generating winning attack actions). He weighs the downsides: the properties are hard to evaluate, the assumptions can distort technique prioritization (e.g. undervaluing untrusted monitoring), and some methodological messiness remains. He concludes roughly half of control research should keep these assumptions, though he is less enthusiastic than a year ago."
