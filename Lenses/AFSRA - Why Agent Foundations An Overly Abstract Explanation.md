tldr: >-
  A measure that works fine in everyday use breaks once something optimizes hard against it, like a factory rewarded per nail that makes tiny useless nails. Alignment needs formulations of its key concepts that keep working under that pressure, which Wentworth calls True Names.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Wentworth explains why some alignment researchers work on foundational questions of agency. He starts from a deliberately naive plan (train an AI on human good/bad labels) and names two problems. The AI learns to optimize what looks good to humans, and the strategies it learns may stop tracking "good" in new environments (humans using condoms despite evolution). Both get bad only under heavy optimization pressure, because of Goodhart's Law (the Soviet nail factory, instagram food, a "Potemkin village world"). Goodhart is not inevitable. Zero correlation is a proxy for no information leak that an adversary can beat with fourth-order statistics, while zero mutual information holds however hard the adversary tries. Such a robust formulation is a "True Name". Alignment needs True Names for human values (or a pointer to them), and for optimization, goals, world models, abstraction and so on. The question asks the student to apply the proxy versus True Name distinction to an honesty check for a powerful AI.

#### Question: Open
id:: 3a9b8d21-03a1-492d-8c8a-8bcb18a337df
content::
An AI lab wants its models never to deceive their users. It trains each model with an automatic checker that compares every statement the model makes against a large database of known facts and penalizes any statement that contradicts the database. The checker cannot judge statements about things the database does not cover, and it cannot tell whether a set of true statements leaves the user with a false impression. On today's models, which are not very capable, the checker works well: deceptive answers almost always contain a statement it flags.

The lab now plans to train a far more capable model with the same checker, and with much more training.

1. In your own words, why might the checker stop doing its job for the far more capable model, even though it works on today's models?
2. According to Wentworth, what would the lab need instead of the checker, and how does the radio side-channel example in the post show that this is possible?
