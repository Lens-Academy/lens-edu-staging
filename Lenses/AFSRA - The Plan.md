title: "The Plan"
tldr: John Wentworth's plan is to first sort out our basic confusions about agency, then build an AI that learns human values. Making a proxy number of alignment better does not bring us closer to aligned AI while we do not know what we are aiming at.
summary_for_tutor: 'John Wentworth''s 2021 Q&A on his research plan. Step 1 is to sort out our fundamental confusions about agency, step 2 is ambitious value learning. He argues that timelines barely change the strategy until they get down to about 18 to 24 months, because nearly all gains come from becoming less confused. Iterative engineering only helps once we understand the goal well enough to recognise progress. Iterating on proxy measures of alignment will not get us meaningfully closer (A/B testing the wrong metrics, gliders'' flight range will not get you to the moon). Engineering still helps when it serves understanding: take a confusing concept such as abstraction, optimization or knowledge and try to build anything that robustly does something with it, like his "thermometer of abstraction". "Fundamental confusion" means not having frames that generalize: not knowing which questions to ask, what approximations work, what to attend to, how to split the problem. He criticizes MIRI for asking how to build AGI with particular properties rather than how agency works in general, explains why abstraction and selection theorems are his focus, why gears-level understanding is needed for generalization, and why he prefers his plan to a Simulated Long Reflection. The question asks the student to apply the proxy-iteration argument to a lab and to say what kind of engineering he does think helps.'

An AI lab tracks one number: the share of test prompts on which its model gives a harmful answer. Over a year of retraining, the number falls from 5% to 0.1%. The head of the lab says: "Each round of retraining brings us closer to aligned AGI. A few more rounds and we'll be there."

1. In your own words, what would Wentworth say is wrong with the lab head's reasoning?
2. Wentworth thinks engineering can still help with alignment. In your own words, what kind of engineering work does he think helps?
3. In your own words, what is that engineering for?
