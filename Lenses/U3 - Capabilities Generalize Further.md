#### Text
content::
\## Before you read

The last two readings described how things go wrong. This one tries to say what is hard,
underneath all of the stories.

The claim is about transfer. A system trained in one setting is used in another. Two
things travel with it: what it can do, and whatever we did to keep it doing what we
wanted. The argument is that these two do not travel equally well.

You have watched this happen to people and to software. Predict it before you read it.

#### Question
content::
\## Your turn first

Think of a case you know well where something was trained or built in one setting and
then used in a harder one. A person, a team, a machine, a piece of software.

Write two or three sentences. Which travelled better, the capability or the constraints
on it? Say what happened.

Then write one more line. Why did the one that travelled badly fail to travel?

assessment-instructions::
CONTEXT YOU NEED. You do not have the rest of this course, so here is the situation.

This is a pre-reading question in a course about AI futures, written for newcomers. The
student has NOT read the article yet. The article is "A central AI alignment problem:
capabilities generalization, and the sharp left turn" by Nate Soares, and it is in your
context.

The student was asked to recall a case where capability and constraint were both moved
to a new setting, and to say which survived.

The purpose is that they meet the claim as a prediction they already made, rather than as
an assertion by a stranger.

WHAT SOARES ARGUES. For your reference only. Do not deliver it.
Capabilities generalise further than alignment does. A system that gets much more capable
carries its competence into new situations, while the constraints we relied on were
tuned to the old situation and do not carry. So the moment a system becomes much more
capable is also the moment your safety measures quietly stop applying.

YOUR TASK, in order.
1. Name which way their example went. Some will produce a case where the constraints held
   and capability failed, which is the opposite of the claim. That is a good answer and
   you should say so rather than steering them.
2. Ask one question about the mechanism: what specifically was it about the constraint
   that was tied to the original setting?
3. Do not tell them whether their example supports or undermines Soares. They are about
   to find out.

Do not grade. Do not score. Do not praise.

Write 60 to 110 words. Short paragraphs. No lists.
