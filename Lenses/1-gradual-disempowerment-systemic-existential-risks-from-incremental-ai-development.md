{++{"author":"Elias's AI","timestamp":1783848603744}@@tldr: "AI risk stories usually feature a sudden robot takeover. What if humanity loses control gradually instead, with no villain and no single dramatic moment? This paper argues that as AI quietly outcompetes people across the economy, culture, and government, the systems we depend on could drift free of human interests one upgrade at a time, until the loss becomes permanent."
summary_for_tutor: "Presents the 'gradual disempowerment' thesis: that incremental AI progress, without any abrupt capability jump or coordinated takeover, could permanently strip humans of influence over society. The paper's six core claims hold that large-scale systems (economy, culture, states) are currently aligned with human interests largely because they depend on human labor and cognition; as AI displaces that participation, both explicit control mechanisms (voting, consumer choice) and implicit alignment erode, systems optimize harder for outcomes misaligned with human preferences, and misalignment in one domain reinforces the others, plausibly culminating in an irreversible existential catastrophe. It argues that aligning individual AI systems is insufficient and calls for technical and governance work targeting systemic erosion of human influence."
