tldr: "When you disagree with the experts, should you say so or defer? This piece introduces a distinction: your 'independent impression' (what you'd believe ignoring others' opinions) versus your 'all-things-considered belief' (which folds in their views). Tracking both, it argues, keeps a community from collapsing into an echo chamber while still letting you act on your best overall judgment."
summary_for_tutor: "Explains Michael Aird's distinction between an 'independent impression' (what one would believe absent peer disagreement, using others' reasons but not the mere fact of their beliefs) and an 'all-things-considered belief' (which incorporates peer disagreement). Presents three epistemic norms: track impressions separately, feel comfortable reporting them even when they diverge from expert consensus, and be explicit about which one is being stated. Argues impressions should be shared to avoid information cascades and overly homogeneous community beliefs, while actual decisions should follow all-things-considered beliefs."
