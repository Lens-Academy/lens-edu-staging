Even researchers who agree on the nature and severity of a threat may propose different ways of addressing it.

Consider scheming: the possibility that an AI system strategically conceals its misalignment to avoid intervention.

- Researchers working on **alignment techniques** might investigate training procedures that prevent such behaviour from developing.
- Researchers working on **interpretability** might investigate whether the computations underlying scheming can be identified inside a model.
- Researchers developing **evaluations** might investigate how to elicit scheming under controlled conditions and determine which systems are capable of it.
- Researchers working on **AI control** might investigate whether a potentially scheming system can be used without giving it an opportunity to cause catastrophic harm.

These are different proposed interventions at different points in a causal chain. Each comes with its own assumptions about what can be achieved and what other safety measures will be available.

An explicit justification for the last approach can be found in [The Case for Ensuring That Powerful AIs Are Controlled](https://www.alignmentforum.org/s/PC3yJgdKvk8kzqZyA/p/kcKrE9mzEHrdqtDpE).

Understanding the origin of a research agenda can help you reconstruct its Theory of Change.
