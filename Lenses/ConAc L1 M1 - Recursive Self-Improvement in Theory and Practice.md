**The recursive self-improvement landscape**

*Recursive self-improvement, or RSI, is AI doing the work of building better AI. Whoever gets that loop running first sets the pace for the rest of the field, which is why it is worth tracking closely. Nobody has closed the loop yet. Plenty of teams say they are close.*

*This directory lists the companies working toward it, from whichever angle they take: models that train their successors, harnesses that run research without a human in the loop, and the infrastructure and data underneath. It stopped being a frontier-lab story some time ago.*

Source: [https://rsi-list.com/](https://rsi-list.com/)

The idea of *recursive self-improvement* (RSI) is no longer confined to theoretical discussions about the distant future of AI. Leading AI companies are already working towards automating increasingly large parts of AI research itself.

OpenAI even has a dedicated [Recursive Self-Improvement team](https://openai.com/careers/research-engineer-research-scientist-ai-systems-engineer-rsi-san-francisco/), tasked with building systems capable of conducting AI research. Yet researchers differ in how optimistic they are about RSI and how seriously they view its risks.

- **Jakub Pachocki, Chief Scientist at OpenAI**, in [An Alien Mind](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/), warns that RSI could advance faster than our ability to align and monitor AI systems, and argues for extreme caution.
- **Mark Chen, Chief Research Officer at OpenAI, and John Schulman, co-founder of OpenAI**, in [Can AI Learn to Improve Itself?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_aA1-R70E8) (~18 min), take a more optimistic view. Chen expresses greater confidence in solving alignment problems, while Schulman emphasises the technical difficulties that may slow progress towards fully autonomous RSI.

Anthropic describes a similar direction in [When AI Builds Itself](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement):

Google DeepMind has already demonstrated narrower examples of AI improving the processes used to develop AI, such as [AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/), a system that discovers and optimises algorithms, including algorithms used in AI development.

These are not yet examples of fully autonomous recursive self-improvement. But they suggest that AI-assisted AI development is becoming an important part of how frontier AI research is conducted.
