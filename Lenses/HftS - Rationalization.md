summary_for_tutor: "A follow-up to \"The Bottom Line\". Yudkowsky calls \"rationalization\" a wrong word, since you cannot make rational what is not already rational. Rationality is a forward flow from evidence to a conclusion that is not yet known. Rationalization is a backward flow from a fixed conclusion to selected evidence. Arguing for a proposition can make more people believe it but cannot make it more true. Improving beliefs requires changing them, and rationalization fixes them in place. Traditional Rationality approves of a scientist who sets out to prove a pet hypothesis, but it would be better to test it out of curiosity, with experiments that could move one's belief in a direction one cannot predict. Curiosity is the sign that you do not yet know where you are going. #### Question: Open
id:: 55e6c332-94dc-432d-9254-374ef8f759d7
content::
A friend says: "Rationalizing is only a problem when the conclusion is false. If I start from a true conclusion and then find arguments for it, I end up with a true belief and some good arguments, so no harm done."

Using the essay, explain what is wrong with this reasoning, and say what your friend should do instead when they feel sure they already know the answer.
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

The idea the answer applies: rationality runs forward, from the evidence to a conclusion not known in advance. Rationalization runs backward, from a fixed conclusion to whatever arguments support it. Arguments can be found this way for false conclusions just as easily as for true ones, so the process carries no information about whether the conclusion is true. Arguing for a claim can make people believe it more but cannot make it more true, and because rationalization never changes the belief, it can never correct a wrong one. The better course is to test the belief out of curiosity, in a way whose outcome could change it.

60: What is wrong. The answer explains at least one of these: the friend cannot know in advance which of their conclusions are the true ones, and the method works just as well for false conclusions, so it gives no evidence either way. Or: arguing for a conclusion cannot make it more true, so a true conclusion reached this way is right by luck and the method would never catch the cases where it is wrong. Give 30 if the answer only says rationalizing is biased or dishonest without saying why it fails even when the conclusion is true.

40: What to do instead. Start from the evidence and let it decide, or test the belief in a way that could show it is wrong (looking for what would change their mind), out of curiosity rather than to defend it. Give 20 if the answer only says "be open-minded" without saying how.

Cap at 40 if the answer agrees that rationalizing does no harm when the conclusion is true.
feedback-instructions::
The student has just read Eliezer Yudkowsky's "Rationalization" and answered a question about a friend who thinks rationalizing is harmless when the conclusion happens to be true. Key ideas of the essay: rationality is a forward flow from evidence to an unknown conclusion. Rationalization is a backward flow from a fixed conclusion to selected evidence. Arguing cannot make a claim more true. Improving beliefs means changing them, and rationalization fixes them in place. A scientist should test a pet hypothesis out of curiosity, with experiments that could move the belief in an unknown direction.

The most likely gaps: not seeing that the friend cannot know in advance which conclusions are true, so the method gives no evidence and cannot correct mistakes, or a fix that stays vague ("keep an open mind").
