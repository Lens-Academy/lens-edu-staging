Wikipedia contributors. "Expected Value." *Wikipedia, The Free Encyclopedia*. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expected_value)
*The encyclopedia article on expected value, the weighted-average concept the research-tips lesson uses for prioritization.*

Probably Good. "The ITN Framework: A Tool for Prioritizing Global Problems." [probablygood.org](https://probablygood.org/core-concepts/itn-framework/)
*An explainer of the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework for prioritizing problems.*

Lutz, Ronja. "Learning to Ask Action-Relevant Questions." *Effective Altruism Forum*, 28 Dec. 2019. [forum.effectivealtruism.org](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/AP9FowhqT7MXDSm5P/learning-to-ask-action-relevant-questions-1)
*A post on testing research questions by asking what would change in the world if they were answered.*

Wikipedia contributors. "Theory of Change." *Wikipedia, The Free Encyclopedia*. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_change)
*The encyclopedia article on theory of change, the planning methodology that maps activities to intended outcomes.*

Scher, Aaron, David Abecassis, Peter Barnett, et al. "An International Agreement to Prevent the Premature Creation of Artificial Superintelligence." *arXiv*, Nov. 2025. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10783)
*MIRI's draft international agreement to prevent the premature creation of artificial superintelligence, the treaty text several lessons read against.*

Scher, Aaron. "Catch-Up Algorithmic Progress Might Actually Be 60x per Year." *MIRI Technical Governance Team*, 24 Dec. 2025. [techgov.intelligence.org](https://techgov.intelligence.org/blog/catch-up-algorithmic-progress-might-actually-be-60x-per-year)
*A MIRI estimate that catch-up algorithmic progress runs 16x-60x per year, far faster than commonly cited frontier figures.*

Barnett, Peter. "Compute Requirements for Algorithmic Innovation in Frontier AI Models." *arXiv*, July 2025. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10618)
*An analysis of 36 algorithmic innovations asking how much compute it takes to invent frontier-model improvements, and whether compute restrictions would slow them.*

Rahman, Robi. "Catching Illicit Distributed Training Operations During an AI Pause." *MIRI Technical Governance Team*, 9 Apr. 2026. [techgov.intelligence.org](https://techgov.intelligence.org/blog/catching-illicit-distributed-training-operations-during-an-ai-pause)
*A MIRI analysis of how a covert actor could train across distributed unregistered clusters during a pause, and how that might be caught.*

Scher, Aaron, and Lisa Thiergart. "Mechanisms to Verify International Agreements About AI Development." *arXiv*, June 2025. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15867#page=128)
*MIRI's mechanism-by-mechanism study of how countries could verify compliance with negotiated AI rules, across three policy scenarios.*

Yudkowsky, Eliezer. "Twelve Virtues of Rationality." *LessWrong*, 2006. [lesswrong.com](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7ZqGiPHTpiDMwqMN2/twelve-virtues-of-rationality)
*An essay naming twelve virtues of rational inquiry, from curiosity to precision.*

"Fermi Estimate." *Brilliant Math & Science Wiki*, Brilliant.org. [brilliant.org](https://brilliant.org/wiki/fermi-estimate/)
*A wiki explainer of Fermi estimation: rough order-of-magnitude reasoning with worked examples.*

Salamon, Anna. "Humans Are Not Automatically Strategic." *LessWrong*, 8 Sept. 2010. [lesswrong.com](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PBRWb2Em5SNeWYwwB/humans-are-not-automatically-strategic)
*A post arguing that systematic goal-pursuit is a learned skill, not a default human behavior.*
