summary_for_tutor: "Explains Eliezer Yudkowsky's argument that altruistic acts serve three distinct ends - emotional satisfaction ('warm fuzzies'), social status, and actual measurable good ('utilons') - and that pursuing them simultaneously optimizes none of them well. Using the example of a lawyer volunteering versus working an extra hour and donating, and advice to a hypothetical new billionaire, he recommends purchasing each separately: getting fuzzies cheaply from vivid, direct, in-person helping; buying status through visible giving to stylish causes; and directing the bulk of resources with cold, cost-effectiveness calculation (avoiding scope insensitivity) to whichever charity yields the most expected utilons per dollar. He suggests spending far more on utilons than on fuzzies."
