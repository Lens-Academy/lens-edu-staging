tldr: "Animal advocates rarely call themselves longtermists, and longtermists rarely prioritize animals. Baumann argues both are missing something: whether the vast future goes well may hinge on whether powerful people come to care about all sentient beings. If moral concern is a key lever on the future, expanding the moral circle could be a longtermist priority, and that changes what advocates should focus on."
summary_for_tutor: "Argues that expanding humanity's moral circle, animal advocacy broadly construed, is a plausible longtermist priority, because the values of powerful people are a fundamental determinant of how the long-term future goes. Considers objections (future moral reflection, shifting populations including possible artificial sentience, and a possibly low required degree of concern) and finds none decisive. Then draws out implications: a longtermist lens shifts emphasis from immediate suffering relief toward lasting social change and locking in persistent moral consideration, avoiding backlash, keeping the movement thoughtful and open-minded, and favoring antispeciesism over veganism specifically, while making quantitative impact estimates much harder."
