\## Where do they disagree?

Redwood and MIRI agree that control will not work for an arbitrarily powerful AI.

1. Where exactly do they disagree?
2. Which kind of criticism is MIRI's: control does not work, works but is not worth the effort, or works and makes things worse? It may have more than one side.
3. What would have to be true for the control authors' plan to work, given this limit?
feedback-instructions:: The learner compares Redwood's and MIRI's positions. Reference points: (1) they disagree on whether controlled early AIs, the first ones able to substantially reduce risk from later AIs, can do useful safety work that helps with what comes next (MIRI: "Plans to align ASI using unaligned AIs are similarly unsound"), and on where effort should go (MIRI: halt progress toward ASI by a worldwide ban). (2) MIRI's criticism has a "does not work" side, for ASI and for the plan of getting alignment work out of unaligned AIs, and a "not worth the effort" side, because the effort should go to halting. Accept reasoned alternatives. (3) For the control plan to work: there must be a window in which AIs are useful enough to help and still controllable, the work they produce must be checkable, and developers or governments must use that window well, for example by slowing down before AIs outgrow control. Up to two turns, 80 to 140 words each. Steelman the answer, name the most important gap, and ask one direct follow-up question. No generic praise. Do not say who is right.
