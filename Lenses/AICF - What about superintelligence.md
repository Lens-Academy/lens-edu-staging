**The criticism: control will not be enough for the AIs that matter most.**

Control is built for AIs that are not much smarter than us. Its authors agree it will not work for far more powerful AI. Greenblatt and Shlegeris, who wrote the case for control you read in Unit 1, say:

#### Text
content::
A 2025 paper co-written by Buck Shlegeris with researchers at the UK AI Security Institute and Apollo Research says the same about control evaluations: making the case for superintelligent AI "will require research breakthroughs".[^cite-korbak-2025]

So the criticism is not that control fails on superintelligence. It is about the plan behind control: controlled early AIs are supposed to do much of the safety work for later, stronger ones. The Technical Governance Team of MIRI (the Machine Intelligence Research Institute) argues that this plan may well fail. In a 2025 paper proposing an international agreement to halt progress toward superintelligence, they answer the objection that AIs could do the alignment research instead. Some terms:
- **AGI:** AI as capable as humans across the board. **ASI:** artificial superintelligence.
- **Prima facie:** at first sight.

[^cite-korbak-2025]: Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni, Buck Shlegeris and Geoffrey Irving (2025), *How to evaluate control measures for LLM agents? A trajectory from today to superintelligence*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05259)
