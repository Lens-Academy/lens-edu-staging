summary_for_tutor: 'Eliezer Yudkowsky''s Arbital entry on "pivotal act", a guarded term for events that make a large positive difference a billion years later, upsetting the gameboard and decisively settling a win or drastically raising its probability. Existential catastrophes are the negative counterpart. The definition is guarded because people are tempted to call their project "relevant" through strained chains of possibility. Examples: a boxed ZF provability oracle advances mathematics but changes nothing basic, papers on truckers losing jobs to robotic cars are inputs into a large pool of goodness with many other inputs, while a genie that uploads fifty researchers and runs them 10,000 times faster would be pivotal. The deck-of-cards metaphor: we keep dealing until a red or black ace (superintelligence) stops the game, and only cards that change the game''s nature count. Without a guarded concept, effort drifts toward standard, comfortable research with low marginal impact on the outcome (bait and switch). The page also lists possible pivotal acts, catastrophes and non-significant events, and ties the concept to limited AI and advanced agents. The question asks the student to judge whether a misinformation-detection tool is a pivotal act and why the term should stay narrow.'

#### Question: Open
id:: 2e47594c-a826-4276-be3c-3f4f42d40a10
content::
A grant applicant writes: "Our project builds an AI tool that flags false news stories. Better news makes voters better informed, better-informed voters choose wiser governments, and wiser governments are more likely to agree on rules for AI. So our tool is a pivotal act."

1. Is the tool a pivotal act in the reading's sense? Why?
2. In your own words, why does the reading say the term "pivotal act" should be kept narrow, so that arguments like this one do not count?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The reading's idea: a pivotal act is an event that makes a large positive difference to the long-term future by changing the basic situation, decisively settling a win or drastically raising its probability. A project that only feeds, through a chain of possible effects, into a large pool of good things with many other inputs has at most a small, non-zero effect, and does not count. The term is kept narrow (guarded) because otherwise people argue that almost anything is "relevant", effort and money drift to easier, more comfortable and standard work, and the hard, neglected, game-changing problems are left undone.
50: Step 1. Says no, and explains that the tool has at most a small, indirect effect (a long chain of maybes, or one input among many into a large pool such as public debate or good government), so it does not change the basic situation or decisively change the odds of a good outcome.
50: Step 2. Says that if indirect "it could help" arguments counted, the term would cover almost any project, so funding and effort would go to easy, familiar or ordinary work that people know they can finish, while the hard problems that could actually change the outcome stay neglected. Accept the reading's "bait and switch" point (borrowing the importance of the huge stakes for work with doubtful impact) as this idea. Also accept, for the full 50, the other reason the reading gives: without a narrow term, people could no longer tell game-changing events apart from things with only a small or merely possible effect, so the term would stop helping anyone find the work that matters most.
Cap at 50 if the answer to step 2 consists mostly of quoted sentences rather than the student's own explanation.
Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "No. A pivotal act changes the game, like uploading researchers so they get centuries to work on alignment. This tool is one small input into a huge pool of things that affect how well governments cooperate, and the link runs through a long chain of maybes. The reading wants the term kept narrow because if anything with some possible effect counts, everyone can call their easy project pivotal. Then money goes to comfortable work people know they can finish, and the hard problems that could actually change the outcome get left alone."
