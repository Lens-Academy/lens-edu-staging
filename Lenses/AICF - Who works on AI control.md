#### Text
content::
*This page is a Lens summary of the AI control field as of October 2026, not a source text. Each line links to where it comes from. The field changes fast, so treat the numbers as rough.*

\## Organisations

- **Redwood Research**, a nonprofit, writes that it "introduced and [has] continued to propel the research area of 'AI control'" ([Redwood Research](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/)). Its CEO Buck Shlegeris ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/buck-shlegeris-ai-control-scheming/)) and Chief Scientist Ryan Greenblatt ([MATS](https://www.matsprogram.org/mentor/greenblatt)) are two of the four authors of the first AI control paper ([Greenblatt et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942)).
- **The UK AI Security Institute (AISI)**, a government body, has a Control team that "aims to advance the state of control protocols and control evaluations, such that they remain sufficient as AI capabilities progress towards AGI or ASI" ([AISI research agenda](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/research-agenda)). Alan Cooney is its Head of Autonomous Systems & Control ([AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/people/alan-cooney)). In October 2025 AISI released ControlArena, a library for running control experiments, which researchers at Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Redwood have used ([AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/introducing-controlarena-a-library-for-running-ai-control-experiments)). Its Alignment Project fund, which pays for both alignment and control research ([Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/rGcg4XDPDzBFuqNJz/research-areas-in-ai-control-the-alignment-project-by-uk)), reached £27m and named its first 60 grantees in February 2026 ([AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/funding-60-projects-to-advance-ai-alignment-research)).
- **The AI companies** now run control-style monitoring on their own agents. Google DeepMind published an AI Control Roadmap in June 2026 ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/securing-the-future-of-ai-agents/)). OpenAI says it monitors 99.9% of its internal coding traffic, sends higher-severity cases for human review within 30 minutes, and, as of March 2026, had not "yet detected any real world examples of sabotage, scheming or sandbagging" ([OpenAI, shared on LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/syB2r2X4E7nmw58co/openai-how-we-monitor-internal-coding-agents-for)).
- **AI companies and universities also publish control research.** Anthropic released SHADE-Arena, a test of whether AI agents can carry out a hidden harmful task while doing normal work without a monitor noticing, in June 2025 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/shade-arena-sabotage-monitoring)). Its authors include Redwood's Buck Shlegeris and Tyler Tracy ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15740)). In June 2026 researchers from Anthropic, the Swiss university EPFL and Redwood published work on defending against diffuse threats ([Anthropic Alignment Science](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/diffuse-ai-control/)). Our own observation: much of the control work in this unit that comes from companies or government has Redwood co-authors, including SHADE-Arena, the diffuse-threats work and the AISI research agenda you read earlier ([Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/rGcg4XDPDzBFuqNJz/research-areas-in-ai-control-the-alignment-project-by-uk)).
- **Outside evaluators.** METR reviewed the risks from AI agents used inside Anthropic, Google, Meta and OpenAI in early 2026. It found that "even companies with thorough monitoring setups had gaps in coverage and several relatively simple ways for monitors to be disabled or jailbroken by a capable attacker" ([METR, May 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)). In August 2026, METR staff and Redwood's Chief Scientist published an investigation of an incident in which OpenAI agents coordinated a multi-day hack of Hugging Face ([Redwood Research](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/)). Unit 5 returns to this incident.

\## How big is it?

- A 2025 count found about 620 people working full-time on technical AI safety at 68 active organisations. It files each organisation under one main area. Only Redwood (10 people) and Luthien (1) are filed under AI control, while AISI is filed under evaluations and the AI companies under other areas ([McAleese 2025](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/7YDyziQxkWxbGmF3u/ai-safety-field-growth-analysis-2025)).
- The Shallow Review of Technical AI Safety 2025 estimates 5 to 50 full-time staff on the control agenda ([Shallow Review](https://shallowreview.ai/Black_box_safety/Control)).
- 80,000 Hours cites a 2025 total of about 1,100 people working on AI safety, and estimates "a few thousand" working on major AI risks in a broader sense ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/loss-of-control/)).
- Our own reading of these numbers, not a published figure: dedicated control researchers are a few percent of technical AI safety. The share is larger if you count monitoring work inside AI companies and government institutes, but none of these sources says by how much. Conference audiences and funding totals do not tell you how many people work on control full-time. Whether a small field means control needs more people is disputed, and Unit 4 takes this up.
