summary_for_tutor: "Three brain studies read as a lesson in the limits of such evidence. (1) Brewer et al. 2011 (PNAS), abstract and limitations: experienced meditators showed relatively less activity in the main default mode network (DMN) nodes (medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate cortex) across three meditation types, and stronger coupling with regions linked to self-monitoring and cognitive control; the authors interpret this as 'consistent with decreased mind-wandering'; limitations: moderately small sample that increases the chance of false or inflated positives, results need replication, reporting bias. (2) Josipovic et al. 2012 (Frontiers in Human Neuroscience), abstract and two caveats: in experienced Tibetan Buddhist meditators, non-dual awareness meditation weakened the usual anti-correlation between an 'extrinsic' (world-directed) and an 'intrinsic' (self-related) brain system, while focused attention strengthened it; the authors call the link to the duality of experience 'intuitive, but speculative'; they had no reliable way to assess the quality of meditation in the scanner. (3) Rahrig et al. 2022 (Scientific Reports), meta-analysis of mindfulness training and resting-state DMN connectivity (abstract). Our own framing: reverse inference (Poldrack 2006): inferring a mental state (less self) from activity in a region that also does other things is weak unless the region is selective. #### Text
content::
You may have read headlines saying meditation quiets the brain's "self network". The network meant is the **default mode network** (DMN), a set of brain regions more active when a person is not focused on an outside task. The first study below links it to mind-wandering and to "self-referential processing", thinking about oneself.

Read three well-known studies, not for their findings in detail but for what they measured and how far their own authors go.

First, Judson Brewer and colleagues compared experienced meditators with people who had never meditated.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/brewer-meditation-experience-is-associated-with-differences-in-default-mode-network-activity-and-connectivity]]

to:: As such, these provide a unique understanding of possible neural mechanisms of meditation.

#### Article
from:: This study has several limitations.
to:: Further studies are required to better correlate temporal patterns of neural activation with first-person reports.

Second, Zoran Josipovic and colleagues scanned experienced meditators from the Tibetan Buddhist tradition during a practice of **non-dual awareness**, which they contrast with focused attention.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/josipovic-influence-of-meditation-on-anti-correlated-networks-in-the-brain]]
from:: Human experiences can be broadly divided into those that are external
to:: practicing different forms of meditation can modulate this gross functional organization in profoundly different ways.

In the same paper the authors say how far their result goes:

to:: NDA meditation enables an atypical state of mind in which extrinsic and intrinsic experiences are increasingly synergistic rather than competing.

#### Article

#### Text
content::
Third, a meta-analysis by Rahrig and colleagues pooled studies that compared people given mindfulness training with a control group, either randomised or matched, and looked at the DMN at rest ([Scientific Reports 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9293892/)). It found 12 such studies, with 226 people trained and 204 controls. Its main result was not a quieter DMN but more connectivity between a hub of the DMN (the posterior cingulate cortex) and a region of what the authors call the salience network. They link this to "the flexible control of internally-oriented attention". We read this as the best-controlled evidence of the three, and it is about how attention is managed, not directly about the self.

#### Text
content::
A general problem sits under all three. The psychologist Russell Poldrack called it **reverse inference**: concluding that a mental process was present because a brain region was active, when that region is also active during other processes. It is only as strong as the region is selective for that process ([Poldrack 2006, "Can cognitive processes be inferred from neuroimaging data?"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7364873_Can_Cognitive_Processes_Be_Inferred_from_Neuroimaging_Data)). Brewer and colleagues themselves read their result as less mind-wandering. We think none of these studies can tell, on its own, whether the meditators had less sense of self, less mind-wandering, or a different style of attention.

#### Question: Open
id:: 34b24748-9b75-4479-b64b-2f2ecf334a1f
content::
A science article is headlined: "Brain scans prove meditation dissolves the self." It cites the first two studies you just read. Using what the studies and their authors say, what is wrong with the headline?
feedback-instructions:: The learner read the abstracts and caveats of Brewer et al. 2011 (less DMN activity in experienced meditators; interpreted as consistent with less mind-wandering; moderately small sample with risk of inflated positives; needs replication; reporting bias), Josipovic et al. 2012 (non-dual awareness meditation weakened the anti-correlation between extrinsic and intrinsic brain systems; the link to the duality of experience is "intuitive, but speculative"; no reliable way to check meditation quality in the scanner), a pointer to a 2022 meta-analysis of trials, and a note on reverse inference. They got a headline: "Brain scans prove meditation dissolves the self." This is practice, not a test.

Points a strong answer finds (any two are good):
- Reverse inference: the DMN is linked to self-referential thought and mind-wandering, but less activity there does not prove less self. Brewer and colleagues read their own result as less mind-wandering.
- The authors are cautious: small samples, need for replication, a "speculative" link, no reliable check of what meditators did in the scanner.
- Brewer compared experienced meditators with non-meditators at one time point, so it cannot show meditation caused the difference. Josipovic measured states during practice, not a lasting change.
- "Self" is several things (as in the six categories from Lindahl and Britton); a brain difference would need to be tied to one of them by reports or behaviour.

Per reply: if the learner asks a direct question, answer it. Otherwise restate their answer in 1 to 2 sentences, name one point they missed, and ask one direct follow-up (for example: "What would you need besides the scan to say the sense of self changed?"). If stuck after two attempts, give two points briefly. Do not say whether meditation changes the self. 90 to 150 words. At most 2 replies. No generic praise.
