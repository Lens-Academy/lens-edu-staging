tldr: "If you handed a superintelligence the keys to the future, whose wishes should it obey -- and which version of you? Yudkowsky's answer is 'coherent extrapolated volition': not what humanity asks for today, but what we would want if we knew more, thought faster, were more the people we wished we were, and had grown up farther together."
summary_for_tutor: "Eliezer Yudkowsky's 2004 statement of what a Friendly AI should be aimed at. Distinguishes three problems of Friendly AI and argues that choosing the goal ('Friendliness content') is the easiest to argue about but not the hardest technical part. Introduces 'volition' -- what you would want if you knew the relevant facts -- via the box-and-diamond thought experiment, and the 'dynamic' that extrapolates it, with the notions of spread, muddle, and distance (short, medium, long). Proposes that the AI's initial dynamic implement humankind's coherent extrapolated volition: our wish if we knew more, thought faster, were more the people we wished we were, had grown up farther together, where extrapolations converge and wishes cohere. Unpacks each clause of that definition, discusses coherence versus influence (conservative about 'yes', attentive to 'no'), safe visible failure when chaos is too high, renormalizing the dynamic, and CEV as an initial dynamic meant to ask what solution we want rather than to be the solution itself."
