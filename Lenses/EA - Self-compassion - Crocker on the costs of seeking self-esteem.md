#### Text
content::
Neff's talk rested on a claim about self-esteem: that chasing it backfires. One researcher who studied that claim directly is Jennifer Crocker, a social psychologist, at the University of Michigan when she wrote the paper below. Neff cites her review with Lora Park as the evidence that attempts to raise self-esteem go with narcissism, prejudice and bullying ([Neff 2023](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf)). Crocker does not argue for self-compassion. Her question is narrower, and we think it is close to this course's own question: what does it cost to need proof that you are worth something?

You will read four parts of her 2002 paper. The first gives her argument.

#### Text
content::
She then followed 642 students through their first year of college. The domains they based their worth on before college predicted how they later spent their time. They also predicted problems during the year. Basing worth on appearance went with more problems of almost every kind, and basing it on virtue or faith with fewer. Many of these links disappeared once she controlled for neuroticism, narcissism and the wish to look good to others, but some held. Basing worth on appearance still predicted disordered eating, and basing it on academics still predicted academic problems ([Crocker 2002, Table 4](https://people.well.com/user/mgarriga/costs.pdf)). This is a correlational study: it shows what goes together, not what causes what.

The third part is a study that tracked how self-esteem moves day to day.

The last part asks what a person can do instead. She compares ways of making worth less dependent on outcomes, which is why we think it is the part most relevant to this course.

#### Text
content::
Notice that her preferred way out is not self-compassion. It is to stop working on the question of one's own worth and to turn attention to goals outside oneself. Neff's answer is different: keep relating to yourself, but with kindness instead of evaluation. Whether those two answers conflict is a question you can keep in mind for the rest of the module.

#### Question: Open

content::
A friend has read Crocker's paper and says: "I'm safe from all this. My self-worth rests on being a good and ethical person, and her data show that basing your worth on virtue goes with fewer problems."

Using the paper, explain why Crocker would not agree that your friend is safe.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The question asks why Crocker would not accept that basing self-worth on virtue makes a person safe from the costs of seeking self-esteem.

60 points: the core reason. The problem in Crocker's view is the pursuit of self-esteem itself, living to prove one meets a condition of worth, not which domain the condition is in. A person whose worth depends on being virtuous is still engaged in proving their worth, so a threat to that domain (being shown to have acted badly, being criticised morally) would still feel like a threat to the self. Accept any wording that says the dependence or the need to prove worth is the problem, whatever the domain.

40 points: either of these, in the learner's own words. (a) Costs to others: someone who needs to be good may need others to be less good or less right, putting the people around them in the position of being bad or wrong, so the costs fall on others even if the person themself does well. (b) Defensiveness: Crocker expects that even people with internal contingencies react to threats to those contingencies with defensiveness, derogation, hostility or aggression, and she was testing this. Either (a) or (b) earns the full 40.

Cap at 40 if the answer says Crocker's data show that virtue-based self-worth causes more problems for the person, because in her freshman study virtue went with fewer problems.
feedback-instructions:: The learner was asked why Crocker would not accept that basing self-worth on virtue makes a person safe. They received a score. In 70 to 130 words: say what their answer got right. Below 100, name the most important missing point: either that for Crocker the problem is the pursuit of self-esteem itself, whatever the domain, or that virtue-based worth can be costly for others (needing others to be less good) and still leaves the person defensive when that domain is threatened. Mention that she did find virtue went with fewer problems for the students themselves, so the friend is partly right about the data. If they ask a direct question, answer it.
