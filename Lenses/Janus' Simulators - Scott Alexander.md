summary_for_tutor: "Scott Alexander reviews Janus' essay 'Simulators'. Early alignment work imagined AI as an agent, genie or oracle, but a base model like GPT is none of these. It is a simulator that predicts how text continues and wears many masks (characters), such as a romance author or Darth Vader. Drawing on nostalgebraist, he argues that RLHF does not stop ChatGPT simulating; it makes it simulate one character, the Helpful, Harmless, Honest Assistant, whose statements about itself are as simulated as the Vader answers. He gives three possible answers on what this means for alignment, including that GPT+RLHF simulates an agent that could be misaligned, and that a simulator might conjure agents such as a paperclip maximizer. A final section, which he flags as personal speculation, compares the human ego or self to a character played by a predictive brain shaped by reward."
