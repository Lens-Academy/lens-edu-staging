\## 3. Landscape of High-Impact Problems

In this week’s worksheet, we’ll work on ranking high-impact problems that *you* are drawn to working on! Now that we have a framework we can use to select problems, we can look at the landscape of different problems that exist in the world - that may be really impactful for you to work on.

**Expansion before committing at this early research stage.** One of the most common pitfalls we see is NOT considering enough options in the first place. Oftentimes, we may quickly feel biased towards our initial ideas - a form of **confirmation bias** where you stop looking once something feels good enough. At the beginning stages of creating your research project, we strongly recommend considering more options than you may initially think first. This is because the cost of considering (and then discarding) a bad idea is low, since you're not committing any real time to it yet. But, the cost of missing a really impactful idea is high, since you’ll never spend your hundreds of hours working on it.

\## 4. ITN and My Ikigai

Finally, we wanted to highlight that while the ITN framework is helpful for analysing the evidence and current state of a problem area, it doesn't, on its own, factor in how *you* relate to the problem you'd actually be working on. A problem can score well on paper and still be the wrong choice for you. A problem that scores more modestly can sometimes be exactly right, if it's one you can bring real, sustained energy to.

As such, in this week's worksheet, we'll ask you to weigh your ITN analysis alongside a more personal reflection: of your candidate problems, which one do you feel most energised by, most naturally suited to, or most quietly pulled toward?

The goal is to find a balance between what the world needs (based on evidence and analysis) and also what sustains you and brings you meaning and purpose. Even a problem that scores highly on importance, neglectedness, and tractability will be hard to sustain effort on if you don't feel personally engaged by it. On the flip side, we may also fall into the trap of investing a lot of our efforts into a problem that doesn’t genuinely make a difference for the lives of other beings.

By connecting ITN to your Ikigai, the intersection of what the world needs, what you can contribute, and what gives you a sense of fulfilment, you can select a problem that's both impactful and personally sustainable.

Having a strong personal fit (i.e. skills) and a strong sense of meaning and purpose doesn't just make the work more enjoyable, it can substantially increase your impact. When you're genuinely engaged and well-suited to your work, you're more likely to develop real expertise, notice opportunities others miss, persist through the inevitable setbacks, and sustain your impact well beyond this thesis!
