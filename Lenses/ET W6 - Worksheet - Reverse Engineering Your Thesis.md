\## Part 1. Reverse Engineer Your Thesis!

For this first part, we'll start by revisiting what your stakeholders have actually told you they need and want - to make sure our outputs are grounded in reality. From there, you'll restate your Theory of Change from Week 5 in a single clear paragraph, then work backwards from it: what output would actually move that ToC forward, and what research question would produce that output?

This is called reverse engineering because it runs in the opposite direction from how theses are often approached. Instead of picking a topic and hoping something useful falls out the other end, you're starting from the change you want to see and the people you want to reach, and only then asking what research would get you there. By the end of this part, you should have a specific output in mind, and a research question that is strong enough to actually produce it!

\### 1. Insights from Stakeholders

Before choosing anything, review any insights/interviews from stakeholders you have conducted. Any output should be shaped by what real people said they needed, not just what sounded impactful in theory! If you haven’t had the chance to interview anyone yet, you can look online or imagine.

\### 4. Research Question

What research question will result in these outputs? Your question should clearly link your work to the pathway to impact and outputs.

For example, if your desired output is a policy brief, your research question would benefit from being framed as *"How can \[research area\] be translated into recommendations that policymakers can act on?"* rather than something broader like *"What are the key issues in \[research area\]?"*, the first version points directly at producing a brief; the second could generate interesting findings without ever becoming one.

\## Part 2. Continue Stakeholder Outreach

**Instructions**: Please continue your stakeholder mapping and outreach in the Stakeholder Outreach table in Week 4’s worksheet. We recommend identifying and reaching out to >20 stakeholders. If you haven’t received any replies from your Week 4 outreach, we encourage you to reach out to additional stakeholders - this is completely normal and expected. Many professionals are busy or respond slowly, and lack of replies is not a reflection of the quality or relevance of your work. The goal of this exercise is to practice reaching out to people whose work you are interested in and increase the chances of meaningful conversations over time.
