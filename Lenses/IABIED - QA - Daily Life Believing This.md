#### Text
content::
The authors describe how believing in imminent AI risk dramatically affects their priorities -- Soares took a one-third pay cut in 2014 to work on the problem, and Yudkowsky has dedicated his life to it since 2000. They push back on the idea that "if you really believed this you'd do X crazy thing," arguing that living in the shadow of annihilation doesn't have to make you stupid.

TLDR: Both authors have dedicated their careers to this issue at significant personal cost. They push back on "gotcha" arguments that they should take out huge loans or act recklessly if they truly believe the world is ending, arguing that living under existential threat doesn't require abandoning rationality.
Discussion topics:
- Does learning about the authors' personal sacrifices affect how you evaluate their arguments?
- What would *you* do differently if you fully believed their timeline estimates?
