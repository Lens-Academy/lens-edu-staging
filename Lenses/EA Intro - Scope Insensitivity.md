tldr: "People will pay about $80 to save 2,000 birds, and roughly the same to save 200,000. Why does our concern flatline as the numbers explode? This article unpacks scope insensitivity, the bias that stops us feeling the difference between big and vast, and shows how it quietly warps our moral choices when the most individuals are at stake."
summary_for_tutor: "Explains scope insensitivity (scope neglect), the cognitive bias where our valuation of a problem fails to scale with the number of individuals affected. Uses the classic willingness-to-pay study, in which people offered roughly equal amounts to save 2,000, 20,000, or 200,000 oil-threatened birds, to demonstrate the effect. Attributes it to our inability to visualize large numbers, the representativeness heuristic, and collapse of compassion, and argues it especially distorts judgments about large-scale animal suffering. Concludes that we must deliberately adjust our intuitions when many individuals are at stake."
