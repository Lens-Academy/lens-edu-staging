In this exercise, you will dive into the actual structure and content of an international agreement that may be subject to verification. You’ll examine in detail MIRI’s draft [[../Lenses/XLab Verification - v-paper-scher-treaty|International Agreement to Prevent the Premature Creation of Artificial Superintelligence]] and consider how its provisions could be interpreted, implemented, and verified. This includes identifying the fundamental policy objectives underlying each legal provision, the verification mechanisms and evidentiary thresholds required to assess compliance, ambiguities, loopholes, and potential strategies for evading the agreement’s requirements.

Before we dive into the MIRI treaty, first read some provisions from the Practice Guide to International Treaties, which Swiss officials use in the negotiation and drafting of international agreements.

\## Assignment

Open [[../Lenses/XLab Verification - v-paper-scher-treaty|the draft agreement]] prepared by the MIRI Technical Governance Team (Scher, Abecassis, Barnett, and Abeyta).

Participants are not expected to read the document continuously from beginning to end. Instead, examine it article by article, focusing on the questions below.

Read all 4 questions before beginning the examination of the agreement. Answer any 3 of the 4. All four are marked optional here so that the lens can be completed with any three.

#### Question: Open
id:: 5c257204-3687-4257-a4f3-ecb75d22e20f
content:: **1. Distinguish between non-binding and binding provisions**

Examine the Preamble and Article I. Identify and mark the exact point at which the text begins to impose a binding obligation on a specific actor. Explain which grammatical or legal features distinguish the provisions on either side of this point.

Guidance: focus on the verbs used rather than on the section headings.
placeholder:: Quote the words you are talking about.
optional:: true
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 40: where the binding text begins, 25: the right transition, the move from the Preamble's recitals into the operative text, at "Have agreed as follows:" or the first sentence of Article I, where Each Party becomes the subject that is bound, and 15: the exact words quoted. 60: the features that distinguish the two sides, shown with words from each side, 30 each: the Preamble binds no one, because it is a list of recitals opened by participles (Alarmed, Affirming, Convinced, Recognizing, Recalling, Undertaking) that state fears, beliefs and aims, with no main verb until "Have agreed as follows:"; Article I names a specific actor, Each Party, as the subject of verbs that state a duty or a prohibition ("does not develop, deploy, or seek to develop or deploy", "prohibits and prevents", "implements and carries out"), the job that shall, shall not and may not do in later articles. A boundary placed elsewhere and argued from the verbs earns 15 of the 25. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "The Preamble binds no one. Its clauses are recitals opened by participles (Alarmed by ..., Affirming ..., Convinced ..., Recognizing ..., Recalling ..., Undertaking to cooperate ...) that state the Parties' fears, beliefs and aims, and the sentence has no main verb until the enacting formula, 'Have agreed as follows:'. The first binding words follow in Article I: 'Each Party to this Agreement does not develop, deploy, or seek to develop or deploy artificial superintelligence ('ASI') by any means. Each Party prohibits and prevents all such development within their borders and jurisdictions.' From here a named actor, Each Party, is the subject of present-tense verbs that say what it must and must not do; later articles use 'shall', 'shall not' and 'may not' for the same job. Even 'Undertaking to cooperate' in the Preamble, which sounds like a commitment, is still a recital: it states an intention and binds no one."
feedback-instructions:: Say whether the learner quoted the words at the transition or only pointed at a location, and whether the verbs on both sides were named. Give the markers they left out: recognizing, affirming and should on the preambular side, shall, shall not and may not addressed to a named Party on the operative side. If the learner argued for a boundary earlier or later than the first operative obligation, answer the argument rather than overruling it. Two short paragraphs, no praise.

#### Question: Open

content:: **2. Analyse the components of a prohibition**

Select one prohibition contained in Article IV, V, VI, or VIII. Identify:

- what a Party is required to do;
- what a Party is required to refrain from doing;
- what a Party is required to permit.

A prohibition may not contain all three types of requirement. Support your answer with references to the relevant text.
placeholder:: Quote the words you are talking about.
optional:: true
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 10: one prohibition from Article IV, V, VI or VIII is named. 90: its three requirement types, 30 each: what a Party must do, what it must refrain from doing, and what it must permit (let inspectors, other Parties or the CTB do). Each type earns its points when it is filled with a duty the text contains, quoted or closely paraphrased with its article and paragraph, or when the answer correctly says that this prohibition has no duty of that type. A duty the text does not contain earns nothing for its type. Cap at 30 if the answer describes what the article is for rather than the duties it imposes. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "Article IV, the ban on training above the thresholds. Do: report any training run above the Monitored Threshold to the CTB before it starts, with all training code, all training data and an estimate of total FLOP (IV.2 and IV.2(a)), and report and halt any run above the thresholds that the Party discovers (IV.2(g)). Refrain: 'not conduct training runs above these thresholds, and ... not permit any entity within its jurisdiction to conduct training runs above these thresholds' (IV.1), and above the Monitored Threshold use only techniques on the CTB's Whitelist (IV.5). Permit: give CTB staff supervised access to all data (IV.2(a)), accept U.S. and PRC inspectors physically present at the training facility, whose written approval is needed before training starts (IV.2(b)), and let the CTB monitor such runs, providing checkpoints on request (IV.2(d)). The 'not permit any entity' in IV.1 is a duty to stop others, not something the Party is required to permit."
feedback-instructions:: Go through the three requirement types in order, do, refrain, permit. For each, say whether it was filled from the text, correctly marked absent, or missed. If a duty was described that the agreement does not contain, quote back what the text does say. If the answer gave the article's purpose instead of its duties, name the sentence that would have carried each one. Close by telling the learner to carry the same prohibition into question 3. No praise.

For the prohibition selected in Question 2, determine where the method for verifying compliance is established. Is it:

- specified in the relevant article;
- set out in an annex;
- or left to an institution or other body to determine?

Support your conclusion with a quotation from the agreement.

Guidance: compare Articles VII and IX.
placeholder:: Quote the words you are talking about.
optional:: true
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The grader cannot see Question 2, so judge only the answer in front of you. 15: the answer names the prohibition it is about. 40: it places the verification method for that prohibition in one of the three locations (in the article itself, in an annex, or left to an institution or other body to determine), and the placement is right for that prohibition; where the method is split between locations, naming the main one and noting the split earns all 40. 45: a quotation from the agreement that shows where the method lives, such as the article's own inspection or monitoring clause, a note that the details will be set out in an Annex, or a clause leaving the choice of methods to the CTB or another body. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "It depends on the prohibition, and comparing Articles VII and IX shows the two main patterns. For the training ban in Article IV, the method is mostly left to an institution: Article VII.1 says 'The methods used for verification will be determined and updated by the CTB', followed by a list of methods they 'may include'; Article IV itself adds some measures, inspectors who 'shall verify that the training code uses only AI techniques approved on the AI Technique Whitelist' (IV.2(b)) and CTB monitoring whose 'initial details ... would need to be described in an Annex' (IV.2(d)). For the research restrictions in Article VIII, Article IX also leaves it to bodies: each Party 'creates or empowers a domestic agency', and 'the Research Controls division will develop and implement verification mechanisms' (IX.2), with interviews, employment monitoring and embedded auditors given only as examples. For chip production in Article VI, the method is deferred to an annex: 'the monitoring methods would also need to be described in an Annex' (VI.1(a)). For chip consolidation in Article V, the article itself lists the methods: on-site inspections, document and inventory verification and accompaniment of transfers (V.4), plus challenge inspections under Article X. So Article VII hands chip-use verification to the CTB with a menu of methods attached, while Article IX creates institutions and leaves the mechanisms to them."
feedback-instructions:: Say which of the three locations the learner chose and whether a quotation supports it; if none was given, name where in the agreement one would come from. If the answer does not say which prohibition it is about, ask for it in one line rather than guessing. Then, if Article VII was not compared with Article IX, say that the two prohibitions locate their methods differently and ask which of the two their own prohibition resembles. No praise.

optional:: true
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 20: entry into force: the draft has no clause saying whether or when it enters into force (it only refers to its entry into force, for example in deadlines counted from it, and Article XV makes it of unlimited duration); an answer that states an entry-into-force date or procedure the draft does not contain earns none of these 20. 45: withdrawal under Article XV, 10: the ground, a Party may withdraw if it decides that extraordinary events related to the agreement have jeopardized its supreme interests; 10: notice to the CTB 12 months in advance; 15: the conditions during that period, cooperating with the CTB and Executive Council to certify that it will be unable to develop dangerous AI after withdrawal, including removing its covered chip clusters and ASI-enabling assets or rendering them permanently inoperable; 10: after withdrawal it remains exposed to Protective Actions under Article XII. 35: at least one concrete comparison with the model final clauses in Annex E, for example that the models fix when the treaty enters into force while the draft does not, or that the model bilateral clause lets a party terminate at any time by written notice through diplomatic channels, effective six months later, with no ground and no conditions. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "The draft does not say whether or when it enters into force. Several provisions count from 'the Agreement entering into force' (V.3 and V.5, 120 days; XIV.5, a review conference after three years), but no clause sets the conditions, such as a number of ratifications, and there are no signature or ratification clauses; Article XV.1 only says the Agreement 'shall be of unlimited duration'. Withdrawal: a Party may withdraw 'if it decides that extraordinary events, related to the subject matter of this Agreement, have jeopardized the supreme interests of its country', and 'shall give notice of such withdrawal to the CTB 12 months in advance'. During those 12 months it must cooperate with CTB and Executive Council efforts to certify that it will be unable to develop, train, post-train or deploy dangerous AI after withdrawal, in particular by removing all covered chip clusters and ASI-enabling assets to Executive Council-approved control or rendering them permanently inoperable. After withdrawal it remains subject to Article XII Protective Actions if credible evidence indicates activities aimed at ASI. Compared with Annex E: the model multilateral clauses have the Convention enter into force on the thirtieth day after the thirty-fifth instrument of ratification or accession is deposited with the UN Secretary-General, and the draft has nothing equivalent. The model bilateral clause lets either party terminate 'at any time by giving notice in writing to the other through diplomatic channels', effective six months later, with no ground and no conditions; the draft doubles the notice period, sends the notice to the CTB rather than to the other party or a depositary, requires a stated ground, attaches disarmament conditions, and keeps the withdrawing state exposed afterwards."

\## Optional: Reassemble the Parts of an Agreement

A recall drill for the anatomy you just dissected.

**Every agreement that restrains anyone has the same seven organs.** Statements have some of them. Treaties have all of them, though not all healthy. In the next fifteen minutes you will read thirteen short texts, real and fictional, and place each one on the organ it implements. Three of them implement nothing at all.

_13 specimens · about 15 minutes · choose the organ each text implements._

Read the text, not the letterhead. Where a tag is arguable, a defensible second-best answer is accepted and discussed, because expert readers disagree about these too.

**The seven organs, and the bin:**

1. **The rule.** Definitions, thresholds, scope, duration. What exactly is prohibited, for whom, above what line, for how long. Rules live in their definitions.
2. **The claims.** What parties must be able to prove: declared & undeclared. Everything declared is compliant, and nothing undeclared exists. Every mechanism serves one branch.
3. **The evidence.** Access, inspections, monitoring, timeliness. How anyone would know. Judge it by access and by speed against the breakout clock.
4. **The confidentiality bargain.** What the verifier must NOT see. What stays secret, and the machinery that makes intrusion acceptable. No one signs without it.
5. **The adversary.** The threat model provisions are written against. Reconstructed from clauses that only make sense as answers to a specific evasion.
6. **The institution & consequences.** Who verifies, who judges, what follows, exit. Findings, consequences, withdrawal. 'Shall' versus 'may' is the whole organ in two verbs.
7. **The gap.** Verified proxy vs. actual goal; decay; review. Compute is not capability, parties are not the world. Good agreements chase their own proxy.
0. **No organ.** Advocacy: sounds load-bearing, binds no one. The tell is 'should' with no bound actor and no procedure.

The fictional agreement several specimens were drawn from. Two specimens used strengthened versions of Articles VI and VIII; the original text appears here. You will work with this document again in the dissection and the stress test.

**Preamble.** The States Parties to this Protocol, recognizing that certain applications of advanced artificial intelligence may pose risks to international security, have agreed as follows:

**Article I. Definitions.** (1) “Covered training run” means the training of a single general-purpose artificial intelligence model using more than 10²⁶ computational operations. (2) “Covered facility” means any installation with power capacity exceeding 10 megawatts operated for the purpose of artificial intelligence computation. (3) The fine-tuning, continued development, or adaptation of models trained before entry into force of this Protocol shall not constitute a covered training run.

**Article II. Core obligation.** No State Party shall conduct, authorize, or knowingly permit within its jurisdiction any covered training run for a period of five years from entry into force.

**Article III. Declarations.** (1) Each State Party shall, within 180 days, declare all covered facilities and all holdings of applicable high-performance computing hardware exceeding one thousand units, as specified in Annex A. (2) Declarations shall be updated annually.

**Article IV. Monitoring.** Declared covered facilities shall install power metering and workload verification instruments approved by the Technical Secretariat, where technically and commercially feasible.

**Article V. Inspections.** (1) The Technical Secretariat may conduct routine inspections of declared facilities upon fourteen days’ notice. (2) Any State Party may request a challenge inspection of any facility of another State Party; such inspection shall proceed upon approval by a two-thirds majority of the Executive Council.

**Article VI. Confidentiality.** Inspectors shall not access, copy, or transmit model parameters, training data, or source code. Managed access procedures shall be specified in Annex B, to be concluded by the Executive Council no later than two years after entry into force.

**Article VII. Non-parties.** States Parties shall not export applicable high-performance computing hardware to non-parties, except as licensed for verified civilian purposes.

**Article VIII. Non-compliance.** Upon a finding of non-compliance by the Executive Council, the Council may recommend measures to restore compliance, and may refer the matter to the United Nations Security Council.

**Article IX. Withdrawal.** A State Party may withdraw from this Protocol upon ninety days’ notice if it decides that extraordinary events related to the subject matter of this Protocol have jeopardized its supreme interests.

**Article X. Review and amendment.** The thresholds and definitions in Article I may be amended by consensus of all States Parties at a Review Conference, the first of which shall convene three years after entry into force.
