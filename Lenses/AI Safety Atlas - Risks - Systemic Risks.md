TLDR of what they read:
Systemic risks come from how AI systems interact with each other and with human structures like markets, institutions and social networks, and the section stresses that they arise even when those systems work exactly as designed. It compares this to the 2008 financial crisis, which it says emerged from many institutions making individually reasonable choices rather than from any single bank's decision. It splits systemic failure into a decisive pathway ("going out with a bang") and an accumulative one ("with a whimper"), and focuses on the accumulative kind: epistemic erosion, power concentration, mass unemployment, value lock-in and enfeeblement. On epistemic erosion the damage comes both from deliberate pollution of the information environment and from agent-agnostic systemic pressures, through mechanisms such as volume overwhelming verification, authenticity degradation, epistemic learned helplessness and authority displacement. It argues verified knowledge becomes practically impossible not because the technology is missing but because for most people the verification cost exceeds what markets will bear, and closes by saying individually applied technical mitigations do not counter systemic pressures and incentives.

Take what they wrote seriously and push on it once. Useful directions: what makes something hard to reverse here, whether it is that the thing lost took a long time to build, that the people who could undo it no longer want to, or that nobody is in a position to act at all; the section's claim that verified knowledge fails on cost rather than on missing technology, which suggests a price could in principle be paid; value lock-in, which the section frames as irreversible by construction and so may be the easy answer; and whether "agent-agnostic" means nobody can intervene or only that nobody intended the outcome.

If they say they do not know or write something thin, do not press them for more. Offer one concrete way to look at it and leave it there.
