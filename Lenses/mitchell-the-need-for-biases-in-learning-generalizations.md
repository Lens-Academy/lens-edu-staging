tldr: "Can a machine that assumes nothing learn anything? Mitchell's 1980 argument shows a learner with no built-in biases can never generalize past the exact examples it has already seen; it can only look them up. The surprising conclusion: the biases we often try to eliminate are the very thing that makes learning possible."
summary_for_tutor: "A foundational 1980 paper defining 'bias' as any basis for preferring one generalization over another beyond strict consistency with the training data. Mitchell formalizes the generalization problem and the notion of a version space, then argues that a completely unbiased learner cannot classify any instance it has not already seen, performing no better than rote memorization. The paper closes by cataloguing useful classes of bias: factual domain knowledge, the intended use of the learned generalizations, knowledge of the training source, a preference for simplicity and generality, and analogy with previously learned concepts."
title: "The Need for Biases in Learning Generalizations"
