title: "Something to Protect"
tldr: You get better at reasoning when something you care about more than being rational is at stake. Then you judge your methods by whether they work, and you can admit when your idea of rationality was wrong.
summary_for_tutor: Yudkowsky argues that people grow as rationalists only when something matters to them more than rationality itself, more than their comfort or their pride in being rational. His examples are Japanese fiction where power comes from someone to protect, science winning over authority because it worked better, and a dilemma between saving 400 lives for certain and a 90% chance of saving 500. If your daughter is among the 500, you notice the gamble gives her 90% against 80%, and that "rationality means choosing certainty" was the wrong idea of rationality. The question gives that dilemma to Priya, who believes rationality means certainty, and asks for her daughter's chances, what she should conclude about her idea of rationality, and why caring about her daughter more than about being rational helps.

#### Question: Open
id:: 6e918c8c-ae44-4e63-a498-a30401c54209
content::
The essay asks you to choose between two options: save 400 lives for certain, or have a 90% chance of saving 500 lives and a 10% chance of saving none. Your friend Priya believes that being rational means picking the option that is certain to work, so she picks the 400. Then she learns that her own daughter is one of the 500 people, and she does not know which one.

1. What chance does her daughter have of being saved under each option?
2. In your own words, what should Priya conclude about her idea of rationality?
3. Why does it help Priya that she cares more about her daughter than about being "rational"?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The scenario: one option saves 400 of 500 people for certain, the other saves all 500 with 90% probability and none with 10%. Priya believes rationality means choosing the certain option and picks the 400, then learns her daughter is one of the 500 (she does not know which one).

30: Gives the daughter's chances correctly: 80% with the certain option (400 of 500 are saved) and 90% with the gamble. Give 15 if only one of the two figures is right.

40: Says Priya should conclude that her idea of rationality was mistaken. Rationality is not about choosing certainty but about choosing what actually gets the result she wants, here the better chance of saving her daughter. The point is that her conception was wrong, not that rationality itself gave a bad answer.

30: Explains that caring about her daughter more than about being "rational" (or more than her pride in being a rational person) gives her a reason to notice the mistaken rule and drop it. If what she cared about most were following her idea of rationality, or her pride in it, she would keep the rule and accept the worse odds.

Cap at 40 if the answer says the certain option is still the right choice for Priya because certainty is what rationality requires.

Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "With the sure option 400 of 500 are saved, so her daughter has an 80% chance. With the gamble she has 90%. So Priya should see that 'rational means certain' was the wrong idea of rationality, because it gives her daughter worse odds. Since she cares more about her daughter than about looking rational, she has a reason to drop the rule instead of defending it out of pride."
feedback-instructions:: The student has just read Eliezer Yudkowsky's essay "Something to Protect" and answered a question about it. Key ideas from the essay:
- With 400 of 500 saved for sure, any one of the 500 has an 80% chance. With the gamble, 90%.
- Many people think rationality means choosing only methods that are certain to work. The essay treats that as a wrong conception of rationality.
- When your idea of rationality gives a bad result, the right reaction is "I had the wrong idea of rationality", not "rationality gave me the wrong answer".
- You only learn this if something matters more to you than your pride in being a rationalist. Caring about an outcome more than about your methods is what pushes you to improve the methods.
