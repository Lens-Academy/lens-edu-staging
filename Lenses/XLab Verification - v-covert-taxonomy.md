\## Before the case

At this stage, identify the move, the channel, and the actors. Do not decide which route is "best" yet. A realistic scheme may combine one primary route with several supporting tactics.

You will come back to this table in the last lens of this module, after you have run the red-team and blue-team exercise, and score the routes on feasibility, detectability, and durability. Rank them now and you are ranking a guess.

| Evasion route | What the actor tries to do | Primary layers targeted | Actors who might participate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Conceal the real actor | Use front companies, nominee owners, brokers, affiliates, borrowed accounts, or layered ownership so the apparent customer is not the true controller or beneficiary. | Identity checks; customer screening; procurement; payments; beneficial ownership | State agencies; firms; brokers; corporate-service providers; cloud customers; procurement staff |
| 2. Divert controlled hardware | Obtain chips or servers through false end-user information, transshipment, misclassification, relabeling, theft, straw buyers, or unauthorized rerouting. | Semiconductor supply chain; customs; export controls; chain of custody | Buyers; distributors; freight forwarders; customs brokers; straw purchasers; supplier insiders |
| 3. Hide an undeclared cluster or facility | Operate equipment that was never declared, conceal additions inside a known facility, or rely on legacy or otherwise unregistered hardware. | Facility declarations; chip registries; power and construction monitoring; inspections | Data-center operators; facilities staff; suppliers; utilities; security personnel; public officials |
| 4. Split the operation across sites | Divide one development project among multiple smaller clusters so that no individual site appears to cross a monitoring or registration threshold. | Cluster definitions; networking; hardware registration; distributed operations | Researchers; infrastructure engineers; network operators; hardware owners; site technicians |
| 5. Repurpose legitimate infrastructure | Use a known, authorized cluster for an unauthorized purpose, for example report training as inference or present prohibited capability work as safety, maintenance, civilian, or defensive research. | Workload declarations; scheduler and cloud logs; research oversight; code and data review | Frontier developers; cloud customers; military or intelligence units; infrastructure staff; project managers |
| 6. Falsify declarations or provenance | Alter or invent records about chip locations, compute use, model origin, data, code, checkpoints, evaluations, or utilization. | Self-reporting; logs; audits; model and data provenance | Researchers; compliance staff; system administrators; executives; auditors; contractors |
| 7. Steal or copy model weights | Obtain weights or related artifacts through insider access, cyber intrusion, supply-chain compromise, physical access, or abuse of legitimate permissions. | Model security; identity and access management; cybersecurity; human layer | Employees; contractors; cyber operators; intelligence services; partner organizations |
| 8. Distill or extract capability | Query a model and train a new model to imitate parts of its behavior. This can transfer useful capability without reproducing the teacher model's exact weights. | APIs; account identity; query monitoring; downstream training | API customers; proxy account networks; model developers; data and training teams |
| 9. Exploit a boundary in the rule | Remain formally below a compute, hardware, or reporting threshold while using better algorithms, data, architecture, post-training, inference-time compute, or combinations of smaller activities to reach the policy-relevant result. | Definitions; thresholds; exemptions; measurement rules | Researchers; developers; lawyers; policymakers; standards bodies |
| 10. Attack the verification system | Spoof or disable monitoring, compromise attestation, tamper with sensors or logs, corrupt or coerce personnel, suppress reports, or flood investigators with misleading signals. | Hardware root of trust; monitoring devices; evidence systems; inspectors; reporting channels | Security engineers; hardware specialists; insiders; inspectors; officials; cyber operators |

assessment-instructions:: This asks the learner to decompose the Training Through the Pause scenario from the module's opening lens using the ten-route table on this page. Two primaries are defensible and both score full marks when defended: route 7, steal or copy model weights, on the ground that the operation is impossible without the Orion-4 parameters and everything else is delivery; or route 5, repurpose legitimate infrastructure, on the ground that the prohibited act in the pause agreement is the training run, and the run happens on authorized rented hardware under a false declared purpose. What is graded is the coherence of the decomposition, not the choice. Award about 40 points for a primary named and defended, and about 60 for the supporting tactics, which should include at least three of: route 1, conceal the real actor, since Lattice and the subcontractors are the layer between the workload and Sable; route 4, split the operation across sites, since the workload is divided among related accounts; route 6, falsify declarations or provenance, since the accounts describe the work as protein inference, benchmark evaluation and safety research; route 9, exploit a boundary in the rule, since each account is deliberately kept under the agreement's review threshold. Require the observer clause: the answer should say what Northstar, the reseller, the data centre and the corporate registry each see, because the scenario's own conclusion is that the operation is made inconclusive rather than invisible. Deduct for listing five or six routes with none named primary, since that is the failure this lens warns against. Deduct for naming route 3, hide an undeclared cluster or facility, as primary, since nothing in the scenario is undeclared hardware. No generic praise.
feedback-instructions:: Say whether the primary was defended or merely asserted. Name any supporting tactic the learner missed, using the route number and its one-line description. If the learner listed routes without ranking them, say so directly: the point of the decomposition is that a scheme has a spine and the rest is cover. Then ask one question: which single supporting tactic, removed, would most raise the chance the operation is caught? One turn.
