The learner first reads the 12 items of the Self-Compassion Scale Short Form and answers, ungraded, which items a depressed person would probably agree with. Then three readings. (1) Muris and Otgaar 2023 (narrative review and meta-analysis), the paragraph on measurement: the Self-Compassion Scale and the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale both contain reverse-scored items for the negative counterparts (uncompassionate self-responding: self-criticism, isolation, rumination), which may constrict links with wellbeing and inflate links with psychological problems; they say self-compassion advocates have dismissed this as 'the differential effects fallacy'. (2) Neff 2022, 'The differential effects fallacy' (Mindfulness), two excerpts: Muris and colleagues argue that because the uncompassionate items predict psychopathology more strongly, the two halves are separate constructs and the total score inflates the link; Neff replies that self-compassion is a bipolar continuum with qualitatively distinct ends, so there is no reason both ends should predict outcomes equally strongly; her temperature thought experiment (coldness predicts frostbite more than warmth does, yet temperature is one construct). Neff declares no competing interests. (3) Muris and Otgaar 2022 reply (abstract): they refute the continuum argument and say separating the two halves gives more valid information on protection and vulnerability. #### Text
content::
Almost every study of self-compassion measures it with Neff's Self-Compassion Scale or its short form ([Muris and Otgaar 2020](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Muris2020.pdf)). So whether the research findings hold depends partly on what that questionnaire measures. Here is the short form:

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/neff-self-compassion-scale-short-form-scs-sf]]
from:: When I fail at something important to me I become consumed by feelings of inadequacy.
to:: I'm intolerant and impatient towards those aspects of my personality I don't like.

#### Text
content::
Six items (2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10) describe kindness, common humanity and balance. The other six (1, 4, 8, 9, 11, 12) describe self-judgement, isolation and being consumed by feelings, and are scored in reverse ([SCS-SF](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/SCS-SF-information.pdf)).

#### Text
content::
Peter Muris and Henry Otgaar, psychologists at Maastricht University, have criticised the scale since 2016. Here is their objection in brief, from a 2023 review. **Uncompassionate self-responding** means the reverse-scored half of the scale: self-judgement, isolation and over-identification, which means being consumed by one's feelings.

from:: Measurement issues might also be partially responsible for the observed overlap
to:: and hence this remains a timely and important topic for further scientific inquiry.

content::
Neff replied in a 2022 commentary titled "The differential effects fallacy". In it, **CS** means compassionate self-responding (the kind half of the scale) and **UCS** uncompassionate self-responding (the reverse-scored half).

#### Question: Open
id:: 36ba2942-1e8b-4a4b-9db9-fe78ee2aadc1
content::
What is Neff's main reply?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 100 points for this idea in the learner's own words: self-compassion is one continuum (a bipolar continuum) running from uncompassionate to compassionate self-responding, so the reverse-scored items belong to it, and it is expected, not a problem, that one end predicts outcomes such as depression more strongly than the other. Different strengths of association at the two ends do not show that there are two separate constructs. Accept the temperature example (coldness predicts frostbite more than warmth does, yet temperature is one thing) as a way of stating this idea. Give 50 points if the answer only says that Neff defends the total score or calls the critics' reasoning a fallacy, without saying why.
feedback-instructions:: The learner was asked for Neff's main reply to Muris and Otgaar. They got a score. In 50 to 100 words: confirm what they got right. Below 100, name what is missing: her claim that self-compassion is one continuum from uncompassionate to compassionate responding, so the two ends can predict outcomes with different strength without being two constructs, as with cold and warmth for temperature. Do not say who is right. If they ask, say the dispute is open and that they can score both halves in their own experiment. At most 2 replies. No generic praise.
