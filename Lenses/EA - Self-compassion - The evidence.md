#### Text
content::
Neff said in her talk that research supports her claims "unequivocally". This page checks four of them: that self-compassionate people do better, that self-compassion can be trained, that it beats self-esteem, and that it does not cost motivation.

**Do self-compassionate people do better?** Most of this evidence is correlational ([Muris and Otgaar 2020](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Muris2020.pdf)): researchers give people a self-compassion questionnaire and see what else goes with high scores. Neff's own review reports meta-analyses that find moderate to large links between higher self-compassion and less depression, anxiety and stress ([Neff 2023](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf)). Correlations like these cannot show what causes what. People who are less depressed may simply find it easier to be kind to themselves.

**Can it be trained?** That needs randomised trials. A 2019 meta-analysis by Madeleine Ferrari and colleagues pooled 27 randomised trials of self-compassion interventions. Compared with control groups, the interventions improved all 11 outcomes they looked at. The effects were large for eating behaviour (g = 1.76) and rumination (g = 1.37), and moderate for self-compassion (0.75), stress (0.67), depression (0.66), mindfulness (0.62), anxiety (0.57) and self-criticism (0.56). Group courses did better than individual ones ([Ferrari et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12671-019-01134-6)).

An **effect size** such as g says how far the average trained person moved compared with the controls, measured in units of how much scores vary between people. By a common convention 0.2 counts as small, 0.5 as medium and 0.8 as large ([Cohen 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19565683/)). In a **waiting-list control** the comparison group gets nothing yet, so any course that offers hope and attention tends to look good against it.

The first trial of Neff and Germer's eight-week Mindful Self-Compassion course shows what the early evidence looked like. 25 people took the course and 27 waited. The course group gained more in self-compassion, mindfulness and wellbeing, and their gains were still there six months and a year later. The authors list the limits themselves: the participants were mostly highly educated middle-aged women who already meditated, and there was no active control group. The waiting group was not surveyed again, so the later measurements have no comparison ([Neff and Germer 2013](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/Neff-Germer-MSC-RCT-2012.pdf)).

Gilbert's compassion-focused therapy has its own, smaller evidence base. An early review found 14 studies, three of them randomised. It called the therapy promising, especially for people high in self-criticism, and said that larger, high-quality trials were needed before it could count as evidence-based ([Leaviss and Uttley 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4413786/)). **Does it beat self-esteem?** This was the centre of Neff's talk. In her large survey with Roos Vonk (2,187 people), self-compassion went with more stable self-worth over eight months, less social comparison and less anger, and self-esteem, but not self-compassion, went with narcissism. In their second study the two were equally good predictors of happiness, optimism and positive mood ([Neff and Vonk 2009](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/publications/NeffVonk.pdf)). #### Text
content::
A correlation (r) of 0 means no relation and 1 means perfect agreement. At 0.65 the two measures overlap a lot. On this summary, self-compassion and self-esteem go with wellbeing about equally, and neither replaces the other.

**Does kindness cost motivation?** Experiments by Juliana Breines and Serena Chen found the opposite: people prompted to be self-compassionate about a weakness or a failure were more motivated to improve and, in one experiment, studied longer. Each experiment had about 70 to 90 people ([Breines and Chen 2012](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/publications/selfimp.motivation.pdf)). We did not find a large replication. The experiments page lets you run a version on yourself.

**A problem with the questionnaire.** About 96% of empirical studies of self-compassion up to 2019 used Neff's Self-Compassion Scale or its short form ([Muris and Otgaar 2020](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Muris2020.pdf)). Half of its items ask about self-judgement, isolation and being consumed by feelings, scored in reverse. Critics argue that those items overlap with symptoms of depression and anxiety and so inflate the link between self-compassion and mental health. Neff replies that self-compassion is one continuum and the full scale measures it correctly ([Neff 2023](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf)). The optional page after this one sets out both sides.

**Who earns money from this.** Neff is the author of the books *Self-Compassion* and *Fierce Self-Compassion* and teaches paid workshops ([self-compassion.org](https://self-compassion.org/workshop-series/)). With Germer she founded the Center for Mindful Self-Compassion, a nonprofit that trains teachers of the course and sells courses ([Neff 2023](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf), [CMSC](https://centerformsc.org/products/foundations)). Her review states that she is not aware of affiliations or financial holdings that might be seen as affecting its objectivity (same source). Gilbert founded the Compassionate Mind Foundation, a registered charity that sells training in compassion-focused therapy ([Compassionate Mind Foundation](https://www.compassionatemind.co.uk/)). Much of the trial evidence comes from the people who developed these programmes. That does not make it wrong, but module 3 showed why it matters.

Last, what the evidence does not cover. The trials measure symptoms such as depression, anxiety and stress, and self-ratings of wellbeing, mostly at the end of a course. We did not find a study that asks whether self-compassion leads to the kind of lasting, unconditional peace this course is about.

A wellness newsletter writes: "Science has settled it. Self-compassionate people are far healthier, an eight-week self-compassion course transforms your wellbeing for good, and self-compassion is simply better for you than self-esteem."

Give three separate problems with this claim, using what this page reports.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100: up to 34 points for the first valid problem, 33 for the second and 33 for the third. Each must be a distinct problem from this list, stated in the learner's own words, with enough detail to show what is wrong.

1. The link between self-compassion and health is mostly correlational, so it does not show that self-compassion causes better health (it could run the other way, or both could come from something else).
2. The pooled trial effects are mostly moderate, not transformative, and a waiting-list comparison (as in the first course trial) flatters any course.
3. The first trial of the eight-week course was small (about 25 per group), had no active control, used mostly highly educated middle-aged women who already meditated, and its six-month and one-year follow-ups had no comparison group, so "for good" is not shown.
4. Self-compassion is not clearly better than self-esteem: the two overlap strongly (r = 0.65) and go with wellbeing and problems about equally, each adding something of its own. One study found them equal predictors of happiness and positive mood.
5. The questionnaire used in almost all studies includes reverse-scored items about self-judgement, isolation and being consumed by feelings, which critics say overlap with symptoms and inflate the link to mental health.
6. Much of the evidence comes from people who developed and sell the programmes (books, paid workshops, training organisations), which can bias results.
7. The studies measure symptoms and self-rated wellbeing, mostly at the end of a course, not lasting change in how people feel.

If the learner gives two problems that are the same point in different words, credit only one. Ignore extra problems beyond three unless one of the first three is invalid.
