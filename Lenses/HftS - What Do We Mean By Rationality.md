title: 'What Do We Mean By "Rationality"?'
tldr: Rationality means two things, forming beliefs that match reality and choosing actions that get you what you value. It is not acting like Mr. Spock, and arguing over the word's definition settles nothing.
summary_for_tutor: Yudkowsky defines epistemic rationality (systematically improving the accuracy of your beliefs, a map that matches the territory) and instrumental rationality (systematically achieving your values, "winning", where values include everything you care about, other people too). Probability theory and decision theory are the formal standards, as in the example where people rate "Bill is an accountant who plays jazz" as more likely than "Bill plays jazz". The full math is intractable for humans, so there is a further art of learning our own flaws. He refuses to argue about the word itself. If you say "the rational thing is X but the right thing is Y", you are using "rational" for something else and should replace it with more specific language (the "foozal" test). The question asks the student to apply this to Sam, who says the rational thing is the high-paying job but what he really wants is to teach, and to rewrite Sam's sentence without the word.

#### Question: Open
id:: 458d4ab0-bc2a-485b-b233-eab86b9195f1
content::
Sam says: "The rational thing for me to do is take the high-paying finance job, but what I really want is to be a teacher."

We don't know whether Sam cares more about the money or about teaching.

1. In the essay's sense, instrumental rationality means systematically achieving your values. Using that meaning, why can't both halves of Sam's sentence be true as he worded them, whichever he cares about more?
2. Rewrite his sentence without the word "rational", so it says what he might mean. Pick whichever reading you find most likely.

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The essay defines instrumental rationality as choosing the actions that best achieve your values, where your values include everything you care about. So "the rational thing is X, but what I want is Y" is a confused sentence: whichever job best achieves everything Sam cares about is the rational one, so the rational choice cannot be set against what he really wants. The question leaves open which he cares about more. The essay says a sentence like this uses "rational" to mean something else, and the fix is to replace the word with more specific language.
