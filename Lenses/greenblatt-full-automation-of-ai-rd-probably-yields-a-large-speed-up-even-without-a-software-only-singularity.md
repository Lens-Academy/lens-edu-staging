summary_for_tutor: "A technical note by Ryan Greenblatt arguing that full automation of AI R&D probably produces a large acceleration in AI progress even without a \"software-only singularity\" (the runaway case where r is significantly greater than 1). It makes two main points: (1) automation gives a substantial one-time speedup (with r=0.7, a model estimates roughly 3.5 years of progress in the first year even holding compute fixed); and (2) after automation, additional compute has higher returns than when humans were the bottleneck, because compute now also makes the AI research workforce smarter, faster, and cheaper, creating a feedback loop that can multiply the progress from any given rate of compute scaling even when the loop is subcritical. It illustrates with the AI Futures Model, lists indirect accelerants (investment, compute consolidation, hardware R&D), and caveats that compute-scaling rates may be lower by the time automation arrives."
