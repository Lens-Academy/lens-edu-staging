This week focuses on how your thesis can make a real-world impact by addressing a problem that is important, neglected, and tractable. Here, we aim to tackle one of the potential pitfalls of research - selecting the wrong problem (such that solving it doesn’t result in any impact).

**Important Note:** We want to highlight that impacting the world through your thesis is a really ambitious goal, especially given your time and resource constraints. As such, the goal of these exercises is not to put pressure on *definitely* having an impact on solving a massive problem. Instead, they are designed to help you practice thinking strategically about how research can increase the chance of real-world impact, and to train the mindset of approaching high-impact work, both for your thesis and beyond.

\## 1. Why does selecting the right problem matter?

Choosing which problem to work on is one of the highest-leverage decisions you'll make, not just for this thesis, but at many decision points across your life and career. This week is designed to help you land on a problem you genuinely believe matters and where you're well-placed to make a real difference.

As a concrete illustration of why this choice matters. Consider two diseases that each affect large numbers of people. Multiple Sclerosis (MS) accounted for roughly 0.034% of the global disease burden in 2021 ([IHME](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-compare/)). Malaria accounted for roughly 1.91%, **around 54 times as much.** Contributing meaningfully to solving either disease would be a genuinely valuable contribution to reducing suffering. But if you could only make progress on one, a comparable amount of effort directed at malaria would likely reduce far more of the global disease burden than the same effort directed at MS.

This isn't to say MS research is a bad choice. It's to illustrate a simple but easy-to-forget point: within any category of "important problem," **some options will help you use your resources, time and effort to go dramatically further than others.** Choosing *quickly and without much thought* may result in you working on a problem that's trivial, already solved, or too difficult to realistically move forward - such that even amazing execution can end up producing very little real-world impact. That's what the rest of this week is designed to help you avoid!
