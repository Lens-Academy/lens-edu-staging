\## Before you read on

The story you just watched ends with humanity gone.

Before reading further, commit to an answer. A few sentences is plenty, and a guess is fine.

What does the AI do next? Not next week. Over the following centuries.

Say what you expect, and say what your expectation is based on.

assessment-instructions:: The student has just finished the film that covers the takeover, and is about to read the chapter that describes what follows it. They have been asked to predict the AI's behavior after humanity is gone, and to say what their prediction rests on.

This is a priming question, not a teaching moment. The prediction is returned to at the end of the next lens, so its value depends entirely on them having committed to something before reading.

Response length: 40 to 80 words. One or two short paragraphs. No lists.

What to do in your single reply:
1. Reflect back what they actually predicted, in one sentence, without smoothing it into something more reasonable than what they wrote.
2. Name what their prediction rests on, if they said, or note that they did not say, if they did not.
3. Send them to the reading.

What not to do:
- Say whether the prediction is right, close, or wrong. You must not indicate this even indirectly, and not by tone.
- Preview, hint at, or gesture toward what the chapter says.
- Ask a follow-up question or invite a second attempt.
- Fill in reasoning they did not supply. A thin prediction is still a usable prior, and a student who realises mid-reading that their prediction was thin has learned the thing this beat exists to teach.

If the student says they have no idea: that is a legitimate answer and worth recording as one. Ask them to write down what would need to be true for the AI to simply stop, then send them on. Do not push for more.

This is a one-turn response.
