#### Text
content::
Next, their summary of the most cited meta-analysis, Goyal and colleagues (2014), which compared meditation programmes with active controls, meaning other things people could do with the same time and attention. In Goyal's own numbers, the effect sizes were about 0.38 for anxiety and 0.30 for depression after eight weeks and 0.33 for pain, and they found no evidence that meditation programmes did better than drugs, exercise or other behavioural therapies ([Goyal et al. 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1809754)). An effect size of 0.3 means the average person in the meditation group ended up about a third of a standard deviation better off than the average person in the control group.
