\## One experiment before next week

This week you wrote your bugs list, named your Hamming question, and tried Murphyjitsu. Now pick one thing to try in your real life before next week. It could be:

- running Murphyjitsu on a plan you are about to start
- using the five-second version at the moment you named: :answer{id="604e32b4-c25e-46db-9cee-9e6df38d7b88" fallback="not named yet"}
- one of the opening-session tips, such as trying something new as written before you change it

Keep it small enough that you will actually do it. Next week starts by showing you this answer and asking what happened. As on the first page, judge an experiment by whether it changes something on a real bug, not by whether you enjoyed doing it. We think "It didn't work" or "I didn't do it" are useful answers next week too, because they tell you what to change.

#### Question: Open

content::
\## Your experiment for this week

1. What will you do, and on which bug or plan?
2. When will you do it? Name a day, or the moment that will remind you.
3. How will you know it changed something? Name something you could see or count, not just how it felt.
placeholder:: 1. Murphyjitsu on my plan to email Dr. X about a project (bug 7) 2. Wednesday, right after lunch 3. The email is sent by Friday
feedback-instructions:: The learner has committed to one real-life experiment for the coming week in the Applied Rationality Workshop, using something from week 1 (Murphyjitsu, its five-second version, or an opening-session tip). They answered what, when, and how they will know it worked. Ungraded. Reply in under 100 words, short paragraphs, no lists, at most two replies. Run a quick Murphyjitsu on the experiment itself: ask how surprised they would be, from 1 to 10, if next week they report that it did not happen. If below about 7, ask for the most likely reason and one small change that would head it off. Also check part 3: if it is only a feeling ("I'll feel more organised"), ask what they could actually observe. If part 2 has no specific time or trigger, ask for one. Never choose a different experiment for them, no generic praise.
