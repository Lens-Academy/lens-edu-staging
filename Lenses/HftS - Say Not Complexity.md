Yudkowsky objected that saying "complexity" does not concentrate your probability mass. It puts a cause behind something mysterious that feels like an explanation but isn't. Complexity is a real concept with legitimate uses (Kolmogorov complexity, judging whether a hypothesis is too complicated). The mistake is the skip-over of the part you do not understand, whatever word covers it, and it happens by default, below the level of words. He and Marcello said "magic" ("X magically does Y") as a placeholder that reminds you of an unsolved gap, instead of "complexity" or "emergence", which create an illusion of understanding. #### Question: Open
id:: f96edfef-a5db-4739-b534-122906540cbe
content::
A nature documentary says: "Termites build huge mounds with air vents that keep the inside cool, even though no single termite has a plan. This happens because, when enough simple termites interact, complex behaviour emerges."

By the essay's standard, what is wrong with this as an explanation, and what would an honest version of it say instead?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

Background the grader needs: the essay argues that words like "complexity" and "emergence" often sound like an explanation while skipping over the part nobody understands. They do not say how the thing happens and do not narrow down what we should expect, so they would fit almost any outcome. The essay's fix is to mark the gap openly, for example by saying "magic" as a reminder that something is still unexplained, and to keep thinking about how the step actually happens.

60: The flaw. The answer says that "complex behaviour emerges" names the mystery without explaining it: it does not say how the actions of individual termites produce a mound with vents, and it would fit almost any result, so it creates a feeling of understanding without any.

40: The honest version. The answer gives a version that admits the gap, for example "termites somehow build vents without a plan, and we don't yet know how" or "the termites magically coordinate", or that states what would still have to be explained (what each termite does that adds up to a vented mound).

Cap at 40 if the answer accepts the documentary's sentence as a good explanation.

Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "'Complex behaviour emerges' sounds like an explanation, but it doesn't say how the termites actually manage it. You could say 'emergence' about any result, so it tells you nothing you could check. An honest version would say: 'Termites build vented mounds without a plan, and we don't know how. Somehow each termite's simple actions add up to the vents, and working out what those actions are is the open question.'"
feedback-instructions::
The student has just read Yudkowsky's "Say Not 'Complexity'" and judged an "emergence" explanation of termite mounds. The essay's key ideas: "complexity" or "emergence" placed behind something mysterious feels like an explanation but does not concentrate probability mass. It fits any outcome. The real mistake is skipping over the part you do not understand, whatever word does the skipping. The fix is to mark the gap, for example with "magic", and keep asking how the step actually works. Complexity itself is a legitimate concept in other uses.

Reply in 80 to 150 words. Short paragraphs only. No lists. Do not over-validate. Avoid generic praise (great job, excellent, well done). Start with the strongest specific part of the answer. If the score is below 100, name the most important gap, often an "honest version" that just swaps in another covering word ("self-organisation", "swarm intelligence") instead of admitting what is unknown. Ask one question that lets them fix it, for example what the sentence would lead you to expect that a different result would not. At 100, confirm briefly.

If the student says they do not understand, do not repeat the question or dismiss it. Give one concrete foothold from the essay, such as Marcello's reaction: "now I've got to think about how X might actually happen". If their next message still does not attempt the question, rephrase the whole task in different terms. Judge only against what the essay and the question supply. Allow at most 2 follow-up turns, then close with one sentence on what they can now do and tell them to move on.
