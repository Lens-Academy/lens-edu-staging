#### Text
content::
This week is a slice of the [Iliad Intensive](https://iliad-intensive.org/), a month-long, full-time AI alignment course. Each day here is one day of their course: their worksheets and reading lists, adapted for working online with a tutor and a small group. The material is licensed CC BY 4.0 by Principles of Intelligence; the authors of each day are named on its pages.

Today is **A.1, AI Alignment Introduction**, written by Leon Lang (Iliad's curriculum lead). His note to teachers describes the day:

> "this is mostly a conceptual day: The aim is to understand a basic decomposition of the problem of safe AI into specifying an alignment target and solving the technical alignment problem, which can itself decompose further. We also discuss the basic role that goal-directedness plays in the risks, and basic views on the risk landscape. The aim is that after this day, all students are broadly on the same page about what the alignment problem is, and have some basic impression of how the rest of the course relates to the problem."

The rest of the week asks why deep learning works at all. Today supplies the reason to care. Leon's own reading guide puts it this way: the out-of-distribution behavior of AI systems "depends crucially on their inductive biases", so "understanding the relationship between inductive biases and the goal formation of AI systems is therefore very important." Tomorrow's author, Zach Furman, makes the stronger claim that understanding how deep learning works "is necessary for any *systematic* (generalizing OOD) alignment interventions or measurements to even be possible". You will be asked what you make of that claim on Day 2.

\## What you will be able to do by the end of today

In Leon's words, you:

- understand the basic decomposition of risks into AI misalignment, misuse, power grabs, and others;
- can explain different alignment targets like coherent extrapolated volition, intent alignment, or AI that follows a constitution;
- can reason about training stories and outer and inner (mis)alignment, challenges to these notions, and the relationship to inductive biases;
- are aware of discussions on whether AI systems develop goals and understand the basic arguments for instrumental convergence;
- are aware of foundational discussions on the level of risk and different high-level approaches to solving the AI alignment problem.

\## How today runs

Four sessions of about 40 minutes each. In each one you spend about 20 minutes reading, concentrating on whichever of the offered texts interests you most, then write a short answer to one discussion prompt. The tutor replies to what you wrote. In Iliad's classroom these prompts were group discussions; here your group meeting at the end of the day picks up the threads you wrote about.

Expected time: about 3 hours on this page and the four sessions, plus the group meeting.

\## Before you read anything

Answer from what you think now. There is no wrong answer, and you will come back to this at the end of the day.

In two or three sentences: what should a very capable AI system be aligned *to*? Then name one way a system trained toward that target could still end up pursuing something else.
