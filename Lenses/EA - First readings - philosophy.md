Open any school you want a first look at. Each box has one short source.

#### Callout: Stoicism
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
The opening of Epictetus's *Enchiridion*, a short handbook of Stoic practice, in Elizabeth Carter's 1758 translation. Chapters 1 and 5, about 3 minutes.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/epictetus-the-enchiridion]]
from:: Some things are in our control and others not.
to:: be prepared to say that it is nothing to you.

#### Article
from:: Men are disturbed, not by things
to:: will place blame neither on others nor on himself.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Epicureanism
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
Epicurus's *Letter to Menoeceus*, his own summary of how to live: [Letter to Menoeceus](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html) (about 10 minutes).
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Philosophy of the self
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
David Hume, in *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739), looks for his self by introspection and reports what he finds. About 3 minutes.

#### Article
source:: [[../articles/davidhume-hume-texts-online]]
from:: farther, what must become of all our particular perceptions
to:: or of the materials, of which it is compos'd.
#### End Callout

#### Callout: Existentialism, absurdism and nihilism
collapse:: closed
#### Text
content::
A 7-minute video by Kurzgesagt on "optimistic nihilism", the view that a universe without given meaning leaves you free to enjoy your life. It is a popular take, not academic philosophy, and the video says so itself.

#### Video
source:: [[../video_transcripts/kurzgesagt-in-a-nutshell-optimistic-nihilism]]

#### Text
content::
For Camus's absurdism, section 3 of the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry [Albert Camus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/) covers suicide, the absurd and happiness in *The Myth of Sisyphus*. For Sartre, his lecture [Existentialism Is a Humanism](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm) sets out his view in his own words.
#### End Callout
