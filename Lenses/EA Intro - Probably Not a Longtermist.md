An EA insider walks through why she is not sold, structured around three cruxes: doubts about utilitarian population ethics, skepticism that humanity is morally admirable, and uncertainty over whether we can reliably shape a future more than a century out. She argues for making the future good, not just long or big."
summary_for_tutor: "Presents Denise Melchin's case for why she is 'probably not a longtermist,' organized by crux across three dimensions. On the quality of the long-term future: she finds total utilitarianism uncompelling (valuing reducing misery over creating happy people), doubts humanity is inherently admirable, and is unsure the future will be better than today. On the length of the future: she is skeptical it can be very long without soft totalitarian lock-in. On the ability to influence it: she doubts impact reliably persists beyond roughly 100 years except by shaping or preventing bad lock-in scenarios. For each crux she states what evidence would change her mind, concluding she favors 'traditional neartermist' approaches unless avoidable bad lock-in scenarios are on the horizon."
