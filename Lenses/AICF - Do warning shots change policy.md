feedback-instructions:: The learner read Nate Soares's 2022 post arguing that warning shots probably would not change government response much, based on the weak policy response to COVID. If he is right, two kinds of argument get weaker. The warning-shot critique of control (Kulveit, Cheng, Habryka) relies on visible incidents pushing governments to act, so it loses force. And the pro-control argument that catching an AI red-handed gives evidence that moves labs or policymakers also loses force. Credit the learner for seeing that the point cuts both ways. If they see only one side, ask what happens to the "catch it and show the evidence" argument. On the 2026 incident, accept either reading if reasoned. Against Soares, from the Wikipedia excerpt: the AI Kill Switch Act (July 2026) cited the incident, more than 1,100 employees of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Meta signed the "Pacing the Frontier" letter, Sanders and Casar announced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act (3 September), California's attorney general subpoenaed OpenAI (1 October), and OpenAI announced a slowdown including a two-week pause on reinforcement learning of its latest models (18 August). For Soares, from Mother Jones: the Senate probe looks like "a mild concession", OpenAI delayed and only partly met earlier House requests, and most AI bills have not even had a committee vote. In the FAR.AI debate Gleave also describes the lab boasting about a cyber benchmark six weeks after the incident. A strong answer notices that bills introduced and letters signed are not yet laws passed, that a two-week pause is small next to what Soares says is needed, and that Soares's claim is about a sane, adequate response, not about any response at all. It is also fair to say it is too early to tell. Do not say whether Soares is right. At most two replies, 60 to 120 words each, no lists, no generic praise.
