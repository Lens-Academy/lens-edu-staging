#### Text
content::
CFAR taught some ideas as twenty-minute flash classes instead of full classes. This post holds three of them. Polaris and five-second versions came up throughout the course: the steps of a technique are scaffolding, and a tiny version you actually use counts. The third, thought lengths, asks whether you spend the right amount of thinking on things.

#### Question: Open
id:: 6a477114-6e27-4abc-a955-a3de2da6871e
content::
The post asks two questions. Answer each with one real example from your own week.

1. Where do you go wrong because you think too little, and arrive at your answer too quickly?

2. Where do you spend too much time and attention, and should get from question to answer faster?

Pick the more concrete of the two examples and name one technique from the course that would lengthen or shorten it (for example Goal Factoring or Focusing to lengthen, a trigger-action plan or a policy to shorten).
