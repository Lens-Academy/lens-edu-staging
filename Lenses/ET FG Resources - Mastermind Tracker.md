\## Mastermind Session Tracker

Create a duplicate of the Mastermind Tracker for your own use. This tracker is used to ensure that everyone has the chance to participate in mastermind sessions across the cohort. The idea is that from **Weeks 2 to 7**, each participant should have the opportunity to be featured in at least **one mastermind session**, with the goal that most participants go **twice** over the course of the program. Masterminds are one of the most powerful learning and support mechanisms in the program. Tracking participation carefully ensures that no one is overlooked, no one dominates the space, and everyone experiences being deeply supported by their peers

\### How to schedule Masterminds

- Plan mastermind rotations in advance so that: Everyone is selected at least once AND the distribution is roughly equal across participants
- In a **4-person peer group**, this typically means two people are the focus every week, so each person will be the focus **every two weeks**.

\### Advance notice for participants

To increase engagement and the quality of sessions:

- Inform participants one week in advance when they are selected for a mastermind
- Let them know they should come prepared with a draft of their worksheet, and from there, a real challenge, decision, or block, or something meaningful they want group input on
- This advance notice helps participants treat the session as a serious, high-value opportunity rather than an on-the-spot activity.

\### Mastermind Session Prompts

*These are questions and prompts you could use during the mastermind session to support in the feedback and ideation process.*

**Clarifying the situation**

- Can you say a bit more about…?
- What led you to this question or uncertainty?
- Can you give an example or context for this challenge?
- How does this connect to your overall thesis or impact goal?

**Perspective & advice**

- If you were in their shoes, what would you do?
- What’s one thing you’d try next if this were your challenge?
- What do you notice about this problem that they might be overlooking?
- Are there ways you’ve approached a similar challenge that could help?

**Exploring alternatives/possibilities**

- Have you thought about…?
- What might happen if you tried this approach instead?
- Are there other angles, methods, or frameworks you could explore?
- What assumptions are you making that could be tested or challenged?

**Sharing resources/connections**

- Are there sources, papers, or tools you’ve come across that could be relevant?
- Do you know someone else who has tackled a similar problem?  
  Are there communities, datasets, or networks that could help?

**Deepening reflection**

- How does this connect to your values, strengths, or Ikigai?
- What would success look like in this scenario?
- If this problem were solved perfectly, what difference would it make?

**Process guidance**

- Try to **ask first, suggest second** - help them think, don’t just solve it for them.
- Encourage participants to **build on each other’s ideas** instead of repeating the same advice.
- Keep feedback constructive and actionable — focus on **what can be tried or tested** next.
