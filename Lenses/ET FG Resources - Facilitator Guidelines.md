\## 💛 Facilitator Guidelines

- **Guide the process, not the answers:** You don’t need to have all the answers - your role is to hold space for curiosity and exploration. Encourage reflection with open-ended questions (“What makes this topic feel meaningful to you?”). Use paraphrasing and mirroring to help participants clarify their own thinking.
- **Practice, not perfection:** The goal of these sessions is to practice applying new ways of thinking  - not to produce a perfect Theory of Change or flawless answers. Encourage participants to experiment, make mistakes, and share incomplete ideas.
- **Ground ideas in the real world**: Encourage participants to bridge reflection with action by talking to real people who are affected by or working on their topic. Remind them that impact grows when research connects with real-world needs and stakeholders.
   - **Prompts you can use:**
      - “Who could use your findings in practice?”
      - “Is there someone you could talk to this week to learn more about how this problem shows up in the real world?”
      - “What would this idea look like if it had to make sense outside of academia?”
   - This grounding helps participants move from abstract ideas to actionable understanding and helps them see their research as part of something living and meaningful.
- **Focus on individual meaning and impact:** The goal is not just to create a better thesis, but to help each participant feel more connected to meaning and impact in their current and future work. Remind participants that impact often unfolds over time. Even if their thesis doesn’t change the world immediately, it can build the skills, mindsets, and relationships that lead to future impact.
- **Focus on community and connection:** Beyond skills, this program is about building community and a meaningful, impactful life together.
   - Create an environment where participants feel safe to be vulnerable, explore uncertainty, and support each other.
   - Value connection as much as content - slow down if a rich discussion or shared reflection emerges.
- **Normalize uncertainty and iteration:** Many participants will feel unsure about their thesis direction or impact - that’s okay and expected. Reassure them that clarity comes through exploration and feedback, not instant certainty.
- **Celebrate small insights and shifts in thinking**: Celebrate small shifts - a reframe, a clearer question, a moment of self-awareness, or a connection made between ideas.
