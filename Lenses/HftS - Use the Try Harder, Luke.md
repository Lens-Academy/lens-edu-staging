summary_for_tutor: Yudkowsky writes an imagined outtake from The Empire Strikes Back in which Mark Hamill argues with George Lucas about the scene where Luke fails to raise his X-wing from the swamp and gives up. Hamill's points are that the ship had already started to rise (a feasibility demonstration), that Yoda, with 800 years of experience, said it was doable, that Luke has not shown it is impossible, only failed once, that the stakes are huge (without the ship he is stuck on Dagobah), and that he should keep trying, rest and try again, at least for five minutes. Lucas closes with the point that people would not try for five minutes before giving up even if the fate of humanity were at stake. The question asks the student to apply two of Hamill's arguments to a friend who calls a bug impossible after an hour and two partly successful attempts.

#### Question: Open
id:: 752fa8f5-568b-427a-bcd4-2edbd078970e
content::
A friend has a bug that stops his app from working, and the app is due in a week. After an hour and two attempts that each fixed part of the problem, he says: "It's impossible. I'll drop the feature." A more experienced colleague had told him it should be fixable.

Using the arguments Mark Hamill makes about Luke in the essay, give two reasons your friend should not accept "impossible" yet, applied to his situation.
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. In the essay, Mark Hamill objects to the scene where Luke gives up on raising his X-wing from the swamp. His arguments: the ship had already started to rise, which shows the task can be done, Yoda, with centuries of experience, had said it was doable, Luke has not shown it is impossible, he just failed once after a very short try and should keep going or try again rested, and the stakes are huge, since without the ship he is stuck. The friend's case matches each of these.
50 for each of two reasons. Any two of: (1) the partial fixes show the problem can be solved, so "impossible" is not supported, (2) an experienced colleague said it is fixable, (3) he has tried only briefly, an hour and two attempts, which is far too little to conclude it cannot be done, and he could keep trying or come back to it rested, (4) a lot is at stake (the feature, the app working by the deadline), so it is worth much more effort before giving up. A reason stated only about Luke, not tied to the friend, earns 25. If two reasons are the same point in different words, credit only one.
