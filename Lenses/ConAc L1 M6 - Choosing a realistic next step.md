At this point in the course, you probably still have substantial uncertainties about what you want to work on. Some of these uncertainties may be difficult to resolve through further reading.

You might be unsure whether you would enjoy research, whether your professional experience is valuable to AI safety organisations, or whether a project you have in mind would actually be useful to anyone.

Each of these questions has a reasonably inexpensive empirical test.

If you are considering research, spend a few weeks trying to answer a small research question. If you are considering a career transition, show your CV to someone who hires for the relevant positions. If you have an idea for a tool, find three people who might use it and discuss their needs. If a particular organisation's work appeals to you, find out whether there is something you could help them with.

Matt Beard recommends a portfolio approach in [How to speedrun your AI safety job search and upskilling](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/jCBtpJovoFgKtpaZP/how-to-speedrun-your-ai-safety-job-search-and-upskilling). He suggests distributing your efforts between ambitious applications, positions for which you are already a plausible candidate, and opportunities that would help you develop missing professional skills. You can pursue several possibilities before deciding which deserves a substantial commitment.

You have spent this course developing your understanding of AI risk, examining theories of change, exploring research agendas and learning how the field operates. We would like you to finish it by putting some of that knowledge to use.

Choose something you could realistically do within the next seven days:

- Submit an application for a position, fellowship or grant.
- Write a short project proposal and send it to someone who could evaluate it.
- Arrange a conversation with someone whose work you are considering pursuing.
- Find a small research question and begin investigating it.
- Identify a problem an organisation faces and offer to help solve it.

Try to choose an action whose consequences extend beyond your own learning, even if those consequences are modest.

A useful outcome of this course would be for you to know more about AI safety. A considerably better outcome would be for something useful to happen in the world because you took it.
