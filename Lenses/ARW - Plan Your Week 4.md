content::
\## Your experiment for this week

This week's experiment is the CoZE experiment you just designed. If you decided CoZE does not fit you right now, plan an experiment with Turbocharging or Taste & Shaping instead.

A CoZE experiment works if you come back with data: how it felt, what actually happened, and how that compares with what you expected. Finding out that a fence is in the right place counts. Next week's module opens with this plan and asks what happened.

#### Question: Open
id:: fe8087d3-d915-4aa9-8337-126108467ac2
content:: Write down your plan for this week.

1. What exactly will you try? Describe the small, safe version.

2. When and where? Name a day and a time.

3. What will you pay attention to while you do it, inside you and around you?

4. Which bug from your list, if any, does it connect to?

5. What would count as useful data, whichever way it goes?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is committing to one real-life experiment for the coming week in a CFAR-style applied rationality workshop. Usually it is a Comfort Zone Exploration (CoZE) experiment: a small, safe try of something they feel somewhat blocked from, after checking that both futures (doing it, not doing it) feel acceptable, followed by paying attention and digesting without forcing. Sometimes it is a Turbocharging change to how they practise a skill, or a Taste & Shaping change to what they reward.

Reply in 3 to 5 sentences. Check the plan against three things and name only the weakest one: is there a specific day and time, is the experiment small and safe enough to actually happen, and do they say what they will watch for? If the plan reads like forcing themselves through a fear ("I'll make myself"), ask whether both outcomes would really feel okay to them. Never push toward a bigger or braver experiment. If the plan is solid, say so in one sentence and stop. No generic praise. If they are taking this course with a group, suggest they bring the plan to the meeting.
