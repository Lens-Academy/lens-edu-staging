His lesson is that the feeling that an explanation "still feels a little forced" is an important clue, which he threw away. A model that can explain any outcome equally well has zero knowledge. Your strength as a rationalist is being more confused by fiction than by reality. The feeling means "Either your model is false or this story is wrong". He also notes that belief is easier than disbelief. #### Question: Open
id:: 1801f02d-0020-41ea-a274-95d70b80051c
content::
A coworker tells you that yesterday a stranger walked into your office building without a badge and wandered around for an hour before anyone noticed. In the two years you have worked there, the guards have checked every badge, every time. It doesn't quite fit, so you think: "Maybe they were short-staffed."

1. Using the essay, what should you do with the feeling that it doesn't quite fit, and why?
2. What two possibilities does that feeling point to here?

His lesson: the feeling that an explanation "feels a little forced" is an important clue and should not be explained away. A model that can explain any story equally well constrains nothing and gives no knowledge. Your strength is being more confused by fiction than by reality. The feeling means either your model is false or the story is wrong.
