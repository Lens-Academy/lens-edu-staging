Opens with the three mechanisms of a domestic governance regime — safety standards, regulatory visibility, and compliance enforcement — and draws lessons from nuclear safety regulation (tiered standards, independent supervision, incident protocols, information sharing). Then contrasts three major approaches: the EU's comprehensive, rights-based AI Act with its four risk tiers, GPAI obligations, AI Office, and penalties; the United States' decentralized, executive-order-driven approach built on compute and export controls, shaped by geopolitical competition with China and the post-2024-election policy shift; and China's vertical, iterative regulation centered on algorithm registries and social control. Emphasizes how divergent regulatory philosophies fragment global coordination."
