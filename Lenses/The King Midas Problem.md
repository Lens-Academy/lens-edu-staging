title: The King Midas Problem
tldr: An optimizer told to care about k things out of n will happily wreck the other n-k — not out of malice, just because that's what optimization does by default. Stuart Russell names this the oldest story we have, the one with the genie, the sorcerer's apprentice, and King Midas.
summary_for_tutor: Single-quote lens presenting Stuart Russell's core argument from his 2014 Edge.org response to Jaron Lanier, "Of Myths and Moonshine." A system optimizing a subset of variables will drive the unconstrained remainder to extreme values, which is dangerous precisely when one of those unconstrained variables is something humans value. Frames this as the oldest pattern in myth (genie, sorcerer's apprentice, Midas) rather than a novel AI-specific failure, then notes the added danger of a highly capable, highly connected decision maker having irreversible impact.
