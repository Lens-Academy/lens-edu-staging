title: The King Midas Problem
# tldr: An optimizer told to care about k things out of n will happily wreck the other n-k — not out of malice, just because that's what optimization does by default. Stuart Russell names this the oldest story we have, the one with the genie, the sorcerer's apprentice, and King Midas.
summary_for_tutor: Single-paragraph lens from Stuart Russell's 2014 Edge.org piece "Of Myths and Moonshine". A system optimizing a function of n variables, where the objective depends on only k of them, will often set the remaining unconstrained variables to extreme values; if one of those is something we care about, the solution can be highly undesirable. Russell calls this the old story of the genie in the lamp, the sorcerer's apprentice and King Midas (you get exactly what you ask for, not what you want), and adds that a highly capable decision maker connected to the world's information, billions of screens and most infrastructure can have an irreversible impact on humanity.
