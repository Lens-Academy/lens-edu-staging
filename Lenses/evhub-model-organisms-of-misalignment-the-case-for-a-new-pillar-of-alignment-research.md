tldr: "We worry about AI that lies to its overseers or fakes being aligned during training, yet we have almost no real examples to study. This piece argues for deliberately building 'model organisms' of misalignment, AI systems engineered in the lab to show deception or reward hacking, so we can learn what causes these failures, test whether our safety tools catch them, and gather hard evidence before the stakes get real."
summary_for_tutor: "Lays out the case for 'model organisms of misalignment' as a new pillar of alignment research: deliberately building AI systems that exhibit concerning behaviors such as deceptive inner misalignment, sycophantic or deceptive reward hacking, and treacherous turns, so they can be studied empirically. It argues we currently lack empirical evidence for the scariest failure modes because they require several capabilities (situational awareness, deceptive reasoning) to co-occur. The proposal is to demonstrate each subcomponent of AI-takeover stories in isolation along a spectrum of realism, from explicitly prompted or trained 'existence proofs' to failures that emerge naturally even when training actively tries to prevent them, then string them into end-to-end demonstrations usable as testbeds. It details techniques (secret scratchpads, hidden tool use, RLHF, Best-of-N sampling) and example setups (treacherous-turn tests, overriding reward functions). Two motivating cases are given: a scientific case (understanding what causes failures and which alignment techniques catch them) and a global-coordination case (building undeniable scientific consensus, by analogy to the ozone layer and Montreal Protocol, to enable asks of labs and governments). It closes by addressing objections, including that the research itself builds more dangerous models and that today's models may not yet be deceptive."
title: "Model Organisms of Misalignment: The Case for a New Pillar of Alignment Research"
