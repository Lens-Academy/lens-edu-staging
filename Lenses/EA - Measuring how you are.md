A self-experiment needs a measure you can take again and again in the same way. This page gives you four, each from its source. The first takes two minutes, and you will take it now as a starting point that module 8 comes back to.

\## The WHO-5 Well-Being Index

The World Health Organization's five-question wellbeing index ([WHO 2024](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mental-health/who-5_english-original4da539d6ed4b49389e3afe47cda2326a.pdf?sfvrsn=ed43f352_11&download=)) is, in the words of a [systematic review by Topp and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1159/000376585), "among the most widely used questionnaires assessing subjective psychological well-being". The instrument below is reproduced from the WHO version, © WHO 2024, licence CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO.

For each of the five statements, choose the answer closest to how you have been feeling **over the last two weeks**. Higher numbers mean better wellbeing.

| Over the last two weeks | All of the time | Most of the time | More than half of the time | Less than half of the time | Some of the time | At no time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I have felt cheerful and in good spirits | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| I have felt calm and relaxed | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| I have felt active and vigorous | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| I woke up feeling fresh and rested | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| My daily life has been filled with things that interest me | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

Add the five numbers to get a raw score from 0 to 25, then multiply by 4 to get a score from 0 to 100. The WHO document notes that a score below 50 has been suggested as a cut-off for poor mental wellbeing and as a reason for further assessment, for example for depression. If your score is below 50, we suggest talking to a doctor. That is worth doing whatever this course is about.

\## An equanimity questionnaire

The WHO-5 measures how well you have been. Many of the schools in this course aim at something else: **equanimity**, which Desbordes and colleagues (read in "How published research goes wrong") define as "an even-minded mental state or dispositional tendency toward all experiences or objects, regardless of their origin or their affective valence". Catherine Juneau and colleagues built a short questionnaire for it, the **EQUA-S** ([Juneau et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7350915/), open access, CC BY). It has two parts. **Even-minded state of mind** is staying calm when things go wrong. **Hedonic independence** is not being carried away by pleasant things or the wish for them. The authors wrote the items in French and give the English wording below. They tested the scale once, on 265 French-speaking adults from the general population rather than on experienced meditators, so it is a young scale.

Answer each item from 1 (never or very rarely) to 5 (very often or always). The paper gives no time frame. For repeated use we suggest "in the past week".

Even-minded state of mind:
1. Whatever happens I remain serene
2. I am not easily disturbed by something unexpected
3. I can hardly tolerate uncomfortable emotions (R)
4. I can easily get carried away by an annoyance (R)
5. I feel that I am a calm person, even in moments of stress and tension
6. Stress situations emotionally disturb me (R)
7. It's hard for me to be serene during the difficult moments of everyday life (R)
8. I feel that the problems in my life are temporary and that they have solutions

Hedonic independence (all reversed):
1. When I look forward to doing something pleasant, I can only think about that (R)
2. When I anticipate a situation or something that I like, I get very excited (R)
3. When I desire an object, I feel a strong attraction to get it quickly (R)
4. I am very excited when I am given something pleasant (like a good surprise or a gift) or when something pleasant happens to me (R)
5. I often wish to prolong the moments when I feel a strong pleasure (R)
6. I can't stop doing something I like (R)

For items marked (R), score 6 minus your answer. Then average each part, so that each gives a score from 1 to 5 where higher means more equanimity. The paper's table also marks item 8 of the first part as reversed, although its wording is positive and it relates to the scale the same way as the positive items. We think that mark is a typo, so score item 8 as written.

\## Sampling moments of your day

Questionnaires like these ask you to remember and summarise weeks. Daniel Kahneman and Alan Krueger describe two methods that ask about single moments instead ([Kahneman and Krueger 2006](https://kahneman.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf3831/files/kahneman/files/development_dk_abk_2006.pdf), pages 9 to 11).

In the **Experience Sampling Method**, a device "prompts them several times during the course of the day (or days) to answer a set of questions immediately". People note what they were doing and with whom, and rate how much they feel each of several feelings, "such as feeling angry, happy, tired and impatient". For yourself: set a phone app or random alarms to prompt you several times a day, and each time rate a few feelings you care about, the same ones every time.

The **Day Reconstruction Method** is cheaper. "Respondents are first asked to fill out a diary summarizing episodes that occurred in the preceding day. Next they describe each episode by indicating: when the episode began and ended; what they were doing (by selecting activities from a provided list); where they were; and with whom they were interacting." They then rate feelings for each episode "on a scale from 0 ('Not at all') to 6 ('Very Much')". The authors report that it "provides an efficient approximation to the results of the more demanding Experience Sampling Method". For yourself: each evening or the next morning, list the episodes of the day and rate each one from 0 to 6 on the same few feelings.

If you want to design an experience sampling study more carefully, Fritz and colleagues wrote [a guide to ten design decisions](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459241267912) (optional, long). The optional page "Describing experience carefully" in the first half of this module shows a related method that interviews you about single random moments.

\## Putting them together

We suggest one main measure taken every day, chosen to fit the claim (for example an evening rating of irritability from 0 to 6, or a short day reconstruction), plus the WHO-5 or the EQUA-S every one or two weeks as a broader check. This combination is our suggestion, not a standard from the sources.
