#### Text
content::
This module is about one claim: that how you treat yourself when you fail or struggle shapes how you feel, and that kindness toward yourself works better than self-criticism. **Self-compassion**, in the sense used here, means meeting your own failures and pain with the kind of care you would give a good friend. Kristin Neff, the psychologist who first defined it as a research construct ([Neff 2023](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf)), lists the one below as Exercise 1 on her website ([self-compassion.org](https://self-compassion.org/exercises/exercise-1-how-would-you-treat-a-friend/)). Do it before reading anything about the theory. Short notes are enough. What matters is what you say to the friend and to yourself, and in what tone, not the details of any situation.

feedback-instructions:: The learner just did Kristin Neff's "How would you treat a friend?" exercise in five answers: how they respond to a struggling friend, how they respond to themselves, what differences they notice, what reasons or fears explain a difference, and what might change. You can see their earlier answers on this page. This is the first page of the module, before any theory. Reflect back in one or two sentences the clearest contrast (or similarity) in what they wrote, using their own words. If they named a reason for treating themselves differently (for example a fear that kindness would make them lazy or complacent), name it as a question the module takes up, without answering it: Neff's talk and the evidence page discuss whether self-criticism motivates. If they found no difference, accept that and say the module still lets them compare the views. Do not give advice, do not teach Neff's three components, do not probe private details they did not offer. If they describe serious distress, respond kindly and suggest talking to someone they trust or a professional. Reply in 50 to 100 words, no generic praise. One turn, then send them on.
