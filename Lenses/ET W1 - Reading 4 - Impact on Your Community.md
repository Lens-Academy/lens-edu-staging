\## 5. Dimension #3. Impact on Your Community 👥

Impact isn’t only global - your thesis can also influence your immediate community: peers, family, professors, or professional networks. We think this dimension of impact is also highly underrated! Impact in your community can ripple outward, multiplying far beyond your individual project. Even if your work doesn’t directly solve a pressing problem, it can still be high-impact by inspiring others to take action or explore meaningful paths. This could look like:

- **Bringing high-impact topics into underexplored disciplines:** Many important problems are under-discussed in specific departments or fields. By tackling one of these topics in your thesis, you can highlight its relevance and put it on the academic map, opening doors for future research and discussion.
- **Shifting academic attention and priorities:** Your work has the potential to influence professors, supervisors, and researchers - encouraging them to take neglected problems seriously. We’ve heard from students who have managed to raise their professors’ awareness of pressing global challenges that previously undervalued them
- **Inspiring others toward impact:** Your work might encourage classmates or colleagues to pursue paths towards impact they’d never considered! When others see you working on meaningful, underappreciated topics, they may be motivated to pursue impactful research or careers themselves.

By strategically choosing a topic that is both high-impact and underrepresented, your thesis doesn’t just advance knowledge - it creates leverage points in your academic community, multiplying the effect of your work beyond what you could achieve alone.

\## 6. Closing Thoughts: Your Thesis as an Ikigai Experiment

Rather than asking, “What is my Ikigai? Or, what is my impactful career path?”, we recommend asking: ***“What could my thesis help me learn about my Ikigai? How could I intentionally design my thesis to explore what feels meaningful and energising for me?”***

Your Ikigai is **not** something you need to have fully figured out, as it is not a fixed destination! It will continue to evolve as you gain experience, test different paths, and learn more about yourself and the world. Importantly, **clarity comes from action, not just reflection**. And your thesis is one of the best low-risk, high-information experiments you can run to explore your Ikigai through action!

A high-impact thesis isn't just about solving a problem (impact on the world), it's about actively learning how you can contribute meaningfully over the long term (impact on you), often alongside the very people who help get you there (impact on your community). When your thesis aligns with your emerging Ikigai, you're more likely to:

- Choose problems you genuinely care about
- Execute well and persist through challenges
- Build a career path that's impactful, meaningful, and sustainable
- Have fun and find joy in the work you do

As you move through this program, we encourage you to keep returning to this question: ***How is my thesis helping me learn what kind of impact I want to have - and what my reason for being is?***

Through this lens, your thesis becomes more than an academic requirement. It becomes a space for inner work, experimentation, and learning - helping you create your sense of meaning and purpose for yourself.
