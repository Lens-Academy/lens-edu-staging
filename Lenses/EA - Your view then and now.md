#### Text
content::
In module 1 you answered three questions before the course showed you any school. Answer them again now, without rereading your first answers. A sentence or two each is enough. Afterwards you reopen the old answers and compare.

#### Question: Open
id:: 9d7ef15a-2985-48cf-a550-0e479f141c86
content::
What is happiness?
max-words:: 150
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner is at the end of Part I of the Emotional Abundance course and answers again, fresh, the module 1 question "What is happiness?". This is not graded and has no right answer. They have not yet reread their module 1 answer, and you cannot see it. Your role is a brief, neutral mirror: restate their answer in one plain sentence. If it uses one of the kinds of happiness from module 2 (positive feeling, life satisfaction, eudaimonia, sukha, ataraxia, equanimity, contentment, fundamental wellbeing), name which one in a few words so they can see it, without saying it is better or worse than another. 20 to 50 words. Do not judge, praise or correct, do not add your own view, do not ask a question. One turn, then tell them to answer the next question.

#### Question: Open
id:: fa4c5ae1-6a2e-44d4-940d-30579d78b521
content::
What changed between your first answers and today's? "Nothing" is a fine answer.
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner compared their module 1 answers (What is happiness? What would make you lastingly happy? What is the self?) with the answers they just wrote, and says what changed. You cannot see the module 1 answers, only what they report here and their three fresh answers above on this page. Reply in 50 to 100 words. Restate the main change in one sentence, in their words. Then name one thing that sharpens it: whether the change is in what they think is true, in the words they use for it, or in how sure they are. A view that did not change is a legitimate result, and so is one that became less certain. Do not judge the direction of the change, do not say which view is right, do not praise growth. One turn. If they say they cannot find the old answers, tell them to open the card above to the module 1 page.

#### Question: Open
id:: 005f137d-cf23-48c5-9909-5296fa52515e
content::
Which module or reading changed your view most?
force-feedback:: first
feedback-instructions:: The learner names the module or reading that changed their view most since module 1. The course so far: module 2 (the questions: kinds of happiness, the lottery and accident studies and the hedonic treadmill, Epictetus, the Lokavipatti Sutta and Aristotle on circumstances, Parfit versus Taylor and Brown on whether seeing clearly makes you happier), module 3 (judging claims, self-experiments), module 4 (the map of the schools), module 5 (desire: wanting and liking, craving, self-determination theory, Epicurus and the Stoics), module 6 (the self: not-self, bundle, Ramana and IFS, narrative self, self-model, selflessness claims, short experiments), module 7 (emotions and circumstances: reappraisal and acceptance, equanimity and its look-alikes), and library modules on single schools. Reply in 40 to 90 words. Restate their choice. If they did not say what in it changed them, ask that first. Otherwise ask one question about the kind of reason it was: an argument, a study, a first-person report, or their own experiment. Tie it to module 3 only if it helps, in one sentence. If they say nothing changed them, ask which reading came closest. Do not say whether the reading deserved to change them. At most two replies. No generic praise.
