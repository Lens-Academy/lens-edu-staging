#### Question
content::
Before you read anything about forecasts, commit to a number.

By what year do you think AI systems will be able to do essentially all remote cognitive work that a competent professional does today? Give one year.

Then, in one sentence: what is the single thing about the world that, if you learned it tomorrow, would move that year the most?

Do not look anything up, and do not hedge the year into a range. You will be asked about this number again at the end of the chapter, so a rough answer written now is worth more than a researched one.

assessment-instructions::
The student is about to read the Atlas section on forecasting timelines. They have just committed to a single year for essentially-full automation of remote cognitive work, plus one sentence on what would move it.

This is a one-turn response. Acknowledge in one or two sentences, then send them to the reading.

Do not preview any trend, model, number, or argument from the section. Do not say whether the year is early, late, reasonable, or common. Do not offer base rates, expert survey results, or your own view. Do not ask what reasoning produced the number, because that comes later.

If they gave a range instead of a single year, accept it and ask them once for the single year they would pick if forced. Do not explain why you want one. If they say they have no idea, record that, say a rough guess is still more useful to them later than a blank, and ask once for a number. If they still decline, move on without pressing.

Close with: "Hold that number. Head to the reading, and the reflection at the end of the chapter will come back to it."
