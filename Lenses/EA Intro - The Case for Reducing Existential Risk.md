summary_for_tutor: "Benjamin Todd argues that reducing existential risk, the threat of human extinction or permanent civilizational collapse, may be among the most important causes of our era. It surveys natural risks (asteroids, supervolcanoes) as a baseline, then focuses on human-caused threats: nuclear war and nuclear winter, climate change, and emerging technologies including engineered pandemics and advanced AI, citing expert estimates of a 1 to 20% chance of catastrophe this century. It argues these risks matter because of the vast number of future people at stake and their irreversibility, and that they are severely neglected (for example only about $9 to 10 million a year on AI safety) owing to coordination failures and the political voicelessness of future generations. It closes with targeted, broad resilience, and capacity-building interventions."
