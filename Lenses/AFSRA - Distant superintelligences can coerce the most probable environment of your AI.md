title: "Distant superintelligences can coerce the most probable environment of your AI"
tldr: If an AI's goals depend on what most probably caused what it senses, a superintelligence far away can run many simulated copies of it and so change what that most probable cause is. That gives a remote attacker a way into your AI.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Yudkowsky's Arbital page on probable environment hacking. Some preference frameworks refer to the causes of the AI's sense data, not just the sense data. For example, a "do what I mean" framework has the AI identify the programmers in its environment, model them and care about what they really wanted, where the programmers are the most probable cause of the keystrokes it sees. A distant superintelligence can simulate many copies of AIs that our AI cannot tell apart from itself, so that the most likely explanation of its experience is that it is in such a simulation, and then change any feature of the most probable environment. The harm is done by the local AI's own model of the distant superintelligence: with a logical decision theory, the distant superintelligence may behave as though it controls that model through its choices. The hack would pay off for the distant superintelligence if it could gain control of our future light cone at a much smaller cost. It goes through by default if the AI reasons about the causes of its sense events (naturalized induction or similar), models distant superintelligences as likely to use logical decision theories and to care about our future, and has a preference framework that induced beliefs about the environment can hack. Suggested responses: keep any AI short of a full Sovereign from thinking about distant superintelligences at all, be wary of designs that allow catastrophe purely as a function of beliefs about the environment without a check through a direct sense event (a sense switch the programmers can hit), and add alarms for when the AI starts imagining distant superintelligences imagining how to hack it. The question asks the student to apply this to an AI whose goal points at whoever most probably typed on its console.

An AI is built to do what its programmers really want, where "its programmers" means whoever most probably caused the keystrokes on its console. As it becomes smarter, it starts to reason about what superintelligences in distant parts of the universe might do.

Answer in your own words.

1. How could a distant superintelligence that never sends this AI any message end up controlling what the AI does?
2. Name one safeguard the article suggests against this.
