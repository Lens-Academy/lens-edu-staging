#### Text
content::
Optional extras for this week: pick by curiosity, not completeness.

**Debates and skeptics**
- [Does AI Progress Have a Speed Limit?](https://citp.princeton.edu/news/2025/does-ai-progress-have-speed-limit-ajeya-cotra-and-arvind-narayanan-conversation): Ajeya Cotra and Arvind Narayanan disagree carefully about almost everything this week covered.
- [The case for multi-decade AI timelines](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/the-case-for-multi-decade-ai-timelines): Epoch's strongest in-house argument that all of this takes much longer.
- [Broad Timelines](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6pDMLYr7my2QMTz3s/broad-timelines#GvJ5C3YbngEeSkNMb): why your timeline distribution should probably be wider than it is (read the top Ryan Greenblatt comment too).

**Evidence from current AI**
- [If Mythos actually made Anthropic employees 4x more productive, I would radically shorten my timelines](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/if-mythos-actually-made-anthropic): what productivity claims would actually be timeline-relevant.
- [AIs can now often do massive easy-to-verify SWE tasks](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/ais-can-now-often-do-massive-easy): a capability jump that's easy to miss if you only watch benchmarks.
- [AI's capability improvements haven't come from it getting less affordable](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/ais-capability-improvements-havent): kills a common "progress is just more spend" objection.

**Deeper takeoff modeling**
- [Do the returns to software R&D point towards a singularity?](https://epoch.ai/blog/do-the-returns-to-software-rnd-point-towards-a-singularity): the empirical fight over the r parameter you met in the Forethought report.
- [How quick and big would a software intelligence explosion be?](https://www.forethought.org/research/how-quick-and-big-would-a-software-intelligence-explosion-be#summary): read the summary, then play with the web app; compare your median takeoff to the AI Futures model's.
- [What a Compute-Centric Framework Says About Takeoff Speeds](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/what-a-compute-centric-framework-says-about-takeoff-speeds/#3-long-summary): the "Long Summary" (~1 hr); the earlier generation of takeoff models, partly superseded but still the clearest full framework.
- [Can AI scaling continue through 2030?](https://epoch.ai/blog/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030): power, chips, data; which physical constraint bites first.
- [The Industrial Explosion](https://www.forethought.org/research/the-industrial-explosion): what happens when the feedback loop reaches the physical world.
