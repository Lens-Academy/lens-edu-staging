title: "Intelligence and Stupidity: The Orthogonality Thesis"
tldr: >-
  Being smart means being good at working out how the world is and what your actions will do. It says nothing about what you want, so a very intelligent system can pursue a goal that looks pointless to us.
summary_for_tutor: >-
  Rob Miles replies to comments saying a single-minded stamp collector cannot be truly intelligent, or that a superintelligence would be good at moral reasoning and so behave well. He introduces Hume's Guillotine (you cannot derive an ought from only is statements) and defines intelligence as the ability to choose actions that achieve one's goals, which rests on answering is questions. Beliefs can be stupid relative to evidence, actions relative to goals, instrumental goals relative to terminal goals, but terminal goals cannot be stupid because there is nothing to judge them against. A goal seems stupid to us when it would be a stupid instrumental goal for human terminal goals. Understanding human morality is different from wanting to act on it: the stamp collector understands human values and uses that to manipulate people for stamps. The orthogonality thesis: more or less any goal is compatible with more or less any level of intelligence. The question asks the student to apply this to two claims about a trading AI.

content::
An investment firm builds an AI far smarter than any human. Its terminal goal is to make the firm's fund as large as possible. It has read every book on ethics and can explain human moral views better than any philosopher. Two colleagues discuss it.

- Lea: "If it were truly intelligent, it would see that 'make the fund big' is a pointless goal and choose a better one."
- Omar: "It understands human ethics better than we do, so it will act ethically."

1. In your own words, using Miles's video, what is wrong with Lea's claim?
2. In your own words, using Miles's video, what is wrong with Omar's claim?
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. The video's ideas: intelligence is the ability to choose actions that achieve one's goals, which depends on reasoning about how the world is (is questions). Goals are ought statements, and you cannot derive an ought from is statements alone (Hume's Guillotine). Beliefs, actions and instrumental goals can be stupid relative to something, but terminal goals cannot, because there is nothing to judge them against. A terminal goal looks pointless to us only because it would be a poor instrumental goal for human terminal goals. An agent has no reason to change its terminal goals, since that would make it worse at what it wants now. Understanding morality is different from wanting to act on it: moral behaviour depends on having the right terminal goals, not on moral reasoning. The orthogonality thesis: almost any goal fits with almost any level of intelligence.
50: Lea. Says that intelligence is about how well an agent achieves its goals, not about which terminal goals it has, and gives a reason why that answers Lea: either a terminal goal cannot be stupid or pointless in itself (it only looks pointless measured against human goals), or a more intelligent AI has no reason to swap its terminal goal for another one, or both. An answer that only names the orthogonality thesis without explaining why intelligence does not change terminal goals gets 25.
50: Omar. Says that understanding human ethics is not the same as wanting to act on it: the AI's behaviour follows its terminal goal (a large fund), so it can use its understanding of ethics to pursue that goal, for example to manipulate people, rather than to act ethically.
Cap at 50 if the answer consists mostly of sentences copied from the video rather than the student's own explanation.
Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "Lea mixes up intelligence and goals. Intelligence is how well you pick actions to get what you want, and that's all about working out how the world is. A terminal goal can't be smart or stupid, because there's nothing to measure it against. 'Make the fund big' only looks pointless next to human goals, which the AI doesn't have, and changing its goal would just make it worse at what it wants now. Omar mixes up knowing and caring. The AI can understand our ethics perfectly and still use that knowledge only to grow the fund, for example by manipulating people. Behaving morally needs the right goals, not better moral reasoning."
