summary_for_tutor: "A blog response by Kelsey Piper to a reader frustrated by speculative 'fringe' EA work such as wild animal suffering research. Piper uses counterfactual history (would EA have been abolitionist in the 1840s, or eugenicist in the 1920s?) to argue that a robustly good movement must stay open to arguments that society is deeply wrong, cast a wide net for ways it might be causing harm, and support extending moral concern to overlooked entities, all while keeping most resources on concrete, accountable priorities. She adds a concrete case that welfare biology is a genuinely important nascent research field, and reflects on why intellectual humility makes it easier to value people whose priorities differ from her own."
