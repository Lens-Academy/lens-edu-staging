Establish what is happening before deciding what you think of it. Try to reconstruct the organisation's strategy in terms that its own researchers would recognise. A premature verdict makes subsequent reading an exercise in collecting supporting evidence.

Read critical assessments alongside favourable ones. Pay attention to the substance of disagreements. Are the critics disputing experimental findings, methodological choices, strategic assumptions, or the importance of the underlying problem? These are different disputes and require different evidence.

Look at how an organisation handles being wrong. The AI Futures Project's Grading AI 2027's 2025 Predictions offers an example of researchers revisiting their earlier claims. Comparing their original predictions, subsequent evidence, and revised forecasts can tell you something about both the forecasting model and the team's epistemic practices. Independent assessments would provide additional evidence.

Reconstruct the causal argument yourself. Start with what the organisation has demonstrably accomplished and work through the steps by which these accomplishments might reduce AI risk. Identify the assumptions at each step and examine the evidence for them. This is time-consuming, but it makes it easier to notice where your judgement depends on reputation, personal sympathies, or an assumption you have never examined.

Finally, keep track of the questions you cannot answer. An absence of publicly available information about an organisation's impact is compatible with several explanations, including confidential work, difficulties with impact measurement, and a genuine lack of results.

Sometimes the most useful result of investigating an organisation is a precise account of what you would need to learn before forming a confident opinion about it.

This applies equally to updating your views on the wider field. A successful experiment may strengthen your belief in the feasibility of a particular safety technique while leaving your views on its eventual adoption largely unchanged. Locating your uncertainty at the level of individual claims and causal assumptions allows you to learn from an organisation's work without having to accept its entire worldview.
