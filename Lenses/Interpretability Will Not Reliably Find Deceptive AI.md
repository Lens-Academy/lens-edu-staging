summary_for_tutor: "Excerpt from Neel Nanda's 2025 post, written in a personal capacity. He responds to the argument, as in Dario Amodei's 'The Urgency of Interpretability', that interpretability is a realistic (or the only) path to highly reliable safeguards, since a deceptive AI can fake its outputs but not its internal thoughts. Nanda, who once held this view, calls it an isolated demand for rigor. Interpretability has its own deep limits: superposition and tool error, no ground truth for measuring progress, a messy long tail of behaviour, the difficulty of proving deception circuits are absent, scaling, and possible obfuscation. Neither it nor black-box methods will give high reliability for superintelligence without major breakthroughs. He argues for a defence-in-depth portfolio, lists black-box ideas (reading or editing chain of thought, questioning non-coordinating copies, finetuning, honeypots), and separates evaluation from monitoring, which must be cheap enough to run in production."
title: Interpretability Will Not Reliably Find Deceptive AI
# tldr: What if we could look inside a model and read its intentions? Interpretability research aims to do exactly that — but this article argues it won't reliably catch the most dangerous failures, especially deception, where a model might hide its true reasoning from the very tools designed to inspect it.
