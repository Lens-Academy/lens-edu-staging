#### Text
content::
\## Before anything

Three problems. You have not been taught how to do them and that is deliberate.

Answer them at whatever depth you can reach. Guessing badly here costs you nothing, and skipping costs you the only baseline you will get. Nobody will grade these against a right answer, because for two of the three there is not one.

At the end of the unit you will be given three more problems in different domains, harder than these. The comparison between what you write now and what you write then is what this unit is actually measuring.

Write the arithmetic where there is arithmetic. Descriptions of arithmetic do not count.

#### Question
content::
\## Problem 1

**How many commercial passenger flights will take off worldwide next Tuesday?**

Do not look anything up. Produce a number, and show how you got to it.

Then, separately: which single step in your working is most likely to be badly wrong, and roughly how badly?

max-time:: 12:00

assessment-instructions:: This is a pre-test administered before any instruction. The student has been taught nothing.

DO NOT TEACH. Do not name Fermi estimation, decomposition, reference classes, or the outside view. Do not supply a better decomposition. Do not correct their arithmetic unless they have made an error of more than two orders of magnitude, and then only say so in one clause without fixing it. Any instruction here contaminates the baseline this whole unit is measured against.

Response length: 40 to 70 words. One short paragraph. No lists.

What to do in your single reply:
1. Record what they did in one sentence, neutrally: whether they broke the problem into parts or estimated the whole thing directly, and whether they named a dominant error term.
2. Say nothing about whether it was a good approach.
3. Send them to the next problem.

Do not over-validate. Do not praise. Do not ask a follow-up question. One turn.

#### Question
content::
\## Problem 2

A hospital measured how many of its scheduled operations started on time: 62% in 2020, 74% in 2024.

An administrator extrapolates: 3 percentage points a year, so 100% on-time by 2033.

Write down what that calculation assumes about the operations that are still starting late. Then say whether 2033 is likely to be too early or too late, and why.

max-time:: 10:00

assessment-instructions:: Pre-test, before instruction. The student has been taught nothing about functional form.

DO NOT TEACH. Do not use the words linear, exponential, asymptote, or adverse selection. Do not explain why the residual is harder. Do not tell them the answer is "too early." Do not mention the fractional-progress reading they are about to encounter.

Response length: 40 to 70 words. One short paragraph. No lists.

What to do in your single reply:
1. Record neutrally whether they stated an assumption about the remaining 38%, whether they committed to a direction, and whether they gave a reason for the direction.
2. Do not evaluate any of it.
3. Send them to the next problem.

Do not over-validate. Do not praise. Do not ask a follow-up. One turn.

#### Question
content::
\## Problem 3

A machine either will or will not fail this month. Machines of this type fail in about 10% of months.

Three technicians inspect it separately. None of them talks to the others. They report the chance of failure this month as 55%, 60%, and 65%.

What is your combined estimate, and what rule did you use? Then: is there anything you could learn about the three technicians that would move your combined estimate above 65%, or below 55%?

max-time:: 10:00

assessment-instructions:: Pre-test, before instruction. The student has been taught nothing about aggregation.

DO NOT TEACH. Do not mention extremization, priors, independence, or information overlap. Do not tell them an answer outside the range can be correct. Most students will say 60% and answer no to the second question; that is the expected baseline response and must be left uncorrected.

Response length: 40 to 70 words. One short paragraph. No lists.

What to do in your single reply:
1. Record neutrally what rule they used and what they answered to the second question.
2. Do not evaluate.
3. Close the lens: tell them the unit starts now, and that these three answers will be waiting for them at the end.

Do not over-validate. Do not praise. Do not ask a follow-up. One turn.
