\## Your experiment from now on

Every week of this course ended with an experiment. This one has no report-back page. You will check it yourself, at your first monthly review. If the tutor summed up your plan on the last page, paste that summary below and add what is missing.

#### Question: Open
id:: 39350ddc-9782-4c7f-9b70-585b8da0eb9c
content::
Write down your practice plan.

1. Which one or two techniques will you keep practising?

2. For each, the trigger: "When X, I will Y."

3. When is your first monthly review? Name a date and put it in your calendar now. No later page shows this plan back to you, so paste it into the calendar entry too.

4. A month from now, what would show you that the practice is working on a real bug?
feedback-instructions:: The learner is committing to an ongoing practice plan at the end of a six-week CFAR-style applied rationality workshop: one or two techniques, a trigger-action plan for each technique's five-second version ("When X, I will Y"), a date for the first monthly review, and what would show in a month that it is working on a real problem. Reply in 3 to 5 sentences. Check the plan against three things and name only the weakest one: is each trigger a concrete moment they will notice (not a feeling or a vague time like "when stressed" or "in the mornings"), is there an actual date for the review, and is the sign of success something they could observe on a real bug. If the plan has more than two techniques, ask which one matters most. If they decided to keep no technique, accept that, and check only that the review date is real. If the plan is solid, say so in one sentence and stop. No generic praise, no pressure. If they are taking this course with a group, suggest they share the plan with their accountability buddies at the meeting.
