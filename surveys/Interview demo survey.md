#### Interview
id:: 5c8b1f2d-7e4a-4c93-b2d6-1f9e3a7c5d84
content:: The interviewer asks three short questions about forecasting methods, one at a time. Answer out loud in your own words. It may ask you to say more. It does not grade you aloud.
questions::
- What is reference class forecasting, and when would you reach for it?
- What is a base rate, and how does it change a forecast you are unsure about?
- Name one way a forecast can go wrong even when the base rate is right.
assessment-instructions:: **Question 1: reference class forecasting**
Level 4: names the method (compare the case to a set of similar past cases and start from their outcomes), gives when to use it (a new or uncertain case where inside-view detail misleads), and names one limit (choosing the wrong class).
Level 3: names the method and when to use it, no limit.
Level 2: gestures at "look at similar cases" without saying why or when.
Level 1: cannot describe it.

**Question 2: base rates**
Level 4: defines a base rate as how often the outcome happens across the class, and says an uncertain forecast should move toward it (anchor and adjust).
Level 3: defines it; vague about how it changes the forecast.
Level 2: knows the words, cannot define.
Level 1: cannot describe it.

**Question 3: failure with a correct base rate**
Level 4: gives one concrete mechanism (wrong class for this case, the world changed since the class was collected, or over-adjusting on weak inside-view evidence) and explains why it breaks the forecast.
Level 3: names a mechanism without the why.
Level 2: says "it can still be wrong" without a mechanism.
Level 1: nothing usable.
