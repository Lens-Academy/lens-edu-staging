### Room 2: Break their model  [0:24–0:42]

New group. Names first, then straight in.


The AI Futures Model says progress happens in three stages:
1\. AI takes over the coding.
2\. AI takes over the research taste. This means choosing which experiments to run.
3\. The intelligence explosion. This starts when AI does all the AI research.


The model says a fast takeoff needs one thing: each doubling of AI capability must take less time than the doubling before it. The model names two forces that push against this. First, good experiment ideas get harder to find. Second, AI research taste improves only so fast. The authors also note that better coding matters less and less, because the work becomes limited by how many experiments you can afford to run.


Your job is to break it. Find the weakest link in that chain.
\- Which stage do you least believe? Say what would have to be true for it to happen, and say why you doubt it.
\- The model says experiment compute becomes the limit. If that is right, what stops being important? What starts being important?


If a question is not clear, copy it into the Lens Coach ([https://lensacademy.org/coach](https://lensacademy.org/coach)) and ask it to explain.

### Room 3: Build your own  [0:52–1:10]

New group. Names first, then straight in.


You have now taken someone else's model apart. Build your own.


As a group, write three or four sentences that say what actually drives AI progress over the next ten years. Name the parts. Say which part controls the speed. You are allowed to disagree with everything in the readings.


Then do the hard part. Write down one thing you could observe in the next two years that would show your account is wrong. Be specific. "Progress slows down" is not specific. "No lab releases a model that can do a full week of software work" is specific.


If your group cannot find anything that would show you wrong, say so out loud. That is a real result, and it tells you something about the account you just wrote.

## Glossary

### Module 1

**Order of magnitude (OOM).** A factor of ten. "12 OOMs more compute" means a trillion times more compute.
**Discontinuity.** A jump in a trend. The jump is large enough that an estimate made from the years before it would have been very wrong.

### Module 2

**Reference class.** The group of past cases that you treat your question as part of. You then use their rate as your starting estimate. The difficult part is choosing the group.
**Time horizon.** METR's measure of how long a task an AI can complete reliably. Most forecasts in this unit depend on this measurement continuing to increase.
**Research taste.** Choosing which experiments are worth running. The AI Futures Model treats this as a separate skill from writing the code.
**Extremizing.** You move a combined estimate further from the middle than a simple average puts it. This is correct when the forecasters saw different evidence. It is not correct when they saw the same evidence.
