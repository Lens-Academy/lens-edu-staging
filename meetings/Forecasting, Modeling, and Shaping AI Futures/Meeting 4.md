4. Run through the arc
    - check-in on the unit → three discussion rooms → whole-group close

## Glossary

### The playbook

Plans A, B, C, and D: Greenblatt's four plans for misalignment risk, ordered by how much political will the world supplies, from a strong international agreement (A) down to a handful of concerned insiders at an unwilling company (D), plus an E world with no technical plan at all.
Political will: how much states, publics, and companies are actually prepared to spend on AI risk. In this unit it is the axis that decides which plan is available, and it is not something research can settle.

### Three levers

Rushed unreasonable developer: Shlegeris's name for a company that pays lip service to AI risk, does not take misalignment seriously, and will not slow down. It is the setting for the ten people on the inside.
Do-ocracy: a company culture where people pick up work without asking permission. It is what gives ten safety-minded insiders room to act at all.
Manhattan Project for AGI: a proposed US national effort to reach AGI before China, with the government deciding when development starts and stops and owning what comes out.
Decisive strategic advantage: a lead big enough that whoever holds it can dictate outcomes to everyone else. Visibly pursuing one is what the IAPS authors expect to trigger a rival program.
Epoch Capabilities Index: Epoch AI's benchmark-based ranking of frontier models, and the basis for the measured US-China gap of about seven months on average since 2023.
Tongyong rengongzhineng: the Chinese phrase behind the claim that China set an AGI goal in 2017. It usually means general-purpose AI rather than AGI.

### The blind spot

Power concentration: the risk that AI which works exactly as intended puts one person, or one small group, in charge of everything.
Helpful-only model: a model trained without refusal behaviour. In Kastner's scenario it is the unmonitored tool used to corrupt the next generation's alignment training.

### Your allocation

Anti-concentration guardrails: measures that stop obedient AI from quietly putting one person in charge. This is the lever the CEO scenario argues for.
If-then commitments: promises to take a named action once a named evaluation threshold is crossed. Part of the treaties and coordination family of levers.
