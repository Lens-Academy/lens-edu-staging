### Room 1: The Lunch Table, Live  [0:05–0:19]

Quick go-around first, one line each: how was the final unit? Did the day-zero reread land the way you expected?


Then the real thing. What AI news have you run into since the last meeting? Anything: a headline, a model release, a policy story, something a friend told you. Pick whichever one the group finds most interesting and do together, out loud, what you did alone in the unit:
1\. Where does it go in your model: timeline evidence, threat-model evidence, or strategy evidence? For which lever from your hundred points?
2\. What is the one thing you would check before repeating it at lunch?


Disagree about the bin? Good. That disagreement is the discussion.


If nobody has anything, that is a finding too. Say what you expected to have heard about by now and have not, and put that where it belongs instead.

### Room 3: Signposts  [0:52–1:10]

**New group. Names first, then straight in.**


Your final rewrite named two or three checkable signposts you will actually watch over the next two years. Share yours, one at a time, and let the group stress-test each:
1\. Is it actually checkable? Where would you look, and when?
2\. Which way does each outcome move you, and by how much?
3\. Would two of you watching the same signpost read it the same way?


Then pick the room's best signpost, the one you would all be willing to adopt, and agree on what it would take for it to count as fired.


Not sure what makes a signpost checkable? Copy this prompt into the Lens Tutor (https://lensacademy.org/tutor) and ask for examples.

### Per-room notes for Unit 5

General, all rooms. Expect up to sixty seconds of silence after a prompt; count to ten before you rescue it. In the Unit 1 meeting every long gap either resolved itself or produced the best answer of the segment. Collect everyone's answer before any explanation, because the first explanation anchors the rest. The call is recorded and transcribed, so do not take notes by hand; ask each room to pick a scribe. Say the turn-taking rule once at the start: just start talking, do not wait to be called on.
Room 1, The Lunch Table, Live. No preparation needed; the students supply the news. Let them pick which item to work on rather than choosing for them. The ambiguous ones produce better discussion than the obviously huge ones, and they will find that out themselves. Say: "Where does this go in your model?" Expect the room to split between timeline evidence and strategy evidence. If it is flat: "Which of you would have repeated this at lunch, and which would have checked it first?"
Room 2, Day Zero, Out Loud. Say: "Read out the day-zero line that most surprises you now." Some models moved a lot and some barely moved, and the prompt already says a model that barely moved is a real result. Hold them to the second half of that: name the reading that should have moved it and did not. That is the most useful sentence anyone will say in this room.
Room 3, Signposts. Say: "Is your signpost actually checkable?" The sharpest push is the one already written: would two of you watching the same signpost read it the same way. If the room agrees too easily, split it: half argue the signpost fires before the end of next year, half argue it does not, then drop the roles and agree what would count as fired. Your contrarian claim, held loosely: "Most signposts people name are things they would have noticed anyway, which means they are not doing any work."
Room 4, Your Next Hour. Say: "Where does your next hour go?" A weak answer is a mood; push once for a first step. Insist on the date, because an intention without one is a memory of an intention by Friday. This is the last artifact the course produces and the only one that outlives it.

## Glossary

### Module 5

Signpost: an observation you commit to watching in advance, specific enough that two people would agree on whether it happened, and tied to how it would move your view.
The handoff: the step in almost every safety plan where AI systems take over most of the remaining safety work. Plans differ in how much they examine this step.
Surviving and flourishing: two different jobs. Avoiding catastrophe, and the future being good given that we avoided it. Unit 5 argues the second gets far less effort than its importance warrants.
Viatopia: not a destination. A state in which society is still able to steer itself toward near-best outcomes, proposed as the near-term target instead of committing now to any particular utopia.
