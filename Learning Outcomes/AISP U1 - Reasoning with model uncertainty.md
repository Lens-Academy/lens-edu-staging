## Test:
id:: 1c775cb0-453d-4f0e-8bfd-27a7d7b28ff0
#### Question: Open
id:: 492da843-ab22-4c6e-a0f4-72f1511ca6b0
content::
Two teams each estimate a 2 percent chance of catastrophic failure. Team A uses a model validated on many related systems. Team B combines several speculative assumptions about a technology with no close historical precedent.

Explain why the identical 2 percent figures should not automatically receive identical decision weight. What additional information would you want before acting on either estimate?

assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

50 points: The answer distinguishes uncertainty in the estimated outcome from uncertainty about the model, assumptions, or estimate-generating process itself.

30 points: The answer identifies relevant additional information about evidence quality, calibration, priors or reference classes, sensitivity to assumptions, or omitted mechanisms.

20 points: The answer explains how that information could change the decision weight assigned to the two estimates.

feedback-instructions::
Give 90 to 150 words. Identify the strongest epistemic distinction in the answer and one important missing source of uncertainty if any.
