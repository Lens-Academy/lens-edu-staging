## Test:
id:: b24d22b5-5471-44c3-bd56-ab6b12ea84fc

#### Question
content:: In your own words, what is the difference between a theory of change and a theory of victory?

assessment-instructions:: Score the answer from 1 to 5. This is an introductory comprehension question. Do not require the learner to construct an original strategy.

**1** — Does not distinguish the terms or defines them in an unrelated way. *Example: "A theory of change is when your beliefs change, while a theory of victory is when you win an argument."*

**2** — Recognizes that a theory of victory is broader, but the distinction remains vague. *Example: "A theory of change is a smaller plan and a theory of victory is the whole plan."*

**3** — Explains that a theory of change links an action to a useful outcome, while a theory of victory continues the causal story to an explicit overall win condition. This is the pass bar. *Example: "A theory of change explains how a particular action produces something useful. A theory of victory explains the full chain of events needed for the overall problem to be solved."*

**4** — As above, plus notes that a theory of victory includes actions or handoffs beyond the original project. *Example: Adds: "It also includes what other people and institutions need to do after the project's immediate result."*

**5** — As above, plus explains that making the complete story explicit reveals assumptions or weak links. *Example: Adds: "Writing out the full chain shows which steps we are assuming will happen and where the plan might break."*

Do not require the learner's wording to match the examples. Accept “local impact pathway” or similar language for a theory of change.

#### Question
content:: Suppose an AI safety organization says: “We produce better evaluations of dangerous AI capabilities, which gives policymakers better information.” Is that already a complete theory of victory? Briefly explain what would still be missing.

assessment-instructions:: Score the answer from 1 to 5. This is a simple application question. The learner should recognize that better information is an intermediate outcome and name what a complete account would still need; do not require a detailed strategy.

**1** — Calls it a complete theory of victory without recognizing any missing steps. *Example: "Yes. Better evaluations give policymakers information, so the problem is solved."*

**2** — Says it is incomplete but cannot explain why. *Example: "No, it would need to be more detailed."*

**3** — Recognizes that better information is only an intermediate outcome and that the account must explain how it leads to action and an overall win condition. This is the pass bar. *Example: "No. It explains how evaluations improve policymakers' information, but not what policymakers do with that information or how their actions prevent dangerous AI outcomes."*

**4** — As above, plus names a specific missing handoff or assumption. *Example: Adds: "It assumes policymakers understand the evaluations, have authority to act, and will delay or restrict a dangerous deployment."*

**5** — As above, plus explains why identifying that missing step is useful. *Example: Adds: "Making that assumption explicit shows that the project's success depends not only on evaluation quality but also on political authority and willingness to respond."*

Do not require the learner's wording to match the examples. Accept any reasonable account of the missing steps between better information and the final outcome.
