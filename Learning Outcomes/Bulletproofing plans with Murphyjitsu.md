## Test:
id:: 9e042355-f6c7-4319-a52d-03efefee18a6
#### Question: Open
id:: d6c339af-3f9d-459b-8172-9dfde50a42be
content::
Your friend Sam works full time as a software engineer and wants to move into AI safety research. Here is Sam's plan, written yesterday:

> Over the next eight weeks I'll work through an interpretability tutorial in the evenings: Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 8 to 10 pm. I've blocked those times in my calendar and told my partner, so they'll hold me to it. Then I'll write up a small replication of one result as a blog post by 1 December.

Two things Sam mentioned in passing. One week in four, Sam is on call at work and often gets paged in the evening. And last spring Sam tried a similar evening course and stopped in week two, after being stuck on one exercise for two evenings: "I just felt stupid and stopped opening it."

Sam asks you to help bulletproof the plan with Murphyjitsu.

1. Which part of the plan is still too vague to picture doing, and what would make it concrete?
2. Imagine it is 1 December and Sam has made little progress. Write the failure story you find most plausible, using the details above.
3. Name one bulletproofing action that would head off that failure.
4. With that fix in place, check your surprise again. Would you be shocked if the plan still failed? If not, name the next most likely failure.
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions::
The scenario: Sam, a full-time software engineer, plans to work through an interpretability tutorial on Monday, Wednesday and Friday evenings, 8 to 10 pm, for eight weeks, then write up a small replication of one result as a blog post by 1 December. Sam has already blocked the times in a calendar and told a partner. One week in four Sam is on call and often paged in the evening. Last spring Sam quit a similar evening course in week two after being stuck on one exercise for two evenings and feeling stupid.

Score out of 100, in four parts.

Part 1, the vague part (20 points). 20: names a part of the plan that cannot yet be pictured as concrete steps, and says what would make it concrete. Good choices include the write-up step (no result chosen, no steps or dates between the tutorial and the post), what "work through" means on a given evening, or what Sam does in on-call weeks. 10: names a vague part without saying how to make it concrete.

Part 2, the failure story (35 points). 35: a plausible story of how the plan fails that uses at least one detail from the scenario (getting stuck and feeling stupid, on-call weeks, the undefined write-up) and says how it unfolds, as a sequence of what happens and what Sam then does or stops doing. An internal cause, an external cause, or both all count. 15: a plausible but generic story ("Sam loses motivation", "Sam gets busy") that does not say how it unfolds or does not use the scenario's details. 0: no failure story.

Part 3, the bulletproofing action (30 points). 30: one concrete action Sam can take or set up now that targets the failure named in Part 2. Examples: decide in advance that after 30 minutes stuck Sam posts the question to a named person or forum, or skips to the next exercise; treat on-call weeks as planned off weeks and move the deadline or add a catch-up slot; choose the result to replicate now and set a date for a first draft. 15: concrete but aimed at a different failure than the one in Part 2, or aimed at the right failure but vague ("be kind to yourself", "push through"). Cap Part 3 at 10 if the action only adds calendar blocks, reminders or telling someone, because Sam already has these, unless the answer explains why the new version would work where the existing one would not.

Part 4, checking again (15 points). 15: gives an honest surprise judgment for the fixed plan and either names a further plausible failure or gives a reason from the scenario why failure would now be surprising. 5: says it would be shocking, or not, with no reason.
feedback-instructions:: The learner ran Murphyjitsu on a friend's plan (Sam: evening interpretability tutorial three times a week for eight weeks, then a blog post by 1 December, with calendar blocks and a partner already in place, on-call one week in four, and a history of quitting an evening course after being stuck and feeling stupid). Reply in under 150 words, short paragraphs, no lists. Name the strongest part of their answer in one sentence. Below 100, name the one part that cost the most points and what a stronger version would do, using the scenario's details. For example, if their fix was more reminders, point out that Sam already has a calendar and a partner, so the plan needs a fix aimed at the reason Sam stopped last time. If their failure story was generic, ask what exactly Sam would be doing on the evening the plan stalls. At 100, confirm briefly. No generic praise. If the learner says they do not understand the task, give one foothold: start from the moment last spring when Sam stopped opening the course, and ask what would happen if that moment came again.
