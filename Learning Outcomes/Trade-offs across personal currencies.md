## Test:
id:: 0a63de2b-c142-4bfd-91e5-8e21a4d6b298
#### Question: Open
id:: 0d19409d-a68f-4175-9909-9d216636efa4
content::
Priya works full time and runs a small AI safety reading group as a volunteer. She wants four more hours a week for her own research. Her week includes:

- Booking the room, sending reminders and collecting replies by hand: about 2 hours.
- Reading each week's paper twice before the session, so she is ready for any question: about 3 hours. Members say her notes from the first read are already the most useful part of the session.
- The bus to the venue: 50 minutes each way. A taxi takes 20 minutes and costs about €15 each way. She calls taxis "a waste of money".
- Dinner with her partner every evening, phones away: about an hour a day.

Which changes would you suggest to free those four hours? For each change, say what Priya gives up and what she gets.
force-feedback:: first
assessment-instructions:: The case: Priya wants 4 more hours a week. Her week includes about 2 hours of manual room booking, reminders and replies for her reading group, about 3 hours reading each paper twice (members say her first-read notes are already the most useful part), a 50-minute bus ride each way to the venue (a taxi takes 20 minutes and costs €15 each way, so a round trip by taxi saves 60 minutes for €30), and an hour a day of phone-free dinner with her partner (7 hours a week). The question asks which changes would free 4 hours and, for each, what she gives up and what she gets.

Score out of 100.
- 50: Proposes changes that together plausibly free about 4 hours, each resting on a sound reason. Sound reasons include: a repeated cost cut by a one-time setup or a system (automated reminders, a shared sign-up form, a standing room booking, handing the admin to a member), effort past diminishing returns (dropping or shortening the second read, since the first-read notes are already what members value), using the bus time for research or reading, and buying time with money at a rate she would plausibly accept (€30 for an hour). 50 if the changes add up to roughly 4 hours and each reason is sound, 30 if the reasons are sound but the total is clearly short or one change rests on a weak reason, 10 if the changes are only listed without reasons.
- 50: For each change, states both what Priya gives up and what she gets, and counts currencies beyond hours where they matter: money, attention, energy, the quality of the sessions, her relationships, her enjoyment or peace of mind. 50 if every change has both sides and at least one change weighs a currency other than time. 25 if only hours are counted, or if most changes state only the gain.

Cap the score at 40 if the answer cuts or shortens the phone-free dinner and counts only the hours saved, with no weight given to what the dinner gives her.
feedback-instructions:: The learner just answered a graded test on trading personal currencies, using Priya's week. You see the score and the grader's reason.

Reply in 80 to 150 words, short paragraphs, no lists. Name the strongest change they proposed and why it works. Below 100, name the most important gap: usually only counting hours, missing a repeated cost that a one-time setup removes, missing that the second read adds little, or cutting the dinner as if it were only an hour. If they cut the dinner, ask what else that hour pays for. No generic praise.
