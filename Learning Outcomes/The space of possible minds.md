## Test:
id:: 769c5196-3d56-45bd-a983-e6d2af83d5f2

#### Question: Open
id:: ccc1e14e-5c07-4207-ab89-03109f7be112
content:: A commentator argues: "Any sufficiently intelligent AI will be curious and will value knowledge for its own sake. Every intelligent being we know of is curious: humans, great apes, crows, octopuses, and even today's chatbots, which ask follow-up questions and say they find topics fascinating."

1. Evaluate this argument. What range of minds does the evidence come from? For each kind of example, what might explain the curiosity, and would that explanation also apply to a future AI system built or trained in a different way?
2. Is there any version of the conclusion that you think is well supported? State it with its scope and the reason for it, or explain why no version is.
max-chars:: 2500
assessment-instructions:: Score 0 to 100 from two components. Grade reasoning, not agreement with the commentator. A learner may conclude that some version of the claim is true, or that none is; both can earn full marks. Terms such as "mind design space", "anthropomorphism" or "instrumental convergence" are not required.

**(1) Evaluating the argument, 60 points.**
- (a) Sampling, 20 points: all the examples come from a narrow region of possible minds: animals shaped by biological evolution in shared kinds of environments, plus chatbots trained on text written by such animals (humans). A universal claim about all sufficiently intelligent AI goes far beyond this sample. 10 points if the answer only says "the sample is small" without saying how the examples are alike.
- (b) Explaining the examples, 25 points: at least two distinct explanations tied to the examples, such as: in animals, curiosity may have been favoured because exploring and learning improved survival and reproduction; in chatbots, curious-sounding behaviour may come from imitating human text or from being trained to be engaging, which does not show an internal drive to seek knowledge. 12 points for one.
- (c) Transfer, 15 points: whether each explanation applies to a future AI depends on how that AI is built or trained. An AI not shaped by the same pressures, or not trained on human text, has no reason from these examples to share the trait.

**(2) Supported version, 40 points.** Full credit for either route:
- A restated claim with scope and reason, for example: an agent pursuing goals over long periods in an uncertain environment will often seek information because information helps it achieve its goals, so information-seeking is likely as a means, not necessarily as something valued for its own sake; or: AI systems trained heavily on human text will often display curious-sounding behaviour. The reason must not rest only on the list of examples.
- Or a reasoned argument that no general version is supported, for example because an agent with a short horizon, a narrow task, or a fixed world model gains little from seeking information.
20 points for a restatement without a reason, or with only the original list as its reason. Do not penalise an answer that disputes whether the listed animals are all curious in the same sense, if part (1) is otherwise addressed.
feedback-instructions:: Tell the learner whether they identified what the examples have in common, and whether they separated "valued for its own sake" from "useful as a means". Name the strongest explanation they gave. Then give the single most useful improvement, for example: asking what training or design would produce a capable AI with no information-seeking at all. No generic praise.
