#### Question: Open
id:: e0561bbb-1b69-4fd3-85c5-6777a6692fb8
content::
**Case A.** A popular book says that a daily five-minute breathing exercise "makes people calmer and happier". Its evidence is one randomised trial: 60 university students in the United States were assigned either to the breathing exercise for six weeks or to a waiting list. At the end of the six weeks, the breathing group reported less stress and more positive emotions on questionnaires than the waiting-list group.

What kind of evidence is this, and what can it show?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Grade the ideas in the learner's own words. The case: a book claims a daily five-minute breathing exercise "makes people calmer and happier". Evidence: one randomised trial, 60 US university students, six weeks of the exercise versus a waiting list, outcomes self-reported stress and positive emotions measured at the end of the six weeks.
- 40: Kind of evidence: a single randomised trial whose comparison group did nothing (a waiting list).
- 60: What it can show: that doing the exercise caused an average difference in self-reported stress and positive emotions over six weeks, compared with doing nothing, in people like these students.
Cap at 50 if the answer says the trial shows the exercise makes people lastingly or generally happier.
feedback-instructions:: The learner classified Case A (one randomised trial, waiting-list control, 60 US students, six weeks, self-reported outcomes) and said what it can show, and received a score. In 40 to 90 words: name the strongest part, then the most important thing missed, usually that the comparison was with doing nothing, or that what it shows is limited to a short-term, self-reported, average difference. If they scored 100, confirm briefly. No generic praise. If they ask, explain the score from the grading reason.

#### Question: Open
id:: 0f20390d-cff8-4724-8147-034f33a3e22e
content::
Name three specific ways the evidence in Case A could mislead.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Grade the ideas in the learner's own words. The case: a book claims a daily five-minute breathing exercise "makes people calmer and happier". Evidence: one randomised trial, 60 US university students, six weeks of the exercise versus a waiting list, outcomes self-reported stress and positive emotions measured only at the end of the six weeks.
Award 33 points for each distinct valid way, up to three (a third valid way brings the total to 100). Valid ways include: (a) the comparison group did nothing, so expectation, attention or simply doing something could explain the difference, and it does not show the exercise beats another activity. (b) Outcomes were self-reported by people who knew their group, so expectation can inflate them. (c) Measured only at six weeks, so nothing is known about whether the effect lasts. (d) A narrow sample (US university students) may not generalise to other people. (e) One small study, which may not replicate, possibly picked by the book from several. (f) The claim ("calmer and happier") goes beyond what was measured. Credit any other way that is specific to this case and correct. Two ways that are the same idea in different words count once.
feedback-instructions:: The learner named ways Case A (one randomised trial, waiting-list control, 60 US students, six weeks, self-reported outcomes) could mislead, and received a score. In 40 to 90 words: name the most important way they gave, and if they missed the waiting-list comparison or the self-reported, unblinded outcomes, name that one. If they scored 100, confirm briefly. No generic praise. If they ask, explain the score from the grading reason.
force-feedback:: first

#### Question: Open
id:: 1ca6d29d-9253-4415-b937-0a6e0495e889
content::
**Case B.** A retreat centre's website shows 40 written reports from past guests. Most say they arrived during a hard period in their lives and that after a ten-day silent retreat they found a deep peace that has lasted ever since. Several add that their teacher confirmed they had reached the first stage of awakening.

What kind of evidence is this, and what can it show?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Grade the ideas in the learner's own words. The case: a retreat centre publishes 40 reports chosen from past guests. Most arrived during a hard period and say that after a ten-day silent retreat they found a deep peace that has lasted ever since. Several say their teacher confirmed they reached the first stage of awakening.
- 40: Kind of evidence: testimonials, that is first-person reports from people who were selected (by themselves or the centre) and published by an interested party, plus a teacher's judgement from inside the tradition.
- 60: What it can show: that some guests sincerely report such an experience and what it was like for them, which makes the retreat a candidate worth testing.
Cap at 50 if the answer says this evidence shows nothing at all.
feedback-instructions:: The learner classified Case B (40 testimonials published by a retreat centre, guests came in a hard period, a teacher confirmed attainments) and said what it can show, and received a score. In 40 to 90 words: name the strongest part, then the most important thing missed, usually that the reports were selected by an interested party, or that they still show what some guests experienced. If they scored 100, confirm briefly. No generic praise. If they ask, explain the score from the grading reason.

#### Question: Open
id:: 05e4b6c5-3c41-4c41-b993-107f84529914
content::
Name three specific ways the evidence in Case B could mislead.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Grade the ideas in the learner's own words. The case: a retreat centre publishes 40 reports chosen from past guests. Most arrived during a hard period and say that after a ten-day silent retreat they found a deep peace that has lasted ever since. Several say their teacher confirmed they reached the first stage of awakening.
Award 33 points for each distinct valid way, up to three (a third valid way brings the total to 100). Valid ways include: (a) guests came during a hard period, so many would feel better later whatever they did (regression to the mean). (b) The centre chose which reports to show, and guests who were not helped or were harmed are missing (selection or survivorship). (c) Guests expected peace, and a tradition's descriptions of stages can shape what people experience and report. (d) "Has lasted ever since" is remembered and self-reported, with no measurement before or over time, and people can misjudge their own states. (e) The teacher's confirmation is not independent: the teacher belongs to the tradition and the centre, and judgements of such experiences depend on who judges. (f) No comparison group, so other changes in the guests' lives could explain the improvement. Credit any other way that is specific to this case and correct. Two ways that are the same idea in different words count once.
feedback-instructions:: The learner named ways Case B (40 testimonials published by a retreat centre, guests came in a hard period, a teacher confirmed attainments) could mislead, and received a score. In 40 to 90 words: name the most important way they gave, and if they missed regression to the mean (guests came at a low point) or the centre's selection of reports, name that one. If they scored 100, confirm briefly. No generic praise. If they ask, explain the score from the grading reason.
