#### Question
content:: Think-Pair-Share is a discussion structure built from three steps: participants first think silently on their own, then talk it through in pairs, then share with the whole group. It looks almost too simple, but it recruits several distinct learning mechanisms at once.

**Describe the three steps, including roughly how long you would give each one. Name at least two situations where Think-Pair-Share is the right move. Then write one Think-Pair-Share opening question for a session you might actually run.**

assessment-instructions::
**1** — Confuses Think-Pair-Share with "ask the group and wait." Can't write a real opening question.

**2** — Describes the three steps roughly. Lists one situation. Question is too vague ("what did you think of the chapter?").

**3** — Correctly describes Think → Pair → Share in that order, and gives a workable duration for each phase. Any plausible allocation counts, in any wording, as long as it would actually work in a session (e.g. a short silent think, a few minutes of pairing, a longer whole-group share — something like 1-3 / 3-5 / 5-10 minutes); no specific numbers are required. Names at least two situations where it is the right move (e.g. the group has gone quiet; the first share dominated the discussion; the topic is sensitive; you want quieter members in). Writes a specific question that requires actual engagement with the material.

**4** — As above, plus identifies, in any wording, a learning mechanism each phase leverages (e.g. think → the generation effect of forming your own answer before hearing others; pair → speaking time for every participant rather than one at a time; share → higher-quality contributions because they have already been rehearsed).

**5** — As above, plus articulates the online-specific version (pair → 4-5 min breakout room of 2-3 people) and notes why this is one of the few moments Zoom's breakout-room feature genuinely earns its weight.
