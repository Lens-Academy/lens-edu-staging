id:: 6f3b0e95-c2d0-4ca0-8d33-8b73557ba01b

#### Question: Open
id:: 0504d3b7-d698-43d8-8a68-339ceb5a48a3
content:: A legislator reviewing next year's budget says: "Twenty years ago this committee funded a programme to harden the power grid against solar storms. No blackout ever happened. The money was wasted, and I will not vote for an 'invisible' AI safety budget on the same reasoning."

Write a reply that:

1. explains what is wrong with the step from "nothing happened" to "the risk was overstated", naming the two different situations that both produce "nothing happened";
2. explains why AI risk is especially prone to this kind of dismissal, compared with a risk like a stockpile of missiles;
3. says what evidence you would ask for to tell an averted risk from an overstated one, and what that evidence would look like for AI.
max-chars:: 2000
assessment-instructions:: Score 0 to 100 from three components. Grade reasoning, not agreement with the legislator or the course. An answer that concludes the grid programme was in fact wasteful can score full marks if it shows what evidence would establish that.

**(a) The inference, 40 points.** Full credit: "nothing happened" is the observed outcome both in the world where the risk was real and prevention worked, and in the world where the risk was never real. The observation cannot distinguish the two, so the absence of catastrophe is not evidence against the risk unless one also knows what would have happened without the intervention. Extra credit within the 40 for noting that successful prevention removes the evidence for its own necessity. 20 points for "correlation is not causation" style answers that do not state the two-situations structure.

**(b) Why AI in particular, 25 points.** Any two mechanisms, for example: AI harms are intangible, with no physical stockpile to point at; the products people meet daily appear helpful; capabilities grow exponentially and people extrapolate linearly; there has been no salient large-scale failure to anchor on. Must be mechanisms, not "AI is complicated" or "people are irrational". 12 points for one mechanism.

**(c) Distinguishing evidence, 35 points.** Full credit: evidence that bears on the counterfactual, such as near misses and incidents caught by the safeguards, the causal mechanism established independently of the outcome, demonstrated capability in controlled settings, or comparable cases where no prevention was done. For AI: documented incidents of models breaking out of test environments or gaining unauthorised access, dangerous-capability evaluations, red-team findings. 15 points if the learner names "the counterfactual" without saying what observable evidence would bear on it.
feedback-instructions:: State whether the learner got the two-situations structure. Name the strongest piece of distinguishing evidence they proposed and one they missed. Ask one follow-up question. No generic praise.
