## Test:
id:: 9dc41f00-273b-443c-b47c-32d1637fa899
#### Question: Open
id:: 19149b46-21dd-400f-9768-4e9ef9b7a8a9
content:: Ana works full time and wants to move into AI safety. She feels she should spend every evening on a technical AI safety course, but she keeps seeing friends instead. She writes down this Internal Double Crux:

Names: **Committed Ana** and **Distracted Ana**.

- Committed Ana: AI could go badly within ten years, and you are wasting your evenings.
- Distracted Ana: But I'm exhausted after work, and I haven't really seen my friends in a month.
- Committed Ana: Fair enough, but the stakes are too high. Also, you promised yourself. Also, other people manage it. Also, your friends will still be there in five years.
- Distracted Ana: ...okay, you're right.

Her note at the bottom: "IDC worked! Resolved in four lines. Studying every evening from now on."

1. What went wrong in this IDC? Name each problem.

2. As the moderator, rewrite the names and the next two lines of the dialogue.
assessment-instructions:: The question shows Ana's written Internal Double Crux (IDC). She works full time, wants to move into AI safety, feels she should spend every evening on a technical course, but keeps seeing friends. Her names are "Committed Ana" and "Distracted Ana". Committed: AI could go badly within ten years and she is wasting her evenings. Distracted: she is exhausted after work and hasn't seen friends in a month. Committed: "Fair enough, but the stakes are too high", plus three more points (she promised herself, others manage it, friends will be there in five years). Distracted: "...okay, you're right." Her note: IDC worked, resolved in four lines, studying every evening from now on. The question asks (1) what went wrong, naming each problem, and (2) as moderator, a rewrite of the names and the next two lines. IDC rules: charitable names each side would choose for itself, a moderator who is not partisan, each turn first acknowledges a real grain of truth in the other side's last point and then adds one point only, and repetition until the disagreement dissolves or turns into a deeper one.

Score out of 100.

20: Says the names take sides ("Distracted" is a put-down, the moderator is on Committed's team) and that each side should have a name it would choose for itself.

20: Names the rule breaks in Committed's second turn: the acknowledgement is perfunctory ("fair enough, but") rather than a real grain of truth, and it makes several points at once instead of one. Give 20 for both, 10 for one.

30: Says the "resolution" is one side overruling the other, not a resolution: Distracted's concerns (exhaustion, friendships) were never answered, and Distracted gave in rather than updated. Credit any answer that says the result looks like Ana talking herself into the conclusion she already favoured, or that a four-line win for the "should" side is a warning sign rather than a success. Give 15 if the answer only says it went too fast, without saying that one side was overruled.

30: The rewrite gives both sides fair names (for example "Make a difference on AI" and "Rest and keep my friendships") and two next lines that follow the format: a real acknowledgement followed by one point. Typically Distracted, renamed, acknowledges something true in Committed's point and adds one point of its own, or the moderator sends Committed back to acknowledge exhaustion or friendship genuinely before it continues. Give 15 for fair names with lines that don't follow the acknowledge-then-one-point format, or for good lines with names that still take sides.

Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "The names take sides: 'Distracted' is an insult, so the moderator is on Committed's team. Committed's 'fair enough, but' isn't a real acknowledgement, and it then fires four points at once. And Distracted didn't update, it got overruled. Its exhaustion and friendships were never answered, so the four-line win looks like Ana talking herself into what she already thought she should do. Rewrite: 'Study for impact' and 'Rest and friends'. Rest and friends: 'You're right that this matters to me and that the timeline might be short. One thing: if I study exhausted every night, I'll burn out and stop altogether.' Study for impact: 'That's true, burning out would cost more than a few evenings. One thing: two evenings a week would still move me forward.'"
feedback-instructions:: The learner has critiqued Ana's Internal Double Crux and rewritten the next turns. In 80 to 150 words, short paragraphs, no lists: name the strongest part of their answer, then the single most important thing it missed or got wrong. If they missed that one side was overruled rather than answered, name that first: the quick win for the side Ana already favoured is the self-persuasion failure CFAR warned about. If their rewrite still takes a side, say which word does it. If they scored full marks, confirm briefly. Say why they got their score if they ask. No generic praise. Do not take a side on whether Ana should study or see friends. If they say they don't understand, give one concrete foothold (for example: "Which of Distracted Ana's two concerns did Committed Ana actually answer?").
