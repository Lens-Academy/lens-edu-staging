## Test:
id:: 5c3b1731-6cc5-45d6-b788-c012c3ae671c

#### Question
content:: The course is over. Answer in your own words, no need for jargon: describe how you actually use AI now, name one concrete change you will make to protect your own judgement, explain in one or two sentences why that change works, and name one place where you could learn more about this topic.

assessment-instructions::
Score the answer according to the rubric below. The learner has just finished Unit 5 of "Thinking about Thinking" (what worry is reasonable, one thing to do today, where to learn more, the parting thought).

What the answer should show:
- An honest description of their own AI use (a "no change" answer is valid only if they still name a change or a reason they are already protected)
- One concrete, specific change (a real guardrail, not a vague intention like "be more mindful")
- A mechanism: why the change protects judgement, tied to the course's evidence (practice stays with the learner, the reasoning is restated, claims are verified, the judgement is not delegated, etc.)
- One resource for learning more: the Kulveit paper on gradual disempowerment, the Bednar legal study, the Seifer survey, the practical writing by Jaspreet Singh, or a course from BlueDot, 80,000 Hours, or Lens Academy

**1** — Offers only vague intentions ("be more mindful") with no mechanism, or names no concrete change.
**2** — Names a plausible change but cannot say why it protects judgement, or cannot name any resource for learning more.
**3** — Names one concrete, specific change, explains its mechanism in terms of the course's evidence, and names at least one valid place to learn more. This is the passing core.
**4** — As above, plus the change is tailored to their own described use (it fixes a real risk in their own workflow, not a generic one).
**5** — As above, plus connects the change to the course thread: the change keeps judgement exercised, so the learner keeps the skill rather than only avoiding a bad outcome.

This is a personal reflection; do not penalise a learner whose honest "no change" is well-reasoned, as long as they still demonstrate the ability to apply the course's framework. Avoid grading the learner's life choices; grade the reasoning. Feedback tone: direct, specific, no generic praise.
