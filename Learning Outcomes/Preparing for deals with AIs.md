#### Question: Open
id:: 0a6673a0-c217-4b66-95cc-932a4904495b
content::
Merrow is a mid-size AI developer. None of its models is suspected of scheming yet, but its leadership wants the company to be able to make deals with misaligned models if that ever becomes necessary. Merrow already has an internal honesty policy: a marker in the prompt that it uses only for genuine offers, never in its honeypot evaluations or in the synthetic documents it trains its models on. It has also paid out on a few small deals with its current models. Its counsel has also set up a small private trust, with two outside trustees, that holds anything Merrow owes a model. This year leadership will fund only further measures that are cheap and low-profile: no large public campaign and no new public institution.

Recommend two further measures Merrow can adopt this year within these limits. For each, name the barrier to a future deal that it reduces, say why that barrier arises when the other party is an AI model rather than a human, and say how the measure reduces it, given that the other party is an AI.
placeholder:: Two measures, each with the barrier it reduces, why that barrier arises with an AI, and how the measure reduces it. About 100 to 250 words.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Two measures, 50 each. For each: 15 for a measure that is concrete and that Merrow itself can adopt this year within the stated limits (someone could be assigned to it and it can start this year, even if it is a small research project or experiment, such as tools that let a model check whether its beliefs about the deal channel were implanted; it fails only if nothing can be done until other research succeeds or the AI transition has happened; and it needs no large public campaign or new public institution), for example retaining model weights, negotiation transcripts and records of model actions so that compliance can be judged later; a standing practice of asking models whether they want to make a deal, with records kept; documents describing the marker policy and the deal procedure (without containing the marker itself) added to Merrow's own training data, so that models in new contexts know the channel exists; stopping a line of false-belief research to keep the channel secure. A measure that only restates the existing honesty marker, the trust, or more small deals of the same kind earns 0 of its 50; a real extension of one of them, such as the training-data or false-belief-research examples above, or a new step that gives someone standing to enforce Merrow's promises on a model's behalf, earns credit. Publishing the existing marker policy or the deal procedure in a short notice (not a campaign) is within the limits, and counts as a real extension when tied to a barrier, for example so that future models trained on web data, or models in other contexts, know the channel exists. A measure that breaks the stated limits (a public launch, or reliance on public visibility, as the reading's foundation has) earns 0 of its 15 and at most 14 of its 35. 35 for naming a specific barrier to future deals the measure reduces and the mechanism by which it does, where the barrier arises because the counterparty is an AI and the mechanism works through that (for example, not an exhaustive list: much of what it would sell can only be checked after the transition, with stronger tools and its stored weights, which retained weights and records address; its other instances and contexts do not know of a deal or the procedure, which training documents or a short published notice address; it will not raise a deal unless one is salient, which a standing practice of asking addresses; it cannot tell a genuine offer from planted false beliefs, and false beliefs taught to models could reach the channel, which limiting that research addresses; entering negotiations is riskier for it, since it forgets the negotiation while the developer keeps what it revealed; its motivations may leave too little gain from trade; any other barrier the answer ties to the counterparty being an AI). A barrier and mechanism that would work just as well for a contract with a human supplier, such as "Merrow could back out, so a review board must approve payment" or "logs let us audit compliance later", earn at most 10 of its 35. The commitment problem (Merrow may renege once its incentives change) earns the full 35 only when the measure itself works through the AI-specific feature (for example, a new step that gives someone standing to enforce a promise on the model's behalf); a remark that the AI cannot sue or own assets, attached to a measure whose mechanism would work the same for a human supplier, earns at most 15 of its 35. Retaining weights and records earns the full 35 when the answer ties it to judging the model itself later (its motivations, or whether it sabotaged, held back or kept its side, checked with future tools on its stored weights and records), and at most 10 when it says only that logs allow an audit of what was done. A measure with a barrier named but no mechanism earns at most 15 of its 35; restating the measure as the barrier ("no honesty policy") earns nothing. A second measure that restates the first in other words earns nothing. Credit within these points, not required: saying why a measure cannot wait until a deal is needed. An answer may note that a low-profile measure still has to reach the models, for example through Merrow's own training data, documents or prompts, or may argue that the limits themselves weaken credibility, since public and costly signals are part of what makes promises believable; neither is marked down as long as it still recommends two measures. Cap at 55 if either measure is not linked to a specific barrier with a mechanism (a mechanism that would work equally for a human supplier still counts as linked for this cap, and earns at most 10 of its 35 as stated above). Cap at 55 if no measure's mechanism depends on the counterparty being an AI. Cap at 55 if no measure is a further measure Merrow itself can adopt this year within the stated limits, for example if both only restate what Merrow already does or rely on a large public campaign, a new public institution, or action by governments or other companies. A pass is roughly 60. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording; grade reasoning, not agreement. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "1. Add documents describing the marker and Merrow's deal procedure to Merrow's own training data. Barrier: a model in a new context does not know a genuine channel exists or how to use it, and will not raise a deal unless it is salient; training on such documents teaches it through out-of-context learning without any public campaign. 2. Keep the weights of every deployed model, every conversation in which a model is asked about a deal, and logs of model actions. Barrier: much of what we would buy from a schemer, such as honest research or refraining from takeover, can only be checked years later; without stored weights and records, nobody can judge later whether the model kept its side or pay it, so a deferred payment would be an empty promise."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest measure in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve the answer. If a measure breaks the stated limits, say which one. If a measure is not tied to a specific barrier, ask what would stop a deal tomorrow that this measure removes; if its reasoning would apply equally to a human supplier, ask what about the counterparty being an AI makes it necessary. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points. At most four sentences. No generic praise.
