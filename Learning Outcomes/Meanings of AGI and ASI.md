## Test:
id:: 961a28f1-8d46-4e25-a030-546a170ccc38

#### Question: Open
id:: 7a453665-e5aa-46eb-935e-76dc422c2d03
content:: Two forecasters are on the same panel.

- **Forecaster A:** "AGI is already here. Current models beat most human experts on most exam questions, coding puzzles and short writing tasks."
- **Forecaster B:** "AGI is at least ten years away. No AI system can yet run a company for a year or make a scientific discovery without people doing most of the work."

A legislator then proposes a bill: "Frontier AI developers must submit to an emergency safety review once AGI has been achieved." The bill's stated purpose is to review developers before their systems can cause large-scale harm without a human in the loop.

1. Explain how A and B can both be describing the same systems accurately. What does each one mean by "AGI", and what evidence would settle each of their claims?
2. Rewrite the bill's trigger so that it picks out what the bill cares about. Explain why your version is better than "once AGI has been achieved", and name one weakness it still has.
max-chars:: 2500
assessment-instructions:: Score 0 to 100 from two components. Grade reasoning. The learner does not need to cite any published definition of AGI or name any author or framework.

**(1) Disambiguation, 50 points.** Full credit needs: (a) the two forecasters use different thresholds, in any wording: A counts systems that beat experts on short, well-defined tasks; B requires the ability to carry out long, open-ended projects on its own (or real-world impact of that kind). Identifying the dimension that differs, such as task length, autonomy, or real-world impact versus test performance, is what matters (20 points); (b) given those meanings, both claims can be true of the same systems at the same time, so the disagreement is partly about the word rather than about the systems (10 points); (c) evidence that would settle each claim, stated concretely: for A, comparisons with human experts on the relevant short tasks; for B, observed performance on long-horizon autonomous work, such as multi-week projects completed with little human help, or measured economic substitution (20 points; 10 if the evidence is only for one of the two claims). Accept answers that add that the forecasters may also disagree on facts, if (a) to (c) are present.

**(2) Rewriting the trigger, 50 points.** Full credit needs: (a) a trigger stated as a checkable capability or behaviour tied to the bill's purpose, for example a system that, in a defined evaluation, can carry out tasks of a stated length or kind without human intervention, acquire resources, or evade oversight (20 points; 10 if the trigger is checkable but not tied to harm without a human in the loop, such as a benchmark score); (b) a reason it is better: it removes the dispute over what "AGI" means, and it is aimed at the capability the bill cares about rather than at general intelligence (15 points); (c) one real weakness, for example: evaluations can underestimate capability, developers may game a fixed test, the chosen threshold may be crossed in a dangerous way not covered by the test, or the line may be set too early or too late (15 points). Do not require any particular trigger; judge whether it is checkable and fits the purpose.
feedback-instructions:: Tell the learner whether they found the dimension on which the two forecasters' meanings differ. Name the strongest feature of their rewritten trigger. Then give the single most useful improvement, for example: making the trigger measurable by a named kind of test, or tying it more closely to harm without a human in the loop. No generic praise.
