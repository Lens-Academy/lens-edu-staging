## Test:
id:: 2147466b-90f5-49ad-99a4-4251c91fc864
#### Question: Open
id:: 0a1b0e45-cf3d-4bcc-996c-6910571b21a8
content::
A biotechnology accident could cause one of two outcomes.

**Outcome A:** 700 million people die, but surviving civilisation is expected to recover technologically within several generations.

**Outcome B:** 50 million people die immediately, but the event permanently prevents civilisation from regaining advanced technology.

Assume the two outcomes are equally likely. A colleague argues that Outcome A is obviously worse because it kills fourteen times as many people.

Evaluate that argument.

assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

40 points: The answer distinguishes immediate death toll from persistent or irreversible effects. It recognises that present casualties do not exhaust the comparison.

30 points: The answer explains at least one reason permanent loss of future capability can add morally relevant harm beyond present casualties, such as loss of recovery, future lives, future options, or future projects.

30 points: The answer recognises that the comparison still depends on normative assumptions about the value of future development and cannot be resolved from the two death counts alone.

Grade the reasoning rather than agreement with any particular moral theory. Do not require source names or course vocabulary.

feedback-instructions::
Give concise feedback in 80 to 140 words. Name the strongest part of the reasoning and the single most important missing distinction or assumption if the answer is below full credit. Do not reward agreement with the course. If the learner rejects the moral importance of future development but reconstructs the comparison correctly, say so plainly and grade the reasoning.
