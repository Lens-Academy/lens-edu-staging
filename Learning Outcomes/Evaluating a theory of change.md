#### Question: Open
id:: eef0b3b9-4fbc-4cd6-9fae-338161b90156
content:: A new organisation, Frontier Compute Watch, sends you its theory of change and asks for a critique before it goes to funders. The organisation is hypothetical.

> **Inputs:** three researchers, one year of funding, access to commercial satellite imagery.
> **Activities:** publish a quarterly report tracking new data-centre construction worldwide, and a public dashboard.
> **Short-term outcome:** the report is downloaded 5,000 times and cited in the press.
> **Intermediate outcome:** policymakers become aware of where large compute is being built.
> **Long-term outcome:** an international verification regime for AI is established.
> **Assumptions:** our data is accurate. Satellite imagery is enough to identify AI data centres.
> **External factors:** geopolitics.

Write a critique that:

1. identifies every place where something the organisation produced is labelled as a change in the world;
2. states which links in the chain are not if-then claims, and rewrites at least one of them as an if-then claim with a mechanism;
3. names the load-bearing assumption the chain depends on that is missing from the list;
4. for the intermediate outcome, says what you would need to observe to know it happened.
max-chars:: 2500
assessment-instructions:: Score 0 to 100 from four components. Grade reasoning, not verdict. A critique that judges the theory of change salvageable and one that judges it broken can both score full marks.

**(a) Outputs versus outcomes, 25 points.** Must flag the short-term outcome (downloads, press citations) as an output or reach measure, not a change in the world. Full credit requires saying what the corresponding outcome would be, for example a named audience now knows something it did not. 10 points if flagged without a replacement.

**(b) If-then links, 25 points.** Must identify the jump from "policymakers become aware" to "an international regime is established" as lacking a mechanism, and rewrite at least one link as a conditional with a stated mechanism, for example: if negotiators have an independent baseline of where large compute is concentrated, then they can propose declaration requirements that can be checked against that baseline. Rewrites that add "if ... then" wording without a mechanism earn 10.

**(c) Missing assumption, 25 points.** Full credit for either: that policymakers act on awareness, so information rather than will is the binding constraint; or that knowing where compute is built is a bottleneck for building a verification regime at all. Assumptions already listed (data accuracy, imagery sufficiency) earn 0. 10 points for naming a different real but less load-bearing assumption, for example that the dashboard stays funded beyond one year.

**(d) Observables, 25 points.** Full credit: names observable evidence for the intermediate outcome that is distinct from the outputs, for example the report cited in a hearing or briefing, a policymaker's office requesting the data, or the dashboard's figures appearing in a draft rule. 10 points if the observables are the outputs again (downloads, press mentions).
feedback-instructions:: Say which of the planted problems the learner found and which they missed: the output labelled as an outcome, the missing mechanism between awareness and a regime, and the unstated assumption that awareness leads to action. Name the strongest rewrite they offered. Ask one follow-up question. No generic praise.
