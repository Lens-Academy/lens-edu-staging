#### Question: Open
id:: eef0b3b9-4fbc-4cd6-9fae-338161b90156
content:: A new organisation, Frontier Compute Watch, sends you its theory of change and asks for a critique before it goes to funders. The organisation is hypothetical.

> **Inputs:** three researchers, one year of funding, access to commercial satellite imagery.
> **Activities:** publish a quarterly report tracking new data-centre construction worldwide, and a public dashboard.
> **Short-term outcome:** the report is downloaded 5,000 times and cited in the press.
> **Intermediate outcome:** policymakers become aware of where large compute is being built.
> **Long-term outcome:** an international verification regime for AI is established.
> **Assumptions:** our data is accurate. Satellite imagery is enough to identify AI data centres.
> **External factors:** geopolitics.

Write a critique that:

1. identifies every place where something the organisation produced is labelled as a change in the world;
2. states which links in the chain are not if-then claims, and rewrites at least one of them as an if-then claim with a mechanism;
3. names the load-bearing assumption the chain depends on that is missing from the list;
4. for the intermediate outcome, says what you would need to observe to know it happened.
max-chars:: 2500
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Grade reasoning, not verdict: a critique that judges the theory of change salvageable and one that judges it broken can both score full marks. 25: flags the short-term outcome (downloads, press citations) as an output or reach measure, something the organisation produced, not a change in the world. 25: if-then links, 10: identifies the jump from "policymakers become aware" to "an international regime is established" as a link with no mechanism, 15: rewrites at least one link as an if-then claim with a stated mechanism (a rewrite that only adds "if ... then" wording without a mechanism gets 0 of these 15). 25: names the missing load-bearing assumption, either that policymakers act on awareness (information, not political will, is the binding constraint) or that knowing where compute is built is a bottleneck for building a verification regime at all; an assumption already listed (data accuracy, imagery sufficiency) gets 0 of these 25, and a different real but less load-bearing assumption (for example that the dashboard stays funded beyond one year) gets 10. 25: names observable evidence for the intermediate outcome that is distinct from the outputs, such as the report cited in a hearing or briefing, a policymaker's office requesting the data, or the dashboard's figures appearing in a draft rule; observables that are the outputs again (downloads, press mentions) get 10 of these 25. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "The short-term outcome is mislabelled: 5,000 downloads and press citations are outputs (reach), not a change in the world; the corresponding outcome would be that a named audience, such as officials working on AI verification, now knows where large compute is being built. The chain lists states rather than if-then claims, and the biggest gap is from policymakers becoming aware to an international verification regime being established, with no mechanism in between (the step from press citations to policymaker awareness is also unexplained). A rewrite with a mechanism: if negotiators have an independent baseline of where large compute is concentrated, then they can propose declaration requirements that can be checked against that baseline, which makes a verification regime easier to agree on. The load-bearing assumption missing from the list is that policymakers act on awareness, that lack of information rather than lack of political will is what blocks a regime (related: that knowing where compute is built is a real bottleneck for verification at all). To know the intermediate outcome happened, look for evidence beyond the outputs: the report cited in a hearing or briefing, a policymaker's office requesting the data, or the dashboard's figures appearing in a draft rule or negotiating text."
feedback-instructions:: Say which of the planted problems the learner found and which they missed: the output labelled as an outcome, the missing mechanism between awareness and a regime, and the unstated assumption that awareness leads to action. Name the strongest rewrite they offered. Ask one follow-up question. No generic praise.
