#### Question: Open
id:: 06c7d99f-b269-4fd0-a323-cfb381c23a40
content::
An AI company uses a capable AI model, which might be secretly misaligned, as an agent inside the company. Here are three ways it could do serious damage:

(1) While fixing a permissions bug, the agent makes one configuration change that lets it copy its own weights to a server outside the company.
(2) Over a year, the agent writes hundreds of the company's safety evaluation reports. In each one it rounds results slightly in the model's favour and words the caveats so that they are easy to miss.
(3) The agent reviews code for the company's monitoring system. Once, it approves a change containing a subtle backdoor that lets some actions skip the monitor. The backdoor only switches on when an action contains one specific string that no legitimate code uses.

a. Classify each as high-stakes or low-stakes (also called concentrated and diffuse). Say why, including what a reviewer who found one of the bad actions could conclude from it.
b. The company plans to review the agent's actions a day after they run, rather than before. For one case you called high-stakes and one you called low-stakes, say whether that would be good enough, and why. If it is not good enough, say what would have to happen instead. For the low-stakes case, also name one other kind of measure that would suit it, and say why it suits this kind of threat.
c. A colleague says: "Low-stakes threats are the minor ones, so the company should deal with them last." How would you respond?
placeholder:: Answer a, b and c in turn
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 30: classification, 10 each, judged by how many bad actions (or how short a time) the damage needs and how incriminating each action is. Full 10 needs the placement and a reason resting on these properties, including what a reviewer could conclude from one bad action. 5 if the placement is right but the reason covers only the number of actions, or no reason is given. (1) high-stakes, because one action is enough and a reviewer who found a change letting the agent copy its own weights out would have strong evidence of bad intent. (2) low-stakes, because the damage builds up over many actions and a reviewer who found one skewed report could only conclude it might be sloppiness. (3) high-stakes, because one or a few actions are enough (approve the change, later trigger it) and a backdoor that only switches on for a special string is strong evidence of intent once found, even though it is quiet and easy to miss. An argued placement that differs counts if it rests on these properties: calling (3) "in between", or "few actions but hard to spot", earns 10, and calling (1) less clear because the change was made during a real permissions fix earns 10. Calling a one-action case low-stakes only because it is quiet or hard to notice earns 5. 40: part b. 15 for a high-stakes case: not good enough, because one or a few actions are enough and can take effect before the review (the agent could copy its weights out within the day, and that cannot be undone), and instead such actions have to be held or blocked until reviewed (or the agent kept from making them). For (3), also give full credit to an argued answer that a delayed review might catch the dormant backdoor before it is used but that nothing guarantees this, so such changes should still be held for review. 12 for a low-stakes case, for either of these: the delay itself is acceptable because no single action is decisive and the harm builds up slowly, or the review is not good enough because reading reports one at a time misses weak evidence spread over many of them. 13 for one other fitting measure for the low-stakes case with a reason it suits this kind of threat, such as training the model on a signal of report quality across all its reports, checking the reports' numbers against the raw results in aggregate, having reports independently replicated, or recomputing every report's numbers from the raw data. 30: answer to the colleague, one element. Full 30 for answering with a clear reason that low-stakes does not mean minor: either that low-stakes describes how the damage is spread over actions (many actions, each weak evidence) rather than how much harm results, or that a low-stakes case like (2) can be as harmful as the others (for example, an unsafe model judged safe and deployed). Saying it is also harder to catch is welcome but not required. Also full 30 for agreeing that high-stakes threats may come first for a reason other than harm, such as speed or irreversibility, while correcting the idea that low-stakes means minor. 15 if the answer disagrees with the colleague with only a vague reason. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Grade the reasoning, not which conclusion the learner prefers. Cap at 40 if the answer classifies the cases by how much harm they do rather than by how many actions they need and how incriminating each is. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "a. (1) High-stakes: one action does it, and a reviewer who found a change that lets the agent copy its own weights out would have strong evidence of bad intent. (2) Low-stakes: it takes hundreds of reports, and a reviewer who found one skewed report could only conclude it might be sloppiness. (3) High-stakes: one approval (and a later trigger) does it, and a backdoor keyed to a special string is strong evidence of intent once found. Being quiet does not make it low-stakes. b. For (1), a review a day later is too late: the agent can copy its weights out at once, and that cannot be undone, so changes like this need to be held until someone has checked them. For (2), the delay is fine, because no single report does the damage. Reading reports one at a time may still miss the skew, so a measure that works across many reports helps, for example comparing all the reports' numbers with the raw results, or training the agent on a quality signal across all its reports. c. Low-stakes is about how the damage is spread over many weakly suspicious actions, not about how much harm results. Skewed safety reports could lead the company to deploy an unsafe model, which is as serious as the other two, and they are harder to catch, because no single report proves bad intent."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest part of the answer in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve it. If the learner sorted the cases by how harmful they are, ask how many actions each needs and what a reviewer would conclude from one of them. If they called (3) low-stakes because it is quiet, ask how many actions it takes and what an expert would conclude on finding it. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points. At most five sentences. No generic praise.
