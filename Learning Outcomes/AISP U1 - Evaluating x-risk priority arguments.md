#### Question: Open
id:: 8ccb12d8-8dfa-47be-b452-d8739af7a27c
content::
A critic says: "Even if advanced AI could eventually become dangerous, there is no reason to spend much on the problem today. Current AI harms are certain, and society will have several opportunities to intervene later if stronger warning signs appear."

Break this argument into its distinct claims. For each claim, name one kind of evidence that would help determine whether it is true.

assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

40 points: The answer separates at least two distinct claims, including the opportunity-cost or current-harms claim and the later-checkpoints claim.

40 points: The answer gives evidence appropriate to each claim, such as actual resource substitution and policy attention for the first, and observability, reversibility, diffusion, or intervention feasibility for the second.

20 points: The answer recognizes that accepting the possibility of future danger does not by itself settle whether current spending is justified.

feedback-instructions::
Give 100 to 160 words. State whether the learner separated the argument cleanly and whether the proposed evidence actually bears on each claim.
