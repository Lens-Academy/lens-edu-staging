## Test:
id:: d08bbaa4-e3fc-4661-a888-6776d79aa4de
#### Question: Open
id:: f918a907-388c-424c-9561-57e93002978a
content::
The evening before a presentation, someone keeps thinking "I'm going to embarrass myself tomorrow". Here are five things they could do with that thought. For each one, say whether it works on the **content** of the thought (whether it is true or reasonable) or on their **relationship** to the thought (how they hold it, leaving the content as it is), and say what in the response shows it.

1. "I've given eleven talks this year and only one went badly. The odds are on my side."
2. They say to themselves: "I'm having the thought that I'm going to embarrass myself."
3. "Who says I must never look foolish? It would be unpleasant, not a disaster."
4. They say the word "embarrass" out loud, fast, over and over for half a minute, until it is just a sound.
5. "There's that worry again. I can feel it in my stomach. It can stay while I rehearse my slides."
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100: 20 points per item, 12 for the right category and 8 for a reason that fits it. Give the reason points only when the category is right. Accept any wording that carries the idea.

1. Content. Reason: it weighs the evidence (past talks) to judge whether the prediction is likely to be true.
2. Relationship. Reason: it labels the thought as a thought (puts "I'm having the thought that" in front of it) without arguing about whether it is true.
3. Content. Reason: it disputes the demand that one must never look foolish, or the exaggeration that it would be a disaster, and replaces it with a less extreme belief. An answer that says it challenges whether the belief is reasonable or rational is correct.
4. Relationship. Reason: repeating the word strips it of its meaning so that it is heard as a sound, while nothing is said about whether the thought is true.
5. Relationship. Reason: it notices the worry and the feeling and lets them stay (acceptance), while carrying on with what matters (rehearsing). It does not ask whether the worry is right.

If an answer says an item is both, give 6 of the 12 category points, and the reason points if the reason given for the correct side fits.
feedback-instructions:: The learner sorted five responses to the thought "I'm going to embarrass myself tomorrow" into ones that work on the thought's content and ones that work on their relationship to it, and got a score. In 80 to 130 words, name each item they got wrong or only half right, give the right category, and say what in the response signals it. Then link the items to the module's sources: 1 is Beck's checking of evidence, 3 is Ellis's disputing of "musts" and catastrophising, 2 is the "I am having the thought that" prefix (Healy and colleagues 2008), 4 is the word repetition exercise (Masuda and colleagues 2004), 5 is ACT's acceptance plus action on values. If everything was right, confirm in one sentence and add that a real thought record often ends with a balanced thought while defusion ends with the same thought held more loosely. No generic praise.
