## Test:
id:: d65b7213-3c92-4ecc-81a9-50fcbd9e2ab1
#### Question
content:: You have one hour with a friend who has never thought seriously about AI, and you genuinely believe the topic is urgent. The intuitive move is to spend the hour making your strongest case — the urgency seems to demand it.

**Why is spending most of that hour listening not a waste of the urgency? What does listening actually do, mechanically, that arguing cannot?**

assessment-instructions::
Score according to the following rubric.
**1** — Treats listening as politeness or a rapport trick that earns the right to lecture. *Example: "Listening makes them like you, so they'll be more willing to agree with you when you make your case."*

**2** — Knows persuasion-first fails but cannot explain the mechanism. *Example: "People get defensive when you lecture them, so it's better to listen first and go slow."*

**3** — Correctly explains the mechanism: people evaluate the messenger before the message, and the messenger evaluation runs on trust, which is built by being understood — not by being correct. A fact delivered before trust exists gets processed as a status move or an identity threat, and triggers counter-arguing instead of updating. Listening also reveals what the person actually cares about, so anything you eventually say can attach to concerns they already have instead of arriving as an alien agenda. *Example: "Arguing transmits information to someone who hasn't yet decided whether to trust the source, so the information bounces. Listening does two things arguing can't: it makes them feel understood, which is what trust is actually made of, and it tells me what they already care about — their job, their kids — so when I do share my view it lands on something they own rather than something I imported."*

**4** — As above, plus articulates that the conversation is not the unit — the relationship is. One hour of listening keeps the channel open indefinitely; one hour of arguing usually closes it. A friend who felt heard returns to the topic on their own, and a self-initiated return is worth more than any concession extracted in the moment. *Example: Adds "The urgency actually argues for listening, not against it. If the topic matters for years, the thing to optimize is whether there's a second conversation. People reopen topics with people who listened, and they avoid people who argued."*

**5** — As above, plus the propagation-level implication: conversations spread through trust networks, and people can only pass on the conversational stance they experienced, not the facts they were hit with. Someone who was listened to can go listen to their own people; someone who was lectured either goes silent or becomes a lecturer, and lecturers don't propagate. *Example: Adds "If this ever spreads past me, it spreads because the person I talked to talks to someone else. They'll reproduce how I treated them, not what I told them. Listening is the only thing on the menu that's actually contagious."*
