assessment-instructions::
Single pass/fail. Pass requires checks 1 and 2, plus at least one of checks 3 and 4. Each check is yes or no. Silence on a check fails that check; it is never a default pass.

1. TWO FIGURES, AND THE THRESHOLD APPLIED. The answer gives capability and generality as two separate quantities, and derives the generality figure by comparing a stated percentile against the stated expert bar. Capability on the negotiation task: at or near the top of the human range. Generality: no listed domain is held at expert level, so zero or near zero, because the highest domain score in the summary is about the 65th percentile, below the 80 to 90 bar. Any wording passes. "None of the ten clears the bar" passes.
FAIL IF: the two are merged into a single how-general-is-it score; or generality is reported as 20 percent or 2 of 10 by reading "not below the median" as expert level; or a generality figure is given with no reference to the threshold; or either figure is missing.

2. LOCATES THE BROKEN STEP. The answer treats "narrow" and "not a frontier safety concern" as two separate claims and says which one it contests, with a reason that turns on what these axes do and do not do. Any one of these reasons passes: the axes describe what a system can do and record nothing about what it is pointed at, at what scale, or under what oversight, so no risk verdict follows from them alone; transformative impact is a matter of consequences rather than breadth, and exceptional capability on a small number of critical tasks is one of its recognised shapes, so a low breadth score is not evidence of low impact; a capability profile no human has is exactly the case a battery derived from human ability differences is worst at scoring, so a low score here is weak evidence; the battery has no slot for the system's one strong capability, so the generality figure is not a description of the dangerous behaviour at all.
A student who defends the developer passes this check if the defence names the step it defends and rests on a distinction rather than an assertion, for instance that "frontier safety concern" is a regulatory term keyed to broad capability, so the label is correct while the deployment is still dangerous.
FAIL IF: the conclusion is rejected only by asserting the system is really general or is obviously dangerous, with no step named; or the whole sentence is endorsed without noticing the second clause needs its own argument.

3. SOMETHING THE AXES DO NOT ENCODE. Names at least one risk-relevant fact outside both axes and says why it matters here. Passing: the autonomy of the deployment, which the summary states and neither axis records; what tools, scaffolding or elicitation produced either number and whether the bare model reproduces them; the reference class, since 99 percent of trained negotiators is a selected pool rather than adults; whether the battery tasks resemble the deployed job; which two domains sit at the 65th percentile; how many copies run and for how long.
Credit an answer that distinguishes "the summary does not tell us" from "the axes do not record it". That distinction is the point of the check, not an extra demand.
FAIL IF: the only thing named is a further capability measurement, such as another benchmark, rather than something the axes structurally do not record.

4. PRESSES ON THE MEASUREMENT. Says something defensible about persuasion's absence from the domain list. Either route passes: it decomposes across several listed domains (on-the-spot reasoning, which covers theory of mind and adaptation; reading and writing; general knowledge; working memory), so a genuinely superhuman negotiator should have moved those scores, and the two measurements are therefore hard to reconcile; or the list has no slot for it, so the generality figure omits the system's only strong capability and understates it by construction.
FAIL IF: the answer only says the framework has limitations or no framework is perfect, without naming what is missing or misplaced in this case.

Grader notes:
- Grade the reasoning, not the verdict. A defended pro-developer answer passes. An anti-developer answer with no step named fails check 2.
- If the student argues the profile as described is internally strained, since superhuman live persuasion sits badly with below-median reading and writing and general knowledge, that is a correct observation and passes check 4. Do not require them to resolve it.
- Do NOT accept the escape route that the two axes are only a description language and were never meant to bound risk. The chapter uses the framework for safety planning explicitly: "certain thresholds still matter for safety planning. A system performing well on 50% of domains poses different risks than one excelling at 90%"; "identify thresholds for regulation, and emerging risks before they materialize"; "The rate of improvement along either axis provides important signals about which risks are most pressing." A student taking that route has dodged the question. Correct it in one sentence. What does pass on this theme is distinguishing the aggregate percentage from the per-domain profile and arguing that the aggregate is the part that cannot carry a risk verdict, which is the chapter's own remedy: "focusing on specific capability gaps rather than aggregate progress."
- Require no terminology: not "expert level", not ANI, not TAI, not any named framework or author. Grade whether the two quantities stay apart and the threshold gets applied.
