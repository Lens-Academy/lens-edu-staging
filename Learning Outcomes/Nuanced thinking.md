#### Question: Open
id:: 4d16c4a5-4f74-4c00-895a-01de3ee1db97
content::
A colleague says: "Releasing the weights of a highly capable AI model is either good because outside researchers can scrutinize it, or bad because malicious actors can misuse it. We need to decide whether capable models should always be kept closed."

Turn this binary claim into a decision-ready analysis. Apply probabilistic thinking, gray thinking, and multi-factor thinking. State a provisional recommendation, and name evidence that would substantially change your view.
assessment-instructions::
Score the response from 0 to 100 using these components:

- Probabilistic thinking, 25 points: Expresses calibrated uncertainty about important claims or outcomes rather than treating them as certain. Full credit requires at least one meaningful probability, confidence level, or bounded qualitative estimate and an explanation of what drives the uncertainty.
- Gray thinking, 25 points: Identifies meaningful benefits and harms or tradeoffs on both sides without assuming they are equally important. Full credit requires weighing them rather than merely listing one pro and one con.
- Multi-factor thinking, 25 points: Replaces the open-versus-closed binary with several decision-relevant factors, such as model capability, access controls, release timing, likely users, monitoring, reversibility, or the marginal value of outside scrutiny. Accept other relevant factors.
- Decision and updating, 25 points: Gives a clear provisional recommendation that follows from the analysis and identifies specific evidence that would materially change it.

Do not require the learner to use the three technique names if the reasoning demonstrates them. Grade reasoning, not whether the learner supports open or closed models. A strong answer can recommend either position or a conditional policy. Give 50 to 74 when two techniques are applied well but the third is absent or confused. Give 25 to 49 when only one is applied well or all three appear only as labels. Give 0 to 24 when the response remains substantially binary or does not address the decision.
feedback-instructions::
Name the strongest part of the learner's reasoning. Then identify the one missing or misapplied technique whose improvement would most change the analysis. If all three are used well, suggest one way to make the recommendation or update condition more precise.
