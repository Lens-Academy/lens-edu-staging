#### Question
content:: Two researchers have spent years arguing in public that building superhuman AI on anything like current techniques would kill everyone. They close their case with two prayers. The first: that they be proven wrong, publicly shamed for having raised a false alarm, and forgotten. The second, which they call their "true last prayer": "Rise to the occasion, humanity, and win."

**Distinguish what each prayer asks for. What would need to happen in the real world for the first prayer to be answered? For the second? And why would people who sincerely want the first still call the second their "true" one?**

assessment-instructions::
Score according to the following rubric.
**1** — Cannot identify what either prayer asks for, or conflates them. *Example: "Both prayers ask for humanity to survive."*

**2** — Can identify one prayer but not the other, or correctly identifies both but cannot explain the conditions for either. *Example: "The first prayer is that they be wrong and forgotten. The second is 'Rise to the occasion, humanity, and win.' I'm not sure what conditions would satisfy each."*

**3** — Correctly identifies both prayers and the conditions for one. States that the first prayer would be answered if AI predictions prove false and humanity lives happily ever after; OR states that the second prayer would be answered if humanity takes action and averts the catastrophe. Does not fully address why the second is called "true." *Example: "The first prayer is that they be proven wrong and forgotten. That would happen if AI turns out not to be dangerous. The second prayer is 'Rise to the occasion, humanity, and win,' but I'm not sure what makes it the 'true' one."*

**4** — Correctly identifies both prayers and the conditions for both, and explains why the second is called "true": they refuse to rely on doing nothing and prefer to trust in human action over passive hope. *Example: "First prayer: they would be wrong, shamed, and forgotten, meaning AI doesn't pose the threat they described, and humanity lives happily ever after without having to do anything. Second prayer: humanity rises to the occasion, meaning people act, fight, and win against the AI risk. The second is 'true' because they won't stake everything on the first outcome happening on its own. They'd rather call for action than just hope to be wrong."*

**5** — As above, plus articulates the deeper epistemic point: the two prayers are compatible — they would prefer the first (to be wrong) but refuse to rely on it, so they issue the second as their actual position. The "true last prayer" is their statement of what they stand for given that being passively wrong may not happen. *Example adds: "The two prayers aren't in tension. They genuinely want to be wrong. That would be the best news. But wanting to be wrong doesn't mean doing nothing. The second prayer is 'true' because it's what they're actually asking of the reader: not just to hope things work out, but to act. Both can be held at once: hope you're wrong, and act as if you're not."*
