#### Question: Open
id:: b3caf462-e023-4022-8f10-2405a526e6a7
content::
Two expert groups agree on current AI capabilities but disagree strongly about long-run catastrophe. One group trusts multi-step theoretical arguments; the other wants direct demonstrations of dangerous behavior before updating much.

How should a decision-maker use this disagreement? Propose one crux or evidence-gathering question that could help, and explain why averaging the two top-level probabilities would lose information.

assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

35 points: The answer identifies that the disagreement concerns evidential standards or how theoretical and empirical evidence are weighted, not simply different current facts.

35 points: The answer proposes a concrete crux or evidence-gathering question whose possible outcomes would update at least one side's long-run view.

30 points: The answer explains that averaging hides the source of disagreement and can discard information about causal models, evidence, or update rules.

feedback-instructions::
Give 100 to 160 words. Assess whether the proposed crux is actually discriminating. Below full credit, identify the main reason the suggested evidence would fail to move the disagreement.
