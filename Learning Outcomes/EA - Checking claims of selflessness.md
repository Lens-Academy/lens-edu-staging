## Test:
id:: 2cd296d9-3b55-4e0d-98ec-1f69f33ffa86
#### Question: Open
id:: df270991-4bef-4b48-af30-1314291d1f5e
content::
A meditation teacher says: "Since a retreat five years ago I have had no self. Nothing upsets me anymore, and I am at peace all the time."

This contains several different claims. Name two of them that could be true or false independently of each other.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100: 50 for each of two distinct claims that could be true or false independently. Credit any two of: a change in the sense of self, or a specific aspect of it (her life story or identity, feeling that thoughts or the body are hers, feeling that she is the one acting, where she feels located, the boundary between her and the world, the basic feeling of being there); that she is no longer upset or reactive to events; that she is at peace all the time (a lasting positive state); that the change has lasted five years. Two aspects of the sense of self count as two claims if they are clearly different aspects. Restating the same claim in other words counts once.
feedback-instructions:: The learner split a teacher's claim ("no self, nothing upsets me, at peace all the time since a retreat five years ago") into separate claims. Name the strongest part. Below 100, name what was missing, for example that "no self" itself could mean any of several changes (narrative self, ownership, agency, embodiment, boundaries, basic self), and that being at peace is a separate claim, since a changed sense of self can be distressing. 50 to 100 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: b6d8db21-54fd-460f-a068-a8e8322eed9c
content::
Propose one way to check part of the teacher's claim that does not rely only on what she says about herself.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 50: the check uses evidence other than her own report, for example asking people who live or work with her, observing her reactions in stressful situations, measuring stress in the body (sleep, heart rate, muscle tension, stress hormones), a behavioural or implicit test, or brain measures. 50: the check is matched to a specific part of her claim and could tell a true claim from a false one, for example partners' reports of irritability or sleep loss during a hard period tested against "nothing upsets me". A standard questionnaire she fills in herself counts as her own report and earns at most 20 for the first element.
feedback-instructions:: The learner proposed a check on the teacher's claim that does not rely only on her report. Name the strongest part. Below 100, say what was missing: an outside source of evidence, or a clear link to one part of her claim. You may mention that in Jeffery Martin's study of people reporting this kind of state, a participant reported deep peace while his partner saw poor sleep, appetite and mood, and participants overrated their body awareness. 50 to 100 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 5ac3ff88-d1c3-4a6a-94b0-1accbdf3509e
content::
What could your check not show?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Give 100 for one real limit of the check the learner proposed, stated clearly. Limits that count include: outside observers and bodily measures cannot show what her experience is like from the inside, so they cannot confirm or refute that her sense of self changed; stress signs might have other causes; brain differences cannot be read directly as "no self", since the same brain areas do many things, and studies are small and cross-sectional; one tense period is a single case; observers may be biased by what they expect. Give 50 for a limit that is vague but points in a right direction (for example "it might not be accurate" with no reason). Give 0 if no limit is stated. If the learner did not propose a check, credit a real limit of any outside check on her claim.
feedback-instructions:: The learner named what their check on the teacher's claim could not show. Name the strongest part. Below 100, name one clear limit that fits their check, especially that outside evidence can test claims about reactions and stress but not what her experience of self is like from the inside. 40 to 90 words. No generic praise.
