It mirrors the module's readings: Lindahl and Britton's six changes in sense of self, Martin's PNSE reports and the partner and body checks that disagreed with them, the NADA questionnaire, and brain studies read as a lesson in limits. The questions are separate because each asks one thing. Review 2026-10-05: the first question used to accept any two claims (for example "no self" and "at peace"), which a careful reader passes without the module. It now asks for two different changes in the sense of self (Lindahl and Britton's categories, credited in plain words), and a new second question asks for a finding from the readings (Lindahl and Britton, Martin) that separates a changed sense of self from lasting peace. ## Test:
id:: 2cd296d9-3b55-4e0d-98ec-1f69f33ffa86
#### Question: Open
id:: df270991-4bef-4b48-af30-1314291d1f5e
content::
A meditation teacher says: "Since a retreat five years ago I have had no self. Nothing upsets me anymore, and I am at peace all the time."

"I have had no self" could describe quite different changes. Name two of them.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100: 50 for each of two clearly different changes in the sense of self, described in any words. Changes that count: her sense of who she is, her life story or personal identity changed or fell away; thoughts, feelings or her body no longer feel like hers; she no longer feels she is the one doing her actions; she feels located somewhere else, or outside or beyond her body; the boundary between her and other people or the world has loosened or gone; the basic feeling of being there at all has gone. Restating the same change in other words counts once. A claim about her mood or peace is not a change in the sense of self and earns nothing here.
feedback-instructions:: The learner named two different changes that a teacher's "I have had no self" could describe. Name the strongest part. Below 100, name what was missing, using Lindahl and Britton's six categories from this module: narrative self, ownership, agency, embodiment, self-other or self-world boundaries, basic self. 50 to 100 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 2b750683-f943-4977-8147-f00c55cc4256
content::
She also says she is at peace all the time. What did the module's readings find that shows a real change in the sense of self need not come with lasting peace?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Give 100 for one finding stated clearly, in any words. Findings that count: among meditators in a study of meditation-related challenges who reported changes in their sense of self, about half reported distress and almost half reported impairment in daily life, and some changes (for example losing the basic feeling of being there) lasted for years and were very hard; people who reported a lasting change in their sense of self together with deep peace showed signs of stress that others saw (poor sleep, tense muscles, low mood) during hard periods, or lost the state under heavy stress; some people at first took such a change for a mental disorder. Give 50 for a finding stated so vaguely that it could come from anywhere (for example "some people find it hard"). Give 0 for an answer that gives a reason with no finding behind it.
feedback-instructions:: The learner said what the module's readings found that shows a change in the sense of self need not come with lasting peace. Name the strongest part. Below 100, name one finding that fits: Lindahl and Britton's 55% distress and 45% impairment among meditators with such changes (in a sample recruited for challenges), or Martin's participant who reported deep peace while his partner saw poor sleep, appetite and mood. 50 to 100 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: b6d8db21-54fd-460f-a068-a8e8322eed9c
content::
Propose one way to check part of the teacher's claim that does not rely only on what she says about herself.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 50: the check uses evidence other than her own report, for example asking people who live or work with her, observing her reactions in stressful situations, measuring stress in the body (sleep, heart rate, muscle tension, stress hormones), a behavioural or implicit test, or brain measures. 50: the check is matched to a specific part of her claim and could tell a true claim from a false one, for example partners' reports of irritability or sleep loss during a hard period tested against "nothing upsets me". A standard questionnaire she fills in herself counts as her own report and earns at most 20 for the first element.
feedback-instructions:: The learner proposed a check on the teacher's claim that does not rely only on her report. Name the strongest part. Below 100, say what was missing: an outside source of evidence, or a clear link to one part of her claim. You may mention that in Jeffery Martin's study of people reporting this kind of state, a participant reported deep peace while his partner saw poor sleep, appetite and mood, and participants overrated their body awareness. 50 to 100 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 5ac3ff88-d1c3-4a6a-94b0-1accbdf3509e
content::
What could a check like yours not show about her claim?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Give 100 for one real limit of a check on the teacher's claim that uses evidence other than her own report, stated clearly. Limits that count include: outside observers and bodily measures cannot show what her experience is like from the inside, so they cannot confirm or refute that her sense of self changed; stress signs might have other causes; brain differences cannot be read directly as "no self", since the same brain areas do many things, and studies are small and cross-sectional; one tense period is a single case; observers may be biased by what they expect. Give 50 for a limit that is vague but points in a right direction (for example "it might not be accurate" with no reason). Give 0 if no limit is stated.
feedback-instructions:: The learner named what their check on the teacher's claim could not show. Name the strongest part. Below 100, name one clear limit that fits their check, especially that outside evidence can test claims about reactions and stress but not what her experience of self is like from the inside. 40 to 90 words. No generic praise.
