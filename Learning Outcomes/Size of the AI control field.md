#### Question: Open
id:: 35a9d990-709e-4114-9f01-47974c823591
content::
Two friends are writing a careers guide and disagree about AI control.

Friend 1: "Only about 11 people work on AI control. I saw it in a published 2025 count."
Friend 2: "AI control is now one of the biggest parts of AI safety. Nearly 800 people signed up for an online AI control hackathon in March 2026, and a government fund is spending £27m."

a. Roughly how many people work full-time on AI control, and roughly how many on technical AI safety as a whole?
b. For each friend, say what their claim gets right or wrong.
c. Name the counting choices that can make careful counts of the same field differ, and say which way each one moves the number.
placeholder:: Answer a, b, then c
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 25: the two numbers. The published figures are these: a 2025 count filed about 11 people under AI control, an expert estimate gives 5 to 50 full-time staff on control, and the same 2025 count found about 620 people in technical AI safety (about 1,100 in AI safety overall). Full 25 for control in the tens (up to about 100) and technical AI safety from a few hundred to a few thousand (about 200 to 5,000). Also full 25 for a larger control number if the answer says what it counts (for example control and monitoring teams inside AI companies and government institutes) and its numbers are consistent with that. 12 if only one of the two numbers is in range, or only a share or direction ("small") is given. 0 if control is put in the hundreds or above a third of technical AI safety with no stated definition. 40: part b. 15 for Friend 1, any accurate point, for example that a published 2025 count did put only about 11 people under AI control, or that it is too low as a count of everyone doing control work. 25 for an accurate, reasoned assessment of Friend 2's evidence, for example: hackathon sign-ups are not people working on control full-time, the £27m fund pays for alignment research as well as control and money is not a headcount, or the evidence shows interest and funding, not size relative to the rest of AI safety. 35: part c. 20 for naming at least one counting choice and what it includes or leaves out, for example: filing each whole organisation under one main area (so control teams inside AI companies and government institutes are counted under other areas), counting only dedicated organisations, or including or excluding nearby work such as monitoring research. 10 if the answer only says the boundary is unclear without naming any choice. 15 for saying which way the named choice or choices move the number, for example that counting in-house teams or nearby work raises it and counting only dedicated organisations lowers it. Any accurate direction earns the full 15. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording, and accept any other accurate, specific point. Grade the reasoning, not whether the learner thinks control deserves more people. Cap at 50 if the answer agrees that control is one of the biggest parts of AI safety without a stated definition that would support it. Cap at 60 if either number in part a is more than ten times off these ranges without a stated definition, and cap at 60 if part c names no counting choice at all. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "a. Tens of people work full-time on control, against roughly 600 in technical AI safety. b. Friend 1 is quoting a real 2025 count, but as a count of everyone doing control work it is too low. Friend 2's evidence shows interest and money, not size: hackathon sign-ups are not full-time researchers, and the fund pays for alignment as well as control. c. One count filed each whole organisation under a single area, so control teams at AI companies and government institutes landed under other areas, which lowers the control count. Counting those in-house teams, or nearby monitoring research, raises it."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest part of the answer in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve it. Do not revise or dispute the score. If the learner sided fully with Friend 2, ask what 800 hackathon sign-ups show about how many people work on control full-time. If they gave no counting mechanism in part c, ask why one 2025 count found only about 11 people. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points. At most five sentences. No generic praise.
