#### Question: Open
id:: 35a9d990-709e-4114-9f01-47974c823591
content::
Two friends are writing a careers guide and disagree about AI control.

Friend 1: "Only about 11 people work on AI control. I saw it in a published count of AI safety organisations."
Friend 2: "AI control is now one of the biggest parts of AI safety. Frontier AI companies run monitors on their own AI agents, and 200 people came to the last control conference."

a. Give your own rough estimate, with numbers or a share, of how many people work full-time on AI control compared with technical AI safety as a whole.
b. For each friend, say what their claim gets right and what it gets wrong or leaves out.
placeholder:: Answer a, then Friend 1, then Friend 2
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 30: the estimate. Full 30 for rough numbers or a share of this order: tens of people working full-time on control against several hundred in technical AI safety, a few percent (for example "5 to 50 out of about 600" or "1 to 10 percent"). Also full 30 for a larger share if the answer states what it counts (for example control and monitoring teams inside AI companies and government institutes) and the numbers it uses are consistent with that definition. 15 if the answer only gives a direction, such as "small", with no numbers or share. 35: Friend 1. 15 for what is right: a published 2025 count did put only about a dozen people (Redwood and Luthien) under AI control. 20 for what it leaves out, with a specific mechanism: that count files each whole organisation under one main area, so control work inside AI companies and government institutes (the UK AI Security Institute was filed under evaluations) is counted elsewhere, or it counts only dedicated organisations and leaves out nearby work. A general remark that boundaries are fuzzy, with no mechanism, earns 10 of these 20. 35: Friend 2. 15 for what is right: control work does now happen inside AI companies, such as monitors on their own agents, so counting research groups alone misses some of it. 20 for what is wrong: running monitors at some companies is not evidence of many full-time control researchers, a conference audience of 200 includes engineers, policy people and newcomers and is not a headcount of full-time researchers, or "the companies run monitors" overstates a mixed picture (outside review found gaps in coverage). Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Grade the reasoning, not whether the learner thinks control deserves more people. Cap at 50 if the answer agrees that control is one of the biggest parts of AI safety without a stated definition that would support it. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "a. Tens of people work full-time on control, against roughly 600 in technical AI safety, so a few percent. Friend 1 is quoting a real count, but that count filed each organisation under one area, so control teams at AI companies and government institutes were counted under other areas. The true number is higher. Friend 2 is right that control now also happens inside AI companies. But companies running monitors does not show how many people work on control full-time, and the conference audience included engineers, policy people and newcomers, so it is not a headcount. Control is bigger than 11 people but still a small part of AI safety by any published count."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest part of the answer in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve it. Do not revise or dispute the score. If the learner sided fully with Friend 2, ask what a conference audience of 200 shows about how many people work on control full-time. If they gave no counting mechanism for Friend 1, ask why that count found only about 11 people. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points. At most five sentences. No generic praise.
