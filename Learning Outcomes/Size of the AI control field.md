#### Question: Open
id:: 35a9d990-709e-4114-9f01-47974c823591
content::
Two friends are writing a careers guide and disagree about AI control.

Friend 1: "Only about 11 people work on AI control. I saw it in a published 2025 count."
Friend 2: "AI control is now one of the biggest parts of AI safety. 200 people came to the last control conference, and a government fund is spending £27m on control and alignment research."

a. Give your own rough estimate, with numbers or a share, of how many people work full-time on AI control compared with technical AI safety as a whole.
b. For each friend, say what their claim gets right or wrong.
c. Why can careful counts of the same field differ this much? How would your estimate change if you also counted the control and monitoring teams inside AI companies and government institutes?
placeholder:: Answer a, b, then c
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 25: the estimate. Full 25 for rough numbers or a share within an order of magnitude of the published figures: for example tens of people working full-time on control against several hundred in technical AI safety, "5 to 50 out of about 600", or about 1 to 15 percent. Also full 25 for a larger share if the answer states what it counts (for example control and monitoring teams inside AI companies and government institutes) and its numbers are consistent with that definition. 10 if the answer only gives a direction, such as "small", with no numbers or share. 0 for a numeric estimate more than ten times off the published figures without a supporting definition. 40: part b. 15 for Friend 1, any accurate point, for example that a published 2025 count did put only about a dozen people under AI control, or that the figure is too low as a count of everyone doing control work. 25 for Friend 2, any accurate point about why the evidence does not show control is one of the biggest parts of AI safety, for example: the conference audience of 200 was researchers, lab engineers and policy people, so it is not a count of people working on control full-time, the £27m fund covers alignment as well as control and money spent is not a headcount, or 200 people is still small next to several hundred to a thousand or more in AI safety. If the answer also notes something Friend 2 gets right, that is welcome but not required. 35: part c. 20 for why counts differ, with a specific mechanism, for example: one count files each whole organisation under one main area, so control teams inside AI companies and government institutes are counted under other areas, some counts include only dedicated organisations, or counts draw the line differently between control and nearby work such as chain-of-thought monitoring. A general remark that boundaries are fuzzy, with no mechanism, earns 10 of these 20. 15 for how the estimate changes: counting those teams raises it, and no published source gives the size of those teams, so the answer cannot say exactly by how much (an answer that says the share would still be a minority, or gives a stated guess, also earns the 15). Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording, and accept any other accurate, specific point. Grade the reasoning, not whether the learner thinks control deserves more people. Cap at 50 if the answer agrees that control is one of the biggest parts of AI safety without a stated definition that would support it. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "a. Tens of people work full-time on control, against roughly 600 in technical AI safety, so a few percent. b. Friend 1 is quoting a real 2025 count, but as a count of everyone doing control work it is too low. Friend 2's evidence does not show size: the conference audience included lab engineers and policy people, not only full-time control researchers, and the fund pays for alignment as well as control. c. Counts differ because one count filed each organisation under a single area, so control teams at AI companies and government institutes landed under other areas, and others draw the line between control and nearby monitoring work differently. Counting those teams would raise my estimate, by an amount no published count gives, but control would still be a minority of AI safety by any published figure."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest part of the answer in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve it. Do not revise or dispute the score. If the learner sided fully with Friend 2, ask what a conference audience of 200 shows about how many people work on control full-time. If they gave no counting mechanism in part c, ask why one 2025 count found only about 11 people. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points. At most five sentences. No generic praise.
