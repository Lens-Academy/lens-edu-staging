## Test:
id:: e591f326-3a0a-46fd-b4dd-b73daef252fb
#### Question: Open
id:: 7ffde190-f983-4de1-ac54-bf6ba3314660
content::
Three epidemiology teams assess the risk from a novel pathogen.

One relies mainly on historical base rates and assigns 1 percent. One uses a mechanistic model of the pathogen and assigns 12 percent. One largely agrees with the mechanistic model but thinks the intervention being considered creates serious behavioural side effects.

You are advising a health ministry. How should the disagreement affect your reasoning?

assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

25 points: The answer distinguishes the sources of disagreement instead of treating the three probabilities as equivalent votes.

25 points: The answer identifies at least one empirical crux that could discriminate between the base-rate and mechanistic models.

25 points: The answer separately recognises that the third team's disagreement concerns intervention consequences, not only the probability of the pathogen event.

25 points: The answer gives a defensible decision response such as further information gathering, a robust or reversible action, conditional planning, or justified deference based on the source of the disagreement.

Do not grade on which intervention the learner ultimately chooses.

feedback-instructions::
Give 80 to 150 words of feedback. State whether the learner successfully separated the disagreements and whether their proposed response follows from that diagnosis. Name one place where they averaged, deferred, or dismissed too quickly if applicable.
