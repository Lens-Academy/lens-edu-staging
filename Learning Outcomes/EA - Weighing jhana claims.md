## Test:
id:: 4e7602f2-560e-42ea-8008-7482aa8ed90d
#### Question: Open
id:: ad98ec32-dafe-4baa-bee4-6cd3b28f9639
content::
A company that sells week-long jhana retreats writes: "[We see] 60–70% of participants access jhana states during their first retreat."

What kind of evidence is this, and what can it not show? Is the claim more likely about light jhana or deep jhana, and what tells you?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 35: names the kind of evidence: the seller's own count of its participants, not independently checked, from a company with a financial interest in the figure (20 if it says only "the company's own data" or only "self-report" without either the conflict of interest or the lack of independent checking). 35: names something this evidence cannot show, any one of: that these are the states the old texts or deep-jhana teachers mean, that the count is unbiased, that the effect lasts or improves life. 30: says light (or intermediate, sutta-style) jhana with a reason, any one of: most participants reach it within a week, deep jhana is described as rare, critics say such states fall short of "true" jhana. 12 for "light" with no reason. Deep jhana: 0 of these 30.
feedback-instructions:: Name the most important thing the answer missed, if any, in one or two sentences. Then say the source: Jhourney's CEO Stephen Zerfas, company blog, 2026. 40 to 80 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 59ec26b2-0201-4a51-b3fa-2a882fbe44b2
content::
A monk who teaches jhana writes: "Within jhana all the five senses are totally shut down. One cannot see, one cannot hear, one cannot smell, taste nor feel touch." He supports this with the story of a lay student who, he says, entered jhana at home, was taken to hospital, showed no heartbeat on the ECG, was given electric shocks and felt nothing.

What kind of evidence supports this, and what can it not show? Is the claim about light jhana or deep jhana, and what tells you?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 35: names the kind of evidence: a teacher's own account plus a second-hand, unverified anecdote (20 if it says only "a teacher's opinion" or "an anecdote" without noting that it is second-hand or unverified). 35: names something it cannot show, any one of: that the event happened as told, that the senses were shut off rather than, for example, the man being unconscious, that this is what jhana must be for everyone. 30: says deep jhana with the reason that the senses are said to shut off completely (in light jhana the body and senses stay present). 12 for "deep" with no reason. Light jhana: 0 of these 30.
feedback-instructions:: Name the most important thing the answer missed, if any, in one or two sentences. Then say the source: Ajahn Brahm's booklet "The Jhanas", whose style of jhana is close to the deep, Visuddhimagga style. 40 to 80 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 7c5bbe94-f87a-4dfc-9ef9-1e8e2c224857
content::
A brain-imaging study of one meditator with about 6,000 hours of practice concludes: "these results demonstrate an apparently novel method of self-stimulating a brain reward system using only internal mental processes in a highly trained subject."

What kind of evidence is this? Name one thing it can show and one thing it cannot show.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 40: names the kind of evidence: a single-case brain study, one expert (n = 1). 30: one thing it can show, for example that this person's reported states go with measurable brain changes, including reward-system activity, so the reports match something physical. 30: one thing it cannot show, any one of: that it holds for other people, that ordinary learners would get the same, that jhana practice makes anyone happier outside the state or over time.
feedback-instructions:: Name the most important thing the answer missed, if any, in one or two sentences. Then say the source: Hagerty and colleagues 2013. 40 to 80 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 903b31a3-c386-4ce8-9864-d87278cf82b3
content::
A retreat company surveyed its own past participants (61 answered) and reports: "those who accessed the jhanas while on retreat were twice as likely to report changes in their relationships and lifestyle" than those who did not.

What kind of evidence is this? Name one thing it can show and one thing it cannot show.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 40: names the kind of evidence: a small, self-selected survey by a company of its own customers, with self-reported change (20 if it says only "a survey" without any of: self-selected, the company's own customers, self-report, small). 30: one thing it can show, for example that people who say they reached jhana also report more life changes. 30: one thing it cannot show, any one of: that reaching jhana caused the changes (people who reach it may differ in other ways), that the changes happened rather than being reported, that they last.
feedback-instructions:: Name the most important thing the answer missed, if any, in one or two sentences. Then say the source: Jhourney's "Life in HD" survey, 2024. Add that none of the four claims in this test is a controlled study of whether jhana practice changes wellbeing over time, and that this module's searches found none. 50 to 90 words. No generic praise.
