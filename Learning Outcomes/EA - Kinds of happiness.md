## Test:
id:: 90694eda-04e9-48ec-8d08-8c35feb75d41
#### Question: Open
id:: b8328d8f-3d28-4dc6-a573-6ed827bd6c11
content::
Ten quotes from real texts follow. For each one, write its number, the kind of happiness it is about, and the word or phrase in the quote that shows it, with a few words on why.

Choose from: **positive feeling**, **life satisfaction**, **eudaimonia**, **ataraxia**, **sukha** (say which grade: of the senses, of meditative absorption, or of a released mind), **fundamental wellbeing**, or **equanimity**. The word a quote uses ("happy", "pleasure", "well-being", "feel") is often not the kind it is about.

1. "We conclude that high income buys life satisfaction but not happiness." Which kind does "happiness" mean here?
2. "Please think about what you have been doing and experiencing during the past four weeks. Then report how much you experienced each of the following feelings (...)": positive, negative, good, bad, pleasant, unpleasant, happy, sad, afraid, joyful, angry, contented.
3. "Please imagine a ladder with steps numbered from zero at the bottom to 10 at the top. The top of the ladder represents the best possible life for you and the bottom of the ladder represents the worst possible life for you. On which step of the ladder would you say you personally feel you stand at this time?"
4. "(...) as it is not one swallow or one fine day that makes a spring, so it is not one day or a short time that makes a man blessed and happy."
5. Six "aspects of well-being": "self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, and personal growth".
6. "By pleasure we mean the absence of pain in the body and of trouble in the soul."
7. "(...) a state of inner equipoise that cannot be upset by gain and loss, honor and dishonor, praise and blame, pleasure and pain."
8. "He permeates and pervades, suffuses and fills this very body with the rapture and pleasure born from withdrawal."
9. "Now whatever pleasure or joy arises in dependence on these five strands of sensuality, that is the allure of sensuality."
10. "All remaining vestiges of self-related thoughts are gone by this point, as are experiences of emotion."
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100: 10 points per quote, 6 for the kind and 4 for the signal. The signal points go to an answer that points to wording in the quote (quoted or described in the learner's own words) and gives a reason that fits the kind the quote is really about. Award signal points only when the kind earned at least some points. Ignore spelling and formatting. If the learner names two kinds for one quote, score the better of the two and subtract 2 from its kind points, unless the item below credits that pair.

1. "High income buys life satisfaction but not happiness": which kind is "happiness". Kind: positive feeling (emotional wellbeing, how people feel day to day): 6. Signal: "happiness" is set against life satisfaction, which is a judgement about one's life, so here it means feelings: 4. Life satisfaction: 0 kind points.
2. Report how often one experienced feelings such as positive, negative, pleasant, unpleasant, happy, sad, joyful, contented over four weeks. Kind: positive feeling (how often pleasant and unpleasant feelings occur): 6. Signal: it asks how often feelings were experienced over a period, not for a judgement of one's life as a whole: 4. Life satisfaction: 0 kind points.
3. Imagine a ladder from the worst possible life (0) to the best possible life (10) and say which step you stand on now. Kind: life satisfaction (life evaluation): 6. Signal: it asks the person to rate their life as a whole against the best and worst possible life, which is a judgement. The word "feel" in the question does not make it a feeling: 4. Positive feeling: 0 kind points.
4. Not one day or a short time makes a man blessed and happy, as one swallow does not make a spring. Kind: eudaimonia (a life lived well): 6. Signal: happiness here belongs to a whole life or a long stretch of living, not to a moment, so it is not a feeling: 4. Life satisfaction: 3 kind points (it is also about a whole life, but it is the person's own judgement, which the quote does not mention). Positive feeling: 0 kind points.
5. Six aspects of well-being: self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, personal growth. Kind: eudaimonia (living well, functioning well): 6. Signal: the items are ways of living and functioning (growth, purpose, autonomy, relationships), not feelings and not an overall judgement of one's life: 4. Life satisfaction or positive feeling: 0 kind points.
6. Pleasure defined as the absence of pain in the body and of trouble in the soul. Kind: ataraxia (freedom from disturbance): 6. Signal: "pleasure" is defined as an absence of pain and of trouble in the soul, a calm state, not an enjoyable feeling: 4. Positive feeling or equanimity: 2 kind points.
7. A state of inner equipoise that cannot be upset by gain and loss, honor and dishonor, praise and blame, pleasure and pain. Kind: equanimity: 6. Signal: an even stance toward everything that happens, including pleasure and pain themselves, rather than one more pleasant state: 4. Ataraxia or Stoic calm (apatheia): 4 kind points, and full signal points if the reason is that the state is not disturbed by gains and losses.
8. The body filled with rapture and pleasure born from withdrawal. Kind: sukha of meditative absorption (jhana): 6. Signal: "born from withdrawal" (withdrawal from the senses into concentration), so the pleasure does not come from sense objects even though it is felt in the body: 4. Sukha with no grade or with the senses grade: 3 kind points. Positive feeling: 1 kind point.
9. Pleasure or joy arising in dependence on the five strands of sensuality. Kind: sukha of the senses: 6. Signal: "in dependence on these five strands of sensuality", so the pleasure comes from the senses: 4. Positive feeling: 5 kind points. Sukha of absorption or of a released mind: 2 kind points.
10. All remaining self-related thoughts are gone, as are experiences of emotion. Kind: fundamental wellbeing (persistent non-symbolic experience): 6. Signal: the change is in the sense of self, and there is no emotion at all, so it is not a feeling, not even a calm one: 4. Equanimity or sukha of a released mind: 2 kind points.
feedback-instructions:: The learner sorted ten quotes into kinds of happiness, named the wording that shows each kind, and received a score. In 120 to 180 words, name the quotes they got wrong or partly right, and for each give the correct kind and the wording that signals it. Then say where the quotes come from, since the test showed them without authors: 1 Kahneman and Deaton 2010, 2 Diener and Biswas-Diener's Scale of Positive and Negative Experience, 3 the Cantril ladder used by Gallup, 4 Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics Book I, 5 Carol Ryff's 1989 dimensions of psychological well-being, 6 Epicurus's Letter to Menoeceus, 7 Bhikkhu Bodhi on upekkha, 8 the Samaññaphala Sutta (DN 2) on the first jhana, 9 the Mahadukkhakkhandha Sutta (MN 13), 10 Jeffery Martin on the far end of persistent non-symbolic experience. If they got everything right, confirm briefly and point out quotes 1 and 3: Kahneman and Deaton measured life satisfaction with the ladder in quote 3 and feelings with questions about yesterday, and income tracked the judgement more than the feelings. You may add that a 2023 reanalysis (Killingsworth, Kahneman and Mellers) found their plateau in feelings only among the least happy people. No generic praise.
