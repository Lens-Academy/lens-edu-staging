## Test:
id:: 90694eda-04e9-48ec-8d08-8c35feb75d41
#### Question: Open
id:: b8328d8f-3d28-4dc6-a573-6ed827bd6c11
content::
For each quote, write its number and the kind of happiness it is about. Choose from: **positive feeling**, **life satisfaction**, **eudaimonia**, **ataraxia** (Epicurean calm), **sukha** (say which grade: senses, meditative absorption, or released mind), **contentment**, **fundamental wellbeing**, or **equanimity**. If you are unsure, add a few words on why.

1. "Nature has placed mankind under the governance of two sovereign masters, pain and pleasure." (Jeremy Bentham)
2. "In most ways my life is close to my ideal." (an item of the Satisfaction With Life Scale, Diener and colleagues)
3. "Evenness of mind, unshakeable freedom of mind, a state of inner equipoise that cannot be upset by gain and loss, honor and dishonor, praise and blame, pleasure and pain." (Bhikkhu Bodhi)
4. "The happy man both lives well and does well." (Aristotle)
5. "Rapture & pleasure born from withdrawal." (a discourse attributed to the Buddha, describing the first jhana)
6. "All remaining vestiges of self-related thoughts are gone by this point, as are experiences of emotion." (Jeffery Martin, on the furthest group of his interviewees)
7. "We conclude that high income buys life satisfaction but not happiness." (Kahneman and Deaton). Which kind does "happiness" mean in this sentence?
8. "By pleasure we mean the absence of pain in the body and of trouble in the soul." (Epicurus)
9. "Whatever of pleasure is there in the world of desires, and whatever of larger happiness is there in the world of heaven, they do not come to the sixteenth part of the joy due to the suppression of desires." (Vyasa, commentary on the Yoga Sutras)
10. "Whatever pleasure arises in a fermentation-ended monk as he is reflecting on his mind released from passion, reflecting on his mind released from aversion, reflecting on his mind released from delusion." (the same discourse as quote 5)
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100: 10 points per quote. The learner names one kind per quote, chosen from a given list. Award 10 for the answer below or a clear equivalent in their own words, and the partial points listed where a partial answer is reasonable. Ignore spelling and formatting. If the learner names two kinds for one quote and one of them is correct, award 5 unless their note explains why both apply in a way that is accurate.

1. Bentham, pain and pleasure as the masters of mankind: positive feeling (pleasure, hedonic happiness). 10.
2. Satisfaction With Life Scale item: life satisfaction (a judgement about one's life as a whole). 10.
3. Bhikkhu Bodhi on evenness of mind not upset by gain and loss, praise and blame, pleasure and pain: equanimity. 10. Award 5 for ataraxia or Stoic apatheia.
4. Aristotle, the happy man lives well and does well: eudaimonia (a way of living). 10.
5. Rapture and pleasure in the first jhana: sukha of the meditative-absorption grade ("not of the flesh"). 10. Award 5 for sukha without the grade or with the wrong grade.
6. Martin, no self-related thoughts and no emotions at the far end: fundamental wellbeing (persistent non-symbolic experience). 10. Award 3 for equanimity.
7. Kahneman and Deaton's "happiness" as opposed to life satisfaction: positive feeling (emotional wellbeing, the emotional quality of everyday experience). 10. Award 0 for life satisfaction.
8. Epicurus, pleasure as absence of bodily pain and of trouble in the soul: ataraxia (Epicurean calm, static pleasure). 10. Award 3 for positive feeling.
9. Vyasa, the joy of suppressed desires exceeds sensual and heavenly pleasures: contentment. 10. Award 7 for ataraxia or the released-mind grade of sukha if the learner says the quote is about happiness from the end of desire.
10. Pleasure of a fully awakened ("fermentation-ended") monk reflecting on a mind released from passion, aversion and delusion: sukha of the released-mind grade (the highest, "more not of the flesh"). 10. Award 5 for sukha without the grade or with the wrong grade.
feedback-instructions:: The learner sorted ten quotes into kinds of happiness and received a score. In 100 to 160 words, name the quotes they got wrong or partly right, and for each give the correct kind and the word or phrase in the quote that signals it (for example "the absence of pain ... and of trouble in the soul" signals Epicurean calm; "my life is close to my ideal" is a judgement, not a feeling; Kahneman and Deaton use "happiness" for emotional wellbeing, the quality of everyday feelings). If they got everything right, confirm briefly and point out quote 7 as the one that shows why this skill matters: the same word, "happiness", meant a feeling there while "life satisfaction" meant a judgement, and their finding about income held for one and not the other. You may add that a 2023 reanalysis (Killingsworth, Kahneman and Mellers) found the income plateau in emotional wellbeing only among the least happy people. No generic praise.
