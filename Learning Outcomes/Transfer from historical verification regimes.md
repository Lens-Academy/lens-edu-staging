## Test:
id:: 710788c5-e14b-4cf0-bd6e-a789e28557fd

#### Question: Open
id:: ba4b879d-bbb0-4221-9d94-918f84af3e3a
content:: The Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty bans all nuclear explosions. Although the treaty has not entered into force, its monitoring organisation already operates a worldwide network of seismic, hydroacoustic, infrasound, and radionuclide stations, most of them outside the territory of any state being watched, and the network detected each of North Korea's nuclear tests. The treaty's on-site inspection provisions cannot be used until it enters into force, so for now the network is the whole regime.

This regime is not covered in the readings. In 300 to 450 words:

1. reconstruct its causal logic: what the stations observe, what compliance judgement that observation licenses, and the assumption about the prohibited act that makes the step from observation to judgement valid;
2. identify what this regime cannot reach, and why more stations would not fix it;
3. say which parts of the logic transfer to verifying a limit on frontier AI training and which do not, giving the reason for each.
max-chars:: 3500
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 35: part 1, the causal logic, 10: what the stations observe: physical signatures of an explosion (ground motion, pressure waves in water and air, radioactive particles), mostly from outside the watched state; 10: the judgement it licenses: a detected event of the right character shows that a nuclear test occurred, regardless of what the state declares; 15: the assumption that makes the step valid: the prohibited act itself unavoidably produces the signal, so any explosion large enough to matter gives a signature above the network's detection threshold and distinguishable from earthquakes and industrial blasts. Cap part 1 at 15 if the answer imports declaration-and-inspection logic from safeguards without noticing that this regime does not rely on declarations. 25: part 2, what it cannot reach, 10: at least one real gap, such as explosions below the detection threshold or deliberately muffled, confirming an ambiguous event without on-site access, or activity that produces no explosion (preparations, warhead design work, experiments without a nuclear yield); 15: why more stations would not fix it: they lower the threshold but cannot turn an ambiguous signal into a confirmation or attribution, which needs access to the site, and cannot observe activity that emits no explosion signature at all, so the gap lies in what the method observes, not in how densely it observes. 40: part 3, transfer, 20: what does not transfer, 10: the core logic of detecting the prohibited act from outside by a signature it cannot avoid, and 10: the reason: a frontier training run emits no such signature detectable from outside the state, since power draw and heat are ambiguous and shared with other industry and the object of interest (the computation, the weights) is silent; 20: what transfers, 10: at least one principle, such as passive monitoring that does not depend on the inspected state's cooperation wherever some external signal exists (power, chip procurement, satellite imagery of construction), corroboration across independent sensor types, or setting the treaty threshold at the level the sensors can detect rather than the level one wishes to prohibit, and 10: its reason. A transfer or non-transfer claim earns its reason points only if the reason rests on the difference or similarity between the two prohibited acts. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Grade reasoning, not conclusions: an answer that argues the regime transfers better than the course suggests can score full marks if each step is reasoned. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "1. The stations observe physical signatures that a nuclear explosion cannot avoid producing: ground motion picked up by seismometers, pressure waves in the oceans and the atmosphere, and radioactive particles and gases carried downwind. Most stations sit outside the watched state, so the observation does not depend on its cooperation or its declarations. From an event of the right character (location, depth, the shape of the seismic waves, and ideally radionuclides) the regime licenses the judgement that a nuclear explosion took place, whatever the state says. The step is valid because of an assumption about the prohibited act: any explosion large enough to matter necessarily produces a signal above the network's detection threshold that can be told apart from earthquakes, mining blasts and other industrial explosions. Unlike safeguards, the regime does not check a declaration; the act announces itself. 2. It cannot reach explosions below the detection threshold, including small tests or tests muffled in a large underground cavity, and it cannot confirm an ambiguous event: a seismic signal without radionuclides shows that something happened but not that it was nuclear, which is what on-site inspection is for and why its absence matters. It also cannot see anything that produces no explosion: preparations at a test site, warhead design work, or experiments that stay below criticality. More stations lower the threshold somewhat, but they do not change the kind of thing the network observes. Resolving ambiguity needs access to the site, and activity without an explosion emits no signal for any number of stations to pick up. The gap is in what the method can observe, not in how densely it samples. 3. The core logic does not transfer. A frontier training run has no unavoidable signature that can be seen from outside the state and told apart from permitted activity: power draw and waste heat look like any other data centre or heavy industry, and what matters, the computation and the resulting weights, emits nothing. So a limit on training cannot rest on detection from afar alone; it needs something like declared and inspected chips, with the completeness problem that brings. Several principles do transfer. Monitoring that does not depend on the inspected state's cooperation is still valuable wherever some external signal exists, such as electricity use, chip procurement and shipments, or satellite imagery of data-centre construction. Combining independent kinds of sensors raises confidence, as radionuclides confirm what seismometers suggest. And a treaty should set its threshold at what the sensors can actually detect, not at what one would like to prohibit, and say plainly what lies below it."
feedback-instructions:: State whether the learner made the load-bearing assumption explicit. Name the best-reasoned transfer or non-transfer point and the one that was asserted without a reason. Ask one follow-up question. No generic praise.
