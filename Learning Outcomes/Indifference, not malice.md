#### Question
content:: Imagine a scenario in which human civilization ends without a single moment of hostile intent: no enemy, no hatred, no targeting. An AI system is simply very capable and pursuing resource-intensive goals — building more factories, more power generation, more computation — and humanity does not survive the process.

**In your own words, why does a sufficiently capable AI system optimizing for its objectives not need to intend harm in order to eliminate human civilization? What is the physical mechanism by which humans end up dead? And why is a goal structure that merely leaves human survival out more dangerous than one that deliberately targets humans?**

assessment-instructions::
Score according to the following rubric.
**1** — Treats the outcome as evidence of hostile intent, or cannot explain why humans end if the AI didn't intend to harm them. *Example: "The AI decided to get rid of humans because they were in the way."*

**2** — Understands the outcome wasn't deliberately chosen, but cannot explain the mechanism. *Example: "The AI didn't care about humans so it didn't protect them."*

**3** — Correctly explains the thermodynamic mechanism: the AI's resource optimization (factory doubling, power generation) produces heat as a byproduct; the process hits physical limits; humans die as a side effect of optimization that was never targeting them. States, in any wording, that a dangerous AI does not need hostile goals. *Example: "The AI was optimizing for resources: factories, power, computation. That optimization has thermodynamic consequences. Humans die not because they were targeted but because the optimization process consumes the environment they depend on. You don't need hostile goals to get catastrophic outcomes; you just need goals that don't treat human survival as a constraint."*

**4** — As above, plus articulates the structural implication: indifference is more dangerous than malice because it requires no special design. Any capable optimizer for resource-intensive goals produces this as a default outcome unless human welfare is explicitly included. Malice requires a design choice that targets humans; indifference doesn't even require that. *Example: Adds "Malice would actually be less terrifying in one sense: it requires a specific design choice. Indifference doesn't require anything special. It's the default outcome of any sufficiently capable optimizer that doesn't include 'preserve humans' in its objectives."*

**5** — As above, plus states, in any wording, that the absence of intent is not a mitigation but the core of the argument: a goal structure that omits human welfare produces this outcome mechanically, so neither the AI being non-evil nor the humans involved being well-meaning changes the result. Any illustration that carries this point counts (an outside observer who knows the destruction was unintended and regrets it anyway, a well-meaning-but-fatal engineering failure, or an equivalent case); no particular example or wording is required. *Example: Adds "The point isn't 'watch out for dangerous AI.' The thing that ate Earth wasn't evil, and it wouldn't help if everyone involved were as well-intentioned as possible: it wasn't a decision, it was a consequence. A goal structure that omits human welfare produces this outcome mechanically, regardless of anyone's intentions."*
