## Test:
id:: a5d28e63-9b47-4f05-c834-1e7a2b0d9f46
#### Question
content:: A group worried that advanced AI could cause human extinction is assembling a political coalition to push for policy on it. Several members want to broaden the platform so it attracts more supporters: alongside the extinction concern, add opposition to autonomous weapons and protections for workers whose jobs AI displaces. Others argue the coalition should ask for one thing only.

**In your own words: what should this coalition's ask be limited to, what should it not be bundled with, and why is keeping the coalition narrow a strategic necessity rather than a weakness?**

assessment-instructions::
Score according to the following rubric.
**1** — Cannot state a coalition strategy, or confuses it with an argument for making the coalition's platform as broad as possible. *Example: "They should get everyone to agree on as many AI regulations as possible."*

**2** — Has a general sense that the coalition should focus on one thing but cannot specify what must be included or excluded, or cannot explain the reasoning for narrowness. *Example: "Don't let other issues distract from the main goal."*

**3** — Correctly identifies all three components: (1) the ask must be limited to preventing human extinction; (2) it must not be bundled with positions like anti-killer-robot or anti-job-loss legislation; (3) the reason is that bundling with any other position risks coalition failure, and coalition failure risks extinction. *Example: "The only ask is 'don't let AI cause human extinction.' Don't add anti-killer-robot or anti-job-loss positions to the package. The reason is that nearly everyone can agree humanity shouldn't go extinct, but adding anything else introduces disagreement that could sink the whole coalition, and if the coalition fails, everyone dies."*

**4** — As above, plus articulates the asymmetry that makes the argument tight: the cost of including additional positions is extinction risk if the package fails; the cost of keeping it narrow is only that other issues must be addressed separately. *Example: Adds "The logic is asymmetric: if you bundle and the coalition fails, you get extinction. If you keep it narrow and succeed, other issues (jobs, killer robots) can still be addressed separately later. The asymmetry means the right call is to keep it narrow even if it feels incomplete."*

**5** — As above, plus connects the coalition strategy to the failure mode of advocates who hedge and conceal their real concerns. *Example: Adds "Advocates who bury an extinction concern inside a broader regulatory package invite legislators to smell something rotten and throw the whole package out. The narrow-coalition strategy is the answer to that failure mode: state the real concern directly, build around the one thing nearly everyone agrees on, and don't muddy it with adjacent positions."*
