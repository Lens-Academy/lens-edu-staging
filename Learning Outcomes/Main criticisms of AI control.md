## Test:
id:: 20e0f8a0-17bb-46cc-8d01-7a2ce0eaf46e

#### Question: Open
id:: 34ddd43c-1c1d-4fed-902b-1651575f35f4
content::
A friend thinks AI control is obviously a good idea. Name at least five different criticisms of AI control that a well-informed critic would raise. For each, explain in a sentence or two what it claims and why the critic thinks so.
placeholder:: One short paragraph per criticism.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Score the five best criticisms in the answer, 20 points each; extra criticisms beyond five cannot raise the score but a weak one does not lower it. For each: 8 for naming a real criticism of AI control that is distinct from the others already counted, and 12 for explaining what it claims and why, with the mechanism or reason the critic gives. Give 6 of the 12 if the explanation only restates the claim without a reason. Two versions of the same criticism count once. Main criticisms, in any wording (illustrative, not a checklist of words): (1) Control guards against the wrong danger: it targets deliberate scheming in early AIs, while the likelier failure is early AIs producing safety work that looks right and is not, or other risks matter more, so the effort would do more good elsewhere. (2) Control will not be enough for the most capable AIs: it does not work on far more capable AI, and the plan it buys time for, getting controlled early AIs to do the safety work for later ones, may fail because early AIs may not solve the problem or may be neither aligned nor usable. (3) Control evaluations may overstate safety: a red team may miss attacks a more cunning model would find, or the model may deliberately underperform in its own evaluation or as a monitor. (4) Catching and retraining an AI may breed better schemers: training against caught behaviour, or keeping the models that pass, can select for AIs that hide better. (5) Control speeds AI up: it makes powerful AI more useful and profitable to deploy, or helps get research out of AIs that speeds capabilities as much as safety; a variant is that a company that only cares about profit would build it anyway, so safety effort there is not neglected. (6) Control hides warning shots: it turns moderate, visible incidents that would push the world to act into quiet catches inside a company, or pushes failures to later, more capable systems. (7) Control gives false confidence: a protocol can make people feel safe enough to deploy, or a controlled model gets called "aligned", so vigilance drops. Also credit, as distinct criticisms, other genuine criticisms of control that are explained correctly, for example that controlling AIs may make them more adversarial through training data, or that it may wrong AIs if they matter morally. Do not credit general criticisms of AI or of AI companies that say nothing about control. "Companies will not adopt it" counts only if the answer explains why that is a problem with control itself rather than with adoption; otherwise give at most 8 for it. Do not reward or penalise whether the learner agrees with the criticisms. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "1. It aims at the wrong danger: control stops scheming early AIs, but the likelier failure is AIs doing safety work that looks right and isn't. 2. It won't be enough later: control doesn't work on far smarter AI, and the plan is to have controlled early AIs solve alignment for later ones, which may fail. 3. Its tests may flatter it: a red team can miss attacks a smarter model would find, and the model can sandbag its own evaluation. 4. It speeds AI up: making untrusted AIs safe to use makes them more useful and profitable, so labs race faster. 5. It hides warning shots: a monitor quietly blocks an escape that, if it had happened in public, would have made governments act. 6. It gives false confidence: if nothing gets caught, people relax and call the model aligned."
feedback-instructions:: Say which criticisms the learner covered well and name one or two main criticisms they missed, each in one sentence: wrong danger (slop, not scheming), not enough for the most capable AIs (the plan it buys time for may fail), evaluations may overstate safety (including the AI gaming its own evaluation), retraining may breed better schemers, speeding AI up, hiding warning shots, false confidence. If an explanation only restated a claim, say what reason was missing. Do not say whether the criticisms are right. At most five sentences.
