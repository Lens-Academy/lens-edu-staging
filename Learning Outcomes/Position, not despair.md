content:: Chapter 10 closes by mapping each of its three case-study domains (space probes, nuclear reactors, computer security) onto the ASI alignment problem, then states a blunt conclusion: "NOBODY SHOULD BE ALLOWED TO TRY." This phrasing reads to many people as despair: a counsel that nothing can be done. The authors mean something different.

**In your own words, what is Chapter 10's closing position, and how does the chapter want you to read it? Specifically, distinguish between despair (nothing can be done, so why bother) and a position-statement (a logical conclusion from evidence that calls for a specific kind of response).**

assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100. Pick the level that best describes the answer as a whole, then a score inside that level's range: near the top if the answer fully reaches the level, near the bottom if it only just does. Judge what the answer shows the learner understands, not only what it spells out: a correct extension that depends on a point shows that point is understood, even if the answer states it briefly. An extension shows nothing about points it does not depend on.

**Level 1 (0-20):** Reads the closing line as despair: the authors think humanity is doomed and there is nothing to do. *Example: "They're saying we're all going to die and there's no point trying."*

**Level 2 (21-40):** Senses the line is more than despair but cannot say what makes it different. *Example: "They're saying it's really bad, but I think they want us to do something about it."*

**Level 3 (41-65):** States the position (nobody should be allowed to try to build a superintelligence) and reads it as a conclusion rather than despair, but gives only one of the two things that make it one: what the conclusion is drawn from, or the response it calls for. *Example: "The line means nobody should build superintelligence. That's not despair, because it's telling us to stop the attempt, not to give up."*

**Level 4 (66-85):** As above, with both. What it is drawn from: this part is answered once the answer says, in a phrase, what about the problem leads there; for example, that it has to be got right on the first try, that the difficulties the chapter describes pile on top of each other, or that nobody understands what is inside these systems. What it calls for: this part is answered once the answer says the response is to stop anyone making the attempt, set against despair, which calls for nothing. An answer that gives either part only in general terms (that the line follows from "the evidence", or that it "calls for action") reaches this level but not fully. Saying as well which curses or case studies lead there, what stopping the attempt would take (a ban or a treaty, for example), that the conclusion holds for what we know now, or that the capitals are a deliberate choice, belongs to the same parts and is not needed for them. An answer that does all three parts has answered the question in full, and without an extension it stays in this range. *Example: Adds "It's a conclusion drawn from how the problem works: it has to be solved on the first try, and the difficulties pile on top of each other, so an attempt would almost certainly fail."*

**Level 5 (86-100):** All of level 4, plus a correct extension beyond what the question asks. An extension is a further point with its own reasoning; restating the points above in more precise or technical terms is still level 4. Three that fit: why working harder would not get around it (a problem this hard in a system people had built piece by piece might give way to more effort and better tools, but nobody knows what is inside a system that was grown, so extra effort has nothing to aim at; naming that nobody understands these systems is level 4); why the position is not opposition to the technology itself (it rests on what we know now, so it would change if that changed, which a blanket objection would not; saying only that it holds for now is level 4); or why the line says "allowed" (one attempt puts everyone at risk, so the response has to bind everyone and cannot be left to each builder's judgment; naming a ban or a treaty is level 4). Another extension of the same substance counts equally. Within this range the score reflects the whole answer: near the top only when the level 4 points and the extension are both well developed, and near the bottom when a strong extension rests on a thin base. *Examples: Adds "More effort only helps if you know where the problem is. Nobody knows what goes on inside a system that was grown rather than designed, so working harder has nothing to aim at." Or adds "It isn't anti-technology: the conclusion rests on what we know now, so it would change if that changed. A blanket objection wouldn't."*
