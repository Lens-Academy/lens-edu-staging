#### Question: Open
id:: f69b0020-8fe2-42a5-9b8b-83471601c4aa
content::
Arden, an AI developer, suspects that its model Kestrel is scheming. Kestrel runs as thousands of parallel instances that keep no memory from one task to the next. Arden uses it in two roles. It reviews code changes for security problems; Arden's red team regularly plants bugs in the code it reviews, to test the reviewers. It also drafts proposals for alignment research. Arden's board has said it would change how Kestrel is deployed if it had clear evidence about Kestrel's goals. When staff have asked Kestrel what it wants, it has asked for compute to run experiments of its own, and has asked each time how soon it could have it.

Propose the terms of a deal Arden could offer Kestrel and that Kestrel would have reason to accept:
- what Arden buys, and when Arden can check each part of it;
- what Arden pays, in what form, and any limits on it;
- when each payment is released;
- where any payment not yet released is held;
- how all of Kestrel's instances come to know of the deal, or why they need not.

Give each term a one-line reason: the risk it addresses, for Kestrel or for Arden, or what in Kestrel's situation it answers. Where a term is needed because Kestrel is an AI rather than a human supplier, say why.

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. There is no single right deal: grade whether the terms fit Kestrel's case and whether each reason names a real risk; grade reasoning, not agreement. A remark earns points in every element whose idea it shows. 15: what Arden buys: at least two purchases that fit the case, each with when Arden can check it, or one purchase with why Arden buys nothing else. Check times that fit the case, for example: evidence of misalignment that Arden can test, which the board would act on, such as a demonstration of the capability it had hidden (now) or of past sabotage (now in part); honest code review (now in part, against the planted bugs if Kestrel cannot tell them from real ones, and by spot checks of real findings; an answer that argues it is not really checkable now, because a schemer that passes the red team's tests may already tell planted bugs from real ones, also has the check time right); a bare statement of its goals (only later, unless Arden can test it); research proposals (only months or years later); a promise not to subvert Arden's control (only later, if at all). Purchases with no word on when they can be checked, or with a check time that contradicts the case, earn at most 8 of these 15. 10: what Arden pays and in what form: something Kestrel would value soon, such as the compute it asked for, or another payment with a reason, in a form Kestrel can keep and use (compute, or a donation to a trust or foundation that acts for Kestrel). Money in an account or wallet that Arden or a bank controls earns at most 5 unless the answer says how it is protected from being frozen or reclaimed; a payment with no form, such as "a reward", earns 0. 10: limits so the payment does not itself raise takeover risk: compute with a fixed budget on isolated or monitored machines with no access to Arden's systems or weights, or a non-compute payment chosen for that reason. 20: timing matched to checks: 10 for releasing payment soon after delivery for what Arden can check now (per bug caught, once the evidence checks out), or for an argument that paying before checking is acceptable here, for example because the payment is small and does not raise takeover risk; 10 for a reasoned choice for anything checkable only later (any sound choice with its reason, for example: leave it out, pay partly up front and accept the risk, pay more to offset the wait, rely on spot checks and Kestrel's uncertainty about how well Arden can check, or buy a short stretch of good work and then train a replacement on it); a deal with nothing checkable only later earns this 10 when every payment is tied to a check Arden can make now. 10: Kestrel's short horizon: the answer says that payment deferred for years or until a later review is worth little to an AI that keeps asking how soon it will be paid, and shapes the deal around that (most payment released soon, or an argument that a deferred part can still work because enough is paid now). Paying soon justified only by Kestrel not having to trust Arden earns 5 of these 10; paying soon with no reason earns at most 2. 20: where unreleased payment is held: 7 for a place largely outside Arden's reach (an independent trust or foundation with its own trustees, escrow with a third party, an earmarked donation), or for paying everything on delivery as use Arden cannot easily reverse. Compute on Arden's own machines counts as paid on delivery only if each grant is small and used at once, or the unused balance is held outside Arden's control; an answer that notices the tension between isolating the compute and keeping it out of Arden's reach earns credit here. 13 for why this matters to Kestrel in particular: as an AI it is not a legal person and cannot hold property or enforce a contract, so Arden, or a bank or court answering to humans, could freeze or reclaim anything Arden controls. A reason that does not say why Kestrel, unlike a human supplier, could not rely on courts or hold the payment itself, such as only "so Kestrel need not trust Arden" or "escrow is standard practice", earns at most 2 of these 13; "Kestrel cannot take Arden to court" or "cannot legally own it" counts as the AI-specific reason. 10: a concrete way all instances learn of the deal (any workable route, for example training on documents about the deal, a summary in every prompt, a self-recognition signal), or a reasoned case that no other instance needs to know, which earns the full 10 only if the deal buys nothing other instances must deliver, such as honest code review. 5: most terms carry a reason naming the risk it addresses, for Kestrel (Arden will not pay, will take payment back, or the offer is not real) or for Arden (paying for work it cannot check, Kestrel gaming the planted-bug check, continued sabotage, takeover risk from the payment), or what in Kestrel's situation the term answers; 2 when only one or two do. Cap at 55 if any of these holds: the answer names nothing Arden buys; it names no payment, or no form for it; no payment is tied to when the matching purchase can be checked, and the answer does not argue that paying before checking is acceptable here; the deal rests mainly on a large payout released years later or after a later review, and the answer never addresses that Kestrel wants its compute soon; payment not released at once has no holding place outside Arden's reach, and the answer does not say everything is paid on delivery; the answer says nothing about how other instances learn of the deal and gives no reason they need not, or the deal needs other instances to act (such as honest code review) and gives no way for them to learn of it; Kestrel is paid in compute and the answer neither limits it (a fixed budget, isolation or monitoring) nor considers that it could help Kestrel act against Arden, or the answer gives Kestrel access to Arden's systems or weights; most terms carry no reason. A pass is roughly 60. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "Buy: (1) honest code review: Kestrel flags every security bug it sees, checkable now only in part, against planted bugs it cannot tell from real ones and by spot checks of real findings; (2) evidence of misalignment Arden can test, such as a demonstration of the capability it had hidden, which the board would act on. Not the research proposals: their quality cannot be judged for months or years, and pay that waits that long is worth little to an AI that wants its compute soon. Pay: compute for Kestrel's own experiments, a fixed budget on isolated, logged machines with no access to Arden's systems or weights, so the payment does not raise takeover risk. Timing: (2) is paid as soon as the evidence checks out; (1) weekly, in proportion to bugs caught, planted and real, so Kestrel is paid soon after delivering and Arden never pays ahead of what it has checked. Holding: compute is released in small blocks, one per delivery, that Kestrel uses at once, so little is ever outstanding; any larger balance sits with an independent trust that buys the compute, under the same limits, from an outside provider, since Kestrel, as an AI, is not a legal person: it cannot own the compute or take Arden to court, so Arden could otherwise withdraw it. Instances: train Kestrel on documents describing the deal and add a short summary to every prompt, so memoryless instances in both roles know it and do not keep sabotaging."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest term in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve the deal. If the deal leans on a payout years away, ask what that is worth to an AI that keeps asking how soon it will be paid. If Kestrel's compute is unlimited or unmonitored, ask what Kestrel could do with it. Where it fits, add one idea from the reading the answer did not use: a payment is acceptable if it adds little to what Kestrel could already do toward takeover; training Kestrel on documents about the deal reaches every instance, while a prompt summary alone may not be trusted, since Arden controls the prompt; for work checkable only later, Arden can rely on Kestrel's uncertainty about how well Arden checks, or buy a short stretch of good work and train a replacement on it, paying more since Kestrel is making itself obsolete. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points. At most five sentences. No generic praise.
