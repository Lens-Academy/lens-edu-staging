## Test:
id:: 70d6e10f-f05b-4f4a-9a84-8ec9c10e99bb

#### Question
content:: In broad terms, how do you develop a theory of change?

assessment-instructions:: Score the answer from 1 to 5. This is an introductory comprehension question. The learner should describe the basic process, not produce a detailed theory of change.

**1** — Gives no relevant process or describes taking action without connecting it to an outcome. *Example: "Pick something useful to do and see whether it works."*

**2** — Mentions setting a goal or listing actions, but does not connect the two through a causal sequence. *Example: "Decide on your goal and make a list of activities."*

**3** — Explains that you define a concrete desired outcome, work backward to identify the intermediate conditions needed to reach it, and then identify actions that could produce those conditions. This is the pass bar. *Example: "Start with a specific outcome, ask what would have to be true immediately before it happens, keep working backward through the needed steps, and then choose actions that can create those conditions."*

**4** — As above, plus identifies relevant actors and checks whether each step plausibly causes the next. *Example: Adds: "For each step, ask who needs to act and why the previous step would make that action happen."*

**5** — As above, plus makes assumptions explicit and identifies ways to test or revise the causal chain. *Example: Adds: "Mark the uncertain links, look for evidence about them, and update the theory when an assumption does not hold."*

Do not require the learner's wording to match the examples. Accept equivalent descriptions that move forward from actions to outcomes if they explicitly build and check the full causal chain.
