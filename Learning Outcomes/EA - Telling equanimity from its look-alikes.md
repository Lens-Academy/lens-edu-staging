## Test:
id:: 87cc43f8-fce8-4062-a86e-d0f24ca3ff32
#### Question: Open
id:: efc90bd6-dcca-4b48-b039-cb234979beca
content::
Sam has meditated for an hour a day for two years. Three months ago his father was diagnosed with cancer. Sam says he feels "completely at peace with it, because everything is impermanent". He has stopped visiting his father, because the visits "stir up old stuff I have already let go of". His partner says he has been sleeping badly and snaps at her more than he used to. A friend says: "Sam has reached real equanimity."

Do you agree with the friend? Explain your verdict from what Sam says, what he does and what others see.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Scenario, for the grader: Sam has meditated an hour a day for two years. His father was diagnosed with cancer three months ago. Sam says he is "completely at peace with it, because everything is impermanent", but has stopped visiting his father because the visits "stir up old stuff I have already let go of". His partner reports poor sleep and more irritability. A friend says Sam has reached real equanimity. 35: the answer sees that stopping the visits keeps the feeling out by avoiding the situation, which points away from equanimity or acceptance: those let the feeling be felt and stay engaged with the people involved. 30: the answer sees that Sam uses a teaching (impermanence, having "let go") to dismiss feelings or a need that is still there, rather than having worked through it. 35: the answer sees that Sam's own report of peace conflicts with what his partner observes (poor sleep, irritability), so his self-report alone cannot settle it and he may be under stress he does not report. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. An answer that disagrees with the friend for these reasons, or that stays undecided for these reasons, can earn full marks. Cap at 40 if the answer concludes that Sam clearly has equanimity because he says he is at peace.
feedback-instructions:: Say in one or two sentences what the answer saw clearly. If it scored below 100, name the most important thing it missed, from these: avoiding the visits keeps the feeling out, which equanimity and acceptance do not do (Kornfield's "balanced engagement", Desbordes et al.'s caring mother, Shinzen Young's line that dropping resistance to pain does not mean giving up on its source); using impermanence to dismiss a need is what Welwood calls spiritual bypassing; a partner seeing poor sleep and irritability while Sam reports peace is the pattern Martin found at dinner with one of his participants. Do not say Sam is ill or diagnose him. 60 to 110 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: eb3f87b8-490a-431a-9cde-8d1804063795
content::
What further information would help you decide whether Sam's calm is equanimity?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Scenario, for the grader: Sam, a meditator for two years, says he is at peace with his father's cancer because everything is impermanent, has stopped visiting his father because it stirs up feelings, and his partner reports poor sleep and irritability. The question asks what further information would help decide whether his calm is equanimity or a look-alike (indifference, avoidance of the feeling or the situation, using spiritual ideas to dismiss a need). 100: the answer names at least one piece of information that would actually tell these apart, and says or clearly implies what each result would suggest. Examples: how Sam reacts when he does face his father's illness, and whether he reacts and then returns to calm quickly or shows no reaction at all; whether he still cares about and does things for his father; how he answers specific questions about feeling flat, numb or cut off; how his sleep, mood and relationships look over a longer time; whether he avoids other reminders of the illness. 60: the answer names relevant information but gives no hint of what would point which way. 20: the answer only asks for more of what is already known, such as asking Sam again whether he feels at peace.
feedback-instructions:: Say in one sentence whether the information they named would tell real equanimity from a look-alike, and why. If it scored below 100, give one check that would: for example, Desbordes et al.'s proposed sign, a reaction followed by a quick return to calm rather than no reaction at all, or Britton's finding that specific questions catch what open ones miss. Do not diagnose Sam. 50 to 90 words. No generic praise.
