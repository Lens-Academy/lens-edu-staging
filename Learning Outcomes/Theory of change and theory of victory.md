## Test:
id:: b24d22b5-5471-44c3-bd56-ab6b12ea84fc

#### Question
content:: Choose a specific AI safety intervention or project. First state its local theory of change: what the project directly does and what useful intermediate outcome that is supposed to produce.

Then extend it into a theory of victory:

1. State the final win condition.
2. Trace the causal steps from the project to that condition, including handoffs to other actors.
3. Identify the three most load-bearing assumptions.
4. Name the most important bottleneck or failure mode.
5. Give one plausible observation that would substantially reduce your confidence in the plan.
6. Conclude whether the intervention still looks worth pursuing and why.

assessment-instructions:: Score the answer from 1 to 5. Judge the causal completeness and quality of the stress-test, not whether the chosen intervention matches the grader's strategic preferences.

**1** — Gives a slogan, activity, or desirable intermediate outcome without a causal pathway to a win condition. *Example: "We should run AI safety workshops because awareness is good and will make AI safer."*

**2** — Gives a local theory of change and gestures toward a final outcome, but leaves major handoffs implicit and lists only generic risks. *Example: "Workshops create informed people, who influence companies and governments, which leads to safe AI. The assumptions are that people listen and governments cooperate."*

**3** — Clearly distinguishes the local theory of change from the full theory of victory, states an explicit win condition, traces a plausible multi-actor chain, and identifies concrete load-bearing assumptions and a failure mode. This is the pass bar. *Example: "The local theory is workshops → participants understand catastrophic risk and gain policy skills. The theory of victory continues: some participants enter relevant agencies → draft enforceable compute-governance rules → political leaders adopt them → major states coordinate thresholds and verification → frontier development stays below dangerous capability levels. The plan depends on training changing career behavior, participants reaching decision-making roles, and verification being technically and politically workable. Its main bottleneck is adoption by competing states."*

**4** — As above, plus prioritizes assumptions by decision relevance, identifies a concrete confidence-reducing observation, and explains how the project should change if a weak link fails. *Example: Adds: "The most decision-relevant assumption is that marginal trained participants influence policy rather than merely joining an already talent-rich network. If follow-up data showed no movement into neglected government roles, I would reduce confidence and redesign the program around placement partnerships rather than simply expanding recruitment."*

**5** — As above, plus compares the proposed pathway with a credible alternative, notices possible harmful or counterproductive effects, and gives a calibrated action recommendation under uncertainty. *Example: Adds: "The workshop could also create false confidence or funnel people toward crowded roles. Directly funding a small verification team might bypass the uncertain training-to-career handoff. I would run a limited cohort only if placement evidence can be measured, while shifting resources to the direct intervention if the first two cohorts do not outperform the counterfactual."*

Do not require the student's wording to match the examples. Accept any intervention and win condition that allow a serious causal analysis. A theory of victory need not be certain or simple; it must make its uncertainty and dependencies legible.
