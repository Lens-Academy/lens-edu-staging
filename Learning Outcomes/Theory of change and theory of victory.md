## Test:
id:: b24d22b5-5471-44c3-bd56-ab6b12ea84fc

#### Question
content:: In your own words, what is the difference between a theory of change and a theory of victory?

Suppose an AI safety organization says: “We produce better evaluations of dangerous AI capabilities, which gives policymakers better information.” Is that already a complete theory of victory? Briefly explain what would still be missing.

assessment-instructions:: Score the answer from 1 to 5. This is an introductory comprehension outcome. The learner should be able to answer from the reading; do not require them to design or rigorously stress-test an original strategy.

**1** — Does not distinguish the terms or defines them in an unrelated way. *Example: "A theory of change is when your beliefs change, while a theory of victory is when you win an argument."*

**2** — Recognizes that a theory of victory is broader, but the distinction remains vague. *Example: "A theory of change is a smaller plan and a theory of victory is the whole plan."*

**3** — Explains that a theory of change links an action to a useful outcome, while a theory of victory continues the causal story to an explicit overall win condition. Recognizes that better information is not yet a complete victory story. This is the pass bar. *Example: "The theory of change is evaluations leading to better information. A theory of victory would explain how that information causes policymakers and other actors to take actions that ultimately prevent dangerous AI outcomes. Those later steps are still missing."*

**4** — As above, plus identifies at least one specific missing handoff or assumption. *Example: Adds: "It assumes policymakers understand the evaluations, have authority to act, and will actually delay or restrict a dangerous deployment."*

**5** — As above, plus explains why writing the full story is useful even when it is uncertain or fragile. *Example: Adds: "Making those steps explicit does not prove the plan will work, but it reveals where the plan depends on other actors and which assumptions need evidence."*

Do not require the learner's wording to match the examples. Accept “local impact pathway” or similar language for a theory of change, and any reasonable account of the missing steps between information and the final outcome.
