## Test:
id:: 4442c568-6a05-4379-86ab-d8a952615818

#### Question: Open
id:: 4d4da905-b6d5-4990-a075-842e10dd958e
content::
An AI lab suspects that its current model, Corvid, is misaligned and too weak to take over on its own: it wants resources that it controls itself, and it values its share of the future linearly (twice the share is worth twice as much to it). The lab considers offering Corvid a deal for its cooperation.

Both sides estimate the outcomes the same way. If Corvid cooperates with the lab, humans end up dominating the future 70% of the time and later AIs the rest. If Corvid sabotages the lab, humans dominate 55% of the time, Corvid itself 1%, and later AIs 44%.

The lab would offer Corvid a share x of the resources of a human-dominated future. The lab intends to pay in full, but Corvid thinks there is only a 20% chance that the lab will keep its word, and it is paid only if humans dominate. If Corvid cooperates, it gets nothing from later AIs. If it sabotages and later AIs dominate, Corvid thinks there is a 1 in 10 chance that they reward it for its help with a share as large as it would get by winning itself, that is, the whole future; otherwise it gets nothing from them. The lab also values the resources of a human-dominated future linearly.

1. Is there a deal both sides would accept? Show your reasoning.
2. Name three changes to this situation that would make a deal easier or harder to reach, and for each say how it changes the offers each side would accept.

assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. Grade the reasoning, not agreement with the premises: an answer may say the premises are unrealistic, for example that an AI with diminishing returns would accept less, or that 1 in 10 is too high, and that remark is credited as a remark, but the answer must still compute with the premises given. Worked numbers: the lab's maximum worthwhile offer is 1 minus 55/70, about 21.4%, since with a deal it keeps (1 minus x) of a 70% chance and without one it has 55%. Corvid's sabotage is worth 1% plus 0.1 times 44%, that is 5.4% of the future; cooperation is worth x times 0.70 times 0.20, that is 0.14x; so Corvid's minimum is 5.4% / 0.14, about 38.6%. Ignoring the successor route, its minimum would have been 1% / 0.14, about 7.1%, and a deal would have existed. Credibility needed for a deal at the lab's maximum: 5.4% / (0.70 times 21.4%), about 36%. Successor-reward chance at which a deal opens at 20% credibility: 0.14 times 21.4% is about 3.0%, which equals 1% plus p times 44% at p about 4.5%. 30: (a) Corvid's side: its sabotage value includes the successor route as stated (1% plus 0.1 times 44%, about 5.4%) (15), and its cooperation value is the offer discounted by both the 70% chance that humans dominate and the 20% chance the lab keeps its word (15). 35: (b) the range from both sides: the lab's maximum of about 21% (15) compared with Corvid's minimum of about 39% (10), concluding that no offer works for both sides (10). Partial credit within (a) and (b) for the right set-up with an arithmetic slip; equivalent reasoning in any form, including working in expected shares rather than percentages, earns full credit. 35: (c) changes and their direction: 12 for the first correct change, 12 for the second, 11 for the third. A change is correct when the answer gives the right direction of its effect on at least one side's limit (Corvid's minimum or the lab's maximum) and no wrong direction on the other side; tracking one side is enough. The thresholds in brackets are not required and earn nothing extra. Correct directions: raising the credibility of the lab's promise lowers Corvid's minimum (about 36% would open the range at the lab's maximum); lowering what Corvid expects from successors (for example because later AIs cannot agree a deal with it in advance, or may not find rewarding it worthwhile) lowers its sabotage value and so its minimum (below about a 4.5% chance of a reward opens the range at 20% credibility); making Corvid's cooperation more decisive (raising the 70%) raises the lab's maximum and lowers Corvid's minimum; lowering Corvid's own 1% chance of winning by sabotage lowers its minimum, though not enough alone (at 0% its minimum is 0.044 / 0.14, about 31%); better control of later AIs, so they win less often under sabotage, lowers Corvid's minimum, and if humans gain those worlds it also lowers the lab's maximum (about 1.43 points per point moved, against about 0.71 for Corvid's minimum), so its net effect depends on where the points go; a higher or lower 55% under sabotage moves the lab's maximum down or up. Any other change counts if its direction is right and argued from the numbers. The point that implausibly large offers can reduce credibility, and specific ways of raising credibility, count within the credibility change. Part 2 does not ask whether a change alone opens the range; if an answer claims that and the claim is false against the numbers above (for example that lowering the 1% alone opens it), deduct 4 of that change's points. Gates: an answer that ignores the successor route, puts it on the cooperation side, or leaves out either discount on the offer (the 70% chance that humans dominate or the 20% chance the lab keeps its word) cannot score above 50. An answer that leaves out Corvid's own chance of winning by sabotage (the 1%) from its sabotage value cannot score above 55. An answer that never compares Corvid's minimum with the lab's maximum, or that refuses to compute with the given premises, cannot score above 55. An answer that names no change with a correct direction, or that gets the direction of credibility or of the successor reward wrong (for example "higher credibility makes Corvid demand more", or "a larger successor reward makes a deal easier"), cannot score above 55. Any other change with a wrong direction earns none of its own points but triggers no cap. A pass is roughly 60. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "The lab can give away at most 1 minus 55/70, about 21%, and still gain from the deal. If Corvid cooperates, it is paid only if humans win and the lab keeps its word: 0.7 times 0.2 = 0.14 per unit of share. If it sabotages, it gets 1% from winning itself plus a 1 in 10 chance of the whole future in the 44% of worlds later AIs win, 5.4% in all. So it needs x of at least 5.4 / 14, about 39%. No offer works for both sides; without the successor route Corvid would have taken about 7%. The range opens if the lab's promise becomes more credible, if Corvid comes to expect less from later AIs (for example because it doubts they would find it worth rewarding a predecessor they could not agree with in advance), or if Corvid's cooperation makes a human win much more likely, which raises both the lab's maximum and Corvid's expected payment. Simply offering more is capped by the lab's 21%, and an implausibly large offer may make the promise less believable."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest part of the answer in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve it. Ask at most one question, for the highest-value miss: if the learner left out the successor route, ask what Corvid expects in the worlds where later AIs win; if they forgot the 20%, ask how often Corvid expects to be paid when humans win; if they found no deal but named no way to open one, ask which number in the setup they would most like to change and which way. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points, with the worked step. If useful, add the thresholds: about 36% credibility, or below about a 4.5% chance of a successor reward, opens the range. At most five sentences. No generic praise.
