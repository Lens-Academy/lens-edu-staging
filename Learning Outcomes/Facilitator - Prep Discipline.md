## Test:
id:: 27062e0b-42db-4241-88b8-9da786790d04

#### Question
content:: You will be tempted to over-prepare. Reading the entire reading three times, writing 15 candidate questions, drafting an opening monologue — these feel diligent. They mostly aren't. There's a small list of prep moves that reliably improve quality and a longer list that's busywork.

**Name three prep moves that actually move session quality, and three prep moves that are busywork. For each "busywork" item — why does it feel useful but actually isn't?**

assessment-instructions::
**1** — Treats over-preparation as a virtue. Cannot distinguish high-value from low-value prep.

**2** — Names some prep moves but conflates volume with quality.

**3** — Names at least three prep moves that plausibly raise session quality AND at least three that are busywork, and gives a defensible reason for each side of the split. High-value moves are ones that change what the facilitator can actually do in the room — e.g. reading the text once with a participant's eye, preparing a few good questions in a deliberate order, one backup plan for one foreseeable problem, a rough session shape and timing — or any equivalent move the student justifies on those grounds. Busywork moves are ones whose effort doesn't survive contact with a live conversation — e.g. scripting the opening word-for-word, writing detailed answers to one's own questions, designing elaborate activities, re-reading the text four times — or any equivalent. For each busywork move, explains why it feels useful but isn't. Grade the reasoning behind the split, not whether the examples match the ones listed here.

**4** — As above, plus identifies that prepared questions should do different jobs across the session rather than being variations on one theme — e.g. a low-stakes opener, a mid-session deepener, and one that applies the idea — or any equivalent account of spreading questions across distinct session roles.

**5** — As above, plus pre-commits to a 20-30 minute prep ceiling for their next session and articulates how they'll resist the pull to do more.
