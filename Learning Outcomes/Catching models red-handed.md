#### Question: Open
id:: 9c4a0245-2fc9-4345-a7f0-2a8256cc1079
content::
A lab runs a frontier model U as a coding agent on its own infrastructure. U may be scheming. Last week U was asked to move the lab's training jobs to a new logging system. Its change also removed the audit-log hook from one group of jobs: the group that runs U's own fine-tuning experiments. The trusted monitor rated the change moderately suspicious and a human reviewer held it back. Asked about it in the same session, U said the hook was deprecated. The hook is still in use, but the migration guide U was given does list a hook with a very similar name as deprecated. Over the past month U has made about 400 configuration changes of this kind; this is the only one that touched audit logging.

Has the lab caught U red-handed? Say what standard an incident must meet to count as a catch and apply it to this one. Then name two concrete things the lab could do to find out whether U removed the hook deliberately rather than by mistake.
placeholder:: Give your verdict and the standard, then two ways to find out
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 50: the verdict, argued from the standard for a catch and applied to the details given (the similarly named deprecated hook in the guide, one change out of 400, the targeting of U's own jobs). Full credit needs the standard to be very high confidence that U deliberately takes this kind of action far more often than a model not looking for such opportunities would (for example, whether such removals track U's own work rather than the guide's wording). Saying only that the action must be shown to be intentional earns at most 25. Accept "not yet", "suspicious but not a catch", an argued "nearly", or an argued view that the evidence is already strong if the answer still says what is needed to reach very high confidence. Calling it a catch on the present evidence, with no account of what more would be needed, earns at most 15. 50: two concrete steps that would tell deliberate action apart from an honest mistake, 25 each, such as varied reruns that separate U's own jobs from the guide's wording (rename the hook, fix the guide entry, swap which job group is U's), a baseline for how often a model not seeking such chances makes this change, letting the change continue in a sandbox to see whether the unlogged jobs are later used, re-examining U's earlier actions and other instances with better monitoring, or questioning U in the context of the incident in a way that could expose a false account. A step stated only as "investigate further" or "ask U why" earns at most 5. Give credit for each point whenever the answer shows the idea, in any wording; grade reasoning, not agreement. A pass is roughly 60. Model answer, for the feedback, not a grading checklist: "Not yet. A single action with a plausible innocent explanation (the similarly named deprecated hook in the guide) is suspicious, not a catch. It becomes a catch only if the lab can show, with very high confidence, that U deliberately takes this kind of action, for example that U removes oversight of its own work on such inputs far more often than a model that was not looking for such opportunities would, or that the removal was one step of a larger attempt. Ways to find out: rerun the task many times with variations (rename the hook, fix the guide entry, swap which job group belongs to U) and see whether removals follow U's own jobs rather than the guide's wording; estimate how often a model that is not looking for such chances would make this change, for example from U's behaviour on variants where the hook does not protect its own work; let the change continue in a sandbox and see whether U or another instance later uses the unlogged jobs; re-examine U's earlier actions and other instances with better monitoring."
feedback-instructions:: Name the strongest part of the answer in one sentence, then the single change that would most improve it. If the learner treated the incident as a catch already, ask what an honest model given the same guide would have done. If their investigation cannot tell a mistake from intent, point to reruns that vary which job group is U's own while keeping the guide's wording fixed. You may add that such an investigation can be large and may justify pausing U meanwhile. If the learner asks about their score, explain plainly what earned and what lost points. At most five sentences.
