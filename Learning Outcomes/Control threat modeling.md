content::
A frontier model U works as an agent with shell access to a lab's training cluster. U may be scheming. The unacceptable outcome is: **a copy of U's weights is running on hardware the lab does not monitor.**

Current safeguards:
- Every command U runs is logged.
- After each task, a weaker trusted model reads the log and flags suspicious tasks for a human to review.
- U's network access is limited to a list of approved internal machines.

1. Build an attack tree for this outcome as a nested list. Put the outcome at the root, mark each node that has children as AND or OR, and keep decomposing until each leaf is a choice U makes, a fact about the environment, or a point a safeguard could act on. Go at least three levels deep.
2. The lab adds one safeguard: any single transfer above 1 GB leaving the lab's network is blocked, and every blocked attempt raises an alert to a human. Mark which nodes in your tree this safeguard prevents, detects or deters, and name one path through your tree that it leaves open, and say why it stays open.

assessment-instructions:: Score 0 to 100. The capability being tested is building a sound attack tree against a scheming model in this particular deployment and using it to analyse a safeguard. There is no single correct tree; grade structure and reasoning. Load-bearing elements: (1) about 20 points: the root is the stated outcome and the first level captures genuinely different routes or necessary stages. A good tree usually needs, in some arrangement, obtaining the weights, getting them onto unmonitored hardware, and starting them running. Credit noticing that "hardware the lab does not monitor" can be inside the lab (an unmonitored internal machine or job) as well as outside; do not require it. (2) about 25 points: correct use of AND and OR. AND is used where every child is needed (for example the stages of an exfiltration), OR where any child suffices (alternative ways to achieve a subgoal). Deduct for gates that are clearly wrong, for children under an OR that are not actually ways of achieving the parent (for example enumerating guesses or conditions), and for single nodes that silently bundle several steps. (3) about 20 points: the tree is about this deployment and a scheming model. The existing safeguards (command logging, the trusted model's review after each task, the network allowlist) appear as things the attack has to get around, for example a node such as "the trusted reviewer does not flag the task", "keep each logged command innocuous", or "find a route beyond the approved machines". Deduct up to 10 of these points if the tree ignores all three existing safeguards and reads as a generic data-theft tree. Leaves are choices U makes, facts about the environment, or points a safeguard could act on, and the tree is at least three levels deep without listing trivial variants of one attack. (4) about 35 points: the safeguard analysis. The new block prevents any single large transfer of the weights out of the lab network, and the alert lets a human detect blocked attempts; full credit on the tagging needs the learner to separate what the block prevents from what the alert detects (deter may also be argued, for example because a blocked attempt risks getting U caught). Routes the safeguard leaves open include sending the weights out in many pieces under 1 GB, possibly hidden in allowed traffic; running an unmonitored copy on hardware inside the lab, which may need no transfer out of the lab network at all; and any route that does not pass through the lab's network boundary. Full credit also needs at least one specific path in the learner's own tree named as still open, with a reason. Half credit on this element if an open path is named but not tied to the tree. Noting that the existing trusted-model review happens after each task, so it detects rather than prevents, may add a few points within this element but is not required and does not replace naming an open path. Gating: an answer that tags no node or names no open path cannot score above 50. A reasoned statement that the safeguard touches no node in the tree, or only nodes the allowlist already blocks, counts as tagging. Do not require the learner's tree to match any reference tree, do not penalise additional valid branches, and do not penalise a learner who points out an ambiguity or flaw in the scenario. Accept any readable nested-list format. A pass is roughly 60.
