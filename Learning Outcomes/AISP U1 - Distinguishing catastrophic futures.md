## Test:
id:: 7bc05f3d-61ff-49c9-b9a0-524579fdaf7d
#### Question: Open
id:: b4becc94-acde-49b1-88b1-af9b4dc3d9b3
content::
A future AI transition leaves every human alive and materially comfortable, but humans permanently lose meaningful control over economic resources, political institutions, and the long-run direction of civilization.

Does the absence of extinction make this scenario non-existential? Explain what kind of loss would matter for the classification, and identify one important moral assumption your answer depends on.

assessment-instructions::
Score out of 100.

40 points: The answer recognizes that extinction is not necessary for an existential catastrophe if humanity permanently loses important future potential, recovery capacity, or control over its future.

35 points: The answer explains the relevant irreversible loss in the scenario, such as permanent disempowerment or inability to redirect civilization.

25 points: The answer identifies a genuine moral assumption, such as whether continued human agency, future options, or future welfare has moral value.

feedback-instructions::
Give 90 to 150 words. Name the strongest distinction and the most important missing assumption if any. Do not grade the learner's preferred moral theory.
