%% Test for module 6 of Emotional Abundance, part 1. The seven statements are our own paraphrases, written without names. Review 2026-10-05: the first draft's statements could be matched on keywords by a well-read person who had not taken the module, so the statements now hinge on the module's own distinctions, with surface words that point to the wrong option: 1 Thanissaro (not-self as a method, judged by stress) versus 2 Sujato and Bodhi (no-self as a fact, strategy reading rejected), 3 Parfit's spectrum case, 4 Schwartz (a Self with qualities, described with the word "awareness") versus 5 Ramana (awareness without personal qualities, world like the rope-snake), 6 Metzinger, 7 Dennett (answerable from the comparison table, since the Dennett and Strawson lens is optional). The open questions test the main analytical point: some disagreements are verbal, others are about what can be observed. The verbal one uses two meditation teachers rather than Metzinger and Thompson, so it is not the same item as the lens's practice question. %%

#### Question: Choice
id:: ba2f5a38-996c-479b-8c09-11b672f67c29
content:: "I will not tell you whether you have a self. Take the irritation you feel right now. Does it stay the same? Can you order it to stop? If not, why hold it as yours? Ask that of each thing you cling to, and judge the practice by whether your stress goes down." Which view does this express?
options::
- [x] Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- The bundle or reductionist view
- A true Self as a core with qualities
- A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: The speaker refuses to say whether a self exists, examines one thing at a time for change and lack of control, and judges the practice by whether stress lessens. That is not-self read as a method (Thanissaro). The no-self reading (Sujato, Bodhi) would add that no self is there to be found anywhere.

options::
- Not-self as a method
- [x] No-self as a claim about what exists
- The bundle or reductionist view
- A true Self as a core with qualities
- A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: The speaker rejects the reading of the teaching as a strategy only and makes a claim about what exists: no self anywhere, not even beyond body and mind. That is the no-self reading of Sujato and Bodhi, against Thanissaro's not-self method.

#### Question: Choice
id:: 939f802d-84ab-4a4e-81be-587d410e78c7
content:: "Suppose surgeons replace my cells one by one with exact copies. There may be no answer to the question at which point I stop existing, even when every physical and mental fact is known. Knowing those facts, I know everything there is to know." Which view does this express?
options::
- Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- [x] The bundle or reductionist view
- A true Self as a core with qualities
- A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: Once the facts about brain, body and experiences are known there is no further fact about whether I survive, so the question can be empty. That is Parfit's spectrum argument for the reductionist form of the bundle view.

#### Question: Choice
id:: 8d1f187e-f67e-4bc0-a89d-cdcb8a3714b1
content:: "When the anxious and critical voices inside you are asked to step back, what is left is not empty. It is an awareness that is calm, curious and caring, and it can listen to those voices and help them. That is who you really are." Which view does this express?
options::
- Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- The bundle or reductionist view
- [x] A true Self as a core with qualities
- A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: Although it uses the word "awareness", what remains has personal qualities (calm, curiosity, care) and acts in everyday inner life by helping the parts that step back. That is a true Self with qualities, as in Internal Family Systems. Ramana's Self has no personal qualities.

#### Question: Choice

content:: "What you really are is not a calmer or kinder version of your personality. It has no qualities of a person at all. It is what is left when even the thought 'I' has died away, and the world that seemed separate from it turns out to be like a snake seen in a rope." Which view does this express?
options::
- Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- The bundle or reductionist view
- A true Self as a core with qualities
- [x] A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: A true Self is affirmed, but it has no personal qualities, it is found when the "I"-thought itself subsides, and the world is compared to a snake seen in a rope. That is a true Self as pure awareness, as in Ramana Maharshi's Advaita Vedanta, not the IFS Self with qualities.

#### Question: Open
id:: 224e46c5-a217-4e0e-b0e5-f9fade71cf59
content::
Two researchers accept exactly the same experiments about how the brain and body build the feeling of being someone. One concludes: "So the self is an illusion." The other concludes: "So the self is real. It is a process, like a dance." Is their disagreement about the evidence? If not, what is it about?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 40: says the disagreement is not about the evidence or the facts of how the feeling is built, since both accept the same findings. 60: says what it is about instead: the meaning of the words, such as what "self" refers to (an enduring inner thing, which both deny, versus the ongoing process, which both accept) or whether something constructed counts as an "illusion" or as real. An answer that says one of them must have the science wrong scores at most 20.
feedback-instructions:: The learner answered whether two researchers who accept the same evidence but call the self "an illusion" versus "a real process" disagree about the evidence. Name the strongest part of their answer. Below 100, name what was missing: usually that both deny an unchanging inner thing and accept an ongoing process, so they differ on whether to keep the word "self" for the process and whether "illusion" fits something constructed. Mention that this is the disagreement between Metzinger and Thompson in this module. 60 to 110 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: e77d55b6-0be1-423e-bf22-80df4b604f7d
content::
Name one disagreement between views of the self that an observation could help settle, and say what observation.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 50: names a disagreement between views of the self that is about something observable, not only about how to use words like "self" or "illusion". Examples that count: whether looking inward finds only passing perceptions or also an awareness that remains; whether letting go of identification with thoughts and feelings reduces distress; whether a reported loss of self comes with less stress as seen by others or in the body; whether people differ in feeling their past as their own. 50: names an observation that would bear on that disagreement and could actually be made, such as repeated, careful introspection with predictions written beforehand, ratings compared against a control practice, reports from people close to the person, or bodily or behavioural measures. If the disagreement named is purely about words (for example whether a constructed self should be called an illusion), give at most 20 for the first element.
feedback-instructions:: The learner named a disagreement between views of the self and an observation that could help settle it. Name the strongest part. Below 100, say what was missing: either the disagreement was about words rather than something observable, or the observation could not be made or would not bear on it. Add one way to make their observation more trustworthy, such as a written prediction, repetition on several days, a control condition, or a second observer. 60 to 110 words. No generic praise.
