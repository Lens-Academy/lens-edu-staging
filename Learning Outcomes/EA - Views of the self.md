%% Test for module 6 of Emotional Abundance, part 1. The seven statements are our own paraphrases, written without names. Review 2026-10-05: the first draft's statements could be matched on keywords by a well-read person who had not taken the module, so the statements now hinge on the module's own distinctions, with surface words that point to the wrong option: 1 Thanissaro (not-self as a method, judged by stress) versus 2 Sujato and Bodhi (no-self as a fact, strategy reading rejected), 3 Parfit's spectrum case, 4 Schwartz (a Self with qualities, described with the word "awareness") versus 5 Ramana (awareness without personal qualities, world like the rope-snake), 6 Metzinger, 7 Dennett (answerable from the comparison table, since the Dennett and Strawson lens is optional). The open questions test the main analytical point: some disagreements are verbal, others are about what can be observed. The verbal one uses two meditation teachers rather than Metzinger and Thompson, so it is not the same item as the lens's practice question. %%

#### Question: Choice
id:: ba2f5a38-996c-479b-8c09-11b672f67c29
content:: "I will not tell you whether you have a self. Take the irritation you feel right now. Does it stay the same? Can you order it to stop? If not, why hold it as yours? Ask that of each thing you cling to, and judge the practice by whether your stress goes down." Which view does this express?
options::
- [x] Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- The bundle or reductionist view
- A true Self as a core with qualities
- A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: The speaker refuses to say whether a self exists, examines one thing at a time for change and lack of control, and judges the practice by whether stress lessens. That is not-self read as a method (Thanissaro). The no-self reading (Sujato, Bodhi) would add that no self is there to be found anywhere.

content:: "Suppose surgeons replace my cells one by one with exact copies. There may be no answer to the question at which point I stop existing, even when every physical and mental fact is known. Knowing those facts, I know everything there is to know." Which view does this express?
options::
- Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- [x] The bundle or reductionist view
- A true Self as a core with qualities
- A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: Once the facts about brain, body and experiences are known there is no further fact about whether I survive, so the question can be empty. That is Parfit's spectrum argument for the reductionist form of the bundle view.

#### Question: Choice
id:: 8d1f187e-f67e-4bc0-a89d-cdcb8a3714b1
content:: "When the anxious and critical voices inside you are asked to step back, what is left is not empty. It is an awareness that is calm, curious and caring, and it can listen to those voices and help them. That is who you really are." Which view does this express?
options::
- Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- The bundle or reductionist view
- [x] A true Self as a core with qualities
- A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: Although it uses the word "awareness", what remains has personal qualities (calm, curiosity, care) and acts in everyday inner life by helping the parts that step back. That is a true Self with qualities, as in Internal Family Systems. Ramana's Self has no personal qualities.

content:: "What you really are is not a calmer or kinder version of your personality. It has no qualities of a person at all. It is what is left when even the thought 'I' has died away, and the world that seemed separate from it turns out to be like a snake seen in a rope." Which view does this express?
options::
- Not-self as a method
- No-self as a claim about what exists
- The bundle or reductionist view
- A true Self as a core with qualities
- [x] A true Self as pure awareness
- The self as a brain model
- The narrative self
explanation:: A true Self is affirmed, but it has no personal qualities, it is found when the "I"-thought itself subsides, and the world is compared to a snake seen in a rope. That is a true Self as pure awareness, as in Ramana Maharshi's Advaita Vedanta, not the IFS Self with qualities.

#### Question: Open
id:: 224e46c5-a217-4e0e-b0e5-f9fade71cf59
content::
Two meditation teachers have looked inward for years and report the same thing: they find only changing sensations, feelings and thoughts, and no fixed controller behind them. One concludes: "So the self is an illusion." The other concludes: "So the self is real. It is this ongoing process, the way a dance is real while it is danced." What are they disagreeing about?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 100: says they disagree about how to use words, not about what they found: what the word "self" should name (a fixed inner thing, which both say is not there, or the ongoing process, which both say is there), or whether something constructed and changing should be called an "illusion" or "real". 50: says they agree on what they observe but interpret it differently, without saying that the difference is about the meaning of "self" or "illusion". An answer that says one of them must have observed wrongly, or that more careful looking would settle it, scores at most 20.
feedback-instructions:: The learner said what two meditation teachers disagree about when both find only changing experiences and no fixed controller, and one calls the self "an illusion" while the other calls it "real, a process like a dance". Name the strongest part of their answer. Below 100, name what was missing: both deny a fixed inner thing and accept an ongoing process, so they differ only on whether to keep the word "self" for the process and whether "illusion" fits something constructed. Mention that this is the same shape as the disagreement between Metzinger and Thompson in this module. 60 to 110 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: e77d55b6-0be1-423e-bf22-80df4b604f7d
content::
Name a point on which two views of the self from this module disagree and which an observation could help settle. Say which two views.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 30: names two views of the self that really do disagree on the point the answer gives (for example Hume's bundle and Ramana's true Self, the IFS Self and the Buddhist not-self, or Thanissaro's not-self method and a view that says letting go of the self does not help). 70: the point of disagreement is about something that could be observed, not only about how to use words like "self" or "illusion". Points that count: whether looking inward finds only passing perceptions or also an awareness that remains; whether that awareness has qualities such as calm and compassion; whether letting go of identification with thoughts and feelings reduces distress; whether people differ in feeling their past as their own. If the point named is only about words (for example whether a constructed self should be called an illusion), give at most 10 for the second element.
feedback-instructions:: The learner named two views of the self and a disagreement between them that an observation could help settle. Name the strongest part. Below 100, say what was missing: either the two views do not actually disagree on that point, or the disagreement is about words rather than something observable. 50 to 100 words. No generic praise.

#### Question: Open
id:: 32e10f8a-452b-4868-bb48-35f6c6c35d5d
content::
What observation could help settle the disagreement you just named?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 60: the observation could actually be made, for example repeated, careful introspection with predictions written beforehand, ratings after a practice compared with ratings after a control of the same length, reports from people close to the person, or bodily or behavioural measures. 40: it is clear how different results of the observation would count for one side or the other. An observation that could not be made at all (for example "see whether the self exists after death") scores at most 10.
feedback-instructions:: The learner proposed an observation that could help settle a disagreement between two views of the self that they named in the previous question. Name the strongest part. Below 100, say what was missing: either the observation could not actually be made, or it is unclear which result would favour which side. Add one way to make the observation more trustworthy, such as a written prediction, repetition on several days, a control condition, or a second observer. 50 to 100 words. No generic praise.
