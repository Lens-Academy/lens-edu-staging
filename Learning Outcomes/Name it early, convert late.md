## Test:
id:: e5579bbc-3019-4874-b11b-86a42ff9e919
#### Question
content:: A course peer tells you: "These two rules contradict each other. If you bring up AI in the very first conversation, you're rushing people. If you hold your views back until they're ready, you're hiding your agenda. You can't have both, we should pick one."

**Where is the flaw in this argument? And how do you tell, in practice, when someone has actually become ready?**

assessment-instructions::
Score according to the following rubric.
**1** — Accepts the dilemma and picks a side. *Example: "They're right — it's better to wait until you know someone well before ever mentioning AI, otherwise you'll scare them off."*

**2** — Denies the contradiction but cannot name the distinction that dissolves it. *Example: "You can do both. Just mention it casually and don't be pushy about it."*

**3** — Names the distinction: raising a topic and converting on it are different acts, and the two rules govern different acts. Naming it early is transparency — the person knows from the start what matters to you, so nothing later can feel like a bait-and-switch. Deferring persuasion is pacing — you wait for the person to signal readiness before making any case. The organizer's dilemma only exists if mentioning and persuading are the same move, and they aren't. *Example: "The flaw is treating 'bring it up' and 'convince them' as one act. In the first conversation I'll say something like 'I spend a lot of my time worrying about where AI is going' — that's naming, not converting. It means I'm never running a hidden agenda. The persuasion waits until they come toward the topic themselves."*

**4** — As above, plus reliably distinguishes readiness signals from politeness. Readiness: they bring the topic back up unprompted, they ask specific questions, they ask what you think. Politeness: nodding, "huh, interesting," a smooth topic change, engagement that never generates a question. Persuading into politeness feels like progress and quietly spends trust. *Example: Adds "They are ready if they do any conversational work: an unprompted 'hey, I thought about what you said' or a real question is a green light. 'Interesting' followed by a subject change is a sign they aren't ready yet, even though it feels friendly. If I'm the only one moving the topic forward, they're not ready."*

**5** — As above, plus sees that the two failure modes are symmetric trust-killers: the stealth advocate (never names it, then springs it after months of friendship) and the door-knocker (names it and converts in the same breath) both teach the person the relationship was instrumental. Name-early-convert-late is the only combination in which the relationship remains visibly primary, which is also why it is the only pattern the person will let run for years. *Example: Adds "Hiding it and rushing it fail the same way: the person concludes the friendship was a delivery vehicle. Naming early and waiting is the one configuration where caring about them and caring about the topic never have to compete."*
