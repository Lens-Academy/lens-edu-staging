## Complementary co-lead profile

You already cover content marketing, outreach/partnerships, qualitative metrics, and volunteer/team management. A strong co-lead should therefore complement you in the areas that usually make growth more scalable, measurable, and operationally tight.

### Best-fit strengths to look for

#### 1. Quantitative growth and experimentation
Look for someone who is comfortable with:
- KPI design and dashboards
- funnel analysis
- A/B testing or experiment design
- attribution and tracking hygiene
- turning data into action

Why it matters: you already have qualitative judgment; this person can make growth more evidence-led and help decide which channels and tactics are actually compounding.

#### 2. Product/UX or activation thinking
Look for someone who understands:
- onboarding and activation flows
- user journey friction
- lifecycle messaging
- retention and re-engagement
- how small product changes affect growth

Why it matters: a lot of nonprofit growth is lost between “interest” and “real use.” Someone who can improve activation, retention, and referral mechanics may outperform a pure acquisition focus.

#### 3. Systems and operations
Look for someone who is strong at:
- process design
- documentation
- project management
- prioritization
- building repeatable operating rhythms

Why it matters: if you are already doing a lot of the human-side leadership, this person can help convert effort into a reliable machine that volunteers and contractors can execute without constant supervision.

#### 4. Technical fluency
Look for enough technical comfort to work well with tools and data, such as:
- CRM and automation tools
- analytics platforms
- spreadsheets / SQL-lite analysis
- low-code workflows
- website/content tooling

Why it matters: even a modest amount of technical fluency can dramatically raise leverage in a small team by reducing manual work and improving tracking.

#### 5. User research and insight synthesis
Look for someone who can:
- run interviews or surveys
- identify patterns in user feedback
- segment audiences
- translate needs into hypotheses
- support message testing

Why it matters: you already understand qualitative metrics. A co-lead who excels at structured user insight can sharpen positioning, activation, and product priorities.

### A few especially good archetypes

- **Growth analyst / experimenter**: best if you need sharper measurement and prioritization.
- **Product growth generalist**: best if activation and retention are weak spots.
- **Growth ops builder**: best if you need systems, dashboards, and repeatability.
- **Technical growth marketer**: best if the team needs automation and channel scaling.

### A simple rule of thumb
If you are the person who says, “I know what matters and can get people moving,” the co-lead should be the person who says, “I can prove what works, build the tracking, and make it repeatable.”

### Interview prompt
A useful question is:

> “Tell me about a time you improved growth by using data, systems, or product changes rather than just more outreach or content.”

That answer will usually reveal whether they are truly complementary or just another generalist.

### What I would prioritize most
For your situation, the strongest complement is probably:
1. quantitative/experimental rigor
2. systems and operations
3. product/activation thinking

That combination pairs well with your strengths in content, partnerships, qualitative insight, and volunteer management.
