# NeuroAI for AI Safety: A Roadmap

## Why neuroscience matters for safe AI

Patrick Mineault and collaborators published a comprehensive roadmap exploring how neuroscience can address critical challenges in AI safety. The core premise is straightforward: "more constraints from human brain biophysics, representations, and behavior have a higher probability of leading to the basin of safe, human-like solutions."

The problem motivating this work is tangible. Despite over 10,000 academic papers, adversarial examples remain unsolved — a vulnerability where imperceptible noise transforms correct classifications into confident misidentifications. The insight that matters: many pathways lead to intelligent behavior, yet most lack the safety properties we associate with human cognition. Rather than wholesale adoption of brain mechanics, the proposed approach is selective — identifying neural mechanisms relevant to safe exploration, graceful handling of novel situations, and cooperation.

## Seven research pathways

### 1. Reverse-engineer sensory system representations
Understanding how brains achieve robust perception despite novel inputs could transfer directly to AI robustness. The roadmap derives scaling laws for sensory "digital twins" and shows early proof-of-concepts for strengthening AI against adversarial perturbations.

### 2. Create embodied digital twins
Physical embodiment shapes intelligent behavior fundamentally. Functional simulations combining brain-activity models with environmental and bodily dynamics could illuminate how embodied cognition produces safer, more robust decision-making.

### 3. Develop detailed simulations
Biophysical neural-circuit simulations capture constraints built into biological intelligence. Connectomics (mapping neural wiring) has seen dramatic cost reductions, making whole-nervous-system simulation increasingly feasible.

### 4. Build better cognitive architectures
Brain implementations of theory of mind, causal reasoning, and cooperation inform modular, probabilistic, interpretable architectures aligned with human values — an analog to "PyTorch for Bayesian cognitive architectures."

### 5. Advance brain-informed process supervision
Neural and behavioral data can fine-tune AI models toward brain-like alignment and safety properties. Large-scale open fMRI datasets make this surprisingly understudied area accessible to many researchers.

### 6. Reverse-engineer brain loss functions
Inferring the brain's loss functions from functional, structural, and behavioral evidence could yield superior AI training objectives and reward structures.

### 7. Apply neuroscience methods to AI interpretability
Neuroscience tools developed for understanding biological networks can illuminate artificial ones, and vice versa — making AI systems more transparent and verifiable.

## The coordination challenge

These seven approaches are interdependent: "progress in one area means progress in others." Advancing neuroscience's contribution to AI safety requires coordinated parallel investment in neurotechnology, scaled neural recording, and multilevel neural modeling — not sequential pursuit of isolated threads.
