One of my most deranged pleasures is haggling over minutia in high-stakes draft documents. Do we think Event “may” happen or “could possibly” happen? Can we “not rule out” Proposition, or do we consider Proposition “plausible”? Is Thing “highly” Adjective or “extremely” Adjective? Was Action “out of line” or “unacceptable”?

The process of going back and forth on the finer details of caveats and emphasis is a synecdoche for a larger process. I have a conviction that [reasonable people should be able to agree about things](https://acotra.substack.com/p/we-can-get-on-the-same-page). When that’s not true, I try to make it true by going around to all the reasonable people and trying to get them to yes on something. Can we agree that [language models surprised us](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/language-models-surprised-us)? Can we agree that [Hugging Face surprised us](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/the-hugging-face-attack-surprised)? Can we agree [we need a way to learn more potentially surprising facts](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/evidence-about-risk-should-be-transparent)?

In some real sense this is my main skill in life and the primary thing I do for work. [The Hugging Face report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/)? Haggling. [The Frontier Risk Report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)? Solid week straight of haggling. [Without specific countermeasures blah blah blah](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/pRkFkzwKZ2zfa3R6H/without-specific-countermeasures-the-easiest-path-to)? The reason that title is so comically caveated was the haggling.[^note-1] Sometimes I cut out the middleman and [just](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/K2D45BNxnZjdpSX2j/ai-timelines) make [haggling](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/10/does-ai-progress-have-a-speed-limit) the whole [product](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/14/how-long-until-ai-doesn-t-need-humans).

Some rationalists seem to act like there are two separate spheres: arriving at correct beliefs, and Comms, a purely instrumental activity where you figure out how to get some audience to agree with the correct beliefs you arrived at.[^note-2] But almost all my writing is done in a complex epistemic negotiation with my audience and within myself simultaneously, where it is hard to tell where Belief ends and Comms begins.

Indeed, most of my important beliefs are _formed_ in the process of trying to explain what I think and hearing what that sounds like and noticing how others react to that and how I react to their reactions and how this inspires me to change.[^note-3] In the yin and yang of yielding and morphing my beliefs so I can better defend them, and vigorously defending what I believe right now so I can better locate where I need to yield.

As I go through this process of negotiation, I’ll sometimes hit upon a particular argument, a striking fact pattern, a certain way of framing things…and I’ll think “This has God on its side.” I’ll have this inner conviction that the whole community of reasonable people would _surely_ get on board with this one if we just talked it out. Maybe people disagree now, but I can tell that if everything were litigated in the eyes of that community, the countervailing view would lose.

Getting God on your side is not the same as just arriving at some subjective probabilities you believe and beaming them out into the world. But it’s also not the same thing as doing Comms to pander to people’s existing views.[^note-4] It allows for a process of working things out with reason, [ringing conclusion after conclusion](https://acotra.substack.com/p/born-sick) from premises that reasonable people accept. It is a [slow process](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/science-and-speculation) and it isn’t powerful enough to let you win every time you’re right. But I find that if you approach it with humility and tenacity, it can give you a [real edge](https://slatestarcodex.com/2017/03/24/guided-by-the-beauty-of-our-weapons/).

And some of the work I’m proudest of is haggling no one will know about on blog posts and tweets and emails and memos written by me or a number of other people.
