**[Recursive self-improvement](https://www.alignmentforum.org/tag/recursive-self-improvement)** happens when a system enters a feedback loop of self-improvement that makes it better at further self-improvement. For example, an AI capable of human-level AI research could design an AI capable of genius-level AI research, which could design an AI capable of supergenius-level AI research, and so on. This process could quickly result in a vastly more capable system.

While we humans also improve our capabilities by studying new topics, practicing new skills, etc., we can’t easily alter our biology.[^note-1] AI agents, on the other hand, are computer programs that can be edited,[^note-2] or run on more and faster hardware. Such [advantages](https://aiimpacts.org/sources-of-advantage-for-artificial-intelligence/) might enable them to recursively self-improve much faster than humans can.

Few AI systems to date have been designed to pursue recursive self-improvement as an explicit goal, and not everybody agrees that recursive self-improvement by AIs is even possible. However, if it is possible, recursive self-improvement is probably an [instrumentally convergent](https://aisafety.info/questions/897I/What%20is%20instrumental%20convergence%3F) strategy that a wide range of AI agents might want to pursue.

[^note-1]: Potential methods for [making more fundamental changes to humans](https://aisafety.info/questions/6590/What%20is%20%22biological%20cognitive%20enhancement%22%3F), like neurosurgery, nootropics, and cognitive implants, are, at least for now, new and/or risky.
[^note-2]: It seems unlikely that weights of the AI models created in the current deep-learning paradigm could be edited in substantial ways after training, but other parts of the architecture might be more amenable to optimisation.
