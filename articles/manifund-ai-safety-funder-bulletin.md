This is a digest of funders in the AI safety space. The goal is to give an overview of who is funding in the space that includes the most relevant information for people seeking funds, looking to donate, or looking to work in grantmaking.

These tables are a rough summary of the numbers in the digest and the numbers are often best guesses based on public info; more detail is in the writeup and the footnotes. For funders that do work in multiple cause areas, it only considers grantmaking and staff focused on AIS. Some of the funding may repeat between rows—e.g. Coefficient donating to BlueDot or AISTOF making grants through Manifund—but this shouldn't substantially change the big-picture numbers.

This is intended to be a living document that updates regularly. If you have any corrections, please reach out!

### Coefficient Giving ^coefficient-giving

-   [Website](https://coefficientgiving.org/)
-   Background:
    -   Previously Open Philanthropy, it grew out of a partnership between GiveWell (founded in 2007 by Holden Karnofsky and Elie Hassenfeld) and Good Ventures (foundation started in 2011 by Cari Tuna and Dustin Moskovitz). They're mainly funded by Tuna and Moskovitz, but looking to work with more donors.
-   Thesis:
    -   The biggest funder in the space by far; AI safety is one of a dozen cause areas they make grants in. Grants are usually sourced through their own research rather than applications.
-   By the numbers:
    -   donations: allocating [$1b to catastrophic risks in 2026](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/sHF2yjAnNNhxZ56jf/coefficient-giving-is-hiring-grantmakers-and-senior)
    -   grant sizes: $12k to $160m
    -   number of grants: the Navigating Transformative AI Fund lists 189 in 2025 ($324.3m) and 75 so far in 2026 ($240.6m); the GCR Opportunities Fund lists 43 ($85.0m) and 13 ($46.3m)
    -   note that the public grants database understates their AI giving: their $160m grant to Resolution, announced in July 2026, does not appear in it, and nor do the Redwood Research grant of more than $70m over two years or the $10m for an institute being established by Fields Medallist Jacob Tsimerman, both named in their September 2026 scaling post. In September 2026 they said they committed [$351m](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/were-urgently-scaling-our-work-on-ai-and-biosecurity/) across technical AI safety, security, and fieldbuilding in 2025 — more than the $324.3m the database shows for the whole Navigating Transformative AI Fund.
    -   staff: 217 total. 43 of the 119 staff working on grants are on AI-focused teams, and I estimated 75 total by amortizing the staff working on operations, communications, and partnerships.
-   Recent updates:
    -   In a post on [October 22, 2026](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/how-we-approach-investing-in-for-profits/) they said that by default they and Good Ventures keep no stake in the for-profit AI and biosecurity companies they invest in: the equity is donated to the Against Malaria Foundation instead, so that their AI policy funding stays independent of any financial interest in companies that policy would affect. They expect several such investments over the coming years, and grants remain their main vehicle. AMF's own [page](https://www.againstmalaria.com/NewsItem.aspx?newsitem=Coefficient-Giving-supports-AMF-with-significant-gift) on the arrangement describes an August 2026 grant as Coefficient's largest ever to AMF and says Coefficient expects these investments to total hundreds of millions of dollars before the end of 2027, though the value is uncertain because it is equity.
    -   On October 30, 2026 they [announced](https://coefficientgiving.substack.com/p/october-2026-newsletter) Joe Huston as Managing Director of Philanthropic Advisory, heading the Partnerships team and overseeing the expansion of their advisory practice for major philanthropists.
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding:
        -   They have open RFPs for:
            
            -   [capacity-building](https://coefficientgiving.org/funds/navigating-transformative-ai/funding-for-work-that-builds-capacity-to-address-risks-from-transformative-ai/) (e.g. training and mentorship programs, events, groups, coworking spaces, media, online infrastructure, career advising) — decisions within 3 months
            -   [programs and events](https://coefficientgiving.org/funds/global-catastrophic-risks-opportunities/funding-for-programs-and-events-on-global-catastrophic-risk-effective-altruism-and-other-topics/) on global catastrophic risk, effective altruism, and related topics — decisions within 3 months
            
            Their career development and transition funding RFP has closed: that program moved to [BlueDot Impact](https://bluedot.org/grants/career-transition) on September 4, 2026.
        -   [Project Tailwind](https://coefficientgiving.org/tailwind/) (launched September 9, 2026) is a call for founders to start new AI safety organizations targeting catastrophic risks, with a [list of initiatives](https://coefficientgiving.org/tailwind/initiatives/) they want funded. Funding runs from pre-seed ($200k-$2m) through seed ($2m-$20m) to scale ($20m-$200m+); express interest [here](https://coefficientgiving.org/tailwind/get-involved/). They automatically filter out submissions unrelated to catastrophic risks from AI, or that appear AI-generated. There is no deadline, though they [say](https://coefficientgiving.org/tailwind/faqs/) they may stop accepting submissions if they receive a large volume.
        -   Their GCR capacity building team also takes a [general application](https://op-gcrcb-general-form.paperform.co/) for work relevant to their goals that doesn't fit one of the programs above.
    -   Donate: they are recruiting funders giving > $250k/year; reach out to [partnerwithus@coefficientgiving.org](mailto:partnerwithus@coefficientgiving.org)
    -   Apply for a job: as of October 15, 2026 their [careers page](https://coefficientgiving.org/about-us/careers/) says “There are no open roles at this time.” You can still submit a general [expression of interest](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/coefficientgiving/2cc48fa8-97aa-47d8-b367-bf3b66cdba3f), and the page now also points would-be founders at Project Tailwind

### OpenAI Foundation ^openai-foundation

-   [Website](https://openaifoundation.org/)
-   Background:
    -   A nonprofit that owns a large stake in OpenAI, spun off in 2025. Their AI resilience team is run by OpenAI cofounder Wojciech Zaremba.
-   Thesis:
    -   They now describe three priority programs: life sciences and curing diseases, AI resilience, and civil society and philanthropy. They treat the economic impacts of AI as “part of the broader AI resilience agenda” but say that, “given the scale of the economic transition,” they are developing that work as a separate program. Within AI resilience, they're focused on bio-resilience, cyber-resilience, AI model safety, and AI's impact on young people.
-   By the numbers:
    -   donations: [$130m+ in AI resilience grants](https://openaifoundation.org/news/resilience-in-the-age-of-ai) that they said in June 2026 they were working to finalize
    -   staff: 3 publicly named in AI resilience — Wojciech Zaremba, Divya Siddarth, and Dan Wattendorf, whom Zaremba [announced](https://x.com/woj_zaremba/status/2083256852255736256) on July 31, 2026 as Head of Bio-Resilience; they said in August 2026 that the team is “still small”
    -   as of November 1, 2026 the Foundation still hasn't published a list of the AI resilience grants it described as being finalized in June
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for a job: they're hiring for 15 roles, all in San Francisco, listed [here](https://openaifoundation.org/careers#open-roles), and the page now also takes a general expression of interest. The most AI-safety-relevant are still the two AI Resilience roles, a Program Officer for AI Model Safety and a Program Officer for AI Resources; the Program Director for Formal Methods and Chief of Staff for AI Resilience postings listed in September 2026 remain delisted.
    -   Apply for funding: the Foundation has no application route for AI safety work, and has not published one.

### Longview Philanthropy ^longview-philanthropy

-   [Website](https://www.longview.org/)
-   Background:
    -   Founded in 2018 by barrister Natalie Cargill, who is now listed simply as Founder; Simran Dhaliwal is CEO.
-   Thesis:
    -   Primarily a donor advisor: they design giving strategies for major philanthropists and move most money via grant recommendations, alongside their own funds. Live fund pages cover the [Frontier AI Fund](https://www.longview.org/fund/frontier-ai-fund/) and the Nuclear Weapons Policy Fund; a private digital-minds fund they have called both the Digital Minds Fund and the Digital Sentience Fund has no live page, and the card for it on their AI page links to a 404. They are closing the Emerging Challenges Fund.
-   By the numbers:
    -   donations: they directed over $75m in 2025 and say they expect 2026 to be “substantially bigger”; their (now removed) hiring materials earlier said they aimed to move $200m in 2026. The only per-fund figure they publish is for the [Frontier AI Fund](https://www.longview.org/fund/frontier-ai-fund/), which “supported 18 organizations” in its first nine months, December 2024 to September 2025.
    -   staff: 37 listed as of November 1, 2026, up from 34, with 10 of them in the AI program and 8 of those with AI in the title (a ninth, an AI Donor Advisor, sits in the advisory team); around half the org working on AIS gets 18. Katie Hearsum, previously COO, is now listed as President.
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding: they post periodic RFPs [here](https://www.longview.org/grantmaking/#funding-opportunities), though none are open right now; otherwise opportunities are sourced via proactive research.
    -   Donate: [contact form](https://www.longview.org/contact/); they're interested in donors giving at least $1m/year for advisory services, and their private funds are open to donors giving over $100k.
    -   Apply for a job: their [careers page](https://www.longview.org/careers/) lists no open roles and takes general applications only.

### Macroscopic ^macroscopic

-   [Website](https://macroscopic.org/)
-   Background:
    -   Swiss nonprofit founded by Ruairí Donnelly, Jonas Vollmer, David Althaus, and Daniel Kestenholz in 2019. Formerly Center for Emerging Risk Research and Polaris Ventures.
-   Thesis:
    -   Within AI safety, they're focused on preventing AI misuse, AI welfare, and cooperation between advanced AI systems. They also donate to reason & democracy and animal welfare and do for-profit investing in their areas of interest.
-   By the numbers:
    -   donating: up to $100m this year
    -   grant sizes: $100k to $15m
    -   staff: 17 listed on their [about page](https://macroscopic.org/about) as of November 1, 2026, up from 16. AI governance is the only named AI program and has one grants director; of three grants associates one works on animal welfare, and three roles are pure operations.
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding: you can email [info@macroscopic.org](mailto:info@macroscopic.org), though most grants are sourced through proactive research and they don't respond to most proposals
    -   Donate: they're not seeking donations, but their about page says that if you're interested in donating > $1m to their focus areas they may be able to advise you, and that they want to connect with foundations and philanthropists with overlapping interests
    -   Apply for a job: no current open roles, but you can express interest [here](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/macroscopic/0d80e3a8-2ffd-4bef-8485-03f764732a6e)

### Astralis Foundation ^astralis-foundation

-   [Website](https://astralisfoundation.org/)
-   Background:
    -   A UK not-for-profit with a Swedish sister foundation, Stiftelsen Astralis; the team works remotely across the UK, US, and Sweden. They pool money from “well over a dozen donors across several countries,” with no single donor dominating, and take no money from major AI companies or their employees, governments, or political parties active in AI policy. Their main vehicle is a pooled fund called Shared Horizons.
-   Thesis:
    -   They fund neglected work to make advanced AI secure and beneficial, focused on AI safety capacity and international cooperation in Asia, including China, and AI progress, safety and governance capacity in Europe. They are also scoping AI company oversight. Named grantees include the Institute for Law & AI's EU team, the Singapore AI Safety Hub, and the Simon Institute for Longterm Governance.
-   By the numbers:
    -   donations: an August 2026 job posting said they had mobilized more than $60m in 2026 and planned to reach around $100m by year end, after raising and deploying more than $30m to more than 15 grantees in their first 18 months; their own site publishes no totals
    -   staff: 12 listed on their [team page](https://astralisfoundation.org/our-people), plus 6 board members across the UK and Swedish entities
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding: they don't take unsolicited requests. You can email [ops@astralisfoundation.org](mailto:ops@astralisfoundation.org) about funding, partnering, or ideas.
    -   Donate: they take donations from qualifying donors under their gift acceptance policy; get in touch at the same address.
    -   Apply for a job: their job board lists no open roles, but you can submit an expression of interest via their [careers page](https://astralisfoundation.org/careers) or email [hiring@astralisfoundation.org](mailto:hiring@astralisfoundation.org); their homepage says they expect to be hiring in operations and donor-facing work over the coming months.

### Survival and Flourishing Fund (SFF) ^survival-and-flourishing-fund

-   [Website](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/)
-   Background:
    -   Founded in 2019 and funded by Jaan Tallinn, who was joined by Dustin Moskovitz as a second Funder in the 2026 Main Round — the first SFF round run for more than one Funder.
-   Thesis:
    -   SFF is a virtual fund focused on organizing grant processes to support the long-term survival and flourishing of sentient life. Most grants go to reducing AI x-risk. Jaan Tallinn's priorities include efforts to restrict AI—datacenter certifications, speed limits, liability laws, labeling requirements, veto committees, and off-switches—as well as constructive efforts to set examples for the positive use of AI—AI assistance for human intelligence, AI healthcare tech, positive moralities, safety specs for AI, and hardware-level AI controls.
-   By the numbers:
    -   their homepage chart puts annual totals at $33m (2023), $41m (2024) and $35m (2025), rising to $64m in 2026, and says SFF “has organized ~$216MM in philanthropic gifts and grants” to date; in 2025 they recommended $34.33m across 88 grants, and the 2026 Main Round recommended $63.13m across 115 grants. Both the lifetime total and the 2026 figures have been revised down since October 1, 2026
    -   staff: 10 people listed at Survival and Flourishing Corp, plus a 2-person board (Andrew Critch and Eric Rogstad). Recommendations are done by part-time recommenders — twelve of them across three tracks in 2026, two anonymous.
-   Recent updates:
    -   The 2026 Main Round, split into Main, Freedom, and Fairness tracks, closed on April 22, and its [recommendations](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/2026/recommendations) were published on September 30, 2026 and have since been revised down. As of October 15, 2026 the page shows $63.13m recommended across 115 grants to 114 organizations, with $63.64m expected to be distributed in association with the round — roughly double their own $14-28m estimate, but about $1.4m below the figures the page carried two weeks earlier, and it warns that “Details of some recommendations may change later.” Jaan Tallinn funds $34.10m of it and Dustin Moskovitz $29.03m. The track totals inverted their expectations: the Freedom Track came in at $31.22m against a $2-4m estimate, making it the largest track, with the Main Track at $13.49m and the Fairness Track at $8.02m; a further $10.40m is attributed to a Synthetic Mean Recommender. In a September 9, 2026 [update](https://survivalandflourishing.com/2026-update), SFC CEO Ethan Ashkie said the round's process “created confusion and uncertainty, backlash from some Evaluators, delays in grant decisions, and ultimately meant that some applications which SFF Evaluators recommended funding for were not facilitated by SFC.” SFC says it does not want to work with people who spread messages advocating for violence, or to recommend funding for work that would foreseeably inflame and coarsen public AI discourse.
    -   On October 3, 2026 SFF publicly withdrew its grant recommendation to John Wentworth, saying that after the September 30 announcement “public statements by Mr. Wentworth came to our attention which we found objectionable, disturbing, and contrary to the values of SFF,” and that the Funders would not fulfil the remainder of the recommendation; an earlier Speculation Grant had already been disbursed. He no longer appears in the 2026 recommendations.
    -   They have announced a [$5-20m ISPA Inspection Prize](https://survivalandflourishing.com/ispa-prize) — now the top item on their announcements list — for initiating “Independently Supervised and Peer-Assisted” inspections between frontier AI labs, aimed at pre-deployment security risks like lab leaks and sandbox exfiltration. Jaan Tallinn will contribute at least $5m, plus another $5m at a 1:2 matching rate with other funders. See Get involved below.
    -   They added three themed rounds for 2026 with $2-4m each — climate change (closed June 10), animal welfare (closed June 24), and human self-enhancement and empowerment (closed July 8). Recommendations for these are expected in November 2026 and have not yet been published.
    -   Their programs list now includes an [Advisory Organizations Panel](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/advisory-organizations-panel), “a set of trusted outside organizations that SFC consults for non-binding input on proposed grants where the balance of potential positive and negative impacts is in live debate” — a response to the 2026 process problems described above. It was formed in 2026 by invitation and has nine participants, of whom four are named (the Center for AI Safety, CHAI, FAR AI, and the Foundation for American Innovation); they were asked to review 30-35 proposed grants and give a positive, negative, or abstaining opinion. SFC says the panel has no decision-making power and no formal role in the S-Process, and that earlier communications wrongly depicted it as a body that could approve or veto grants.
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding: [instructions](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/speculation-grants) for applying to a Speculation Grant, and the [ISPA Inspection Prize](https://survivalandflourishing.com/ispa-prize) is open with no deadline (there is no formal prize application, but an eligibility inquiry form for optional advance vetting, and SFF will consider grants to support prospective inspections)
        -   Speculation Grants are faster grants made outside the S-Process timeline. Around 40 people serve as Speculators, each holding a budget of roughly $400-500k to approve them from, for a combined budget of about $20m, up from $4m when the program started.
        -   Submitting an application also puts you in consideration for the next S-Process Grant round. In fact, being awarded a Speculation Grant is how you guarantee eligibility for a round, and SFF [says](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/2026/application) over 95% of applications evaluated in past rounds received one.
    -   Donate: No page states a policy on cofunders either way, but they did add a second Funder in 2026; consider reaching out to [sff-contact@googlegroups.com](mailto:sff-contact@googlegroups.com).
    -   Apply for a job: Survival and Flourishing Corp is hiring three roles — two [engineering roles](https://survivalandflourishing.com/careers/engineering-roles) (full-stack and design) at $250k-$350k, plus a [Product Manager](https://survivalandflourishing.com/careers/product-manager) at $100k-$150k. All are remote, with 2-4 in-person team weeks a year in the Bay Area. The security and QA/testing engineering roles posted earlier in 2026 are no longer listed.

### Lightcone Commons ^lightcone-commons

-   [Website](https://www.lightconecommons.com/)
-   Background:
    -   Announced by Oliver Habryka in July 2026. The [launch post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tjeoLz2GzfFMysZhg/lightcone-commons) gave a tentative first-round funder list of Jaan Tallinn ($10m, conditional on $10m coming from other funders), Dustin Moskovitz (~$5m for the first round, $10m over the first year if it goes well), two anonymous donors ($3m+ and $2m+), the LTFF and the AI Risk Mitigation Fund (~$2m each), Timothy Telleen-Lawton (~$200k), and Andreas Stuhlmüller (~$100k). The site itself no longer publishes per-funder amounts: as of November 1, 2026 it names the first round's funders as Tallinn, Moskovitz, the LTFF, the ARM Fund, Telleen-Lawton and Stuhlmüller, with only the LTFF and ARM Fund figures given (~$2m each), and drops the anonymous donors.
-   Thesis:
    -   Lightcone Commons is aiming for near-feature-parity with the S-process used by SFF, with several parts redesigned to make the application and evaluation processes less time-intensive. They now say they are aiming for a round every month, up from every three months at launch. Invited “direct grantors” also get a budget to make immediate unilateral grants, reimbursed out of the next round's first allocations to that project.
-   By the numbers:
    -   first round: originally expected to be around $15-25m; on October 12, 2026 Nathan Young said it [looked set to advise on around $50m](https://x.com/NathanpmYoung/status/2109700584693768321), and the site now says it expects ~$45m to be granted overall
    -   fees: 5% on top of grants — 3% to Lightcone Commons and 2% to evaluators, lowered for funders moving large amounts
    -   staff: recommendations done by part-time evaluators, fifteen confirmed as of November 1, 2026 — Zvi Mowshowitz, Yafah Edelman, Eliezer Yudkowsky, Nate Soares, Caleb Parikh, Elizabeth Van Nostrand, Oliver Habryka, Scott Alexander, Robert Mushkatblatt, Richard Ngo, Nathan Young, Tsvi Benson-Tilsen, Lowe Lundin (who also heads the Transformative AI Fund, below), Zach Stein-Perlman and Stuart Buck. Katja Grace, previously listed as confirmed for future rounds, is no longer named
-   Recent updates:
    -   Applications for the first round closed on August 23, 2026 and recommendations were expected around October 23, 2026. As of November 1, 2026 Lightcone Commons has still published no results, grantee list or total — the only figure on the site is “We expect ~$45M to be granted overall in the first round” — but the round does appear decided and grantees notified: the site now describes the first round's funders in the past tense, and Manifold Markets said on [November 1, 2026](https://manifold.markets/Gen/how-much-money-will-manifold-receiv) that “funders on Lightcone Commons have decided to collectively grant $275,000 to Manifold Markets.” Applications remain open on a rolling basis and roll into the second round, which the site now says will close mid-December, with evaluator recommendations due December 1 and a cutoff three weeks before the round closes. They say they are now aiming for a round every month; Oliver Habryka had said in September 2026 that he hoped to reach monthly rounds in Q1 2027, cutting average decision time to 2-3 weeks.
    -   The [Corrigibility Research Fund](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/FBqe5dt8ZjaHN4Xj9/announcing-the-corrigibility-research-fund), a Lightcone Infrastructure program managed by Max Harms that will award at least $200k in grants and prizes for corrigibility research in 2026, is moving onto Lightcone Commons for its second round, with applications due October 31, 2026; as of November 1, 2026 the fund's site still lists that as its “next deadline” and has posted nothing about the round closing or its results, its update log ending September 30, 2026. Its first round was run over email and awarded $132k to eight of more than 100 applicants. Separately it has paid out retroactive prizes for existing work: $27k in July 2026 and a further $48k announced on September 30, 2026, for $75k so far, with more than $60k more hoped for in December 2026. So the fund has already passed its “at least $200k” floor for 2026. Note that October 31 is the CRF's own deadline, not a Lightcone Commons round deadline.
    -   In August 2026 the Long-Term Future Fund announced it was closing, and that it would spend down around $2.8m on existing applicants and longtermist projects, primarily through this first round. Lightcone's own funder list puts the LTFF contribution at around $2m; the LTFF's closure post says Lightcone agreed to waive its 3% platform and 2% evaluator fees on it.
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding: [here](https://www.lightconecommons.com/apply)
        -   They also import applications from grantmaking.ai, Manifund, and LTFF.
    -   Donate: They have no strict requirement, but ask that you assign some non-trivial probability to distributing at least $50k in the coming round; book an onboarding call [here](https://calendly.com/oliver-habryka/lightcone-commons-funder-call).

### AI Safety Tactical Opportunities Fund (AISTOF) ^aistof

-   Background:
    -   Founded by JueYan Zhang (former BlackRock PM) in 2023
-   Thesis:
    -   A multi-donor fund focused on moving fast to fill time-sensitive gaps.
-   By the numbers:
    -   funds raised: > $30m, per their own description
    -   grants made: > 150 since September 2023
    -   their [Manifund account](https://manifund.org/JueYan), which is only one of their channels, shows $1.75m across 17 grants in 2025 and $2.77m across 28 grants in 2026 through November 1; their most recent grant there was on October 4, 2026
    -   Manifund's [Trace](https://trace.manifund.org/orgs/aistof) database estimates their overall giving at $15.0m in 2025 and $25.0m in 2026, most of it as a lump estimate rather than itemised grants ($47m all-time across 49 records, of which it itemises 9%)
    -   they are one of the named backers of the UK AI Security Institute's [Alignment Project](https://alignmentproject.aisi.gov.uk/about), a pooled fund of over £27m, alongside Schmidt Sciences, Halcyon Futures, Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, AWS and others; no per-funder amount is published
    -   staff: ~1 FTE
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding: there's no open application, but consider posting a proposal on [Manifund](https://manifund.org/). A site has appeared at aistof.org but as of November 1, 2026 it serves only a “Coming Soon” placeholder
    -   Donate: there's no formal way to do so, but you could reach out to JueYan on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jueyan/)

### Schmidt Sciences ^schmidt-sciences

-   [Website](https://www.schmidtsciences.org/focus-area-ai/)
-   Background:

### Transformative AI Fund (TAIF), formerly the Long-Term Future Fund (LTFF) ^transformative-ai-fund

-   [Website](https://funds.effectivealtruism.org/funds/transformative-ai)
-   Background:
    -   The LTFF started in 2017 as a project of Centre for Effective Altruism. In August 2026 EA Funds [closed the LTFF](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/dtZ9wbKWjtvGWDRJx/closing-the-ltff-spending-down-funds-and-a-new-ai-fund-at-ea) and [launched the Transformative AI Fund](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/dYuNi5Rh68o9YKstg/ea-funds-is-launching-the-transformative-ai-fund) in its place, with Lowe Lundin as full-time Head of Fund. The LTFF's outgoing managers said the closure was partly about institutional friction around grants to individuals, for-profits, and policy-adjacent work; several of them plan to keep granting part-time through the AI Risk Mitigation Fund and Lightcone Commons.
-   Thesis:
    -   Early-stage grants to individuals, new organizations, and existing organizations with new projects. Primary focus is technical AI safety and AI governance, including post-AGI governance, plus field-building and forecasting.
-   By the numbers:
    -   the LTFF donated around $5-6m/year through 2024, but only $1.2m in 2025 and $0.69m in 2026 as recorded in the public grants database, up from $0.3m two weeks earlier as seven back-dated 2026 Q1 grants were added; EA Funds' own payout chart shows more for 2026, $1.52m across 18 grants
    -   number of grants: 100-200/year through 2024, but 20 in 2025 and 13 in 2026 in the public database, which still runs only through 2026 Q2
    -   grant size: typically $10k-$150k, rarely above $300k — now TAIF's stated policy, not just LTFF's historical pattern
    -   staff: 1 full-time (Lowe Lundin); advisors Caleb Parikh and Catherine Low are part-time
    -   stated response time: 6-8 weeks, though the application form currently warns that “response times are longer than usual at the moment”
-   Recent updates:
    -   No narrative payout report has been posted since the one covering May 2023 to March 2024, though the [grants database](https://funds.effectivealtruism.org/grants) is updated through 2026 Q2. TAIF says it will publish its first quarterly report before the end of 2026. As of November 1, 2026 no TAIF grants appear in that database and the fund page lists no payout reports, so nearly three months after launch none of its grantmaking is public yet. In fairness, the database still holds no 2026 Q3 round for any EA Fund, and TAIF's first grants would fall in that quarter.
    -   The LTFF is spending down around $3.7m: roughly $2.8m to existing applicants, mostly through the first [Lightcone Commons](https://www.lightconecommons.com/) round, and around $0.9m as a seed grant to TAIF.
-   Get involved:
    -   Apply for funding: use [this form](https://av20jp3z.paperform.co/?fund=Transformative%20AI%20Fund); applications are rolling. The LTFF is no longer taking new applications, and live LTFF applications were imported into the first Lightcone Commons round.
    -   Donate: donate to TAIF [here](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/charities/transformative-ai-fund); TAIF is actively fundraising beyond its ~$1m seed grant
    -   Apply for a job: the Associate Program Officer search has closed. CEA's [job board](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/centreforeffectivealtruism) has no TAIF-specific role open; its grantmaking openings as of November 1, 2026 are Head of the EA Infrastructure Fund and a Grants Associate posted October 15, 2026, a grants-operations role spanning CEA's grantmaking rather than TAIF specifically, whose applications closed on October 30, 2026 though the posting is still listed. The Head of Grantmaking Operations posting has since been delisted.

They also appear as a cofunder of the UK AI Security Institute's Alignment Project.

They're one of the funders of Lightcone Commons, contributing around $2m to its first round. Their [apply page](https://www.airiskfund.com/apply) says they have no set date for opening applications and currently fund only opportunities they source themselves.

Their [PhD fellowship](https://futureoflife.org/grant-program/phd-fellowships/) is now paused — they aren't accepting applications in fall 2026 while they reassess the program. Their one open program is the [Digital Media Accelerator](https://futureoflife.org/project/digital-media-accelerator/), which funds AI safety content creators through a form that is continuously open — it publishes no deadline and no grant sizes. A September 2026 update to that page says they are prioritizing content on the AI industry's “race-to-replace,” escalating harms from advanced systems, and loss-of-control risk, and warns that “review timelines for applications are longer than normal at the moment.” Their grantmaking page otherwise lists no open programs and says they do not accept unsolicited requests.
-   [Frontier Model Forum AI Safety Fund](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/ai-safety-fund/): It was established in October 2023 as a “$10 million+” initiative. Most of this was distributed in 2024 and 2025 — their most recent grantees were announced in December 2025, and there have been no 2026 grantees or news — and they now appear to be winding down, saying their remaining funds “will be used to support narrowly-scoped research projects that target urgent bottlenecks.” The fund was initially administered by the Meridian Institute, and the Forum has managed it directly since Meridian announced in June 2025 that it was closing.
-   [Foresight AI for Science & Safety Nodes RFP](https://foresight.org/grants/ai-science-safety-nodes-rfp/): Their standalone AI for Safety and Science program page now redirects here, so the Nodes RFP is the whole of their current AI offering. The RFP closed on October 31, 2026 at 23:59 PDT; as of November 1, 2026 the page is unchanged and no extension or new round has been announced, with decisions expected about three months after the deadline. It funded work on local compute, coordination and accountability, and AI-first science, “typically $30,000 – $100,000 for this RFP but larger amounts are possible for the right project”, with overhead capped at 10%. All funded work must be open-sourced. They strongly prefer applicants who will be in-person members of their SF or Berlin hubs; they have also added a community xNode in Cape Town.
-   [Astera](https://astera.org/ai-safety/): Their webpage mentions that they're primarily backing [Simplex](https://www.simplexaisafety.com/).
-   [grantmaking.ai](https://www.grantmaking.ai/): New initiative housed under Manifund, with an initial $1m funded by Anton Makiievskyi, see [launch post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/hDQZZzYkcipgaZfxy/usd1m-ai-x-risk-grant-round-is-live-on-grantmaking-ai-apply). They published a [retrospective](https://grantmaking.substack.com/p/grantmakingai-launch-round-retrospective) on August 14, 2026 saying they have “many ideas for the future” but that plans will take time to finalize. No second round has been announced.
-   [AI Forge](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/ai-forge): A joint DARPA/NSF program, working with CAISI at NIST, funding university-led research on AI interpretability, control, robustness and assurance. Its [Cycle 2 Request for Abstracts](https://aiforge.govsignals.ai/) is open to US universities, with abstracts due November 6, 2026 and awards of about $1.5m over one year.

As of November 1, 2026 their funding page lists only a rolling [open call for research projects](https://www.iliad.ac/opportunity/open-call-fund-your-bet) — the earlier request for proposals on epistemics-raising projects and theory-driven research on ReLU networks is no longer posted — and they still don't publish grant sizes or deadlines.
