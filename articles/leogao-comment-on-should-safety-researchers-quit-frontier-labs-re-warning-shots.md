i agree it’s good to be suspicious of “we should make things worse to make them better in the long run“, and for example, i think it would be bad if people actively tried to cause warning shots—but i’d argue there are subtypes of this which are much more robustly good, where you are taking an action that isn’t immediately maximally harm reducing because you’re upholding some other principle (and i do think there’s an important difference between arguing that we should not maximally reduce things that are bad in some aspect, as opposed to saying we should aim to increase badness).

for example, ”it’s bad to mitigate harms when doing so would prevent recklessness from being properly punished.” this implies being less than maximally willing to prevent immediate harms, yet doesn’t imply you should ever try to cause more harm than was going to happen outside of your control.
