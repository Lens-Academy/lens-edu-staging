Rapid capability gains, or just large capability gains between a training paradigm and a test paradigm, are one of the primary expected reasons why AGI alignment might be hard. We probably want the first AGI or AGIs ever built, tested, and used to _not_ self-improve as quickly as possible. Since there's a very strong [convergent incentive](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/instrumental-convergence) to self-improve and do things [neighboring](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/nearest-unblocked-strategy) to self-improvement, by default you would expect an AGI to search for ways to defeat naive blocks on self-improvement, which violates the [nonadversarial principle](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/niceness-is-the-first-line-of-defense). Thus, any proposals to limit an AGI's capabilities imply a very strong desideratum for us to figure out a way to [avert the instrumental incentive](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/averting-instrumental-pressures) to self-improvement in that AGI. The alternative is failing the [Omni Test](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/omnipotence-test-for-ai-safety), violating the nonadversarial principle, [having the AGI's code be actively inconsistent with what the AGI would approve of its own code being](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/reflective-consistency) (if the brake is a code-level measure), and setting up a safety measure that the AGI wants to defeat as the [only line of defense](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/niceness-is-the-first-line-of-defense).
