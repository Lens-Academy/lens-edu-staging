# Brain-Computer Interfaces and AI Alignment

> Summary placeholder — full verbatim text to be landed from the scraper output (`extract_pipeline.py`). Read the original: https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rpRsksjrBXEDJuHHy/brain-computer-interfaces-and-ai-alignment

Niplav gathers the arguments for using brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) as a lever on AI alignment and finds them mostly unconvincing. The main proposals are (1) using BCIs to extract richer, higher-bandwidth data about human values and preferences to train or supervise AI, and (2) "merging" with AI so humans are not left behind. The piece argues the merge framing is underspecified — it is rarely spelled out what "merging" concretely buys you — and that BCI-based alignment tends to presuppose you already have an aligned AI to safely connect to, making it partly circular. The stronger residual use is the data angle (closer to brain-informed supervision) rather than merge-as-control. Overall assessment: BCIs are unlikely to be a decisive alignment strategy, and no one has written up a detailed, credible path from BCIs to good AI outcomes.
