Describe the main existing international efforts on AI (the summit series, UN processes and the International AI Safety Report), what each has produced, and how far each is from binding rules on advanced AI.

## Requires

## Helpful first
- [[AI governance]]

## Why it matters
Any future global agreement on AI will build on these forums or replace them. Knowing what they have achieved, and where agreement broke down, lets you judge treaty proposals realistically, read summit news correctly, and see where shared scientific evidence already exists.

## Lenses
- [AI summitry: growth beats safety (CEPA)](https://cepa.org/article/ai-summitry-growth-beats-safety/): how the summit series moved from safety at Bletchley to "action" in Paris, where the US and UK refused to sign, and to a New Delhi Declaration with no mention of risk or safety (March 2026).
- [AI Safety Summit (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Safety_Summit): the plain facts of the first summit at Bletchley Park: who signed the Bletchley Declaration (including the US and China), the AI Safety Institutes it led to, the safety report it commissioned, and the later summits.
- [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026): read the forewords and "About this Report" for what the report is, who backs it, how it began at Bletchley, and why it makes no policy recommendations (February 2026).
- [UN reaches consensus on AI, now comes the hard part (Tech Policy Press)](https://techpolicy.press/un-reaches-consensus-on-ai-now-comes-the-hard-part): what the August 2025 UN resolution set up (a 40-member scientific panel and a global dialogue on AI governance), what each will produce, and the gaps: no binding commitments, slow pace, thin funding (September 2025).
- [[../Lenses/AI Safety Atlas - Governance - Governance Architectures|Governance Architectures]]: read the "International Governance" section on why countries can't regulate AI alone, the AI Safety Institutes network, the obstacles to cooperation, and why binding agreements are still ahead (2025).
