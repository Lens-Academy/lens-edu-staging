Find the cruxes of a disagreement, meaning the beliefs that would change a conclusion if they changed, in your own reasoning and in other people's, and use double crux to look for one you share.

## Requires

## Helpful first

## Why it matters
https://www.youtube.com/shorts/hYI3ikRbKGE

Disagreements about AI risk often stall because people trade arguments that wouldn't change anyone's mind. If you can find the cruxes, you can spend your effort on the evidence that would actually move a conclusion, and learn what someone would need to see before changing their view.

## Lenses
- [[../articles/cfar-duncan-double-crux|Double Crux (CFAR Handbook)]]: what a crux is, why arguing to win hides it, and the double crux method of searching together for a belief that would change both minds, with its five-step algorithm; read up to "Double Crux—Further Resources" (2022 edition of a 2017 method).
- [The Basic Double Crux pattern (LessWrong)](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/hNztRARB52Riy36Kz/the-basic-double-crux-pattern): a CFAR instructor's short, step-by-step pattern for one move: check you understood a point, ask whether it is a crux, then whether it is a crux for the other person too (July 2020).
- [[../Lenses/HftS - Is That Your True Rejection|Is That Your True Rejection?]]: why the reason someone gives for rejecting an idea, including ideas about AI, is often not the one that decided it, and why to ask yourself the same question.
- [Contra double crux (LessWrong)](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nm6XuC9CzNBrthpPB/contra-double-crux): a critic's case that shared cruxes are rare, because most views rest on many considerations of moderate weight; one side of a debate with CFAR staff in the comments (October 2017).
- [A taxonomy of cruxes (LessWrong)](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/p35o3BvDfyiPWkpv8/a-taxonomy-of-cruxes): kinds of crux, including several beliefs that are each needed for a view, reasons that only look like cruxes because the view is held for other reasons too, and cruxes that only work in one direction (May 2020) (more advanced).
- [[../Lenses/cotra-the-art-of-the-epistemic-deal|The art of the epistemic deal]] (BlueDot Deep Field pre-reading)

#### Question: Open
id:: 05aac3c8-6552-4c73-aa67-0c0944bb25c2
content:: Someone says AI can't become dangerous because it only predicts text. Write one question that would test whether this belief is a crux for them, and say which answer would show it is.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 100 for a question asking whether their conclusion would change if the belief were false (for example, "If an AI that predicts text could plan and act, would you think it could be dangerous?") plus correctly naming the answer that shows it is a crux ("yes" for that example; "no" for a question phrased the other way, such as "Would you still think it safe?"). 50 for a suitable question with no answer named or the wrong answer named. 0 for a question that just argues the point.
