Explain Narayanan and Kapoor's view of AI as a normal technology: why they expect its effects to arrive slowly and favour governing it like other technologies, and where it differs from and agrees with the superintelligence view.

## Requires

## Helpful first
- [[AI scenarios]]

## Why it matters
This is a leading mainstream counter-view to superintelligence concerns and shapes much policy thinking. If you can state it fairly, you can tell real disagreements about the speed and nature of AI progress from talking past each other, and build on the ground both sides already share.

## Lenses
- [[../Lenses/narayanan-a-guide-to-understanding-ai-as-normal-technology|A Guide to AI as Normal Technology]]: Narayanan and Kapoor restate their thesis in plain terms: impact comes with deployment, not development, so effects are slow even if capabilities race ahead; why "normal" doesn't mean mundane; and how the view differs from AI 2027. The section "It is hard to understand one worldview" can be skimmed (Sep 2025).
- [AI as Normal Technology (Knight First Amendment Institute)](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology): the original essay. Read these sections: "Diffusion is limited by the speed of human, organizational, and institutional change", "Benchmarks do not measure real-world utility", "Control comes in many flavors", "The primary defenses against misuse must be located downstream of models", "Catastrophic misalignment is a speculative risk" and "Nonproliferation is infeasible to enforce and leads to single points of failure" (April 2025).
- [Common Ground between AI 2027 & AI as Normal Technology (Asterisk)](https://asteriskmag.substack.com/p/common-ground-between-ai-2027-and): the AI 2027 and normal-technology authors list what they agree on, including that strong AGI arriving soon would not be a normal technology, and where they still differ (November 2025).
