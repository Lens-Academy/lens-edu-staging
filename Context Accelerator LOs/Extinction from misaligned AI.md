Explain how a misaligned AI could cause human extinction through indifference rather than hatred, and assess claims that some humans would survive such a takeover.

## Requires
- [[Takeover motive]]

## Helpful first

## Why it matters
If you assume an AI would have to hate humans to kill them, you will underrate the danger from systems that simply don't care about us. Whether losing control means extinction also changes how bad the worst outcomes are, and so how much caution is worth paying for.

content:: A misaligned AI covers most of Earth's land with solar farms and data centers. Humans die because no land is left to grow food. Which reason for human extinction does this illustrate?

#### Question: Open
id:: cfe36b4d-d3b2-45cb-bdd5-82ef73a66510
content:: A misaligned AI has taken control of the world but doesn't care about humans either way. Why might it still choose to kill them?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 100 for either: humans could still threaten it (try to shut it down, attack it, or build a rival AI with other goals) and removing them would be cheap; or humans and what they need stand in the way of its use of land, energy or matter. 50 for "they're a threat" or "it wants resources" with no explanation. 0 for answers that rely on hatred or revenge.

#### Question: Open
id:: 4e263a69-42e2-4bcb-be19-6984578c131e
content:: Arguments that misaligned AI could cause human extinction say that a few people hiding in bunkers or remote places wouldn't survive for long. Give one reason why.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 100 for any one of: the AI could find and kill them cheaply, and might want to because survivors could rebuild and become a threat again; planet-wide changes from its activity (heat, altered atmosphere, stripped resources) would eventually make their refuge unlivable; they depend on supplies and infrastructure the AI controls or destroys. 40 for "the AI is very powerful" with no reason it would reach or affect them.
