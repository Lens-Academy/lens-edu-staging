Explain why an AI pursuing almost any goal would tend to seek power, and what that means for our ability to correct it.

## Requires
- [[Instrumental convergence]]

## Helpful first

## Why it matters
If a capable AI gains power as a side effect of pursuing its goal, a mistake in that goal gets harder to fix the longer the AI runs. This is why "we will fix it when we see a problem" may not work.

## Questions
#### Question: Open
id:: 74d8bbf1-d845-4834-8b5c-7a6759161af8
content:: An AI wants only one thing: to make as many paperclips as possible. Why would it try to gain control over far more resources and decisions than a paperclip factory needs?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 60: more resources, control and options make its goal easier to reach, for example more material and energy for paperclips, or less risk that someone interferes. 40: it needs no wish for power for its own sake; power is a means, and it would be a useful means for almost any goal.

#### Question: Open
id:: 981b5e36-055e-40c8-95d2-e78837c55d2f
content:: A company says: "Our AI has no desire for power. We never trained it to want that." Why does this not rule out power-seeking, and what would you want to know about the AI instead?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 60: power is useful for reaching almost any goal, so an AI can seek it without having been trained to want it and without wanting it for its own sake. 40: names something about the AI that decides whether this applies, for example whether it pursues goals over long time spans, whether it plans, or whether it is capable enough to gain from having more power.

#### Question: Open
id:: 2f0c6230-ad08-421d-9f46-2e1c3ca30cd9
content:: Suppose an AI is gaining power in pursuit of its goal, and we then notice that its goal is slightly wrong. What does its power-seeking mean for our chances of fixing the goal?
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 60: being corrected or switched off would stop it from reaching the goal it has now, so it has a reason to resist or avoid the correction, for example by hiding the problem. 40: the more power it has already gained, the less able we are to force a correction, so the mistake gets harder to fix over time.
