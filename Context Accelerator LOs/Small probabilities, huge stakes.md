Set out the objection that concern about AI risk is a Pascal's mugging, and the main replies to it, and judge whether a given argument really rests on a tiny probability.

## Requires
- [[Subjective probability]]

## Helpful first
- [[Expected value]]

## Why it matters
People often dismiss AI risk as a tiny chance made to look important by enormous stakes. If you can tell when an argument really leans on a tiny probability and when it doesn't, you can answer this objection fairly, without brushing off serious risks or being swayed by inflated ones.

#### Question: Open
id:: b573e6d5-5262-4e48-b501-19df30f0c4b5
content:: State the Pascal's mugging objection to working on AI risk, and give one reply to it.
assessment-instructions:: Score out of 100. 50: the case rests on a tiny probability multiplied by huge stakes, a pattern that would let any wild claim demand action. 50 for any one reply: many researchers' estimates aren't tiny; a risk of 1% or more is large for a catastrophe; the estimates rest on checkable arguments; uncertainty doesn't make a probability small; harm to people alive now is enough.
