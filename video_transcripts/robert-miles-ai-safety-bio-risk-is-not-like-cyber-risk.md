So, it seems like AI is now superhuman at hacking. Does that mean that the future is going to look like everything getting hacked by AI forever? Probably not, because an AI that's very good at hacking is probably also very good at defending against hacking. Some people think it might even go the other way, with AI enabling us to write formally verified software that has no security vulnerabilities at all. I think reality is going to end up somewhere in the middle.

But okay, AI seems like it might soon get superhuman at bioengineering. Soon it might be the case that somebody with no particular expertise and not that much time or money could design and synthesize a novel pathogen. Does that mean it's just going to be AI-manufactured pandemics forever? Well, a similar thing applies. If AI makes it quick and easy to design new viruses, maybe it also makes it quick and easy to design new vaccines.

The problem is there's a natural asymmetry. An attacker only has to make a small amount of the virus and distribute it to a small number of people, and it will then go on replicating on its own. We, on the other hand, have to manufacture billions of doses of vaccine and distribute them. There's a huge asymmetry in favor of the attacker. Does that mean we're just going to get an endless stream of novel AI-generated pandemics? Unless we can do something about it, maybe yes.
