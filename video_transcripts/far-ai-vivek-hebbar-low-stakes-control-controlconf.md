We are worried that AIs might sabotage  safety research, for example, by withholding their best ideas or by putting subtle bugs in  experiments that cause them to give wrong results. One central thesis of this talk is that this might  be what we call a "low-stakes" threat model. What we mean by low-stakes is that it takes a great  number of actions to cause a catastrophe—when I say a great number, it might just be like  hundreds. But this is in sharp contrast to, say, the original control paper where one  backdoor getting through causes a catastrophe.

Why might this still be a problem? Well, each action might, on its own, look like an innocent mistake. So, even if  you catch many, you may still be in trouble. Low-stakes control requires different techniques, but also different evaluation  methodology than high-stakes control. What we mean is not only that it takes a lot of  actions to cause the catastrophe, but along with that, each action is not very incriminating. It's  difficult to tell them apart from benign mistakes. So, first I'm going to lay out a general way  to factor control strategies into two levels of abstraction. I claim for any control strategy, you  can think of it as having some metrics which give evidence about how safe or performant an output is  or how indicative the output is of malign intent.

These metrics can be things like trusted  monitor scores, untrusted monitor scores, audit scores. There could be other things  like resampling from the untrusted model and seeing how consistent it is between  what it says each time. And then the other level of abstraction  is like, okay, you have these metrics, how do you actually use them? Here,  I see basically three main uses. One is output filtering. And you need to decide which of these strategies to accept as your actual output  that you're going to execute on each task. And so, here you can use these metrics to decide when  to use untrusted and when to use trusted. The second thing is incrimination. So  your metrics might give you evidence of misalignment. And then the third thing you can do is online  training. You can simply train on your metrics. And hopefully, this forces the model's policy to  score highly on the metric. For incrimination, the basic idea is  you want it to be the case that the model cannot cause a catastrophe without  getting caught.
