# CV2 streamlining: summary

What we did to Compute Verification 2 (units 6 to 10) between 22 and 27 November 2026, ahead of the cohorts starting 30 November and 14 December. [[CV2 Streamlining Proposal]] holds the plan and [[CV2 Streamlining Log]] the full record, decision by decision. This page is the short version: what came out of it, where it departed from the plan, and what is left for others.

## Outcome

- **Structure.** Eight modules instead of five: units 6, 7 and 8 have two modules each, units 9 and 10 one each. "Reporting and Protection" is split in two, the institutions page is merged into the red-line page, and the Cankaya close reading and the red team review stay whole.
- **Questions.** 34 questions, 13 graded: the hardware assurance brief, the anchor essay, the red-line, the binding guideline on the institutions page, Cankaya 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9, the taxonomy decomposition and question 10 of the red team review. Every question with a brief gets feedback with the first answer, except red team review questions 1 to 9, which give it on request. Five widgets that carry a page's main exercise are required.
- **Text.** Module and page titles in CV1's form, link labels in plain words, every tutor field rewritten, plain-language flags rewritten by Andreas, American spelling, no em dashes, and the article tables checked.
- **Times.** Units take 133, 181, 221, 192 and 202 minutes of self-study, plus 4 for the overview.
- **Meeting docs.** Five new docs, Meetings 6 to 10, linked from the course file.
- **Tested on staging.** Titles, page order, times, optional segments and grading all show as intended.

## Where we changed course

- **The CIA excerpt stays required** (B6, reversed). The proposal made it optional, but the page's time already assumed the reading.
- **Grading** (B18, B19). The proposal said every graded question keeps its grade, meant as "synthesis questions stay graded". Applied question by question, by CV1's rules, it ungraded 21 questions: the red team review's parts, questions whose answer is on the page, optional questions, and Cankaya 1 and 2, where a grade invites gaming a short answer. Their rubrics now guide the feedback instead.
- **More modules** (B12). Not in the proposal: units 6 to 8 became two modules each, so no module is too long to finish in a sitting.
- **Meeting docs follow the house rules on previews.** No next-unit summary for participants, only the navigator's one sentence.

## For the next course

- **Validation agents are expensive.** A full round of three agents that each re-read the whole unit cost about 400,000 tokens. Meeting 6 took five such rounds. Meetings 7 to 10 cost about 400,000 tokens each, because the agents were given only the reading they needed, their reports had length limits, only the agents whose checks had failed or whose part had changed were re-run, and glossary placement was checked by hand first.
- **Apply a grading rule question by question.** "The synthesis questions stay graded" became "everything stays graded" when it was carried over as a blanket rule.
- **Check the course page itself.** The landing page's text lives outside the vault and drifted from the course.
