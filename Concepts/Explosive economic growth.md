The long-run hyperbolic growth of population and the world economy from a resources -> people -> ideas loop, why it stalled at the demographic transition, and whether AI that can substitute for workers and researchers would restore it and cause explosive, super-exponential growth.

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/1960, The Year The Singularity Was Cancelled]]
Core source: von Foerster's population-technology loop gave hyperbolic growth until 1960; AI could restart it. Builds the model step by step (Garden of Eden, coconut island, worked population tables, doubling-time graphs), explains how the demographic transition broke the loop, and reports Christiano's argument that the Industrial Revolution was no break in trend. The AI turn ('convert money into researchers') comes only in the last paragraphs; long, with digressions on Malthusian and plague history, and Scott notes the on-trend argument rests on heavily transformed graphs.

### [[../Lenses/rationalanimations-everything-might-change-forever-this-century-or-well-go-extinct]]
Clear explainer of hyperbolic growth (gold pile, Kremer, Roodman's 2047), the Duplicator, and AI as copyable workers. Good for first-time learners: explains the people -> ideas -> resources loop, why the demographic transition stopped it, and why copyable AI could restore it. States caveats about unreliable data and physical limits, and hedges that the singularity date should not be taken literally.

### [[../Lenses/The most important century]]
Concise statement that AI automating innovation (PASTA) would restart the resources -> ideas -> resources loop. Clear diagrams of the loop, its stall at the demographic transition, and the AI version; summary depth, with detail in the linked 'Duplicator' post. A course author's note disputes that the loop ever broke.
