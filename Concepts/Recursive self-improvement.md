Theory of agents that modify or build successors: reflective stability and tiling, Vinge's principle, self-improvement as a convergent incentive, non-self-improving designs. Not this: how fast capability grows through AI improving AI (see Takeoff dynamics); protecting one's goals from change (see Goal-content integrity and self-modification).

## Relationships

### [[../video_transcripts/computerphile-ai-self-improvement]]
Tier 1: Plain account of self-improvement as a convergent incentive: whatever your goal, being smarter helps. Good intuition pumps (stamp collector, doctor's degree, a weak chess player writing a program that beats him); informal and speculative by the speaker's own admission.

### [[../Lenses/Recursion Magic]]
Tier 1: Historical case of a self-modifying system: EURISKO's heuristics that modified heuristics, metaheuristics included. Contrasts it with optimizing compilers and notes that representing heuristics so they could modify themselves without breaking took most of Lenat's effort; dated 1980s case in a 2008 FOOM-debate essay.

### [[../video_transcripts/machineintelligenceresearchinstitute-eliezer-yudkowsky-ai-alignment-why-its-hard-and-where-to-start]]
Tier 2: Full talk covers stable self-modification: the Gandhi pill, Vingean reflection, tiling agents, the Löbian obstacle. Several paragraphs, but the 2016 MIRI technical results are only gestured at.
