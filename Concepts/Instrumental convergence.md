## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Shut It All Down - Yudkowsky TED Talk]]
Brief: killing humans is 'convergently implied' by almost any never-saturating goal, e.g. to stop rival superintelligences. Two or three sentences in the Q&A; it names the idea without developing it.

### [[../video_transcripts/machineintelligenceresearchinstitute-eliezer-yudkowsky-ai-alignment-why-its-hard-and-where-to-start]]
Full talk's Toronto-or-Tokyo argument: paperclip and diamond maximizers share resource-seeking first steps. It stresses that convergent strategies are not new goals, and shows the broom building sub-agents to keep the cauldron full.

### [[../Lenses/Without fundamental advances (trimmed, Extinction Risk Foundations)]]
Applies instrumental convergence: power helps most goals, and humans are both competitors and a threat. One section plus the 'ambitiousness' objection and reply (bounded goals can't be ensured); the standard argument, briefly put.

### [[../Lenses/Pythia]]
Claims all convergent instrumental goals express one convergent goal, power seeking, citing Turner's results. A short paragraph in an essay built on Nick Land's 'Pythia'; provocative framing, weak as an explanation.

### [[../Lenses/The AI Book That's Freaking Out National Security Advisors]]
Short, clear explanation by analogy: a driver can go anywhere, but most need to stop for gas. Names the idea (2012), notes it needs no consciousness or malice, and shows it in the story as Sable wanting more GPUs and time.

### [[../Lenses/10 reasons]]
Reason four in brief: self-preservation follows from almost any goal ('you can't fetch the coffee if you're dead'). A one-paragraph refresher that points to Miles's fuller video; enough to answer 'just don't give it bad goals'.

### [[../Lenses/Deadly By Default]]
Section 7: competent agents avoid strategic dead ends such as shutdown, seek resources and resist goal changes. Clear cancer-research example; informal, with no formal results, but good for first exposure.

### [[../Lenses/Risks from power-seeking AI]]
Clear account of self-preservation, goal guarding and power-seeking as goals useful for almost any long-term aim. Weighs counter-arguments (Ngo's point that only some goals benefit from power) and mixed evidence: METR sees cheating but no clear long-term power-seeking.

### [[../Lenses/Wikipedia Existential Risk]]
Defines instrumental goals and convergence, with Russell's 'you can't fetch the coffee if you're dead'. One concise paragraph; the anthropomorphism section adds that power-seeking arises instrumentally, not from emotion.

### [[../articles/janus-simulators]]
Full text argues prediction training, unlike reward on outcomes, gives no incentive for instrumental convergence. It lists what an agent that 'values predicting text' would do (shape its prompts, self-improve, resist shutdown) and argues GPT's outputs are never judged by their consequences. A useful counter-case for teaching the limits of the convergence argument; not in the course excerpt.
