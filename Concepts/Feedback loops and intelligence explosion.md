Returns on reinvesting cognitive output into improvement, and whether feedback loops produce an intelligence explosion. Not this: software-only version (see Software intelligence explosions); speed and smoothness (see Takeoff speed and discontinuity); the economic growth loop and explosive growth (see Explosive economic growth).

## Relationships

### [[../articles/yudkowsky-optimization-and-the-singularity]]
Tier 1: Full post's core argument: a recursive self-improver breaks the 'protected meta-level' that bounded all past optimization. History so far is optimization power in vs optimized product out, with rare meta-level wins (sex, science); a fully wraparound AI 'folds the graph in on itself'. Yudkowsky calls this analogic reasoning; written as a down payment in his debate with Robin Hanson.

### [[../Lenses/AI for AI safety]]
Tier 2: Diagrams the AI capability feedback loop (automated AI R&D, 'intelligence explosion') against an AI safety feedback loop. Section 2.1 uses a single-actor toy model to show the capability loop pushing the frontier past the 'safety range', and the safety loop needing to outpace or restrain it. Good framing of the race; no evidence on how fast the capability loop runs.

### [[../articles/good-speculations-concerning-first-ultraintelligent-machine]]
Tier 1: Defining source: Good's 1964 coinage of the 'intelligence explosion', a machine that designs better machines. The course uses only the famous three-sentence passage, which states the loop and the 'docile enough' caveat but gives no account of returns or limits. The rest of Section 2 adds that a machine could gradually improve itself and puts its value at a 'megakeynes'; most of the paper is a 1960s neural 'subassembly' theory of meaning and recall.

### [[../Lenses/1960, The Year The Singularity Was Cancelled]]
Tier 1: Worked non-AI case of a loop that reaches 'infinity in finite time' until a bottleneck (population) breaks it. Good for teaching that a loop needs every link to keep scaling ('tractors can't invent things'); Section IV says AI could close the loop again, but briefly and in economic, not intelligence, terms.

### [[../Lenses/What are the differences between a singularity, an intelligence explosion, and a hard takeoff]]
Tier 1: One-line definition of Good's 'intelligence explosion', and its separation from hard takeoff. Its value is the distinction: hard takeoff need not involve recursive self-improvement, and an intelligence explosion can be gradual (Christiano expects one despite a slow takeoff).

### [[../video_transcripts/computerphile-ai-self-improvement]]
Tier 1: Clear spoken account of the recursive loop: a smarter system is better at AI design, so it improves itself further. Adds that a software-only change could be very fast (within a day, or a second) and asks whether the process is subcritical or supercritical; informal, with no evidence on actual returns.

### [[../Lenses/Cascades and Cycles]]
Tier 1: Key source for cycles: output piped back into input, with the Fermi pile's k = 1.0006 as the worked example. Vivid narrative of the first critical reaction; asks what an AI's 'neutron multiplication factor' would be, while noting self-improvements, unlike neutrons, are each new, so the analogy is inexact and left 'as an exercise'.

### [[../Lenses/Recursion Magic]]
Tier 1: Condition for positive feedback: improvements must improve the ability to improve ('a mouse isn't recursive enough'). Worked k < 1 cases (a self-applied optimizing compiler, Engelbart's tools) plus a five-level causal tree where changes nearer the root cascade further; 2008 blog style, sketchy by its own admission, assumes the previous post.

### [[../Lenses/Wikipedia Existential Risk]]
Tier 2: Irving Good's 1965 intelligence-explosion passage, Bostrom's version, Hanson's objection, and fast versus slow takeoff. Good primary quotation; the argument itself is summarised in a few sentences.

### [[../Lenses/byrnes-foom-doom-1-brain-in-a-box-in-a-basement]]
Tier 2: Argues sharp takeoff needs no feedback loop: low recalcitrance alone suffices, and self-improvement only clips the end. Uses Bostrom's optimization-power/recalcitrance formula and contrasts his path with AI-2027 and with Yudkowsky's emphasis on AI R&D. A useful dissent; a few paragraphs (section 1.8.2).
