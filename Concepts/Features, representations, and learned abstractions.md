## Relationships

### [[../articles/sarah+bluedot-introduction-to-mechanistic-interpretability]]
Plain-language account of features, circuits and the universality hypothesis, following the 2020 'Zoom In' paper. Worked example: curves and fur combine into a dog ear, then a dog head. Notes that there is no agreed definition of features or circuits and not all researchers accept the framing. Introductory only.

### [[../Lenses/sanderson-but-what-is-a-neural-network]]
Intuitive picture of layered features (edges, then loops and lines, then digits), offered explicitly as a hope. Good gentle introduction to hierarchical representations; the lesson says whether the trained network really works this way is 'another question', to revisit after training.

### [[../Lenses/ericjmichaud-on-neural-scaling-and-the-quanta-hypothesis]]
Proposes networks are sets of discrete 'quanta' of skill or knowledge, learned in order of how often they are used. Gradient clustering on pythia-70m finds skills like counting and predicting newlines in line-length-limited text; 'polygenic' tokens complicate the picture. A hypothesis, and the author worries it may be adding epicycles.
