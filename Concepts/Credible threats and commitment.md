Threats as a strategy distinct from outright attack, made credible by binding oneself to a costly response (burning bridges, rigid self-modification), and why such commitment is far easier for AI than for humans.

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Credible Threats]]
Introduces commitment as bargaining power (Schelling), with Cortes burning his ships as the worked example. Clear and non-technical: removing one's own options makes a threat credible and can make the other side concede; a union strike shows the everyday case. The lens's closing frame adds that an AI could commit instantly by rewriting its own code; that AI link is the lens author's, asserted not argued.

### [[../articles/houba+bolt-credible-threats-in-negotiations]]
Full chapter: formal game theory of optimal threats in Nash's variable-threat game, with and without commitment. Treats alternating offers and subgame-perfect equilibria with theorems and examples; graduate-level, with nothing on AI. Only for designers who want the formal basis for the intuitive point.
