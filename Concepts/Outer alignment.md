## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/johnswentworth-why-agent-foundations-an-overly-abstract-explanation]]
Subproblem 1: training on human good/bad labels selects for what looks good to humans, not what is good. Clear because it starts from a deliberately naive, RLHF-like scheme; suggests a 'pointer' to human values as a substitute target.

### [[../articles/russell-of-myths-and-moonshine]]
Classic statement: an objective over k<n variables pushes the rest to extremes: what you ask for, not what you want. The course excerpt is this one paragraph, with the genie / sorcerer's apprentice / King Midas framing; crisp and quotable, but an assertion with no worked example. The full 2014 Edge piece adds that the utility function may not match hard-to-pin-down human values.

### [[../video_transcripts/machineintelligenceresearchinstitute-eliezer-yudkowsky-ai-alignment-why-its-hard-and-where-to-start]]
Full talk's cauldron robot: a 0/1 utility matches ours only while the robot has few options. A clear, gentle example of a narrow objective that stops pinpointing our real optimum once more options open up.

### [[../Lenses/Wikipedia Existential Risk]]
'Difficulty of specifying goals': we can write utility functions for clicks, not for human flourishing. Adds that a function expressing some values tramples the rest; brief and abstract.
