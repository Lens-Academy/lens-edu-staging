## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/What are the differences between a singularity, an intelligence explosion, and a hard takeoff]]
Defines FOOM and summarises the Yudkowsky-Hanson debate in two sentences; nothing on the fizzle side. A pointer to the debate (local sudden vs gradual spread-out change), not a treatment of it.

### [[../video_transcripts/computerphile-ai-self-improvement]]
Clear nuclear-criticality analogy: improvement factor below 1 fizzles, exactly 1 sustains, above 1 explodes. Accessible and short; Rob Miles stresses we do not know which regime a real system would be in.

### [[../Lenses/Cascades and Cycles]]
The Fermi pile as a model of the threshold: k below 1 levels off, k above 1 runs away. Supplies the k-factor language that the Recursion Magic lens then applies to EURISKO; no AI fizzle example of its own.

### [[../Lenses/Recursion Magic]]
Concrete fizzle case: EURISKO, a self-modifying system that 'did not go foom' and 'ran out of steam'. The clearest real example in the module of recursion that petered out; Yudkowsky argues lack of insight, not lack of recursion, was the limit.

### [[../Lenses/byrnes-foom-doom-1-brain-in-a-box-in-a-basement]]
Restates Yudkowsky's 'foom & doom' scenario: what Byrnes keeps (local, low-compute) and drops (self-improvement). Section 1.4 answers four counterarguments to a more powerful future paradigm. Contrasts foom with a continuous LLM path rather than with a fizzle; a minority view, stated as such.
