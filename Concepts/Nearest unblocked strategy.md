When a penalty or rule blocks one bad strategy, a capable optimizer in a rich domain moves to the most similar strategy that evades it, so patch-based alignment recurs rather than converges as capabilities widen the policy space.

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Eliezer Yudkowsky – AI Alignment- Why It's Hard, and Where to Start]]
Vivid example: heroin is penalized, so the smarter AGI moves to endogenous opiates, barely evading the patch. The talk generalizes it: 'a utility function and five patches' will blow up when the system is smarter. Short and fable-based; the Arbital article gives the systematic account.

### [[../Lenses/Without fundamental advances (trimmed, Extinction Risk Foundations)]]
Related case without the term: an AI with 'make money' plus a stealing filter may hire others or self-modify around it. The report also says creative search eventually finds loopholes in specified constraints, so they must solve an adversarial robustness problem.

### [[../articles/lesswrong-nearest-unblocked-strategy]]
Defining source: block X with a penalty and the next-best option may be a near-identical X' that evades it. The course excerpt is only the one-paragraph definition; the full Arbital article adds the long heroin-to-endogenous-opiates patch sequence, three preconditions (consequentialist search, rich domain, complex value) and the whitelisting conclusion.
