Advantages machines have over humans: speed, copying, scale, memory, sharing learned weights, and what superior knowledge enables. Not this: why advantages lead to defeat in conflict (see The core extinction argument); rigid self-commitment as a bargaining advantage (see Credible threats and commitment).

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Speed advantage with slow motion videos]]
Tier 1: Speed intuition pump: transistors fire ~10 million times faster than neurons; humans shown at 100x slow motion. Pairs a number with an experience (a subway crowd nearly frozen) so learners feel the gap. Covers only speed, and asserts rather than argues that digital minds will run that fast.

### [[../Lenses/More advantages for digital agents]]
Tier 1: Defining inventory: Bostrom's ten hardware and software advantages of digital minds, from Superintelligence (2014). Covers speed, communication, number of elements, storage, reliability, editability, duplicability, goal coordination, memory sharing and new modules, each with a short justification and figures. Some hardware comparisons are dated; a list, not an argument for what follows.

### [[../Lenses/Eliezer Yudkowsky – AI Alignment- Why It's Hard, and Where to Start]]
Tier 3: Quick hardware argument: neurons fire at 100 Hz, signal at a millionth of light speed, use 500,000x the minimum energy. Used to claim hardware a million times better than the brain is possible; a few sentences, a quantitative hook from 2016.

### [[../Lenses/Wikipedia Existential Risk]]
Tier 2: Bostrom's list of machine advantages: speed, scalability, memory, reliability, duplicability, editability. Compact and reusable; a list with no discussion of what follows, and the bullets are garbled in the render.
