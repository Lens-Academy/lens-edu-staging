Harm from AI that does not hate humans but simply does not value them, so humans are lost as a side effect.

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Speed advantage with slow motion videos]]
Tier 2: One vivid paragraph: to fast AI, near-stationary humans could be taken apart for biofuel or carbon atoms. Draws the analogy to human civilization's disregard for plants, wildlife and insects. Imagery that links the speed gap to indifference-driven harm; no argument for why AI goals would ignore humans.

### [[../Lenses/Shut It All Down - Yudkowsky TED Talk]]
Tier 2: Q&A answer: an AI wanting endless 'molecular squiggles' could kill us as a side effect, not from hatred. Short but vivid; suitable as an intro example.

### [[../Lenses/The AI Book That's Freaking Out National Security Advisors]]
Tier 2: Soares's correction: the danger is indifference, not ruthlessness, as when people pave over an anthill without noticing. Brief but quotable; it corrects the presenter's (and a learner's) assumption that the argument needs an AI that 'goes hard'.

### [[../Lenses/Deadly By Default]]
Tier 2: Section 9, a full treatment: indifference looks like hostility, as when builders clear a forest full of squirrels. Argues there is no reason to assume a superintelligence will care about us; answers 'why would it want to kill us?'
