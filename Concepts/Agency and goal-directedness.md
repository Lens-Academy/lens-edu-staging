## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Without fundamental advances (trimmed, Extinction Risk Foundations)]]
Argues that capability for hard, novel, outcome-specified tasks implies approximate consequentialism (Section 2). An argument from task demands, not coherence theorems; it allows non-consequentialist constraints and denies needing optimal goal-directedness, but treats constraints as fragile.

### [[../Lenses/Discovering when an agent is present in a system]]
Defining source: agents are systems that would adapt their policy if their actions affected the world differently. Explains mechanised causal graphs with a mouse-and-cheese example and sketches algorithms to discover agents from interventions. A DeepMind blog summary of a technical paper; diagrams carry much of the content.

### [[../Lenses/Pythia]]
Frames agency as time travel (Garrabrant): agents let predicted futures shape present actions. One quote and a paragraph; the step from prediction to agency is asserted, and the excerpt never explains how an oracle or predictor gains real-world control. Literary style.

### [[../articles/janus-simulators]]
Full text argues GPT is not an agent: the policy and the simulated agent come apart, and agency belongs to simulacra. The 'Agentic GPT' and 'Roleplay sans player' sections make the case and reject 'an agent that wants to roleplay'; the oracle/genie/tool comparisons are in the same part. Good for questioning the assumption that capable AI must be an agent; not in the course excerpt.

### [[../Lenses/Janus' Simulators - Scott Alexander]]
Uses the agent/genie/oracle taxonomy to argue GPT is genuinely not an agent, even though it can simulate one. The 'call the Oxford librarian' example shows GPT not pursuing its prediction 'goal' by agentic means. Clear and short.
