## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/AI for AI safety]]
Names automated alignment research as the most important case of AI for AI safety, but the excerpt only previews objections. The introduction lists evaluation failures, differential sabotage and dangerous rogue options in one sentence and defers the detail to the next essay. Pair it with a source that argues the case.

### [[../articles/carlsmith-ai-for-ai-safety]]
Full essay argues automated alignment research is urgent and puts a large burden of proof on restraint-only strategies. Section 2.2 contrasts it with views that need radical human-labor-driven alignment progress first. Section 5 lists three core objections and practical worries (uneven capability arrival, too little time and investment), deferring the analysis to the next essay. An advocate's pitch.

### [[../Lenses/bowkis-automated-alignment-is-harder-than-you-think]]
Core argument: automated alignment research can yield confident but wrong safety assessments, even without scheming AIs. The abstract blames hard-to-supervise 'fuzzy' tasks and gives four reasons AI errors are harder to catch: optimisation pressure, alien mistakes, unevaluable arguments, correlated outputs. Compact and recent, but only the abstract: claims stated, not argued.

### [[../articles/bowkis-automated-alignment-is-harder-than-you-think]]
Full paper models a bootstrapped automated alignment program and its 'undetected systematic errors' failure mode. It separates output-level errors from aggregation-level errors (mis-modelled correlation between papers), with concrete examples such as an agent naming an eval scheming_test.py. Ends with a research agenda. Rigorous but long and technical.

### [[../Lenses/johnswentworth-why-not-just-outsource-alignment-research-to-an-ai]]
Vivid argument against outsourcing alignment to AI: we would be an ignorant Client who cannot tell good work from bad. The sketch 'The Expert' carries the point, and the excerpt says fancier schemes (prompting, RLHF, HCH, debate) all ignore this bottleneck. Memorable and accessible, but a short opinion piece argued by analogy.

### [[../articles/johnswentworth-why-not-just-outsource-alignment-research-to-an-ai]]
Full post adds why the Client problem is worse: a Client who cannot see failure cannot fix it by iteration. It argues a non-expert cannot pick an expert simulacrum or reward expert work under RLHF, treats better user interfaces as a partial fix, and ends by advising readers to build their own alignment expertise. Informal LessWrong style.

### [[../Lenses/The Case Against AI Control Research]]
Median doom path: early AI produces slop that looks fine, and a lab that cannot verify it thinks alignment is solved. Variants (safetywashing, sycophancy, self-red-teaming that only sounds good, mis-measured checks) and the 'baby peacock' image make the verification problem concrete; opinionated.

### [[../Lenses/byrnes-foom-doom-1-brain-in-a-box-in-a-basement]]
Argues AI-assisted alignment research is 'pretty doomed': AI able to help would find inventing ASI much easier. Section 1.8.5 adds this to the usual chicken-and-egg objection; brief, specific to the next-paradigm scenario, and he still welcomes LLMs as ordinary research tools.
