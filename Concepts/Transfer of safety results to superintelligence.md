Whether alignment and control methods, and evidence from current or roughly human-level AI, will hold for much stronger systems, and what safety strategy should differ between them; this is sometimes called asymptotic alignment, although strictly it is one subquestion within it. Not this: capabilities outgeneralizing alignment as a named argument (see Sharp left turn).

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Interpretability Will Not Reliably Find Deceptive AI]]
Tier 2: Argues no method gives high reliability against superintelligence, but methods for pre-superintelligent AI still matter. A short section linking this to the key insight of the AI control agenda: early transformative systems are easier to evaluate and control. Also lists three options: don't build ASI, use automated researchers, or roll the dice.

### [[../articles/greenblatt+buck-the-case-for-ensuring-that-powerful-ais-are-controlled]]
Tier 2: The control side's own concession: control likely fails for AIs qualitatively wildly superhuman in problematic domains. Useful with Wentworth's critique, since it shows control targets early transformative AI, not superintelligence; developed in a main section and an appendix.

### [[../Lenses/The Case Against AI Control Research]]
Tier 1: Two-stage model: early transformative AI must actually solve superintelligence alignment; not killing us is not enough. Argues prosaic and control methods will not generalize to superintelligence, and extends this to a chain of AI generations. Simplified and opinionated, but clear.

### [[../Lenses/byrnes-sympathy-for-both-sides-of-the-egregious-misalignment-debate]]
Tier 1: Central argument: alignment works for LLMs, yet ASI would be egregiously misaligned, because LLMs won't scale to ASI. Good for discussing whether early success transfers. Short and openly opinionated ('my brief idiosyncratic take', caricatured positions); key arguments sit behind links to Byrnes's Foom & Doom posts.

### [[../Lenses/Eliezer Yudkowsky – AI Alignment- Why It's Hard, and Where to Start]]
Tier 2: 'Context disaster': tests on a weaker AI give no guarantee once it is smarter (balls from a different barrel). One paragraph, plus the later claim that a design should be safe even if the AI 'suddenly becomes God'. A clear intuition pump for why early success may not transfer; no empirical detail.

### [[../Lenses/alexander-nicholas-decker-in-hell]]
Tier 1: Parable against 'fix problems as they arise': demons who beat a weak slave into line plan to rule a million strong ones. Aimed at Decker's aviation-safety analogy; it argues by intuition pump ('would this work on you?') rather than technical detail. Satirical and memorable.

### [[../Lenses/Sharp left turn]]
Tier 2: Argues alignment results in pre-turn systems (moral answers, game shutdown) give false security and won't transfer. Made through the OpenMind story; brief, and openly asserted rather than defended.

### [[../Lenses/Wikipedia Existential Risk]]
Tier 2: Why aligning superintelligence may be harder: riskier iteration, radical solutions (electrode smiles), deception. Gives the counter-view that a superintelligence might understand values better, and notes OpenAI's dissolved Superalignment team.
