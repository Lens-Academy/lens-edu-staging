The engineering habit of resting safety on few, checked assumptions rather than parrying imagined attacks (ordinary paranoia), when worst-case assumptions about a system are warranted, and the claim that AGI alignment needs this habit.

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Worst-Case Thinking]]
Defining source on when worst-case thinking is warranted: Buck Shlegeris separates six reasons to assume the worst. Sharp and practical: says how to answer 'that's far-fetched' under each reason, and agrees with Yudkowsky only where you are optimized against. Uses alignment vocabulary (SGD, mesa-optimizer) throughout.

### [[../Lenses/Security Mindset and Ordinary Paranoia]]
Defining source: Yudkowsky's dialogue splitting security mindset into 'ordinary paranoia' and deeper assumption-hunting. Rich worked examples: hashing versus hiding password files, donotreply.com, Fast XOR, the OpenBSD attitude to crashes, Mad-Eye Moody as the shallow paranoiac. Long, idiosyncratic and mostly about computer security; the AGI link is a few author asides.
