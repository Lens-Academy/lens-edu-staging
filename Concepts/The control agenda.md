The case for and against AI control as a research agenda: alignment claims versus control claims, trusted versus untrusted models, main criticisms, and ways control could backfire. Not this: specific techniques (see Control protocols); what to do once control stops working (see Post-control plans).

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/The case for ensuring that powerful AIs are controlled]]
Tier 1: Founding pitch by Greenblatt and Shlegeris: control (safe even if models scheme) as a second defence beside alignment. Defines the 'control property' and uses the security analogy of staying robust to compromised employees. The excerpt is a small fraction of a very long post, so the case for feasibility is in the full text.

### [[../articles/greenblatt+buck-the-case-for-ensuring-that-powerful-ais-are-controlled]]
Tier 1: Full argument that early transformatively useful AI will be very dangerous yet controllable, with proposed actions. Covers why qualitatively wildly superhuman skills in problematic domains (hacking, persuasion) are probably avoidable, why iteration helps, and recommendations; an appendix asks whether takeover-concerned people should invest in control.

### [[../Lenses/AI Control May Increase Existential Risk]]
Tier 1: Short critique: control may raise x-risk by turning moderate warning shots into ineffective ones or hidden near-misses. Kulveit argues civilizational resistance would stop most such AIs anyway, and labs would sanitise incidents control caught. Nearly the whole post; argued by heuristic, and it lists counter-arguments without answering them.

### [[../Lenses/The Case Against AI Control Research]]
Tier 1: Main critique: scheming by early transformative AI is a small slice of x-risk, so control research barely matters. Wentworth defines control research by two features (only scheming, only early transformative AI) and answers Buck's pitch directly. Nearly the whole post; pairs with Greenblatt and Shlegeris.

### [[../Lenses/Without fundamental advances (trimmed, Extinction Risk Foundations)]]
Tier 2: Sceptical case: control of misaligned powerful AIs is very difficult, with transparency the key missing piece. Section 5 is trimmed to the pillars, non-cooperation, sabotage and transparency; useful to set against pro-control sources.
