How fast takeoff is and whether it is continuous or has sudden jumps, including compute or hardware overhangs that make progress jump when a constraint lifts. Not this: mechanisms of acceleration (see Feedback loops and intelligence explosion).

## Relationships

### [[../articles/yudkowsky-optimization-and-the-singularity]]
Tier 2: Full post's last section rejects predicting one economic doubling time for whatever the next big invention is. It contrasts 5-IQ-point drugs (doubling time maybe 7 years) with a recursive AI not bound to one-month doublings. Brief and dated; useful as an early primary source for Yudkowsky's side against Hanson, not a full argument.

### [[../Lenses/Shut It All Down - Pause For Thought]]
Tier 2: Compute-overhang argument: a pause slows algorithms more than hardware, so progress jumps when it ends. One clear paragraph on why gradual progress is held safer; the point recurs in the Belrose summary (a forced lift raises fast-takeoff risk).

### [[../Lenses/What are the differences between a singularity, an intelligence explosion, and a hard takeoff]]
Tier 1: Glossary definitions of hard vs soft, fast vs slow, and discontinuous vs continuous takeoff. Useful for vocabulary; notes that a continuous takeoff can still become very fast (Christiano's 'slow takeoff'). Definitions only, with links for depth; argues nothing.

### [[../Lenses/Cascades and Cycles]]
Tier 1: Argues cascades and cycles yield apparent discontinuities even under steady optimization (chimps to humans, Fermi). Fits the module's 'feedback cycles and discontinuity' outcome; part of Yudkowsky's 2008 FOOM-debate case, with a speculative evolutionary story he admits is hard to verify.

### [[../articles/yudkowsky-cascades-cycles-insight]]
Tier 1: The full post adds 'insight' as a source of discontinuity: knowledge that cheapens a whole class of problems at once. Ends with the claim that AIs able to do AI theory mark 'a watershed moment in the FOOM'; part of the Hanson-Yudkowsky debate.

### [[../articles/recursion-magic]]
Tier 1: Adds 'magic' as the last source of discontinuity, closing the list: cascades, cycles, insight, recursion, magic. Useful only together with 'Cascades, Cycles, Insight', since the two lenses split the list; argumentative FOOM-debate post.

### [[../Lenses/Sharp left turn]]
Tier 2: One paragraph gives a discontinuity view (humans kinked capability curves); the 'turn' itself is about generalization. Soares declines to argue further, and the full article's footnote 4 says the turn need not involve recursive self-improvement, so don't use it as a takeoff-speed source.

### [[../Lenses/byrnes-foom-doom-1-brain-in-a-box-in-a-basement]]
Tier 1: Argues for an extremely sharp, local takeoff: 0-2 years, maybe one training run, from 'seemingly irrelevant' to ASI. Reasons: maybe 0-30 person-years of R&D, no imitation-learning ceiling, the AlphaZero analogy; answers Christiano-style objections ('what's the rate-limiter?'). Openly a minority view (he puts >95% of the field on the other side); argued, not modelled.
