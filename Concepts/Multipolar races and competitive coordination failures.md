Moloch and multipolar traps: competition among many actors leading to outcomes nobody wants. Not this: racing between AI labs specifically (see Race dynamics and governing under pressure).

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Pythia]]
Tier 2: One paragraph: in a multipolar world, agents that invest less in power are outcompeted, so other values are lost. Ties Moloch-style selection to power seeking and adds that the same pressure acts on subagents inside a singleton; pairs well with Meditations on Moloch.

### [[../Lenses/Meditations on Moloch]]
Tier 1: Defining source: Alexander's Moloch, where competition rewards sacrificing other values until all are lost. The lens keeps 2 of the essay's 14 traps (Malthusian rats, arms races), the general principle, four restraining forces and why technology erodes them. Long, literary and poetic.

### [[../Lenses/The Goddess Of Everything Else]]
Tier 1: Verse fable in which competition ('KILL CONSUME MULTIPLY CONQUER') is repeatedly turned into cooperation. Scott Alexander traces cells, packs, tribes and civilisations, with cancer and war as relapses; a hopeful counterpoint to Moloch-style traps. Poetry, not analysis: no mechanism, Moloch is never named and AI is never mentioned.
