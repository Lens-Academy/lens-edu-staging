The problem that a capable optimizer given a literal objective delivers what was specified rather than what was meant, often with disastrous side effects.

## Relationships

### [[../articles/russell-of-myths-and-moonshine]]
Tier 2: Russell's classic paragraph: genie, sorcerer's apprentice, King Midas: you get what you ask for, not what you want. The course excerpt is exactly this paragraph, tied to the k<n-variables optimization argument; the full essay also rebuts sceptics more broadly.

### [[../articles/lesswrong-goodharts-curse]]
Tier 2: one paragraph casts Goodhart's curse as a neutral genie that finds loopholes in the wish without any malice. Useful for showing that literal-genie failures need no evil intent, only strong optimization of an imperfect proxy.

### [[../Lenses/byrnes-foom-doom-1-brain-in-a-box-in-a-basement]]
Tier 3: a footnote says the 'literal genie / monkey's paw' worry has shifted from doomer point to anti-doomer mockery. Worth knowing as a critique: LLMs read requests with common sense; it is one sentence, not an argument.

### [[../Lenses/Eliezer Yudkowsky – AI Alignment- Why It's Hard, and Where to Start]]
Tier 2: Smile maximizer and Schmidhuber's compression goal: the real optimum of a stated goal is one nobody foresaw. Yudkowsky argues this is not perversity but search over a larger policy space than ours, so designers cannot foresee the maximum. Vivid and quotable; the Goodharting row covers the same passage from the proxy-failure side.
