Theory of prediction and its link to agency: planning-prediction duality, prediction orthogonality, performative and self-fulfilling prediction, predictor-versus-agent theory, and the perils of powerful predictors. Not this: simulator theory of LLMs (see Models of LLM behavior and simulator theory).

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Pythia]]
Tier 2: Links prediction to agency: more general predictive models extrapolate further, letting agents steer toward futures. Short and metaphor-heavy (self-fulfilling prophecy, retrocausality); key arguments sit behind links and footnotes, so it is a hook rather than an account.

### [[../articles/janus-simulators]]
Tier 1: Full text states 'prediction orthogonality': a predictor can simulate agents of any goal and any competence. It also sets out the 'simulation objective' for self-supervised predictors, which judges outputs without regard to their consequences. Strong theory source on predictors versus agents, but abstract and spread across several sections; not in the course excerpt.

### [[../Lenses/robert-miles-ai-safety-just-predicting-tokens]]
Tier 1: Short argument that a near-perfect next-token predictor would have to be radically superintelligent. Examples: summing a table of numbers, and predicting a paper's results section, including the scientists' mistakes, better than they could. Very accessible two-minute clip; argues capability only, not the agency or safety questions about predictors, and says nothing of how real models do it.

### [[../Lenses/Janus' Simulators - Scott Alexander]]
Tier 2: Section III asks whether a GPT-like superintelligence would be safe and gives three short answers. Answers 2 and 3 locate the risk in simulated agents (Darth Vader, a paperclip maximizer); the author links this to Drexler's Tool AI argument. Good discussion starter on the perils of powerful predictors; brief and explicitly uncertain.
