## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/The power of intelligence]]
Answers 'intelligence is no match for a gun', 'where will AI get money?' and 'it needs a robot body'. Each reply is a line or two pointing out that guns and money were themselves made by intelligence. Good as a model reply or discussion prompt; the tone is sarcastic and it does not engage the strongest forms of the objections.

### [[../articles/russell-of-myths-and-moonshine]]
Full text rebuts AI-risk sceptics: the concern is decision quality, not 'spooky emergent consciousness'. Only the full Edge response does this, not the course excerpt. It also says a more-than-infinitesimal possibility needs no proof, and calls for 'provably aligned' AI; short, conversational and dated (2014).

### [[../Lenses/alexander-nicholas-decker-in-hell]]
Models a reply to 'alignment is like aviation safety' and pre-empts the 'don't anthropomorphize' counter. Gives a reusable test ('would this work on you?') and concludes AI sits between airplanes and humans; the tone is mocking, so it may not suit calibrated outreach.

### [[../Lenses/10 reasons]]
Defining source: Stuart Russell's ten common objections to AI safety, each answered with an analogy (Miles's take). Covers 'AGI is impossible', 'too soon', 'Mars overpopulation', 'just don't add bad goals', 'just turn it off'. Clear and funny (bus to the cliff, Mars airlock, seat belts not car bans), but some rebuttals are one line; dated (2019).

### [[../Lenses/Deadly By Default]]
Each section ends by answering a named objection, e.g. 'we'll just stop before it's dangerous', 'program it to obey'. A bank of objection-answer pairs tied to the step of the argument they target, always from one side.

### [[../Lenses/Risks from power-seeking AI]]
Long section answering about ten objections, from 'AIs will just be tools' to 'just unplug it' and 'just sandbox it'. Fair-minded: states each objection at its strongest and concedes uncertainty; a good model of calibrated advocacy. Callouts are collapsed.

### [[../Lenses/Wikipedia Existential Risk]]
Lists sceptic positions (Ng's Mars overpopulation, Pinker, LeCun, AI-ethics critics) and the anthropomorphism debate. States objections fairly but mostly without rebuttal, so it pairs well with a source that answers them.

### [[../Lenses/robert-miles-ai-safety-just-predicting-tokens]]
Ready-made rebuttal of 'it just predicts the next token': the task places no limit on the system's abilities. Useful for teaching how to answer a common objection; it concedes that real models are imperfect predictors and gives no evidence about what they can actually do.

### [[../Lenses/The World Keeps Getting Saved]]
Answers 'alignment is overblown, chatbots refuse bomb recipes': that safety is itself a human achievement. One paragraph applies the Y2K/ozone pattern to AI; the last takeaway tells safety workers to say aloud what risk they averted, or funding and support may dry up. An analogy, not evidence about how hard alignment is.
