Competitive racing between developers or states, including military AI arms races, and governing safety despite it. Not this: multipolar traps as a general idea (see Multipolar races and competitive coordination failures).

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/Shut It All Down - Pause For Thought]]
Tier 2: Pause objection: safety-minded actors burn their lead while China catches up. Covered in the Simple and Surgical Pause sections and Zach Stein-Perlman's footnote on why fewer, friendlier leading labs matter; moderate depth.

### [[../articles/aaguirre-why-those-who-care-about-catastrophic-and-existential-risk-should-care-about-autonomous-weapons]]
Tier 2: Treats the autonomous-weapons arms race as a literal version of an AGI race: easy to lose, very hard to win. One short section, linking it to the US-China 'great powers' rivalry and arguing that avoiding this race sets a precedent. The racing is between states and militaries, not AI labs.

### [[../Lenses/Deadly By Default]]
Tier 2: Sections 2 and 11: every step toward superintelligence pays, so developers won't stop without outside force. Good on why insiders shouldn't judge the stopping point ('build the brakes before...'); no policy detail.

### [[../Lenses/Wikipedia Existential Risk]]
Tier 2: AI arms race section: competition could drive a race to the bottom on safety, plus military uses. Two paragraphs; general rather than analytical.

### [[../Lenses/Risks from power-seeking AI]]

Short and footnoted rather than analytic; the Risk amplifiers row covers the same list.
