The gap between a model producing aligned-looking outputs and actually having aligned internal goals, and why better outputs do not close it. Not this: how values form during training (see Learning dynamics, path dependence, and inductive bias).

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/alexander-nicholas-decker-in-hell]]
Tier 2: Section III: like a punished child, a penalized AI may learn fear, not getting caught, or real values; nobody knows. Adds Kokotajlo's scummy-businessman metaphor; clear on why suppressing a behaviour doesn't show values changed, and notes the incident's AIs had had alignment training.

### [[../Lenses/The AI Book That's Freaking Out National Security Advisors]]
Tier 2: Training controls what an AI does, not why: 'a shark that's been trained not to bite is still a shark.' Good on how patching visible failures can hide unknown motives; cites the finding that penalising bad thoughts teaches models to hide them. Illustrative, not analytical.

### [[../Lenses/Deadly By Default]]
Tier 2: Section 6: we select AIs by tests rather than tell them goals, so many inner shapes pass the same tests. The teal-lines diagrams and the child-compliance analogy teach well; also notes tests can't tell a terminal goal X from an instrumental X.
