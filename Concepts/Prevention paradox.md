Successful prevention makes a danger look as if it was never real, so averted risks (Y2K, the ozone hole, current model safety) get called overblown and the people who averted them lose recognition and support.

## Relationships

### [[../Lenses/The World Keeps Getting Saved]]
Defining source, informal: averted disasters (Y2K, ozone hole) get read as proof the danger was never real. Short, plain-spoken blog post that argues by examples: people notice events, not non-events, so prevention is invisible and its workers go unrewarded. It never uses the term 'prevention paradox', gives no data, and offers no way to tell an exaggerated danger from an averted one.
