# AI Safety and Philosophy

## Unit 1: Existential Risk, Uncertainty, and Action

**Philosophical question:** When should uncertain catastrophic possibilities change what we do?

**Safety decision:** When does concern about advanced AI justify substantial research, policy, funding, technical work, institutional preparation, or career effort?

**Capability:** By the end of this unit, participants should be able to identify the catastrophic pathway an argument relies on, distinguish several kinds of uncertainty within it, evaluate whether precaution or further information is justified, identify the strongest objections to prioritising the risk, and state what evidence would change their current action.

## Source coverage

The unit now draws on the original reading set and the later additions supplied for the revision. Major source clusters include:

- Nick Bostrom on existential risk, irreversibility, and future value
- Eliezer Yudkowsky on Pascal's Mugging and the Pascal's Wager Fallacy Fallacy
- Holden Karnofsky on explicit expected-value estimates, sequence versus cluster thinking, AI capability, and AI aims
- Hilary Greaves on cluelessness
- Sven Ove Hansson / the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy on risk, uncertainty, inductive risk, precaution, and safety engineering
- Rush T. Stewart on precaution under deep uncertainty and incommensurability
- Dan Hendrycks, Mantas Mazeika, and Thomas Woodside on catastrophic AI risk categories
- Jan Kulveit and coauthors on gradual disempowerment
- Atoosa Kasirzadeh on decisive and accumulative AI existential risk
- Torben Swoboda and coauthors on skeptical arguments against AI x-risk prioritisation
- Katja Grace on counterarguments to the basic AI x-risk case
- Center on Long-Term Risk and suffering-focused ethics materials on s-risks
- Brian Tomasik on near-miss alignment and soft-takeoff / suffering-focused considerations
- the Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament, Scott Alexander's discussion, and Forecasting Research Institute work on roots of disagreement and cruxes

This is a review draft on staging only. It is not a production course.
