%%
Target audience (people with one or more of these):
- Finished an intro course such as AI Risk Fundamentals and wants to find where they fit (Fit track)
- Finished an intermediate course and wants a field project in technical work, policy, or fieldbuilding (Field track)
- Wants a next step, but intermediate programs reject 80 to 90% of applicants
- Can give 3 to 5 hours per unit

Value prop:
- A peer-reviewed project you can share and a project card for applications, in five units
- One lean process for every track; only the artifact differs
- Structure, peers and the Lens Coach do the work that scarce mentors usually do; experts review at the end, not throughout

Tracks:
- Fit: a career doc (can stay private, shared with reviewers), a career transition post, or a "why I work on AI safety" statement. Grounded in short tasters of real work and conversations with people doing it.
- Field: a project in technical work, policy, or fieldbuilding.
%%

Unit 1 goals:
- Choose your track: Fit, or a field project
- Agree the AI use policy: AI for doing, never for deciding
- See what earlier participants made (example gallery) and browse the project bank
- Write the proposal canvas: a problem, a specific audience, an observable short-term change, and a $0 version (proposal draft 1)

Readings:
- Core: "Nailing the basics: theories of change" (already in Lens); example gallery; AI use policy
- Optional: Shevlane, "How to succeed as an early-stage researcher: the lean startup approach"; Hamming, You and Your Research; 80,000 Hours, "AI safety fieldbuilding" career review (already in Lens)
